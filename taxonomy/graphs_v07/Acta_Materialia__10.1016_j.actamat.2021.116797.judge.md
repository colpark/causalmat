# Judge review: Acta_Materialia/10.1016_j.actamat.2021.116797

**Verdict:** accept with fixes. This is a structure-only review. No figure or crop was opened, and no panel citation or technique was ruled on; every cited panel is checked separately by the blind second read.

After the fixes the graph has 54 nodes, 71 edges, 19 spine nodes and 5 audits.

## 1. Spine

One connected, stage-ordered path runs from n1 to n19, with the two parallel branches PROTOCOL.md allows:

**n1** (need: Mg's plastic anisotropy leaves the soft orientations yielding at a few MPa) -> **n2** (hypothesis: a minute Zn/Y co-addition forming LPSO-related nanoplates, not a perfect LPSO phase, should raise the yield stress) -> **n3** (DS single crystal, so the CRSS of each mode can be resolved by orientation), **n4** (Mg99.2Zn0.2Y0.6) and **n5** (four orientations x RT-400 C) -> **n6** (directional solidification).

- **structure branch:** n6 -> **n8** (the planar defects are LPSO nanoplates, not conventional faults) and n6 -feeds_into-> **n7** (520 C/5 h + WQ, then a further 400 C/5 h) -> **n9** (density and thickness, 9.8 per um / ~28 nm as-grown, ~0.3 / ~84 nm, ~9.0 / ~14 nm) -> **n12** (orientation-dependent yield stress).
- **deformation branch:** n5 -> **n10** (compress to ~5% strain, RT-400 C) -> **n11** (the wavy bands are kink bands: rotation angle 5-55 deg, not the fixed ~85 deg of twins) -> **n13** (mode set by orientation and temperature).

The two meet at **n14** (the CRSS partition: twinning and prismatic raised 5-10x, basal left at ~4 MPa) -> **n15** (twinning and non-basal slip must cut the (0001) plates; basal slip glides parallel) -> **n16** (kink banding takes up the plasticity) -> **n18**; n12 -> **n17**; n17 and n18 -> **n19** (conclusion). n15 also explains n12 and n16 explains n11, which is what closes the loop between the two branches.

Every spine node except n1 has an incoming spine edge by an allowed rel, and every spine STR/PRP/PRF/MEC claim has OBS evidence or a KNW premise: n8 (o2, o3 + n25), n9 (o4, o5), n11 (o10, o13, o14, o15, o16, o17 + n27), n12 (o6, o7, o20 + n29), n13 (o8, o9, o10, o12), n14 (o18 + n26), n15 (`basis: attributed`, with n20 as its geometric premise and n14 upstream), n16 (o21 + n28), n17 (o8 and, after the fix below, o7), n18 (o23, o24). OBS and KNW are never spine. 19 spine nodes, inside the 10-20 band.

Audits are exactly 5 - o19 (the basal CRSS is itself raised ~8x, which the text denies), o20 (the heat-treatment swing), n21, n22, n23 - and each bounds a spine claim, so the budget holds. There is no PRF beyond n17/n18 and no DSC beyond the conclusion, the comparison n30 and the three limitations; n30 is periphery, not spine, because the paper's headline is the strengthening, not the comparison with the 18R-LPSO crystal.

**v04 decision rules.** Calls I checked and left alone: n8 is `phase/identity` rather than `defect/extended` because what the claim fixes is which structure the contrast is (an LPSO-related nanoplate, a phase-level identity that the whole obstacle argument then uses), not a dislocation or fault density; the next inference is precipitate-style obstacle reasoning. n11 is `microstructure/orientation` (the reading is a misorientation, which is that leaf's own definition) while n13, the mode set, is `PRP/behavior_class` - the r04 rule "how it fails -> behavior_class" applies to how it deforms. n14 is `PRP/value` with `aspect: partition` exactly as r04 ruling #4 requires, not a separate partition leaf. n16 is `identification` and not `pathway` because kink banding is picked out of a known catalogue (n28: kink bands in the LPSO phase and in Zn single crystals, with boundaries of basal dislocations), which is the identification leaf broadened by ruling #26; n15 is a `pathway` built from the evidence. n10 is `PRC/stimulus` and not a PRF condition because the exposure produces an argued state (kink bands, twins, slip traces), which is ruling #36's test. o15 is `response/distribution` and not `signal/feature_quantification` because it is read from a plotted histogram, not from the raw map (the r04 rule for that pair).

## 2. Splits and merges

**No splits.** Three candidates, each refused with a reason:

- **n9** names two quantities (density and thickness). They rest on one panel (#F1f, both series), move together, and are used in one place (n9 -causes-> n12 through n15's obstacle spacing). Splitting would change no inference.
- **n13** names an orientation claim and a temperature claim. I nearly split it, and did not, for consistency: n14, the node it feeds, spans the same modes and the same temperatures in one claim, and that claim (the *selective* CRSS rise) cannot be split without destroying it. n5 makes orientation x temperature one comparison axis, so the mode set over that axis is one classification. The evidence differs by figure (o9/o10 for orientation, o12 for temperature) but both halves enter n14 and neither is used where the other is not.
- **n7** names two anneals. They are one designed experiment whose whole point is that it is reversible: n9 states the dissolve-and-recover cycle as a single claim, o4 reads both panels and o5 plots all three states. Splitting n7 would force splitting n9 and o5 and would break the reversibility that carries n9 -> n12.

I also checked o8, which reads three things off #F2b and #F2c (>5% strain in three orientations, [0001] fracturing without yielding, work hardening in [01-10] at RT). It stays one node: one act on one panel pair, with all three readings feeding the same two claims.

**One merge.** n12's trailing clause "and stays above 100 MPa at 400 C" was n17's claim ("keeps a yield stress above 100 MPa ... from RT to 400 C") almost word for word. n17 is the node whose type states performance under a use condition and which adds the deformability (>5% plastic strain) that n12 does not carry; n12 is now the level and its orientation dependence, which is what PRP/value is for. o7, the readout that actually carries the temperature retention, now evidences n17 as well as n12 (o7 -evidences-> n17, `read_trend`, added), so n17 no longer rests on o8 alone. No other duplicate pairs: the readout/claim pairs (o5/n9, o6+o7/n12, o18/n14, o22/n30) are observations and the claims they evidence, and n20 (where the plates are) is not n8 (what they are).

## 3. Source and read_from changes

Four source changes. The line I applied: caption facts that identify the panel - technique, specimen, condition, viewing direction, which panel is which - are how every packet in this batch works and belong in `image_note`; they do not by themselves force `source != figure`. A fact forces it when the label carries a value no panel yields, an identification made by an analysis that is absent from the packet, or a statement about material the packet never shows.

- **n12: figure -> text.** The "~11x the pure-Mg single crystal" in the label is the Fig. 2 linked text's factor (~12 MPa -> ~130 MPa). The panel's own alloy points read ~148-165 MPa at RT, and the gray pure-Mg points are not labelled by orientation, so the factor is not obtainable from #F2a. Both facts are now listed; o6's and o7's image_notes already recorded the conflict.
- **n13: figure -> text.** #F4a and #F4b print the trace-plane indices, but that the {10-10} traces are {10-10}<1-210> *prismatic slip* comes from a two-face trace analysis plus a TEM g-b contrast analysis, and that the straight {10-12} bands are *deformation twins* from ref. [33]. Neither analysis is in the packet.
- **n21: figure -> text.** #F3g and #F3h are the RT specimens; "no deformation traces at any temperature" rests on Supplemental Fig. S4, which the packet does not contain.
- **n30: figure -> text.** #F9 is a tier-B whole-figure crop with no OCR at all. The legend gives the comparator and the two angles, but reading 45 deg as "the basal-slip orientation" is the Fig. 9 linked text's argument.

Kept as figure, deliberately: **n20**, although "parallel to (0001)" follows from the caption's [11-20] viewing direction - that is panel identity, and the graph would otherwise have almost no figure-sourced node left; **n14, o18, o19**, although "pure Mg" for #F7b is a caption fact - the 5-10x ratio is computable from two panels that share identical axes; **n11**, because the discriminating reading (a continuous 5-55 deg spread against a fixed 80-90 deg population) is in #F5c, #F5d and #F5g, "kink band" is written on #F5f, and the 86 deg twin ideal is supplied by n27, a KNW premise, which is the graph's own mechanism for an external fact.

Five read_from changes, all **axis -> annotation**, under one rule: annotation when a string listed under the panel's `annotations` supplies the class or the series identity the node asserts; axis when the listed strings are axis titles or category tick labels and the reading is a number against the axes; pixels when the listed strings are only scale bars, panel letters or orientation markers.

- **o6** ("fracture", #F2a): the node asserts that [0001] fractures without yielding, and that is the printed string beside those points.
- **o7** ("gray plots:", "pure-Mg", #F2a): the whole reading is the comparison with pure Mg, and the gray series is identified only by that in-plot label.
- **o8** ("atRT", "at300C", "[01TO]", #F2b/#F2c): which panel is RT and which 300 C, and which curve work-hardens, are printed; the strain axis itself carries no ticks, only a 1% bar.
- **o18** and **o19** ("{10T2}twinning", "{1100}prismatic slip", "(0001)basalslip", #F7a/#F7b): the nodes read a CRSS *per deformation mode*, and each curve's mode is an in-plot label.

Kept as axis: o5 and o20, whose listed strings ("as-grown", "anneal", "nanoplates", "perlum", "(nm)") are x-axis categories and fragments of the two axis titles, with the readings being the plotted numbers; o15 and o22, whose panels list no annotations (#F9 has no OCR). Kept as pixels: o13, o16 and o24 - "side surface" on #F4a/#F4b, "basal plane trace" on #F6a and "[0001] tensile direction" on #F10b are orientation and viewing markers that say where the reader is looking, while the nodes read the step-like ridge, the varying bend of the marked trace and the inclined band set; and o1-o4, o9-o11, whose panels list nothing but garbled scale bars, or (for #F3d-f) garbled forms of "deformation band" that the nodes do not restate. Note for the record that the OCR classification is uneven here: "0002" on #F1b/#F1d/#F1e and the trace-plane indices on #F3c-f are clearly written in the image but are not classified as annotations, while the same indices on #F4a/#F4b are. I applied the packet's own `annotations` lists rather than the raw token lists, and o12, o14, o17 and o21 were already annotation on that basis and stay so.

## 4. Mode changes

n12 is the only claim with two or more incoming `causes` edges (n9 and n31), and **both were "alternative" and are now "joint"**. The v07 rule admits "alternative" only when a caption shows the choice. No caption in the packet names solid-solution hardening at all; the Fig. 8 caption reports only "Variation in the yield stress with heat treatment", and the old `mode_basis` on both edges cited Fig. 8(a) - a figure, not a caption.

Joint is also the better physics. o19, the text-silent audit, shows the basal CRSS itself rose from ~0.5 to ~4.3 MPa, which basal slip cannot get from plates it glides parallel to, so the solute contribution does act; o20 shows it is not what carries the 167 -> 113 -> 160 MPa swing (the 520 C anneal dissolves the plates and so puts *more* Zn/Y into solution, yet the stress falls). Two consequential edits follow: n31's label loses its exclusive framing and now reads "Zn and Y retained in solid solution also harden the matrix, raising even the basal-slip CRSS", with a note that the paper attributes the whole increase to the nanoplates; and **o20 -> n31 changes from `rules_out` to `qualifies`**, because o20 bounds that contribution rather than rejecting it. The audit count is unchanged at 5.

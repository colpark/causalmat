### F1 [Others; microscopic=False]
Engineering 2D Multifunctional Ultrathin Bismuthene for Multiple Photonic Nanomedicine  2D monoelemental nanomaterials (Xenes) have shown tremendous potential for versatile biomedical applications. Bismuth, as a heavy element in pnictogens, has acquired massive research interest due to its unique optical performance, high biocompatibility, stability, and relatively low cost. However, the utilization of 2D bismuthene in nanomedicine has not been achieved because of the difficulty in engineering bismuthene with crucial structural/ compositional characteristics for satisfying strict biomedical requirements. Herein, to address this Gordian knot, a facile strategy to intercalate and delaminate Bi bulk for generating mass few-layered 2D bismuthene with high yield by employing a water molecule mediated freezing–thawing process and sodium borohydride-triggered reduction treatment is proposed. The resulting 2D bismuthene displays good optical performance in the near-infrared (NIR) biowindow and can be excited via red light for reactive oxygen species generation, enabling applications in multiple photonic cancer nanomedicine settings, including photothermal hyperthermia and photodynamic therapy. Utilizing the intrinsic desirable optical absorbance and strong X-ray attenuation of bismuthene, dual photonic therapy can be conducted under the supervision of photoacoustic/computed tomography guided multimodal imaging. This research not only offers a potential mass-production ready, cost-effective, and eco-efficient methodology for engineering 2D Xenes, but also exploits an innovative 2D bismuthene based photonic cancer nanomedicine.  According to the first-principle calculations on Bi with puckered layered crystal structure via the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP), the average distance between the layers of lamellar structure Bi is $3.979\mathring\mathrm{A}_{\cdot}$ and its lattice para­meters are calculated to be $a=$ $b=4.546\mathrm{~\AA~}$ and $c=11.802\mathrm{~\AA~}.$ [33] The channels along the $\chi$ -axis are calculated to be $5.62\textup{\AA}$ , which are spacious enough to accommodate water molecule $(\approx3.2\mathrm{\mathring{A}})$ ,[37] while the channels along the γ-axis are only 0.71 Å.[33] Besides, reducing the thickness of bulk heavy pnictogen can convert the metal Bi into semiconducting materials,[32] which are capable of acting as an intriguing photosensitizer to generate reactive oxygen species (ROS). On this ground, we herein develop a facile and exquisite physical exfoliation strategy for engineering 2D bismuthene nanosheets via water molecules mediated freezing and thawing process, which is adapted from liquid nitrogen mediated cryogenic exfoliation strategy.[38] In addition, sodium borohydride $\mathrm{(NaBH_{4})}$ was employed for efficiently controlling the reduction of oxygen groups of the bismuthene surface, as illustrated in Figure  1a. The illusory van der Waals’ attraction between the layers in Bi bulks can be tactfully broken by the volume expansion from liquid $\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ molecule to solid ice, which generates strong extrusion force during the process of phase transformation. This physical exfoliation strategy for bismuthene fabrication is ingenious and straightforward, which dispenses with supererogatory chemical reactions, superfluous impurities, and irrelevant disturbances. After that, we conceived a robust theranostic nanoplatform by embellishing 2D bismuthene nanosheets with biocompatible bovine serum albumin (BNs-BSA) for multimodal imaging guided synergistic photonic treatment (photodynamic therapy [PDT] and PTT) of tumor xenograft on mice (Figure 1b). Although monoelemental Bi nanomaterials, such as ultrasmall Bi nanoparticles $(3.6~\mathrm{nm})^{[27]}$ and Bi nanodots $(2.7\mathrm{nm})$ ),[26] have been reported for dual-modal CT/PA guided cancer treatment (PTT and RT), the ultrathin 2D structure of Xene endows monoelemental bismuthene with fresh vitality, which is reflected as high specific surface area for potential drugs delivery and excitable semiconductor properties for PDT to hit a new spark in oncological nanomedicine. Herein, we systematically studied the optical-absorption property, biocompatibility, photothermal-conversion performance, and ROS generation capability of 2D bismuthene as the photonic theranostic agent both in vitro and in vivo. As expected, the obtained BNs-BSA displayed outstanding photothermal-conversion efficacy of $19.4\%$ and powerful ROS-production capacity, which could destroy cancer cells and induce complete tumor eradication. Notably, no obvious toxicity was observed at the designed doses after intravenously injected into mice by the histology examination and routine blood chemistry measurements. 
### F2 [Characterizing Structure; microscopic=True]
The synthesis of 2D ultrathin bismuthene was conducted via a facile strategy involving two crucial procedures: i) water molecule mediated freezing–thawing process and ii) $\mathrm{NaBH_{4}}^{}$ triggered reduction treatment, which is an ultrafacile, lowcost, and organic solvent free synthetic strategy. The detailed morphological evolution and structural features of as-prepared bismuthene were characterized by transmission electron microscopy (TEM), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and atomic force microscopy (AFM). Like graphite, SEM image of Bi bulk crystal exhibits the compacted topology (Figure S1a, Supporting Information). After one cycle of freezing and thawing process, the tightly stacked layers expand and generate an accordion-like multi-layered morphology (Figure S1b,c, Supporting Information). TEM images reveal that homogeneous few- or single-layered 2D ultrathin bismuthene oxide nanosheets are stripped off from Bi bulk crystal triumphantly after five cycles of repeated freezing and thawing accompanied with ultrasonic processing (Figure  2a), which feature the typical free-standing nanosheet morphology with relatively high transparency and lateral size of ${\approx}200\mathrm{nm}$ . The energy dispersive X-ray spectro­ scopy (EDS) and corresponding element mapping further confirm the coexistence and homogeneous distributions of Bi and O elements on the surface (Figure S2, Supporting Information), indicating inevitable oxidation in the process of exfoliation procedure. The selective area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern signifies the high crystallinity of bismuthene oxide, coincided with cubic lattice system with the lattice spacing of $0.274~\mathrm{nm}$ along the [321] preferential orientation (Figure S3, Supporting Information). To further explore the water molecule mediated freezing–thawing with ultrasonication treatment, we prepared the products via various strategies, including i) only bath ultrasonication, ii) only freezing-thawing without ultrasonication, and iii) combination of freezing–thawing with bath ultrasonication. As shown in Figure S4, Supporting Information, bath sonication only broke the Bi bulk samples into smaller fragments, which was subjected to ultrasonic cavitation. However, almost no obvious thickness reduction can be observed. The Bi bulk dispersion was placed into the refrigerator at $-80~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for the freezing and swelling of intercalated water, showing large freestanding bismuthene with high transparency and emphasizing the importance of slow freezing and expansion for thickness reduction. The combination of freezing–thawing with bath ultrasonication could effectively exfoliate Bi bulk into small flake of ${\approx}200~\mathrm{nm}$ in size, indicating that sonication not only boosts the yield of exfoliation but also decreases flake size, which is critical for biomedical applications, particularly for precise tumor theranostics. The results prove that repeated freezing and thawing process could conquer the interlayer bond of Bi bulk and the ultrasonic wave with low energy could assist the inerratic breaking of Bi nanosheets with a wet finger. The bismuthene with large lamellar structure by single freezing–thawing cyclic treatments may have potential applications in other domains, such as catalysis. In fact, as shown in Table S1, Supporting Information, sonochemical exfoliation of Xenes often employs probe ultrasonication with higher power and longer time, which would transmit ultrasonic energy to bulk more directly than bath sonication. However, common probe ultrasonication method failed in the synthesis of bismuthene for nanomedicine as shown in Figure S5, Supporting Information. It is noted that, after $\mathrm{NaBH_{4}}$ treatment, bismuthene shows distinct lamellar morphology in SEM image (Figure 2b) and $5{-}25~\mathrm{nm}$ ultra-small nanoflakes have grown onto the surface of 2D bismuthene oxide, which distribute uniformly to form heterojunction (Figure  2c). The bright-field and dark-field scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) images combined with corresponding EDS mapping corroborate that the nanoflakes are mainly composed of Bi element (Figure  2d–f). The atomic-resolution STEM in high-angle annular dark-field (HAADF) mode reveals that the as-grown Bi layer is featured with different crystalline structures and the lattice spacing is measure to be 0.227 nm with [110] preferential orientation (Figure  2g,h). The SAED pattern signifies that the as-constructed nanosheets are polycrystalline composite structure (Figure  2i). The average topographic height of bismuthene was acquired to be ${\approx}14$ nm by AFM (Figure 2j), which exhibits prominent lamellar structure.  The structural identification and transformation process of bismuthene are demonstrated via X-ray diffraction (XRD) and Raman scattering techniques. According to the XRD patterns (Figure 2k), all diffraction peaks of bismuthene oxide are consistent with the standard $\mathrm{Bi}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3}$ (JCPDS No. 45-1344) and the typical diffraction peaks of bismuthene are well indexed to the standard hexagonal PDF card (JCPDS No. 05-0519) of Bi. The scattering peak of bismuthene at $39.6^{\circ}$ represents for the [110] face with a $0.227\mathrm{nm}$ lattice spacing, which is in accordance with the XRD consequence of bulk Bi. Additional diffraction peak at $32.6^{\circ}$ appears in the XRD pattern of bismuthene that pertains to the [321] face in $\mathrm{Bi}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3}$ (JCPDS No. 45-1344), further demonstrating that the triumphant constitution of heterojunction structure is constructed in the as-obtained bismuthene. Like the Raman spectrum of the Bi bulk, including in-plane vibrational and out-of-plane vibrational mode, the characteristic Raman peaks of bismuthene locate at 68.9 and $95.1~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ (Figure  2l), indicating that the as-obtained nanosheets hold semblable structure of bulk Bi.[35] Additionally, a slight blue shift of bismuthene is most likely attributed to the variation of topographic thickness from micron level order to nanoscale.[39] The composition and chemical state are further investigated through X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS), showing the existence of Bi and O elements without other impurities both in bismuthene and bismuthene oxide (Figure S6, Supporting Information). Furthermore, the relevant normalized high-resolution spectrum of Bi 4f reveals that the peaks at the binding energies of 164.2 and $158.9~\mathrm{eV}$ are assigned to $\mathrm{Bi^{3+}}$ in $\mathrm{Bi}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3}$ , and the peaks at 162.1 and $156.8~\mathrm{eV}$ are corresponding to $\mathrm{Bi^{0}}$ in Bi layers.[33,40] As a result, according to the analysis of XRD, TEM, and XPS, bismuthene was oxidized inevitably during the process and deoxidizer was introduced to reverse oxidation, where the $\mathrm{Bi}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3}$ :Bi ratio was estimated to be 12:5 based on the calculation of the integrated area of $\mathrm{Bi^{3+}}$ and $\mathrm{Bi^{0}}$ 4f peaks. The bismuthene dispersion is black and exhibits colloidal properties with the typical Tyndall effect (Figure $\mathrm{S7a}$ , Supporting Information). The size distribution as confirmed by dynamic light scattering (DLS) in aqueous solutions indicates that the average hydrodynamic diameters of bismuthene are normally distributed around $250~\mathrm{nm}$ (Figure S7b, Supporting Information). 
### F3 [Characterizing Property; microscopic=False]
1,3-Diphenylisobenzofuran (DPBF), a commonly used probe molecule to detect ROS generation by the decay of the absorbance at $410\mathrm{nm},^{[44]}$ was next employed to reveal the ${}^{1}\mathrm{O}_{2}$ production of photo-activated bismuthene. It is obvious that the absorbance at $410\mathrm{nm}$ of the DPBF was gradually weakened with the prolongation of irradiation time of $660~\mathrm{nm}$ laser at $100\mathrm{mW~cm}^{-2}$ , while a slight decline was observed in pure deionized (DI) water with the same laser irradiation, demonstrating the remarkable photodynamic performance of ultrathin bismuthene (Figure 3a,b; Figure S11, Supporting Information).  Furthermore, on account of the prominent optical-absorption capacity of bismuthene at NIR biowindow (Figure S8, Supporting Information), its photothermal-conversion performance at ${808}\mathrm{nm}$ was assessed. According to the Lambert–Beer law, the extinction coefficient $(\alpha)$ of bismuthene at $808~\mathrm{nm}$ is calculated to be $6.4\mathrm{L}\mathrm{g}^{-1}\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ (Figure 3c),[45] much higher than that of certain representative photothermal agents such as graphene oxide $(3.6\mathrm{~L~g~}^{-1}\mathrm{cm}^{-1})$ ,[46] indicating that 2D bismuthene could serve as a desirable photothermal agent for light-to-heat conversion. The temperature of bismuthene aqueous dispersions $(200~\upmu\mathrm{g}~\mathrm{mL}^{-1})$ elevated from 31.7 to $57.7~^{\circ}C$ when irradiated via $808~\mathrm{nm}$ laser $(1.5\mathrm{W}\mathrm{cm}^{-2})$ for 6 min (Figure 3d). Comparatively, unnoticeable temperature change of DI water was monitored at the same power density. With increased bismuthene concentrations, the temperature of bismuthene aqueous dispersion can increase by $7.4~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $25~\upmu\mathrm{g}~\mathrm{mL}^{-1}$ , $11.3~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $50~\upmu\mathrm{g}~\mathrm{mL}^{-1}$ , $16.3~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $100\upmu\mathrm{g}\mathrm{mL}^{-1}$ , and $27.3~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $200\upmu\mathrm{g}\mathrm{mL}^{-1}$ in 6 min (Figure 3d,e). In addition, the enhanced photothermal performance can also be achieved by reinforcing radiation power density (Figure 3f,g), further demonstrating the regulable performance of bismuthene in photothermal conversion. The photothermal conversion efficiency $(\eta)$ of the bismuthene was calculated to be $19.4\%$ (Figure 3h),[45] higher than copper nanowires $(12.5\%)$ ,[47] Au nanoshells $(13\%),^{[48]}$ and close to that of traditional graphene-oxide $(22\%)$ .[49] Additionally, neglectable deterioration of the temperature elevation was observed during the cycles of laser on/off irradiation (Figure  3i), highlighting the photo­ stability and thermostability of 2D bismuthene. The photothermal conversion efficiency $(\eta)$ of the bismuthene is less than some types of Bi-based nanoparticles, such as ${\mathrm{Bi}}_{2}{\mathrm S}_{3}$ nanorods $(\approx28.1\%)$ ,[50] $\mathrm{Bi}_{2}\mathrm{Se}_{3}$ spherical sponge $(\approx31.1\%)$ ,[51] $\mathrm{Bi}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{3}{\cdot}\mathrm{Au}$ heterojunction nanorods $(\approx51.06\%)$ ,[52] and hydrophilic flower-like ${\mathrm{Bi}}_{2}{\mathrm{S}}_{3}$ nanoparticles $(\approx64.3\%)$ ,[53] which could be attributed to the ultrathin sheet nanostructure, single component, and partial oxidation of as-obtained bismuthene. However, the partially oxidized component and the reducing thickness endow bismuthene with the semiconductor properties, which is potential photosensitizer for multiple photonic treatment to combine PDT and PTT. Moreover, the specific 2D structure arms bismuthene with high specific surface area, which provides more space and binding sites to load some small molecular drugs for prospective chemotherapy. Therefore, the as-prepared bismuthene only sacrifices its photothermal conversion capacity to some degree for more possibilities of multifunctional photonic and potential drugs-synergetic nanomedicine. 
### F4 [Revealing the influence from Property to Performance; microscopic=False]
Prior to subsequent treatment in vivo, we systematically evaluated the in vivo biocompatibility and biodistribution of BNsBSA via intravenous injection. Mice were intravenously injected with BNs-BSA, sacrificed after 7, 14, 21, and 28 days, and then collected their main organs and blood for relatively long-term toxicity assessment. In the four-week period, compared to control group, no obvious alterations in behavior and no significant abnormalities in body weight was monitored (Figure S15a, Supporting Information), which indicated no acute toxicity of BNs-BSA in vivo. Blood routine and blood biochemical indices evidenced no noticeable sign of inflammation or pathological damage to the mice (Figure 5a; Figure S15b, Supporting Information). In addition, no notable histological abnormality was observed in main organs (Figures S16–S19, Supporting Information). The biodistribution of BNs-BSA in major organs and tumor was conducted by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) analysis, showing that the BNs-BSA could rapidly accumulate at tumor within $^{4\textrm{h}}$ and attain the maximum tumor uptake of $4.13\pm0.10\%$ ID ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ (Figure S20, Supporting Information). Moreover, the biodegradation is vital for guaranteeing the in vivo biosafety, especially for the inorganic nanomaterials. It was noted that BNs-BSA nanosheets displayed an obvious time-dependent degradation effect and about $34\%$ BNs-BSA nanosheets were degraded within $^{18\mathrm{~h~}}$ in simulative physiological environment (Figure  S21, Supporting Information).  Advanced diagnostic techniques play a vital role in state-ofthe-art cancer treatment.[55] The excellent NIR absorbance of BNs-BSA endows them with high potential for photoacoustic (PA) imaging (Figure  5b), which is a noninvasive biomedicalimaging modality with reasonable penetration depth and excellent spatial resolution/sensitivity.[56] The BNs-BSA can absorb and convert optical energy into heat, which results in subsequent transient thermoelastic expansion and induces the corresponding acoustic pressure waves in the tissues. The signal could be detected and analyzed by ultrasound transducers to form images. The PA signal intensities of BNs-BSA are linearly increased with the elevated nanosheets concentrations (Figure  5c,e). Subsequently, obvious PA signals are observed in the tumor area after both intravenous and intratumor injection into 4T1 tumor-bearing mice (Figure  5d,f; Figure S22, Supporting Information). The PA intensity $(\approx0.94$ a.u.) of BNs-BSA after $4\mathrm{~h~}$ accumulation achieves the maximum value, which is 3.6-fold stronger than that before injection. After that, the PA intensities exhibit tardy decline, possibly owing to the degradation or oxidation of BNs-BSA in tumor section. Encouraged by the superior radiant energy absorption coefficient of Bi element,[57] we further investigated the potential application of BNs-BSA as CT contrast agents in vivo (Figure 5b). The accumulation of BNs-BSA in tumor section provides contrastenhanced CT signals under the irradiation of X-rays, and CT intensities in the tumor region increase over time (Figure 5g,h). The results suggest the high potential of BNs-BSA as a desirable PA and CT dual-modality imaging contrast agents to guide and monitor the photo-therapeutic procedure, which is essential for safe multiple photonic tumor therapy. 
### F5 [Revealing the influence from Processing to Performance; microscopic=False]
In vivo therapeutic evaluation was performed on $\mathtt{B A L B/c}$ female mice with subcutaneous 4T1 breast cancer xenografts, which were randomly divided into six groups, including PBS (group 1), BNs-BSA (group 2), lasers $(100\mathrm{mW}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ for $660\mathrm{nm}$ , 1 W $\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ for $808~\mathrm{nm}$ , group 3), BNs-B $\mathrm{SA}+660\mathrm{nm}$ laser $(100~\mathrm{mW~cm}^{-2}$ , group 4 for PDT), $\mathrm{BNs{\cdot}B S A}+808\mathrm{nm}$ laser $(1~\mathrm{W}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , group 5 for PTT), and $\mathrm{BNs{\cdot}B S A}+660/808~\mathrm{nm}$ lasers $\mathrm{[100mW~cm^{-2}}$ for $660\mathrm{nm}$ , $1\mathrm{W}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ for $808~\mathrm{nm}$ , group 6 for synergy). When the size of tumors reached $100~\mathrm{mm}^{3}$ , the mice were anesthetized and carried out with different treatments as specified in Figure  6a. Meanwhile, thermal images and tumor-site temperature were recorded using an infrared thermal camera (Figure  6b,c). Upon $808~\mathrm{nm}$ laser irradiation, the local temperature of tumor rapidly increased to more than $49~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ within $5\mathrm{min}$ , which is sufficiently high to ablate the malignant cells. In contrast, the tumor temperature of the mice treated with PBS only increased about $3~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ under laser irradiation within 5 min.  After various treatments, the tumor volume (Figure  6d,f) and body weight (Figure 6e) of each mouse were recorded every other day and the photos of tumor sections were recorded every 7 days (Figure S23, Supporting Information). No abnormality was detected in the body weight of all mice during the treatment period (Figure  6e), indicating no obvious side effect of these therapeutic modalities. The PDT treatment resulted in tumor regression of $33.4\%$ compared with the untreated group and the mice in this group died gradually (Figure  6g). The tumor xenografts in photothermal ablation group were superficially thermally eliminated at the early stage but regrettably reappeared after 14 days (Figure $_\mathrm{6d,f,h}$ ; Figure S23, Supporting Information), the relative tumor inhibition rate of which was $86.6\%$ . Importantly, the mice treated with BNs-BSA followed by dual lasers (808 and $660\mathrm{nm}$ ) irradiation completely inhibited tumor growth during the investigation period, which was originated from the synergistic therapeutic effect of thermal ablation and ROS generation. It is gratifying that the relative tumor inhibition rate and 54-day-survival rate of mice in the synergistic therapy group reached $100\%$ , which were much higher than that of the other groups (Figure $6\mathrm{f-h}$ ; Figure S24, Supporting Information), indicating that photodynamic tumor therapy combined with photothermal ablation could significantly prolong the survival time of tumor-bearing mice.  Significant histological damages and excellent tumor-cell inhibition in the tumor sections were recognized by the hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining, transferase dUTP nick end labelling (TUNEL) staining, and Ki-67 immunohistochemistry staining, respectively (Figure  6i). Tumor cells in treatment group were shrinking and shattered in different degrees, especially in the synergetic treatment. Besides, the maximal area of apoptosis and the lowest proliferation level were observed in the same group, which were consistent with the above in vivo tests. All these results demonstrate that the rational combination of multiple photonic treatment (PDT and PTT) as enabled by the constructed 2D BNs-BSA could achieve strengthened and synergistic therapeutic outcome as compared to either single photothermal hyperthermia or PDT treatment.

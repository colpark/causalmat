### F3 [Characterizing Processing; microscopic=True]
Further analysis was performed by DSC test. Fig. 3 shows the DSC curves of as-cast three alloys. Two major endothermic peaks present on the DSC curves of Al-Cu-Mn and Al-Cu-Mn-Sm alloys. Considering the temperature at which the peaks appear and their sequence, it is thought that the two endothermic peaks are closely related to dissolving of ternary eutectic phase and $\mathfrak { a }$ -Al dendrite. Nevertheless, the peak of dissolution of ternary eutectic phase disappears for Al-Cu-Mn-La alloy. In addition, there are a weak peak A and strong peak B in Al-Cu-Mn-La alloy. On the basis of the results of SEM and XRD, it is believed that peak A and peak B correspond to the dissolution of $\mathsf { A l } _ { 2 } \mathsf { C u }$ phase and $\mathrm { { A l } _ { 6 } C u _ { 6 } L a }$ phase, respectively. 
### F4 [Characterizing Structure; microscopic=True]
TEM bright field images of the aged three alloys viewed along $[ 1 0 0 ] _ { \mathrm { A l } }$ electron beam direction are shown in Figs. $4 ( \mathrm { a } ) \mathrm { \sim } ( \mathrm { c } )$ . The images reveal that the dominant $\theta ^ { \ast }$ precipitate with the morphology of platelet on $\{ 0 0 1 \} _ { \mathrm { A l } }$ planes are uniformly distributed in the three alloys. In comparison with the RE-free alloy, addition of RE elements results in a considerable increase in the density of $\theta ^ { \prime }$ precipitate and much finer in size. From Figs. 4(b) and (c), it is shown that La addition has a larger contribution to the fineness and densification of  the $\theta ^ { \prime }$ phase than Sm addition.  From the above results, rare earth addition can change the size, density of the predominant hardening precipitates $\theta ^ { \prime }$ phase and mechanical properties. However, La addition has much better effect on the morphology and mechanical properties than $\mathrm { S m }$ addition. TEM analysis shows that the $\theta ^ { \prime }$ phase in Al-Cu-Mn-La alloy becomes denser and finer, suggesting that La addition can promote the precipitation of the $\theta ^ { \prime }$ phase. The diameter of La atom $( 0 . 1 8 7 ~ \mathrm { n m } )$ is larger than that of Al atom $( 0 . 1 4 3 ~ \mathrm { n m } )$ , which affects the nucleation of precipitates in the alloy [6]. When La atoms enter the Al matrix, they induce severe lattice distortions, thus increase system energy. To sustain a low energy system, more oversaturated vacancies may aggregate around La atoms. Because of their high binding energy of vacancies, La atom is much easier to migrate and provide the nucleation sites for the formation of $\theta ^ { \prime }$ phase. Fang et al. [8] believed that higher lattice distortion energy and vacancy energy induced by La atoms can provide the driving force for the nucleation of the $\theta ^ { \prime }$ phase. Hence, $\theta ^ { \prime }$ phase prefer to nucleate near La atoms, resulting in the increased density of $\theta ^ { \prime }$ phases. Moreover, the finer and more uniform $\mathrm { { A l } _ { 6 } C u _ { 6 } L a }$ phase in the Al-Cu-Mn-La alloy can strengthen the boundaries of $\mathfrak { a }$ -Al dendrites, which has some contributions to the improvement on the tensile strength.  Sm atoms is lower than that of La atoms. Therefore, the density of the $\theta ^ { \prime }$ phase is lower and, thus, the tensile strength of Al- $\mathbf { \cdot } \mathbf { C u - M n - S m }$ alloy is less than that of Al- $\mathrm { C u }$ -Mn-La alloy.

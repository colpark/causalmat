### F2 [Characterizing Structure; microscopic=False]
To reveal the excellent electrochemical performances of the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\mathrm{NC}(\varpi\mathrm{N}{\cdot}\mathrm{RGO}$ the hybrid material was well characterized using different techniques, including X-ray absorption near edge structure (XANES), scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM), energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX), and element mapping. Compared to N-RGO, $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\mathrm{NC}@\mathrm{N}{\cdot}\mathrm{RGO}$ displays an obvious increase of C K-edge peak intensity at ca. 285 and ca. $288~\mathrm{eV,}$ corresponding to carbon atoms in graphene bonded with nitrogen and oxygen species. This indicates the possible generation of interfacial $\mathrm{Sn{-}N{-}C}$ and $\mathrm{Sn{-}O{-}C}$ bonds in ${\mathrm{SnO}}_{2}{\mathrm{NC}}@{\mathrm{N}}$ -RGO (Figure 2f).[43] Moreover, an apparent decrease of $\mathrm{Sn~L}_{3}$ -edge peak intensity was observed in the ${\mathrm{SnO}}_{2}{\mathrm{NC}}@{\mathrm{N}}-1$ RGO electrode is excellent, showing a reversible charge capacity as high as $1346~\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ after 500 cycles. The increase of capacity in Figure 3c may be attributed to the improvement of lithium ion accessibility in the hybrid during the cycling process, which leads to an increased accommodation behavior for lithium.[53] Moreover, the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\mathrm{NC}\textcircled{a}.$ N-RGO electrode also exhibits excellent rate performance (Figure 3d). As the current densities increase from 0.5 to 1, 2, 5, 10, and $20\mathrm{A~g^{-1}}$ , the electrode shows good capacity retention, varying from 1074 to 994, 915, 782, 631, and $417\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{hg}^{-1}$ , respectively. Remarkably, when the current density returns to $0.5\mathrm{Ag}^{-1}$ the charge capacity reverts to $1034~\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ . In control experiments, we prepared the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\mathrm{NC}@\mathrm{RGO}$ hybrid material, $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\mathrm{NC}+\mathrm{N}-$ RGO mixture, $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\mathrm{NC}/\mathrm{N}$ -RGO-HS composite, $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ nanocrystals, and N-RGO (synthetic procedures are described in detail in the Experimental Section). TEM and HRTEM images of the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\mathrm{NC}@\mathrm{RGO}$ , $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\mathrm{NC}~+~\mathrm{N{-RGO}}$ the hybrid compared with pure $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ nanocrystals (Figure S7, Supporting Information), suggesting a higher electron density at the $\mathrm{Sn}$ site in the hybrid.43,54] This result further confirms the formation of $\mathrm{Sn{-N}}$ bonds in the hybrid, which is probably indispensable for the high and stable electrochemical performance of the hybrid. Figures 4b and $^{\mathrm{g}}$ show the EDX spectra of the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\mathrm{NC}@\mathrm{N}{\cdot}\mathrm{RGO}$ before cycling and after 500 cycles. A limited increase in the intensity ratio of oxygen to tin from 0.87 to 1.31 refects that a stable SEI layer formed on the ${\mathrm{SnO}}_{2}{\mathrm{NC}}@{\mathrm{N}}.$ - RGO because the major component of the SEI is $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{CO}_{3}$ ,[55] Combined with the STEM images, the carbon, oxygen, and tin element mappings reveal that the homogeneous distribution of $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ nanocrystals in graphene sheets is maintained even after 500 cycles (Figures 4a,c-f,h-j), suggesting the suppression of the aggregation of Sn nanoparticles during lithium insertion. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was also performed for the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\mathrm{NC}@\mathrm{N}$ -RGO electrode before cycling and after 500 cycles (Figure S8, Supporting Information). In Nyquist plots the diameter of the semicircle for the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\mathrm{NC}@\mathrm{N}{\cdot}\mathrm{RGO}$ electrode at high frequency does not increase much even after 500 cycles, thus suggesting that the stable SEI layer might form on the surface of the electrode in the initial cycles.[5] The kinetic differences of the ${\mathrm{SnO}}_{2}{\mathrm{NC}}@{\mathrm{N}}$ - RGO electrode before and after 500 cycles were further surveyed by modeling the EIS spectra based on the modified Randles equivalent circuit (Figure S9, Supporting Information).56] The values of the film resistance $R_{\mathrm{f}}$ of the ${\mathrm{SnO}}_{2}{\mathrm{NC}}@{\mathrm{N}}$ -RGO electrode before and after 500 cycles are 7.4 and $10.1~\Omega$ , respectively (see Table S1, Supporting Information). The result confirms that the graphene sheets could not only preserve the high conductivity of the bulk electrode, but also significantly enhance the electrochemical activity of $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ nanocrystals during the cycling process.41] Furthermore, SEM images of the electrode before and after 500 cycles confirm the formation of the SEI layer on the surface of the electrode (Figure S10, Supporting Information). The HRTEM image of the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\mathrm{NC}@\mathrm{N}$ -RGO electrode after 500 cycles shows that Sn nanoparticles exist in the cycled electrode, as confirmed by the SAED pattern, which displays a polycrystalline structure of Sn (Figure S10, Supporting Information). This suggests that the first reaction between lithium and $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ is not totally reversible, resulting in the generation of Sn nanoparticles and the initial capacity loss. 
### F3 [Characterizing Property; microscopic=False]
The high capacity and rate capability as well as excellent cycling performance of the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}\mathrm{NC}(\varpi\mathrm{N}{\cdot}\mathrm{RGO}$ hybrid material could be attributed to the uniform $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ nanocrystals bound in the graphene sheets and the micropores formed between graphene sheets and $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ nanocrystals, which made $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ nanocrystals electrochemically active since the electrons could be rapidly transported to $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ nanocrystals via the highly conducting graphene network and the lithium ions could effectively diffuse to $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ nanocrystals through micropores. The graphene coating also accommodates the enormous volume change of the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ nanocrystals and restricts the growth of the SEI layer. The bond formed between $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ nanocrystals and graphene avoids the aggregation of $\mathrm{Sn}$ nanoparticles during cycling. All of these points lead to the long-term cycling stability of the hybrid material.

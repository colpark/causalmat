- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In the present study, the macrostructural variations of the SLMfabricated AlSi10Mg alloy samples with various sizes have been systematically investigated. In the all samples, the columnar $\alpha$ -Al grains (initially solid) surrounded by fine eutectic Si particles (corresponding to the Si-rich liquid with a lower melting temperature) were observed in the melt pools (Fig. 5 and Fig. 8). These microstructural characteristics correspond well to the solidification sequence on the basis of the calculated phase diagram (Fig. 2(a)), indicating the observed microstructure could develop through local melting and rapid solidification by the SLM process [20,21]. One of important findings in this study is that finely distributed Si particles were observed inside the columnar α- Al grains of the smaller-sized samples (as shown in Figs. 8(c) and (d)). Their microstructural morphology was similar to that of the SLM-fabricated $\mathsf { A l S i 1 0 M g }$ alloy annealed at elevated temperatures [21,30]. Thus, it was assumed that the fine Si phase could precipitate inside the columnar $\mathtt { a - A l }$ grains during the SLM process. The observed microstructures (Fig. 8) were consistent with the higher number densities (Fig. 10(b)) and area fractions (Fig. 10(c)) of the Si particles in the smaller-sized samples. Furthermore, the difference between the measured and calculated fractions of the Si phase decreased with a decrease in the sample width (Fig. 10(c)), indicating that the content of the solute Si (in supersaturation) in the $\alpha$ -Al matrix was lower for the smallersized samples. The obtained results were in reasonably good agreement with the assumption that the precipitation of the Si phase during SLM occurred more often in the smaller-sized samples at elevated temperatures. Consequently, the presence of fine Si precipitates in smaller-sized samples indicates that samples have been exposed to elevated temperatures for a prolonged period during the SLM procedure.  One of the possible contributors to the sample size dependence of the microstructure of the SLM-fabricated AlSi10Mg alloy is its low thermal conductivity at the boundaries between the manufactured (locally melted and rapidly solidified) portions and adjacent (unmelted) alloy powder. The selective laser heating process utilized for the SLM fabrication of the alloy samples with different sizes and their microstructures are schematically shown in Fig. 11. It is generally understood that the SLM process results in the local heating of the fabricated sample due to laser irradiation. However, the supplied heat can be rapidly released from the AlSi10Mg alloy sample (Fig. 11(b)) with a high thermal conductivity of $1 2 0 { - } 1 7 0 \mathrm { W } \mathrm { m } ^ { - 1 } { \cdot } \mathrm { K } ^ { - 1 }$ [18], leading to an extremely high cooling rate of the fabricated portion (around $1 0 ^ { 5 } \mathrm { K } { \cdot } s ^ { - 1 }$ [31,32]). In contrast, the side surfaces of the fabricated portions can be in partial contact with the surrounding particles of alloy powder, which decreases the efficiency of the heat flow paths toward the sample sides (Fig. 11(a)). This phenomenon is consistent with the relatively low thermal conductivity of the metal foams with high porosity [1,7]. The lower heat removal capacity of the smaller-sized SLM-fabricated samples at the boundaries between the manufactured portion and the alloy powder can decrease their cooling rates, leading to a local heating of the bedded powder layer above the manufactured portion (Fig. 11(a)). The smaller-sized portion can be reheated to higher temperatures by applying laser irradiation to the bedded powder layer. Therefore, it can be concluded that the slower cooling rate and additional heating effect originated from the sample dimensions contribute to the precipitation of the fine Si phase inside the columnar $\alpha$ -Al grains of the small-sized samples (as shown in Figs. 8(c) and (d)). In addition, this process also promotes the recovery or recrystallization [33] in the $\alpha$ -Al matrix of the smaller-sized samples during SLM. The results of EBSD analysis revealed a slightly lower density of the low-angle boundaries of the elongated $\pmb { \alpha } \cdot$ -Al grains inside the smaller-sized sample (Fig. 7). Since these low-angle boundaries are mostly composed of a tangled array of dislocations [33], the obtained data suggest the enhanced recovery of the $\alpha$ -Al matrix in the smaller-sized samples.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The microstructure of the TNM and the TNM0.75C alloys as observed by SEM in BSE mode is shown in Fig. 1. In the investigated condition the microstructural constituents of the TNM alloy (Fig. 1(a)) reveal predominantly globular morphologies, whereas in the TNM0.75C alloy (Fig. 1(b)) also coarse lamellar features are discernable. The differences in the observed morphologies are related to changes in phase transformation pathways due to alloying with C. The transformation sequence of the TNM alloy, representing a classic $\beta$ -solidifying alloy, can be summarized as follows, neglecting for the moment the formation and presence of borides: $\operatorname{L}\to\operatorname{L}+\beta\to\beta\to$ $\beta+\alpha\rightarrow\beta+\alpha+\gamma\rightarrow\beta+\beta_{o}+\alpha+\gamma\rightarrow\beta_{o}+\alpha+\gamma\rightarrow\beta_{o}+$ $\alpha+\alpha_{2}+\gamma\rightarrow\beta_{o}+\alpha_{2}+\gamma$ [16]. Due to the strong stabilization of the $\alpha/\alpha_{2}$ phase by C, the TNM0.75C alloy solidifies peritectically, which can be represented as follows: $\mathrm{L}\rightarrow\mathrm{L}+\beta\rightarrow\mathrm{L}+\beta+\alpha\rightarrow\beta+\alpha\rightarrow\alpha\rightarrow\alpha+\gamma\rightarrow\beta+\alpha+$ c ! b + $\beta_{o}+\alpha+\gamma\to\beta_{o}+\alpha+\gamma\to\beta_{o}+\alpha+\alpha_{2}+\gamma\to\beta_{o}$ $+\alpha_{2}+\gamma$ [16]. Moreover, the different observed morphologies are influenced by differences in recrystallization behavior, which is effective during the homogenization heat-treatment after the forging process [37]. From the XRD data the phase fractions of each phase were determined and are given in Table 2. Especially, the low volume fraction of $\beta_{o}$ phase in the $\mathrm{TNM}0.75\mathrm{C}$ alloy discriminates the alloys in the investigated condition. Essentially, this difference is due to the pronounced $\alpha/\alpha_{2}$ -stabilizing effect of $\mathrm{\textbf{C}}$ in intermetallic $\gamma$ -TiAl based alloys, which agrees well with the increased $\alpha_{2}$ phase fraction of the TNM0.75C alloy [11,16].

In (a) finely dispersed precipitates of $\omega_{o}$ phase are visible, whereas none are evident in (b). - Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Schloffer et al. [49] determined the hardness of pure $\beta_{o}$ phase in a TNM alloy to be $8.0\mathrm{GPa}$ . Moreover, the hardness of a $\beta_{o}+\omega_{o}$ phase mixture was measured to lie between $9.6\mathrm{GPa}$ and $11.3\mathrm{GPa}$ , depending on the heat-treatment applied, which controls the size of the $\omega_{o}$ precipitates. The hardness value that we have evaluated for the $\beta_{o}$ phase of the TNM alloy lies within the range of the higher values corresponding to a $\beta_{o}+\omega_{o}$ phase mixture. The hardness of the $\beta_{o}$ phase of the TNM0.75C alloy, however, is similar to the value of pure $\beta_{o}$ phase determined by Schloffer et al. [49]. Discrepancies between the latter values might essentially be an effect of an increased amount of Mo in the $\beta_{o}$ phase of the TNM0.75C alloy (as previously discussed, an effect of the reduced phase fraction of $\beta_{o}$ phase) and, thus, be related to solid solution hardening. The similarity of the hardness value of the $\beta_{o}$ phase of the $\mathrm{TNM}0.75\mathrm{C}$ alloy and of pure $\beta_{o}$ phase can be taken as an indication for the absence of $\omega_{o}$ particles in this alloy. Furthermore, the fact that the hardness value of the $\beta_{o}$ phase of the TNM alloy and that of a $\beta_{o}+\omega_{o}$ phase mixture are in accordance is an indication for the presence of $\omega_{o}$ particles in this alloy. In order to verify these assumptions, the presence of $\omega_{o}$ phase in both alloys was studied by TEM. Due to the well-established orientation relationship (OR) between $\beta_{o}$ phase and $\omega_{o}$ phase, i.e. $\langle1\hat{1}1\rangle_{\beta_{o}}\{1\bar{1}0\}_{\beta_{o}}\|[0001]_{\omega_{o}}\{1\hat{1}\bar{2}0\}_{\omega_{o}}$ , it is possible to detect diffraction spots of both phases by tilting the specimen into a suitable zone axis [53]. Fig. 4 shows bright field (BF) TEM images of the $\beta_{o}$ phase present in the TNM alloy and the TNM0.75C alloy and the corresponding diffraction pattern (as insets) taken in $\langle111\rangle_{\beta_{o}}$ zone axis. In the TNM alloy (Fig. 4(a)) the contrast visible in the $\beta_{o}$ phase arises from the presence of fine $\omega_{o}$ precipitates. The consequent selected area diffraction (SAD) pattern yields a direct proof for the presence of both phases $\beta_{o}+\omega_{o}$ in this alloy under the heat-treatment applied. In the TNM0.75C alloy (Fig. 4 (b)) no evidence for the presence of $\omega_{o}$ phase was observed by TEM. Thus, the lower hardness observed by nanoindentation of the $\beta_{o}$ phase of the C-containing alloy can be related to the absence of fine $\omega_{o}$ precipitates in this phase.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The obvious difference in the local chemical environment within the $\beta_{o}$ phase of the investigated alloys is the Mo concentration as shown by APT (Table 3), which might affect the formation of $\omega_{o}$ phase. $\omega_{o}$ particles embedded in the $\beta_{o}$ phase are commonly observed to be depleted in elements belonging to group VIb of the periodic table of elements, as shown qualitatively for the alloying elements Mo [49] and W [54] by energy-dispersive $\boldsymbol{\mathrm X}$ -ray spectroscopy. During the formation of the $\omega_{o}$ phase, these elements have to be rejected into the surrounding $\beta_{o}$ matrix. In order to quantify the extent of solute redistribution a sample of the forged and homogenized TNM alloy was additionally heat-treated at $1150^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $15\mathrm{min}$ and subsequently oil quenched. Thereafter, the sample was annealed at $850~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $^{6\mathrm{~h~}}$ with subsequent slow cooling in the furnace. This treatment was conducted to precipitate $\omega_{o}$ particles in the dimension easily detectable by APT. Subsequently, APT tips were site-specifically prepared and the presence of $\omega_{o}$ phase was verified by $\mathrm{\Deltat}$ -EBSD (after the APT measurement so as to avoid contamination by the electron beam). In Fig. 5(a) an APT tip containing $\beta_{o}$ phase and $\omega_{o}$ phase is depicted as visualized by t-EBSD. The detected phases are labeled and the green lines between the adjacent phases delineate regions, which fulfill the previously mentioned OR of $\langle\bar{1}11\rangle_{\beta_{o}}\{1\bar{1}0\}_{\beta_{o}}\|[0001]_{\omega_{o}}$ $\{11\bar{2}0\}_{\omega}$ In Fig. 5(b) an APT reconstruction of this tip is shown. Thereby only Mo atoms and isoconcentration surfaces corresponding to a Mo concentration of $1\mathrm{{at.}\%}$ are indicated. The $\omega_{o}$ grains appear elongated and some adjacent grains grow parallel. In order to analyze the Mo redistribution occurring during growth of $\omega_{o}$ phase, a proximity histogram (Fig. 5(c)) has been calculated for all isoconcentration surfaces. Strong partitioning of Mo toward the $\beta_{o}$ phase is discernable. In the region corresponding to $\omega_{o}$ phase the Mo content amounts to ${\approx}0.37$ at. $\%$ , whereas in the region corresponding to $\beta_{o}$ phase far off the interface the Mo content amounts to ${\approx}2.95\mathrm{at.}\%$ . In the vicinity of the interface a pile-up of $c_{\mathrm{max}}\approx4.75\mathrm{at.}\%$ is observed. This significant accumulation is a result of the process of $\omega_{o}$ formation. The growing $\omega_{o}$ particle rejects Mo, which has to be transferred into the surrounding $\beta_{o}$ phase. However, the finite diffusivity in the $\beta_{o}$ phase results in the observed accumulation. These observations are in agreement with the previously stated hypothesis of Mo redistribution during growth of $\omega_{o}$ particles. It is therefore reasonable to conclude that an increased amount of Mo could impede or even prevent the formation of $\omega_{o}$ precipitates. This phenomenon is realized in the investigated alloying system due to alloying with C, which results in a stabilization of the ${\mathfrak{x}}/{\mathfrak{x}}_{2}$ phase and concomitantly in a reduction of the $\beta_{o}$ phase fraction. Therefore, the $\beta_{o}$ phase of the $\mathrm{TNM}0.75\mathrm{C}$ alloy accommodates a higher Mo content in comparison to the TNM alloy, which in turn results in the observed suppression of the $\omega_{o}$ phase formation.

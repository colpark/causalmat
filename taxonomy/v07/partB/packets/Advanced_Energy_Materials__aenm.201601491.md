- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Figure 1A–C shows the surface morphology of V/Fe PBA after the different synthetic procedures. Notably, the shape and average size of the V/Fe cathodes was not altered by the ballmilling process, as shown in Figure S1 in the Supporting Information. All three methods produced powders with an aggregated form of nanoparticles with irregular shapes. The powders synthesized by simple mixing (Figure 1A) exhibited a greater distribution in particle size than that of the co-precipitation, which may have been caused by the continuous changes in the molar ratio of $\mathrm { N a _ { 4 } F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } / V C l _ { 2 } }$ during the synthesis. In the early stage of the synthesis, each vanadium ion poured into the $\mathrm { N a } _ { 4 } \mathrm { F e } ( \mathrm { C N } ) _ { 6 }$ solution react with a much larger concentration of hexacyanoferrate complexes compared to in the later stage of the synthesis; thus, the powder formed in the early stage grew to larger particles. However, in co-precipitation (Figure 1B,C), the molar ratio was maintained at a constant value throughout the synthesis, leading to limited particle growth and a narrow size distribution. Figure 1D shows the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of V/Fe PBA powder synthesized by co-precipitation (with and without HCl) and simple mixing. All the diffraction peaks were indexed with the cubic phase of $\mathrm { V } _ { 3 } [ \mathrm { F e } ( \mathrm { C N } ) _ { 6 } ] _ { 2 }$ (PDF Card No. 00–042–1440), thus it is expected that the synthesized V/Fe PBAs has the same crystal structure with cubic phase of that. The diffraction pattern of simple mixing exhibited broadened shape and the peaks corresponding to the planes of (200)/(220) and (400)/(420) are difficult to distinguish, indicating that the crystallinity was relatively poor. The XRD patterns of the cathode from co-precipitation (without HCl) show more clearly separated diffraction peaks compared to that from simple mixing, but the broad background, indicative of remaining amorphous phase, was still observed. On the other hand, the pattern of co-precipitation synthesized with HCl exhibited much sharper and well-defined peaks for each diffracted plane without a broad background. The growth rate of PBA is reportedly retarded by increases in the acidity of the reaction solvent media,[25] which provides sufficient time to construct the PBA crystal. Therefore, it is concluded that maintaining the hexacyanoferrate complex/ vanadium ion ratio during the synthesis and adding HCl into the reaction media are key factors to achieving high crystallinity, which is beneficial for the fast and reversible insertion/ extraction of the electroactive mobile ions. The effect of the synthesis method on the crystallinity was further examined by transmission electron microscope (TEM). For the V/Fe PBAs fabricated by simple mixing, fine nanocrystals with sizes of a few nanometers (indicated with arrows) were distributed in the matrix with amorphous-like structure, as shown in the HRTEM images of Figure S2 in the Supporting Information. Unlike the case of simple mixing, the powders synthesized by the co-precipitation method (with HCl) contained well-grown crystallites a few tens of nanometers in size, as marked by red arrows in Figure 1F. An amorphous-like region was still observed (white arrows), although the overall crystallinity was improved by the co-precipitation method and HCl. The relatively lower degree of crystallinity of the vanadium hexacyanoferrates compared to other PBA series was also reported by Imanishi et al.[26]  The chemical characteristics of the V/Fe PBA powder were investigated by Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy (top of Figure 1G). The spectrum of the V/Fe PBA powder shows $\mathrm { M } { - } \mathrm { C }$ (or $\mathrm { M - C } { \equiv } \mathrm { N - } \mathrm { M }$ ) stretching, $\scriptstyle \mathrm { V } = \mathrm { O }$ , and $- C { \equiv } \mathrm { N - }$ stretching, which are distinctive PBA chemical bonds, corresponding to wavenumbers of 450–650,[25,27] 980,[28] and  $2 0 9 5 ^ { [ 2 9 ] } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively. In addition, the broad peak observed centered at $\approx 3 4 3 0 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ reflects the existence of the zeolitic and ligand water molecules typically incorporated in the structure of PBAs. The atomic composition and the distribution of the constituent elements in the V/Fe PBA cathodes were investigated by energy-dispersive X-ray spectrometry (EDS). Figure 1H presents the EDS mapping images with selected elements of Na, V, Fe, C, N, and O present in the cathode powders. These images confirm that the atomic composition was highly uniform in the cathodes. From the EDS result, the atomic ratios of $\mathrm { N a } / \mathrm { V }$ and V/ Fe were measured at 0.13 and 1.52, respectively. Furthermore, based on the presence of vanadyl $( \mathrm { V = O } )$ ions revealed by FT-IR and the adsorbed/ligand water molecules confirmed by FT-IR and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) (bottom of Figure 1G), the chemical formula of the as-synthesized V/Fe PBAs was determined to be $\mathrm { N a _ { 0 . 4 } ( V O ) _ { 3 } [ F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } ] _ { 2 } . 1 2 H _ { 2 } O }$ , assuming that all of the C and N atoms of the cyanide groups are perfectly bonded to each other. The inset of Figure 1E is a schematic representation of the crystal structure of the V/Fe PBA cathodes. Because of the presence of vanadyl ion and non-stoichiometry of $\mathrm { V }$ to Fe ion in the V/Fe PBAs, $1 / 6$ of the bridging cyanide groups bonded to vanadium ions were replaced with oxygen atoms, and $1 / 3$ of hexacyanoferrate complex sites were vacant, which are surrounded by oxygen-terminating bonding.[30–32] Therefore, it is speculated that structural distortion caused from the replacement of the cyanide to oxygen is compensated by the vacancies, and the unit cell of the synthesized V/Fe PBAs is maintained as cubic structure, although local disordering nearby the vacancies is existed.

The V/Fe PBA cathode exhibited a capacity of ${ \approx } 5 5 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { ~ h ~ g ^ { - 1 } ~ }$ at the end of the cycling (250th cycle), which was still higher than or comparable with the values in the previous reports on PBAs listed above.  The changes in potential (Figure 2B) during charging/discharging showed a sloping tendency, and three regions with gentle slopes were distinguished. The three distinguishable segments of the potential regions implied that the electrochemical process of the V/Fe PBA cathode proceeded via three types of redox reactions based on a solid-solution behavior. The overall shape of the profile was not altered by the cycling, although the charge/discharge capacity slightly decreased. Figure 2C depicts the cyclic voltammograms (CV) of the V/Fe PBA cathodes within a potential range of $0 . 4 { - } 1 . 2 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ versus $\mathrm { A g / A g C l }$ for initial 20 cycles. As noted in the potential profile, the CVs confirmed that the V/Fe PBA-based cathodes electrochemically reacted through three redox processes, with middle reaction potentials $( E _ { \mathrm { m i d } } )$ , defined as $( E _ { \mathrm { o x i d a t i o n } } + E _ { \mathrm { r e d u c t i o n } } ) / 2$ , of 0.54, 0.90, and $1 . 0 8 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ versus $\mathrm { A g / A g C l }$ from the CVs. The clearly distinguishable three pairs of redox reactions continuously occurred and were reversible during the cycles investigated. According to a previous study on electrochemically deposited V/Fe PBA thin films,[37] the first, second, and third pairs of anodic and cathodic peaks are assumed to be related to the reversible electrochemical reactions of $\mathrm { V ( I I I ) } + \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }  \mathrm { [ V ( I V ) O ] ^ { 2 + } } + 2 \mathrm { H } ^ { + } + \mathrm { \Lambda } \mathrm { e }$ , Fe(II) $ \mathrm { F e } ( \mathrm { I I I } ) + \mathrm { e }$ , and $\mathrm { [ V ( I V ) O _ { 2 } H ] ^ { + } }  \mathrm { [ V ( V ) O _ { 2 } ] ^ { + } } + \mathrm { ~ e ~ } + \mathrm { H ^ { + } } ,$ , respectively, although the corresponding half reaction potentials are slightly different, especially for the first pair of redox reactions. This difference may have been caused by the dissimilarity in stoichiometry of the synthesized materials and resulting local crystallographic structure. The small polarization observed in the CVs, indicating fast ion diffusion kinetics, was attributed to the structural features of the PBA framework. First, the zeolitic and ligand water molecules constituting the framework formed a 3D hydrogen-bonding network inside the PBA structure that allowed rapid proton kinetics,[38] which is known as the “Grotthuss mechanism”. Second, because of the large lattice parameter of PBA, it is possible that the guest ions were inserted into the structure without or with only partial dehydration and that the solvation shell surrounding the ion acted as a shield layer to mitigate the electrostatic repulsion among the guest ions, lowering the activation energy for ion diffusion.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): An analysis based on investigated by ex situ X-ray absorption near edge spectroscopy (XANES) was performed to monitor the changes in the electronic structures of vanadium and iron ions during cycling. The open circles and arrows shown in Figure 3A indicate the potentials at which the ex situ XANES measurements were performed. Prior to XANES analysis, a single cycle of charging followed by discharging with a current density of $1 1 \mathrm { \ m A \ g ^ { - 1 } }$ proceeded as a formation cycle; then, all the cathodes were recharged to certain potentials and held at the corresponding potentials for $3 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ to induce a complete electrochemical reaction. Figure 3B,C shows the typical Fe and V K-edge spectra observed from V/Fe PBAs depending on the electrode potential during charging, respectively. The reference spectra of FeO, ${ \mathrm { F e } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 3 }$ , ${ \mathrm { V } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 3 }$ , $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 }$ , and ${ \mathrm { V } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 5 }$ were also included in the figure. As shown in both XANES results, two intensity peaks, a so-called main absorption peak and a pre-edge peak, which is located at $1 0 { - } 2 0 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ lower energy relative to that of the main peak, were observed. The intensity of the pre-edge peak of the Fe K-edge was relatively weaker compared to that of the V K-edge (right of Figure 3B). Theoretically, the main absorption peak originated from the electron transition to the 4p continuum state with 1s–4p electronic transition and the shape resonance of the transition metal ions.[39] The pre-edge peak is generally recognized in the XANES spectra of first-row transition metals in the periodic table, e.g., Ti to Cu.[40] The peak is dipole-forbidden in nature, reflecting the excitation of the electron related to a bound-to-bound 1s-to-3d transition, and becomes a partially allowed transition due to $3 \mathrm { d } \mathrm { - } 4 \mathrm { p }$ orbital hybridization.[41] In addition, the intensity of the pre-edge peak is correlated with the degree of distortion of the sublattice, i.e., the peak intensity increased with the degree of deviation from the ideal octahedral (centrosymmetry).[42] Regarding the geometric coordination of Fe ions, the weak intensity of the preedge peak was characteristic of a high centrosymmetric sublattice structure of Fe ions surrounded by cyanide groups. The more remarkable pre-edge peak was identified in the V K-edge presumably due to the local coordination of the V ions, which slightly deviated from the ideal octahedral coordination because the ions were bonded with not only cyanide group but also oxygen atoms with probabilities of 5/6 and 1/6, respectively, as mentioned in the crystal structure shown in Figure 1E.  Overall, the systematic shifting of the spectra to higher X-ray energy was observed in both the Fe and V K-edges with increasing electrode potential. The valence states of transition metal ions are linearly dependent on the particular energy point of XANES, e.g., the peak energy position or edge step of the XANES spectra.[40,43,44] The energy position at a normalized intensity of 0.5 and at the pre-edge were calculated for Fe and V, respectively, and the energy is indicated by triangles in Figure 3B,C. Figure 3D depicts the calculated energy as a function of the normalized charge capacity, i.e., the capacity obtained at the potential at which the ex situ XANES analysis was performed, divided by that at the end of charging (1.15 V). The calculated X-ray energies of the Fe and V K-edges from V/Fe PBAs systematically increased with the increase in the depth of charging. In addition, from comparison with the energy position of the reference oxides, it is confirmed that charge compensation upon the charging/discharging was occurred by the changes in valance state of V and Fe ions with $\mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }  \mathrm { V } ^ { 4 + }  \mathrm { V } ^ { 5 + }$ and $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + }  \mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ , respectively, although exact formal oxidation states of the ions could not be determined due to the large difference in crystallographic structure between V/Fe PBAs and standard oxides. Based on this result, the Fe and V ions involved in the entire energy storage process of V/Fe PBA cathodes was revealed, although the electrochemical reaction of most of the previously studied PBAs was solely manifested by that of the Fe ions. Therefore, the multiple redox reactions occurring at the Fe and V sites contributed to the superior energy storage ability of the V/Fe PBAs.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To identify the origin of capacity fading for the V/Fe PBA cathodes, the changes in the microstructural, chemical, and spectroscopic characteristics during the cycling were analyzed. The surface morphologies of pristine and cycled electrodes shown in Figure 6A confirmed that the overall surface morphology of all the cycled V/Fe PBA cathodes was almost identical to that of the pristine cathode in that the particulate shape of all of the cathodes was retained as nanoparticle aggregates throughout all of the investigated cycles. Figure 6B shows the XRD patterns of pristine and cycled cathodes at the 50th, 100th, and 250th cycles. There were no noticeable changes in the diffraction peaks upon repeated insertion and extraction of the guest ions, indicating that the overall crystal structure of the cathodes was preserved during repeated cycling. Therefore, the V/ Fe PBA cathodes were highly resistant to macroscopic damages during cycling, i.e., the reduction in the degree of crystallinity or amorphization.  As shown in Figure 2B, the potential gap between the charge and discharge profiles gradually widened, and the boundary between the three potential regions was less distinguishable with increasing cycle number, indicating that polarization was accumulated with cycling. EIS was performed to determine the main factor detrimental to increase in the polarization. Figure 6C presents Nyquist plots of the observed data (open circles) and simulated curves (dotted lines) as a function of the cycle number. Magnified plots of the highfrequency region are shown in Figure S6A in the Supporting Information. The observed result was simulated using the equivalent circuit shown in the inset of Figure 6C. The circuit model was designed to separate each resistance contribution for the overall polarization and was composed of three resistance components (the electrolyte $( R _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ , film $( R _ { \mathrm { f i l m } } )$ , and charge transfer $\left( R _ { \mathrm { c t } } \right) )$ ), two constant phase elements, and the Warburg impedance (W). V/Fe PBA crystalline phase was surrounded by an amorphous-like region (Figure 1F). Also, the enhancement in specific capacity (Figure 2A) and reduction in polarization (Figure S3B–E, Supporting Information) was observed at the higher degree of crystallinity. Based on the results, it can be speculated that the surrounding amorphous structure act as an additional interface hindering the facile electrochemical process. Therefore, the effect of the amorphous region on the polarization accounting for the resistance component of the film $( R _ { \mathrm { f i l m } } )$ is reasonable. Additionally, Figure S6B,C in the Supporting Information depicts the comparison of the simulation accuracy of the observed data with different types of equivalent circuit models and confirms that the model shown in the inset of Figure 6C suitably reflects the measured data. The calculated resistance components as a function of the cycle number are shown in Figure 6D. $R _ { \mathrm { e } }$ was independent of the cycle number because the galvanostatic cycling was conducted within a stable potential region of the aqueous electrolyte, which was supported by an absence of the current tail vicinity of the lower and upper potential limits of the CVs. The change in the resistance of the film $\mathrm { [ } R _ { \mathrm { f l m } } )$ was hardly recognizable with increased cycling, indicating that the amorphous region of V/Fe PBA surrounding the crystalline region was retained during the charge/discharge process. The most prominent increase in resistance was observed in the charge-transfer characteristics $( R _ { \mathrm { c t } } )$ . The $R _ { \mathrm { { c t } } }$ of the V/Fe PBA cathodes sharply increased during the early cycles and hardly varied over the remainder of the cycles. Interestingly, the variation tendency of $R _ { \mathrm { c t } }$ was similar to the variation in specific capacity in that most of the changes, i.e., reduction in capacity and increase in the charge transfer resistance, were observed in the early cycles and subsequently saturated. To identify the reason for increase in $R _ { \mathrm { c t } } ,$ investigation on dissolved mass of the transition metal ions was conducted.  The dissolution behavior of the metal ions during the cycling was monitored by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES), as shown in Figure 6E. The V and Fe ions gradually dissolved upon cycling, and the mass concentration of the dissolved V ions was more prominent than that of the Fe ions, even considering that the ratio of V to Fe ion concentration in V/Fe PBA is $3 / 2$ . Notably, the fractions of dissolved V and Fe ions relative to the total mass of ions present in the electrodes were less than ${ \approx } 1 . 5 \%$ and ${ \approx } 0 . 6 \%$ at the end of cycling (250th cycle), respectively; therefore, the dissolution of these ions did not cause the changes in the XRD patterns and scanning electron microscopic (SEM) images during cycling. The dissolved mass of these ions increased rapidly from the start of cycling, and the increase in these dissolved ions was mitigated or negligible after prolonged cycling. Therefore, the decay of the delivered capacity is closely related to the accumulation of the charge-transfer resistance, and the dissolution of the V and Fe ions, mostly occurring at the vanadium sites, caused increase in $R _ { \mathrm { c t } }$ .

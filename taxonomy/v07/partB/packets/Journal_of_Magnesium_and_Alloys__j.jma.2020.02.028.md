- path: /home/aid1/Documents/causalmat/matmech/Journal_of_Magnesium_and_Alloys/j.jma.2020.02.028/images/8e4212f67ae08129713415e2b4004ace57d84e26dc07f6bd5528301d88404d11.jpg
- caption: Fig. 15. the relationship between ECAP passes and YS of AZ61 alloy samples at $3 7 3 \mathrm { ~ K ~ }$ and $4 2 3 \mathrm { ~ K ~ }$ .
- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The increase in the dislocation densities was thought to be the main contributor to the rapid increase in strength in AZ61 alloys processed by ECAP. The total yield stress can be described as the sum of all calculated strengthening contributions at the onset of yield $0 . 2 \%$ strain), e.g. $\sigma _ { \mathrm { y s } }$ $= \sigma _ { \mathrm { G B } } + \sigma _ { \mathrm { P } } + \sigma$ , where $\sigma _ { \mathrm { G B } }$ refers to grain boundary strengthening caused by grain refinement based on the Hall–Petch relation, $\sigma _ { \mathrm { P } }$ is precipitation strengthening of precipitated $\mathrm { M g } _ { 1 7 } \mathrm { A l } _ { 1 2 }$ phase dispersion distribution, $\sigma$ is the dislocation strengthening related to the flow stress and DSE. Fig. 15 gives the relationship between ECAP passes and YS of AZ61 alloy at $3 7 3 \mathrm { ~ K ~ }$ and $4 2 3 \mathrm { ~ K ~ }$ . It can be inferred that YS of samples extruded at $3 7 3 \mathrm { ~ K ~ }$ increased with the pressing passes while YS of samples extruded at $4 2 3 \mathrm { ~ K ~ }$ increased first and then decreased after 5-pass ECAP. This significant strength increase of AZ61 alloy mainly results from the following mechanisms: dislocation strengthening, precipitation strengthening and grain boundary strengthening. Firstly, depending on the classical hall-petch relationship, the strength of the conventional block metal is inversely proportional to the grain size and a strong grain-refinement effect introduced during ECAP tremendously enhances the strength. Secondly, the $\mathrm { M g } _ { 1 7 } \mathrm { A l } _ { 1 2 }$ precipitates not only hinder the dislocation movement but also further accumulate the dislocation density during ECAP, which leads to work hardening and higher deformation storage energy (DSE). This behavior is consistent with the structure shown in Fig. 3. However, it also results in a low elongation of only about $7 \%$ . To a certain extent, this phenomenon demonstrates that more deformation storage energy was accumulated during ECAP. Combined with microstructures in Fig. 5, obviously, dynamic recrystallization occurred at $4 2 3 \ \mathrm { K } { - } 5$ . During the recrystallization process, the DSE was exhausted. As a result, the YS of $\mathbf { M g }$ alloy decreased. Besides with the same pressing pass, the YS of ECAPed-samples at $3 7 3 \mathrm { ~ K ~ }$ are higher than that of $4 2 3 \mathrm { ~ K ~ }$ , which reveals that the deformation temperatures played an important role in the deformation storage energy.

- caption: Scheme 1. Synthesis procedures of FTPU and epoxy oligomer as well as the mixing process of the blends with BMI.
- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The preparation of FTPU/epoxy blends is mainly divided into the following three procedures, which is also shown in Scheme 1.  As shown in Scheme 1, PCDL was reacted with IPDI first before the further reaction using AFD as chain extender and FA as chain terminator. AFD and FA provide disulfide groups in the main chains and furan groups as the ending group, respectively. FTPUs with disulfide groups in their main chains are considered as good candidates for selfhealing polymers due to disulfide exchange reaction and intermolecular hydrogen bonding among urea groups [42,43]. The ending furan groups were designed to facilitate the construction of temporary interfacial connection with epoxy oligomers having dangling furan groups as well through DA reaction with BMI. More importantly, the formation of reversible crosslinking points among epoxy oligomers via DA reaction induce the phase-separation between epoxy domains and FTPU, which are expected to tune the coating properties.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Compared with the previous work [41], furan rings were used as the ending groups of the FTPU linear chains. The DA reaction between FTPU chains and epoxy oligomers via BMI enhanced the interaction among phase separated epoxy domains and FTPU matrix. As shown in Fig. 4a, the storage modulus $\left( E ^ { \prime } \right)$ of the blends at $2 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ followed the decreasing order of $\mathrm { F T P U } > \mathrm { F T 5 B E S } > \mathrm { F T 7 B E 3 } > \mathrm { F T 5 B 0 . 5 E 5 }$ , i.e., compared with the pristine FTPU, the storage moduli of the blends with the phase separated domains were decreased to some extent. The introduction of epoxy oligomers may weaken the hydrogen bonding among urea groups in FTPU chains. The further increase in epoxy oligomers increased the amount of phase separated epoxy domains at nanoscale, which contributed to the increase in $E ^ { \gamma }$ . Unreacted epoxy oligomers decreased the $E ^ { \prime }$ of FT5B0.5E5 when the BMI loading was reduced.  In addition, as shown in Fig. 4b, FTPU had an obvious glass transition temperature $( T _ { g } )$ at $- 2 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ possibly due to soft segments. The $T _ { g }$ of the hard segment may be higher than $1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , determined from the $E ^ { \gamma }$ curve. After the introduction of epoxy oligomers and BMI, the glass transition behaviors of the blends varied greatly with the pristine FTPU. An evident $T _ { g }$ peak appeared at about $6 3 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for FT7BE3 possible because of the reduction in intramolecular hydrogen bonding of hard segments. The increases in epoxy oligomers as well as BMI increased the $T _ { g }$ peak to $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } .$ . The existence of phase separated epoxy domains at nanoscale limited the mobility of polymer chains. Two adjacent $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ peaks appeared at about $2 7 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and $4 7 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for FT5B0.5E5. Some unreacted linear epoxy oligomers may be miscible with FTPU chains, leading to the reduction in the $T _ { g }$ peak of the soft segments and the existence of a new $T _ { g }$ peak. The other $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ peak can be attributed to the phase-separated epoxy domains.  The tensile strength and elongation at break of pure FTPU were $7 . 8 \mathrm { M P a }$ and $2 3 1 . 2 ~ \%$ (Fig. 4c and Table 2). After the introduction of reversible chemical bond among FTPU and epoxy oligomers, the mechanical properties of the blends were changed. The existence of phase separated epoxy domains at nanoscale and the strong interfacial bonding significantly improved the tensile strengths of FT7BE3 and FT5BE5. However, the lack of flexibility of epoxy domains decreased the elongation at break greatly. The performances of FTPU and its blends as coating materials were also studied. As shown in Fig. 4d and Table 2, although FTPU had good flexibility as the coating material, there is a need to improve the adhesion and hardness. The pristine epoxy oligomers with stoichiometric BMI were also used as the coating material here. All the adhesion, flexibility, and hardness were satisfied.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To understand the subsequent repairing mechanism of the blends with phase-separated epoxy domains having reversible network, the DA reaction that occurred in the blends was confirmed first by FTIR spectra at different temperatures here. The change in the temperatures altered the featured peaks of FT5BE5 (Fig. 8a). A new peak at $1 1 4 6 \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ was found at $1 3 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ because furan rings were re-generated from the retroDA reaction. The comparatively low temperature of $6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ facilitated the DA reaction, and accordingly the peak was not noticeable after $^ { 2 \mathrm { h } }$ . Another new peak at $6 9 6 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ that is attributed to the maleimide characteristic band was observed at $1 3 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ as well. The peak became neglected as well due to the subsequent DA reaction at $6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ . The change of peaks at high and low temperatures can be repeated for several cycles due to reversible retro-DA and DA reactions.  In addition, a specimen of FT5BE5 was heated at $1 3 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and quenched in liquid nitrogen to preserve the domain morphology at the high temperature. As shown in Fig. 8c, a comparatively smooth surface morphology was observed, suggesting the disappearance of phase-separated domains. When the re-DA reaction occurred at $1 3 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ , the reversible bonding between FTPU and epoxy domains was destructed. The reversible crosslinking among epoxy oligomers was disintegrated as well. The FTPU and epoxy oligomers were miscible and caused the disappearance of phase-separated domains, which can be also determined by the AFM phase image (Fig. 8b). After reheating the specimen at $6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 2 \mathrm { h } }$ , the surface morphology was still relatively smooth (Fig. 8d), suggesting that the phase-separation somehow was still neglected.  Moreover, we also used transmittance curves of FT5BE5 at different temperatures to evaluate the morphological variations (Fig. 8e). The transmittance of a blend material should be improved by increasing the miscibility of two domains due to the reduction of light scattering. However, the change in transmittance was not evident for FT5BE5 during heating and cooling cycles possibly due to the influence of a great amount of BMI. Therefore, we used FT5B0.5E5 instead to evaluate the blend transmittance during heating and cooling cycles. As shown in Fig. 8f, the retro-reaction occurred at $1 3 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ greatly improved the transmittance of FT5B0.5E5 because of the disappearance of phase-separated domains. The reduction in the temperature to 90 and $2 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ to some extent decreased the transmittance that was still relatively higher than the original state, indicating that the phase-separated epoxy domains were not formed greatly during the second DA reaction.

The DA reaction also created reversible interfacial bonds between FTPU main chains and epoxy domains. When the temperature was increased to $1 3 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , retroDA reaction occurred.

## Tetrahedron elements
- **Processing**: Surface modification with cerium nitrate at pH 3.8 followed by functionalization with 3-aminopropyltriethoxysilane (APTES); dispersion into epoxy coating via homogenization and sonication; curing at 120°C for 30 min
- **Structure**: Ce oxide/hydroxide layer (CeO2, Ce2O3, Ce-OH) deposited on Al nanoparticle surface; APTES grafting forming Si-O-Al bonds and hydrophobic amino-silane layer; improved dispersion in epoxy matrix
- **Properties**: Corrosion resistance–electrochemical property; hydrophobicity–surface property; adhesion strength–mechanical property
- **Performance**: Enhanced corrosion protection of epoxy coatings in salt spray and 3.5 wt.% NaCl environment; reduced hydrogen evolution in acidic solution; improved nanoparticle dispersion and coating durability

### M1  Processing → Structure
- cause: Surface modification of aluminium nanoparticles with cerium nitrate at pH 3.8 and subsequent functionalization with 3-aminopropyltriethoxysilane (APTES)
- effect: Formation of a Ce oxide/hydroxide layer (CeO2, Ce2O3, Ce-OH) on the nanoparticle surface and covalent grafting of APTES forming Si-O-Al bonds and a hydrophobic amino-silane layer
- experiment: Surface modification with cerium nitrate and APTES | Chemical deposition and sol-gel silanization | params: Ce(NO3)3·6H2O (5 mmol/L), pH 3.8, H2O2 (10 mL/L), APTES at stoichiometric ratio (0.043 g per 1 g Al nanoparticles), 60°C reflux, pH adjustment to 8–9 | result: Successful deposition of Ce layer and covalent APTES grafting confirmed by XPS, EDS, FTIR, and TGA
  - [experimental result] Aluminum nanoparticles were treated with Ce(NO3)3 at pH 3.8 in the presence of H2O2.
  - [experimental result] EDS and XPS analyses confirmed the presence of Ce on the nanoparticle surface with binding energies corresponding to CeO2, Ce2O3, and Ce-OH.
  - [non-referenced_knowledge] In acidic conditions, localized corrosion of Al oxide layer generates OH⁻ ions, promoting Ce(III) precipitation and H2O2 oxidizing Ce(III) to Ce(IV), forming CeO2.
  - [experimental result] APTES was grafted onto Ce-Al nanoparticles via sol-gel reaction under controlled pH and reflux conditions.
  - [experimental result] FTIR showed Si-O-Si and Si-O-Al vibrations, and TGA quantified mass loss from grafted APTES decomposition, confirming covalent bonding.
  - [deductive reasoning] The combination of Ce oxide/hydroxide layer and APTES silane layer creates a chemically modified surface structure with new bonding and hydrophobic character.
### M2  Structure → Properties
- cause: Ce oxide/hydroxide layer and APTES-grafted hydrophobic surface on aluminium nanoparticles
- effect: Enhanced corrosion resistance and improved hydrophobicity
- experiment: Hydrogen evolution test in acidic solution (pH=1.0) | Gas volumetric corrosion test | params: 0.1 g nanoparticles in 40 mL HNO3 (pH=1.0), 15 h exposure | result: Ce-Al and Si-Al nanoparticles showed reduced hydrogen evolution compared to unmodified Al; Si-Al-2 and Si-Ce-Al had the lowest gas volume
  - [experimental result] Ce oxide/hydroxide layer detected by XPS and EDS covers the Al nanoparticle surface.
  - [referenced knowledge] CeO2 and Ce(OH)3 are known to inhibit corrosion by blocking active sites and undergoing redox reactions with Cl⁻ ions.
  - [experimental result] Hydrogen evolution test showed significantly reduced H2 gas production for Ce-Al and Si-Ce-Al compared to unmodified Al.
  - [experimental result] APTES grafting introduced hydrophobic propyl and amine groups, confirmed by FTIR and TGA.
  - [experimental result] Turbidity tests in polar (MEK) and non-polar (butyl acetate) solvents showed Si-modified nanoparticles are hydrophobic and stable in non-polar media.
  - [non-referenced_knowledge] Hydrophobicity reduces water adsorption at the nanoparticle surface, decreasing the electrochemical driving force for corrosion.
  - [deductive reasoning] Thus, the combined Ce and APTES structure enhances both barrier protection and surface hydrophobicity, improving corrosion resistance.
### M3  Structure → Properties
- cause: Improved dispersion of Si-Al and Si-Ce-Al nanoparticles in epoxy coating due to APTES functionalization
- effect: Increased adhesion strength and reduced coating porosity
- experiment: Pull-off adhesion test before and after salt spray | Adhesion strength measurement | params: Posi pull-off tester, ASTM D4541, 192 h salt spray exposure | result: S2 (Si-Al-2) and S4 (Si-Ce-Al) showed lowest adhesion loss (18.5% and 18.2%) vs. blank (44.5%) and Ce-Al (22.7–29.3%)
  - [experimental result] FTIR confirmed the presence of NH2 groups from APTES on Si-Al and Si-Ce-Al nanoparticles.
  - [non-referenced_knowledge] Epoxy resins contain reactive epoxide groups that can bond with amine groups (NH2) from APTES.
  - [image description] FE-SEM images show uniform dispersion of Si-modified nanoparticles in epoxy, unlike agglomerated unmodified or Ce-Al particles.
  - [experimental result] Pull-off tests show significantly lower adhesion loss for Si-Al-2 and Si-Ce-Al compared to Ce-Al and unmodified Al.
  - [deductive reasoning] Better dispersion reduces voids and defects, increasing coating compactness and interfacial bonding.
  - [deductive reasoning] Thus, APTES-induced structure enhances adhesion strength by promoting chemical bonding and reducing agglomeration.
### M4  Processing → Performance
- cause: Incorporation of Si-Ce-Al nanoparticles into epoxy coating via homogenization, sonication, and curing at 120°C
- effect: Superior corrosion protection performance of epoxy coating in salt spray and EIS tests

  - [experimental result] Nanoparticles were modified with Ce at pH 3.8 and then functionalized with APTES.
  - [experimental result] Modified nanoparticles were dispersed in epoxy resin using homogenization and sonication, then cured at 120°C.
  - [experimental result] EIS results show Si-Ce-Al/epoxy (S4) has impedance >10¹⁰ Ω·cm², indicating near-perfect barrier behavior.
  - [experimental result] Salt spray test showed S4 had the least corrosion products, while Ce-Al coatings showed severe delamination.
  - [non-referenced_knowledge] Better dispersion (from APTES) and active inhibition (from Ce) synergistically enhance coating performance.
  - [deductive reasoning] Thus, the combined processing of surface modification and optimized incorporation leads to peak corrosion performance.
### M5  Structure → Performance

- effect: Highest corrosion protection performance in salt spray and EIS tests

  - [deductive reasoning] Thus, the structure of Si-Ce-Al nanoparticles enables both active corrosion inhibition and excellent barrier properties, maximizing performance.

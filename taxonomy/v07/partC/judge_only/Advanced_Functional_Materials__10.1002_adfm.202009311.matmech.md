### M1  Processing → Structure
- cause: Dry-jet-wet spinning combined with forced assembly to create multilayered fibers with controlled layer thickness
- effect: Hierarchically structured fibers with alternating layers of PAN and CNT/PAN, with layer thickness down to 170 nm
- experiment: Multilayer fiber fabrication using forced assembly | Dry-jet-wet spinning with multiplier die | params: Layer numbers: 8–512; Layer thickness: down to 170 nm; CNT loading: 0.5 wt% | result: Fibers with alternating PAN/CNT/PAN layers confirmed by SEM imaging and cross-sectional analysis
  - [experimental result] The two polymer solutions (PAN and CNT/PAN) enter a 3D-printed multiplier die side-by-side and are physically separated along the horizontal direction and repositioned vertically, transforming two feedstock materials into four side-by-side layers.
  - [image description] Figure 1b shows how the multiplier die transforms the input streams into alternating layers, demonstrating the physical separation and repositioning mechanism.
  - [non-referenced_knowledge] With increasing layer numbers, viscosity increases due to interfacial resistance between adjacent PAN and CNT/PAN layers, stabilizing the layered morphology during spinning.
  - [deductive reasoning] As layer thickness decreases below initial CNT cluster size, shear force generated between layers exfoliates clusters, leading to thinner and more uniform layers.
  - [image description] SEM images confirm the presence of alternating PAN and CNT/PAN layers down to ~170 nm thickness after drawing, validating the structural outcome of the processing technique.
### M2  Structure → Property
- cause: Hierarchically structured fibers with alternating layers of PAN and CNT/PAN, with layer thickness down to 170 nm
- effect: Young’s modulus–mechanical property, ultimate tensile strength–mechanical property, toughness–mechanical property

  - [experimental result] ImageJ analysis of SEM micrographs revealed smaller CNT aggregates and larger nearest neighbor distances in fibers with higher layer numbers, indicating better dispersion.
  - [experimental result] XRD results showed increased crystallinity and reduced d-spacing in 512-layered fibers, suggesting tighter packing of PAN molecules due to layer confinement.
  - [experimental result] DMA data indicated higher interfacial energy dissipation in 512-layered fiber, implying stronger interactions between CNTs and PAN matrix due to improved dispersion and alignment.
  - [image description] Polarized Raman spectroscopy showed higher degree of CNT alignment in 512-layered fiber, contributing to mechanical reinforcement.
  - [non-referenced_knowledge] Improved dispersion and alignment of CNTs enable efficient load transfer from the PAN matrix to CNTs, increasing stiffness, strength, and toughness.

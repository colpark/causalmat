## Extracted mechanisms (MatMech)
### M1  Processing → Structure
- cause: Ball milling with surfactant-assisted aqueous processing
- effect: Uniform microclusters of P(VdF-HFP) nanoparticles with hierarchical porous structure (micro- and nanopores among secondary and primary particles)
- experiment: Ball milling process optimization | Mechanical processing & SEM imaging | params: Milling speed: 285 rpm; milling time: 2 hours; surfactant: SDS; slurry composition: P(VdF-HFP), water, binder, thickener | result: Formation of uniform microclusters (2.0 ± 0.6 µm diameter) with micropores among clusters and nanopores within primary particles
  - [referenced knowledge] Ball milling is conventionally used to break down materials, but with appropriate energy input, it can cause plastic deformation favorable for self-assembly.
  - [experimental result] At 285 rpm (stage II), P(VdF-HFP) nanoparticles undergo slight plastic deformation, promoting their binding into uniform microclusters (~2 µm).
  - [image description] SEM images show distinct morphologies depending on milling intensity: low speed causes elastic deformation (no clustering), moderate speed forms microclusters, high speed leads to dense plates.
  - [non-referenced_knowledge] The resulting hierarchical porosity—micropores between microclusters and nanopores within primary particles—enhances light scattering efficiency.
  - [deductive reasoning] Therefore, optimized ball milling parameters enable formation of the desired hierarchically porous structure critical for PDRC performance.
### M2  Structure → Performance
- cause: Hierarchically porous structure with micro- and nanopores among secondary and primary particles
- effect: High solar reflectance (0.94) and high LWIR emittance (0.97), leading to subambient cooling of ~3.3°C below commercial white paints
- experiment: Optical and thermal performance testing | Spectrophotometry and field trials | params: Spectral reflectance measurement (Lambda 950 UV-Vis-NIR spectrophotometer); emissivity in LWIR range (FTIR spectroscopy); field tests in Los Angeles under peak solar flux (~1100 W/m²) | result: Solar reflectance of 0.94, LWIR emittance of 0.97, surface temperature ~3.3°C cooler than TiO₂-based paint under full sun
  - [experimental result] Ball-milled P(VdF-HFP) forms microclusters with both inter-cluster micropores and intra-cluster nanopores.
  - [experimental result] Numerical simulations and optical measurements confirm that this hierarchical porosity significantly enhances broadband solar reflectance compared to nanoparticle-only or non-uniform cluster films.
  - [image description] The spectral reflectance data shows high values across UV–NIR wavelengths, contributing to minimal solar heating.
  - [non-referenced_knowledge] The material also exhibits high LWIR emissivity due to molecular vibrations in P(VdF-HFP) that match atmospheric transparency window.
  - [deductive reasoning] Together, these optical properties allow the coating to reject more heat via thermal radiation than it absorbs from sunlight, resulting in passive daytime radiative cooling below ambient temperature.

- effect: Formation of α-Ge nanolayers (NLs) with hexagonal symmetry and thicknesses between 5 and 40 nm
- experiment: Liquid-phase exfoliation (LPE) of α-germanium | Wet ball-milling procedure | params: Ball-milling time: 60 min; speed: 3000 rpm; solvent: 4:1 isopropyl alcohol–water mixture | result: Gram-scale production of stable suspensions containing micrometer-scale α-Ge NLs with thickness range of 5–40 nm

  - [image description] AFM topography shows well-defined terraces and steps consistent with layer-by-layer exfoliation.
  - [referenced knowledge] Germanium typically cleaves along the (111) plane due to lower bond strength compared to other orientations.
  - [non-referenced_knowledge] Surface energy calculations suggest (111) as the most favorable cleavage plane in α-germanium.
  - [deductive reasoning] Thus, wet ball-milling exploits the intrinsic cleavage tendency of α-germanium along (111), leading to formation of 2D nanolayers.

  - [image description] EELS mapping confirms experimental bandgap increase from 0.65 eV to 0.85 eV in thinning [112]-oriented α-Ge NLs.
  - [referenced knowledge] Quantum confinement restricts electron motion in reduced dimensions, effectively widening the bandgap.
  - [non-referenced_knowledge] Orbital orientation differences among crystallographic directions modulate the extent of quantum confinement effects.
  - [deductive reasoning] Therefore, thinner α-Ge NLs exhibit higher bandgaps due to quantum confinement, especially in (110) and (112) orientations.

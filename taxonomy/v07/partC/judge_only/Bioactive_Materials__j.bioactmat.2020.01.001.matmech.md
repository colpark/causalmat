### M1  Processing → Structure
- cause: Alkali-heat treatment followed by hydrothermal treatment in solution containing Ca-EDTA, NaH₂PO₄, NaOH, and Na₂SiO₃
- effect: Formation of hexagonal Si-substituted hydroxyapatite (Si-HA) nanorods with diameter of 70 nm, length of 3 μm, and lattice distortion due to Si substitution in HA crystal structure
- experiment: Hydrothermal synthesis of HA and Si-HA nanorods | Hydrothermal treatment | params: 0.1 M Ca-EDTA, 0.1 M NaH₂PO₄, 0.01 M NaOH, with or without 0.2 M Na₂SiO₃; 240 °C for 24 h | result: Nanorods formed only after alkali-heat pre-treatment; Si-HA nanorods exhibited lattice distortion compared to pure HA
  - [experimental result] Ti substrates were pretreated in 5 mol/L NaOH at 60 °C for 2 h to form sodium hydrogen titanate.
  - [referenced knowledge] Sodium hydrogen titanate provides favorable nucleation sites for HA due to lattice matching with hydroxyapatite.
  - [experimental result] Hydrothermal treatment in solution containing Ca²⁺, PO₄³⁻, and SiO₃²⁻ led to formation of nanorods.
  - [experimental result] SiO₃²⁻ ions partially replaced PO₄³⁻ in HA lattice, confirmed by XRD peak shifts and XPS deconvolution.
  - [non-referenced_knowledge] Si substitution (ionic radius and charge mismatch) causes lattice distortion and a-axis elongation in HA crystal structure.
  - [referenced knowledge] Nanorods grew via Ostwald ripening under hydrothermal conditions, as supported by consistent morphology and crystallinity.
  - [deductive reasoning] Thus, the processing route (AHT + HT with Si) directly results in Si-substituted HA nanorods with lattice distortion.
### M2  Structure → Properties
- cause: Hexagonal nanorods with 70 nm diameter and 3 μm length; Si substitution in HA lattice causing a-axis elongation and lattice distortion
- effect: Enhanced release of Ca²⁺ and Si⁴⁺ ions into surrounding medium; maintained nanotopography and surface wettability
- experiment: Ion release kinetics measurement | ICP-MS | params: Immersion in 0.9 wt% NaCl at 37 °C for 1–28 days | result: Si-HA released significantly more Ca and detectable Si ions than pure HA; Si release reached ~0.5 ppm after 28 days.
  - [experimental result] Si substitution in HA lattice causes a-axis elongation and peak broadening in XRD, indicating lattice strain.
  - [non-referenced_knowledge] Lattice distortion destabilizes the crystal structure, enhancing ion dissolution.
  - [experimental result] ICP-MS data show higher cumulative Ca and Si release from Si-HA than HA over 28 days.
  - [image description] SEM and TEM confirm that nanorod morphology, diameter, and spacing are unchanged by Si substitution.
  - [deductive reasoning] Thus, the structural change (lattice distortion) directly enhances ion release, while nanotopography is preserved.
### M3  Properties → Performance
- cause: Enhanced release of Ca²⁺ and Si⁴⁺ ions; nanotopography of 70 nm hexagonal nanorods with 130 nm spacing
- effect: Enhanced fibroblast proliferation, collagen secretion, α-SMA expression, and reduced epithelial downgrowth in vivo, leading to improved biosealing
- experiment: In vitro fibroblast response assays | CCK-8, qRT-PCR, Sirius Red staining | params: L-929 fibroblasts cultured on Ti, HA, Si-HA for 1–7 days | result: Si-HA showed highest cell proliferation, highest expression of TGF-β1, CTGF, α-SMA, Col-I, and highest collagen secretion.
  - [experimental result] Si-HA nanorods release more Ca and Si ions than HA, as measured by ICP-MS.
  - [referenced knowledge] Ca²⁺ and Si⁴⁺ ions stimulate fibroblast proliferation and collagen I synthesis via TGF-β1 and FAK pathways.
  - [experimental result] qRT-PCR shows upregulated expression of TGF-β1, CTGF, α-SMA, and Col-I on Si-HA compared to HA and Ti.
  - [experimental result] Sirius Red staining confirms higher collagen secretion on Si-HA.
  - [image description] Fluorescence imaging shows enhanced cell spreading and actin organization on Si-HA nanorods.
  - [referenced knowledge] Nanorod spacing (130 nm) is slightly above optimal for proliferation, but ion release compensates and enhances overall response.
  - [image description] In vivo histology shows reduced epithelial downgrowth and no fibrous capsule on Si-HA, indicating superior biosealing.
  - [deductive reasoning] Thus, the combined ion release and nanotopography synergistically enhance fibroblast performance and biosealing.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The modified Ni-rich NCM particles were prepared by a surface passivation strategy. Pristine NCM and $\mathrm{NaH}_{2}\mathrm{PO}_{2}$ with a various mass ratio of 30:1, 6:1, and 1:1 were respectively placed at the upstream and downstream of a horizontal tube furnace and then heated. The $\mathrm{PH}_{3}$ (g) from the decomposition of $\mathrm{NaH}_{2}\mathrm{PO}_{2}$ was carried by $\Nu_{2}$ gas over the NCM where it reacted with the surface of NCM and formed P-doped interface. Subsequently, the modified NCM samples were obtained via air exposure (Figure S1, Supporting Information). After the system was cooled down to room temperature, the modified NCM particles were obtain, marked as NCM-1, NCM-2, and NCM-3 with different thickness of passivation layer on NCM, respectively. The analysis of morphologies for pristine NCM (NCM-0) and modified NCM samples was conducted via scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Figure S2a–d, Supporting Information exhibits the morphologies of NCM-0, NCM-1, NCM-2, and NCM-3, while Figure 1a–d displays a more detailed comparison at higher magnification. Distinctly, there is a smooth surface of NCM-0, in contrast to the rough ones for the modified samples, especially for NCM-3. Furthermore, the spatial distribution of elements for NCM-2 was investigated through the TEM-Energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) and SEM-EDS mappings and the elements (Ni, Co, Mn, O, and P) are evenly distributed on NCM (Figure 1e and Figure S3, Supporting Information). It indicates that the passivation layer is uniformly formed on the surface of NCM after treatment. This result is also verified through a high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) of NCM-2 (Figure 1f) as example for the modified NCM materials, compared with NCM-0 with a cleaner surface (Figure S2e,f, Supporting Information). This comparison signifies the formation of an amorphous passivation layer with a thickness of ${\approx}1{-}2\mathrm{nm}$ 1 on the rather smooth NCM after modification. There are the obvious diffraction rings of amorphous passivation layer as the inset in region I of Figure  1f, which correspond to (200) and (101) of orthorhombic $\mathrm{LiTMPO}_{4},$ respectively. Notably, the thickness of passivation layers shows difference on the (003) surface and non-(003) surface. Given the direction of lithium diffusion as 2D diffusion in NCM is the horizontal direction of the (003) surface, as mentioned before (the formation of passivation by $\mathrm{PH}_{3}$ gas reacting with NCM), the $\mathrm{PH}_{3}$ preferentially diffuses horizontally as lithium diffusion and reacts with NCM at the deep position from the surface. In addition, in term of (003) surface with the lowest surface energies, the atoms on the non(003) surface are more active.[7] These may result in the difference. The detection of d(003) lattice fringe with an interplanar spacing of $0.48\mathrm{nm}$ suggests the well-crystallized structure of internal NCM is well maintained after modification (the “I” and “II” area in Figure 1g),[26] which is also confirmed by means of X-ray diffraction (XRD, Figure  2a). The all recorded XRD patterns of NCM-0, NCM-1, NCM-2, and NCM-3 consist with the ${\mathrm{NaFeO}}_{2}$ layered structure (space group R-m), indicating that the modified process does not damage the fundamental structure of the NCM.[9] Moreover, no additional reflections are found for the modified samples, revealing that the coating layer maintains an amorphous or quasi-amorphous nature, which is in agreement with the result of HRTEM (Figure 1g). It is noted that the (003) and (104) of NCM-2 shift to the lower angles compared with other samples, suggesting that the spacings of crystal planes for layered structure are increased due to the partial $\mathrm{P}$ involved in the crystal lattice. However, the $\mathrm{P}$ completely exists in NCM-1 with the formation of tetrahedral $\mathrm{PO}_{4},$ and a thick passivation layer for NCM-3 possesses large amounts of $\mathrm{PO}_{4},$ which causes the shift hardly (detailed in the subsequent X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis). Furthermore, according to the XRD patterns, the integrated intensity ratio of I(003)/I(104) is 1.298 and 1.308 for NCM-0 and NCM-2, suggesting a negligible change of Li/Ni cation mixing degree before and after modification.[6] To further corroborate the formation of passivation, XPS was complemented (Figure 2b and Figure S4, Supporting Information). The survey scan for NCM-0 and modified NCM is exhibited in Figure $\mathrm{S4a}$ , Supporting Information. All spectra were calibrated based on the C 1s peak of adventitious carbon, centered at $284.8~\mathrm{eV}$ (Figure S4b, Supporting Information). As expected, the ${\mathrm{~P~}}2\mathrm{p}$ peak is present only for modified NCM (Figure  2b). The binding energy of $133.4~\mathrm{eV}$ is corresponding to tetrahedral $\mathrm{PO}_{4}$ .[10,24] At the same time, we construct the $\mathrm{LiNiO}_{2}/\mathrm{LiNiPO}_{4}$ and $\mathrm{LiNiO}_{2}/\mathrm{Ni}_{3}(\mathrm{PO}_{4})_{2}$ heterogeneous models (Figure  S5, Supporting Information) and the calculation result based on binding energies $(E_{\mathrm{b}})$ shows that $\mathrm{LiNiPO_{4}}$ $(-0.12~\mathrm{eV})$ is more likely to form on the surface of $\mathrm{LiNiO}_{2}$ instead of $\mathrm{Ni}_{3}(\mathrm{PO}_{4})_{2}$ $(-0.27~\mathrm{eV})$ (Table S1, Supporting Information). In term of the pristine NCM, these reveal the passivation coating layer is formed by $\mathrm{LiTMPO_{4}}$ ( $\mathrm{TM}=\mathrm{Ni}$ Co, Mn). It is worth noting that for NCM-2 and NCM-3, there are the peaks at 130.1 and $129.5\mathrm{eV}$ related to PTM at the surface of NCM after phosphorization,[24] which suggests that the formation of a dense passivation layer on the surface of NCM-2 and NCM-3 via air exposure. Given a better electronic conductivity of transition metal phosphides for transition metalphosphorus bond with a lower $\Delta\chi$ $\alpha$ represents Pauling electronegativity), $\mathrm{P}{-}\mathrm{TM}$ is supposed to improve the electronic conductivity of surface for NCM.[25] The O 1s peak located at $529~\mathrm{eV}$ is ascribed to the $_{\mathrm{TM-O}}$ (labeled as lattice O) and the peak at a high binding energy of $532~\mathrm{eV}$ is attributed to the chemically absorbed O (hydroxyl, $\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ , and ${\mathrm{CO}}^{3-})$ .[12] It should be noted that the peak position of lattice O is slightly shift toward high-energy region in the modified sample arising from the formation of $\mathrm{LiTMPO_{4}}$ . In the case of NCM-3, the excursion of peak position is weaker and the peak of absorbed O is slightly shift toward low energy position, which may be related to the formation of a large amount of $\mathrm{P}{-}\mathrm{TM}$ . For $\mathrm{Ni}~2\mathrm{p}$ spectra (Figure  2b), the Co 2p spectra (Figure $\mathtt{S4c}$ , Supporting Information), and the $\operatorname{Mn}~2\mathfrak{p}$ (Figure S4d, Supporting Information), the tiny difference in binding energy is within the experimental error, which indicates that the structure of NCM is well maintained after treatment. The weak peak at $851.4~\mathrm{eV}$ can be attributed to NiP (Figure 2b), which is not completely oxidized under isolation air on account of a dense passivation layer formation.[27]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To detect the effects of this passivation interface on the electrochemical characteristics, the electrodes made by these four different samples were prepared to test cyclability at $0.25\mathrm{~C~}$ $(50~\mathrm{mA}~\mathrm{g}^{-1})$ in a potential window of $3.0{-}4.5\mathrm{~V~}$ (versus $\mathrm{Li^{+}/L i})$ (Figure  4a). The initial columbic efficiency (ICE) of NCM-2 is $81.3\%$ . Compared with NCM-0 $(77.5\%)$ , the higher ICE of NCM-2 electrode can be generally originated from the stable interface preventing from side reactions between NCM and electrolyte. Among the samples, NCM-2 presents the highest discharge capacity of  $206.7\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ at first cycle and the discharge capacity can reach $190.1~\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ at the $100\mathrm{{th}}$ cycle, corresponding to a low fading rate of $0.08\%$ per cycle. In comparison, the fading rate of NCM-0 is $0.27\%$ per cycle over 100 cycles (from 205.0 to $149.7\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{hg^{-1}})$ . For NCM-1 (from $175.5$ to $141.8\mathrm{mA}$ h $\mathrm{g}^{-1})$ and NCM-3 (from 182.3 to $161.2\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{hg^{-1}})$ , the fading rates are $0.192\%$ and $0.116\%$ , respectively. These results further confirm that utilization of this passivation strategy for NMC dramatically boosts the cycling stability, demonstrating a strong coating-thicknesses dependence. Apparently, NCM-2 possesses a much superior capacity retention ratio of $92.0\%$ and the optimized passivation layer could effectively improve the cycling stability of NCM, which is linked to passivation for impeding its side reactions with electrolyte. In particular, NCM after treatment possesses an excellent electrochemical durability ( $\mathrm{^{715.7~mA}}$ h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ after 400 cycles) with a favorable coulombic efficiency at the current of $2\mathrm{~C~}$ during the long-term cycling, which is significantly higher than that of the pristine NCM $(88.2\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{hg^{-1}})$ . The rate capabilities of the pristine and modified materials are presented in Figure 4b. Owing to the improved charge transfer at the cathode–electrolyte interface, NCM-2 exhibits the highest capacities at all current densities, that is, 211.4, 204.4, 190.0, 178.2, 160.0, 141.0, and $112.6~\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ from 0.25, 0.5, 1.25, 2.5, 6.25, 12.5 to $25.0~\mathrm{C},$ , respectively, which are higher than those for NCM-0 (i.e., 201.8, 191.3, 175.2, 161.1, 141.0, 120.9, and $93.1\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{hg}^{-1})$ . Further, compared with the previously reported NCM charged to $4.5~\mathrm{V}$ at the room temperature,[26,34–42] the electrochemical performance of the pristine NCM in this work is normal in the initial discharge capacity (Table S2, Supporting Information) and the optimized sample exhibits the outstanding rate capability (Table S3 and Figure S6, Supporting Information). These indicate that our modification strategy for NCM has an excellent effect on improving the performance of NCM. In addition, as the discharge curves of NCM-0, NCM-1, NCM-2, and NCM-3 shown in Figure 5a–d, the decay of capacity and working voltage of NCM with a thicker passivation is further suppressed, which may be ascribed to a negligible increase of polarization upon cycling (Figure 5e). Contrarily, the NCM-0 reveals a significantly higher polarization upon cycling, demonstrating that the passivation layer exhibits a positive effect. However, although the stability of NCM is improved when the passivation layer is thick, the initial capacity seriously decreased due to a limited $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ diffusion capability in a thick passivation layer.

As the galvanostatic intermittent titration technique measurements in Figure S7, Supporting Information, the cells for all samples are charged and discharged in the range of $3.0{-}4.5\mathrm{V}$ (versus $\mathrm{Li^{+}/L i})$ at a current density of 0.05 A ${\bf g}^{-1}$ for $20\mathrm{min}$ , followed by an open circuit relaxation for $2\textrm{h}$ . Compared to NCM-0, there is significant current response in low voltage area (3.6–3.7  V) during the first oxidation (Figure  5f), which indicates the $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ in NCN-2 begin to deintercalate first. Given the $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ diffusion based on density functional theory (DFT) calculations, as the diffusion pathways in Figure $^{7\mathrm{a}}$ , the extremely low energy barrier (0.11 eV) indicates an excellent $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ transport at the heterostructure (Figure 7b). For $\mathrm{LiNiPO_{4}}$ with a slow $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ migration capability, the calculated energy barrier (0.37 eV) is slightly higher than that of $\mathrm{LiNiO}_{2}$ $(0.26~\mathrm{eV})$ (Figure S8, Supporting Information). Herein, the passivation layer consisted of $\mathrm{LiNiPO_{4}}$ with heteroatom (Co, Mn) doping could improve $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ migration.[19] Moreover, the density of states for heterogeneous interface exhibit metallic properties (Figure  7c) and fractional P–TM bond is preserved in the interface of NCM,[31] which enhances the electronic conductivity of the material at the heterogeneous interface. Therefore, overall, this strategy enhances the electrochemical activity of electrode materials and does not limit the $\operatorname{Li}^{+}$ diffusion across cathode materials except at the initial stage.

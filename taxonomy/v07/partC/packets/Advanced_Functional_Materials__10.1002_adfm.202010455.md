In detail, the PDA thin layer was in situ polymerized on the surface of commercial PP membrane, forming a stable protective shield. Such an elaborated structural design toward commercial PP membrane achieves the high-performances LSBs in terms of the following aspects: 1) suppressed LiPSs shuttling; 2) rapid catalysis of LiPSs redox; 3) desirable separator thermostability/wettability; 4) minimum energy density loss.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): An exfoliation and disintegration approach[13,14] was used to prepare the sCN. The strong reaction between K atoms intercalated $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ (CN) and ethanol/ $\mathrm{^{\prime}H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ provide huge energy and gases to split CN layers into small pieces, while the color of the powder (Figure S1a, Supporting Information) changes from light yellow to taupe. According to the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns (Figure  2a), both of the light yellow powder and K treated taupe powder obtained after freeze drying reveal the typical characteristic peak at $27.5^{\circ}$ , which was recognized as the interfacial stacking (002) of CN, respectively.[15] However, an in-plane packing (100) at $13.2^{\circ}$ could be witnessed in the bulk CN, while no obvious peak could be seen in sCN at the same position, which was probably due to the existing mono-layer or few-layers of CN after K treatment. As shown in Figures S1b and S2a,b, Supporting Information, the obtained sCN powder exhibited favorable dispersion capability in water and displayed a light-yellow color. Further upon ultraviolet light irradiation, the sCN aqueous dispersion demonstrated strong fluorescence characteristics. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were conducted to investigate the surface chemical composition of as-prepared sCN powders, as shown in Figure $^{2\mathrm{b},\mathrm{c}}$ . For bulk $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4},$ the C 1s XPS spectra could be deconvoluted into three peaks at 288.2, 286.6, and $284.8\mathrm{eV,}$ which could be attributed to $\scriptstyle\mathrm{N=C-N}$ in the original CN structure, $\mathrm{C}{-}\mathrm{NH}_{x}$ on the edge of layers and $\operatorname{sp}^{2}$ hybridization of C, respectively.[16,17] While in sCN, both of the in-plane $\scriptstyle\mathrm{N=C-N}$ and edged $\mathrm{C}{-}\mathrm{NH}_{x}$ peaks exhibited a slight shift to lower binding energy (BE) $(288.1~\mathrm{eV}/286.5~\mathrm{eV}$ for ${\tt N}\mathrm{{=}}{\tt C}\mathrm{{-}}{\tt N}/\mathrm{C}\mathrm{{-}}{\tt N H}_{x})$ . In addition, considering the similar BE of $\mathrm{C-NH}_{x}$ and cyano groups, the relative intensity of edged $\mathrm{C}\mathrm{-NH}_{x}$ peak is much higher than the same feature for that of bulk CN, which indicated the increasing content of edged C and cyano groups.[18] In the cases of N 1s spectra of CN, three peaks located at 401.2, 400.2, and $398.8~\mathrm{eV},$ which were assigned to $\mathrm{C-NH}_{x}$ on the edge, tricoordinated $(\mathrm{N}{-}\mathrm{C}_{3})$ , and bicoordinated $\scriptstyle(\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{N}\cdot\mathrm{C})$ peaks in plane.[16,17] After K treatment, the weaken intensities of $\scriptstyle\mathrm{C=N-C}$ and ${\mathrm{N}}{-}{\mathrm{C}}_{3}$ peaks suggested that massive of N defects were existed in the surface sCN, which is due to the following two reasons: 1) decrease of electron cloud density in formal N sites;[18,19] 2) generation of new cyano groups as strong electron acceptors (detailed XPS peaks information is presented in Table S1, Supporting Information). Additionally, FTIR spectra results (Figure  2d) verified the enlarged absorption of $\mathrm{N{-}H/N{=}C{-}N}$ stretching $(3000-3500~\mathrm{cm}^{-1})$ and formation of cyano groups[20] (around $2200~\mathrm{cm^{-1}})$ due to the introduction of edge defects. Besides, the peak intensity at $806~\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ (original heptazine rings) was observed clearly decreased, which further confirmed the severe destruction of CN. With a light shift, it shows the instability and high activity of groups on the edge. Further evidence can be obtained from the electron paramagnetic response (EPR) spectra, as shown in Figure 2e. In contrast to the flat spectrum of CN, sCN powder exhibits a considerable EPR signal with a $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}.$ -value of 2.004, which corresponds to the unpaired electrons induced by the partial removal of $\mathrm{N}$ atoms in the heptazine rings. The geometrically stable configurations of sCN 3D model are illustrated in Figure ${2}\mathrm{f},\mathrm{g}$ .

UV–vis spectra revealed that the blank $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ solution shows two typical absorbance peaks at around 350 and $420~\mathrm{nm}$ , which were assigned to ${\mathrm{S}}_{6}{}^{2-}$ and $\mathrm{S}_{4}{}^{2-}$ species,[27] respectively. These results intuitively validate the great $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ absorbability of the obtained sCN. Combined with the aforementioned XPS results, the variation of electrolyte color optically verified the admirable LiPSs absorbability of $\mathrm{PS}/\mathrm{sCN}$ via obvious chemical interactions, which are capable of resisting highly concentrated LiPSs. The peak at $-0.11$ V (Peak c) in the cathodic scan was due to the reduction of $S_{8}$ to $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ . Two peaks at 0.9 and $0.33\mathrm{~V~}$ (Peaks d and e) were attributed to the continuing conversion of $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ to $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}/\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{2}$ . It was observed that the unmodified PP membrane could not inhibit the migration of LiPSs at a high concentration of $0.2\textbf{M}$ (Figure S11a, Supporting Information), and the color of the blank electrolyte (the right chamber) became yellow in only one hour and deep orange after one whole day. PDA/PP membrane also could not block the diffusion of LiPSs completely (Figure S11b, Supporting Information) and the color in the blank electrolyte for PDA/PP was only lighter than that for pristine PP membrane. In contrast, when two chambers were separated by the as-modified sCNPP, the blank electrolyte maintained its transparency, which attributed to superior adsorption capability of the rich nitrogen defects on sCN nano spindles. The positive shift suggested that C atoms near nitrogen defects were endowed with the ability to form $_{\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{S}}$ bonds after nucleophilic reaction, uniting the formation of $\mathrm{N-Li}{-}\mathrm{S_{T}}$ bridge. This could be interpreted as the electrostatically induced strong interaction between LiPSs and sCN, which could be ascribed to the strong affinity on the surface of sCNPP and changed the bonding configurations of LiPSs. Considering the bonding of $_{\mathrm{N-Li}-\mathrm{S}}$ bridge, it could be reasonable to infer that the adjacent S preferred the relatively electropositive C site due to the dipolar–dipolar interaction.[31] Moreover, in the $\textsf{S}2\mathrm{p}$ spectra, the intensity of $\mathrm{S_{T}}$ to ${\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{B}}}^{0}$ was lower than 1:2 after being adsorbed with $\mathrm{PS}/\mathrm{sCN}$ , since its BE region overlapped with an additional carbon-bonded ${\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{B}}}^{0}$ species arising.[32] In addition, two pairs of new peaks located at 167.1 and $169.3~\mathrm{eV}$ were referred to the sulfite and sulfate species emerged at the high BE range,[33] which manifested the interactions between $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ and the oxidic species on the surface of PS.[32] Subsequently, the N 1s spectra show all peaks were all shifted to higher BE after adsorption by sCN (Figure  6f). The slight shift was interpreted by the above results and it also witnessed the emergence of a new sub peak ascribed to the $_{\mathrm{N-Li}}$ bonding, which was expected to chemically strengthen the sulfur immobilization in LSBs.

It is worth noting that increasing the amount of $\operatorname{sCN}$ could alleviate the capability loss and the sCNPP with higher sCN mass loading $(0.15\mathrm{mgcm^{-2}})$ ) exhibited improved performance (the fading rate was decreased from $0.54\%$ to $0.52\%$ per cycle at a circulation of 50). According to Figure  7C, the sCNPP-assembled battery exhibited a favorable rate capability with high discharge capacities of 1316, 1141, 1009, 918, 841, and $764\mathrm{mAhg^{-1}}$ at 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, and $5.0\mathrm{~C~}_{:}$ , respectively. In addition, Figure  7d shows the long-term cycle performance of sCNPP assembled LSBs at a high rate of $5.0\mathrm{~C~}$ . It delivered a high initial discharge capacity of $637\mathrm{mAhg^{-1}}$ for the first cycle and remained $476\mathrm{mAhg^{-1}}$ after 500 cycles with a capacity fading rate as low as $0.05\%$ per cycle. During the whole cycle, the Columbic efficiency was maintained over $98\%$ throughout. To further demonstrate the superiority of the sCNPP separator, its application in the high areal sulfur loading was also explored. Upon the sulfur loading to $4~\mathrm{mg~cm}^{-2}$ , the sCNPP-assembled battery could still deliver a high initial discharge capacity of 764 mAh ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ at $5.0~\mathrm{C}$ and capacity decreasing to $614~\mathrm{mAh~g^{-1}}$ after 200 cycles, which showed a low capacity fading rate of $0.09\%$ per cycle (Figure  7e). This desirable cycling durability was due to the superior LiPSs meditation capability of sCNPP even under high sulfur loading, which is conducive to the construction of high performance LSBs. As shown in Figure  7f, the sCNPP-assembled battery exhibited better rate capability in comparison with those assembled with PP and PDA/PP separators. Upon different rates of charge/discharge procedures, the sCNPP-assembled battery showed a highly reversible sulfur electrochemistry. Specifically, when the current rate was switched from $5.0~\mathrm{C}$ back to $1.0~\mathrm{C}$ , the discharge capacity was regained at about $855\mathrm{mAhg^{-1}}$ , indicating its superior rate performance and electrochemical reversibility. The reason for superior performance of sCNPP-assembled LSBs was further studied by electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS), and it was demonstrated that the $R_{\mathrm{ct}}$ was maintained at a low value (from 70 to $75~\Omega$ ) after 100 cycles. According to Figure $7\mathrm{g}$ , after a circulation of 100 and 200 at a high current density of $5.0~\mathrm{C}$ , an increased $R_{\mathrm{ct}}$ after cycling was observed, which could be resulted from the inevitable corrosion of lithium metal anode and the accumulation of “dead sulfur” at cathode side.[6,9,40] In situ EIS under different states of cycling were also listed to gain more insights into the kinetic properties of the sCNPP assembled cell. Figure 8a shows the galvanostatic charge (from 2.35 to 2.05 V) and discharge (from 2.10 to 2.40 V) stages for in situ EIS measurement. During the discharge process, the $R_{\mathrm{ct}}$ decreased slightly due to the dissolution of the sulfur. While in terms of the charge process, the impedance plots turned to two semicircles at $2.10~\mathrm{V}$ and returned to one semicircle upon further charging. The additional semicircle at $2.10\mathrm{~V~}$ might be due to the existence of insoluble $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}$ at that point, and it disappeared later because of the following oxidated reaction. Dissimilar trend was found for PDA/PP assembled battery, and the persistent additional semicircle during the charging/ discharging indicated its sluggish kinetics redox of polysulfides and insufficient $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}$ conversion throughout the whole electrochemical process.

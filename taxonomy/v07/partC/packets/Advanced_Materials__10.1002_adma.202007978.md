- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Charge and thermal transfer from matrices to analytes play vital roles in spectrometric technologies.[12] In particular, noble metals promise to enhance LDI efficiency, in terms of the surface plasmon excitation to generate large density of charges[13] and carrier relaxation to induce local heating.[14] However, most current matrices featured on either monometallic nanoparticles (e.g., Au,[15] $\mathrm{Pt},$ [16] and $\mathrm{Pd^{[17]}}$ ) or their bimetallic alloys (e.g., $\mathrm{PdAu^{[18]}}$ ), showing unsatisfied LDI sensitivity for clinical use. In addition, none of the work elucidates the relationship between surface structure and selective LDI.[19] Therefore, trimetallic alloys with synergistic effect and mesoporous morphology would address the limitations of mono/bimetallic matrices and facilitate the downstream metabolic profiling towards precision diagnostics.  In the present work, we studied mesoporous PdPtAuassisted LDI MS for metabolic extraction of plasma profile (Scheme 1a), followed by the biomarker discovery and early diagnosis of GC via sparse learning (Scheme 1b). The superior LDI assisted by PdPtAu was both experimentally and theoretically validated, with regard to the generation of hot electrons, enhanced electromagnetic (EM) field (≈sevenfold magnitude higher), photothermal conversion $(\approx50$ -fold increase), and mesoporous structure with size-exclusive effect. Taking advantage of the optoelectric/thermal effect and surface morphology, the optimized PdPtAu alloys enabled direct metabolic profiling in seconds, consuming $500~\mathrm{nL}$ of native plasma without enrichment and purification. Further coupling with the sparse learning algorithm, we identified a distinct metabolic signature consisting of six biomarkers for GC. Notably, the specific signature yielded high sensitivity of $92.0\%$ and specificity of $92.0\%$ for discrimination of early GC patients from normal controls (NC) in a retrospective cohort study. Our work opens new possibilities to improve surveillance of GC, using a fast and minimally invasive blood analysis towards precision diagnosis in clinical situations.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In a typical synthesis, mesoporous PdPtAu spheres were prepared by a modified surfactant-directing strategy,[20] achieving ${\approx}20~\mathrm{mg}$ of products per batch of synthesis. Briefly, the trimetallic alloys were obtained by stoichiometric seeded growth of Au on PdPt micelles, followed by the removal of Pluronic F127 surfactant. As shown in scanning electron micro­ scopy and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images (Figure 1a), uniform PdPt spheres with mesopores were formed after the surfactant removal process. An increase of particle size to ${\approx}150\mathrm{nm}$ and a decrease of pore diameter down to ${\approx}15~\mathrm{nm}$ were observed for PdPtAu spheres (Figure 1b, denoted as PdPtAu-1), after the reduction of gold precursor $(10\times10^{-3}\mathrm{~M~}$ , $1.2~\mathrm{mL}$ ). The obtained trimetallic alloys exhibited the distribution of Pd, Pt, and Au from elemental mapping analysis (Figure  1c) and a line scanning profile (Figure  1d). Unlike the reported core–shell structure,[21] the profile widths of the three metal compositions appeared the same, implying the formation of trimetallic alloy. Specifically, the mesopores on the surface of PdPtAu alloys were deeply concaved into the central nuclei, which was essential to accommodate metabolites but exclude large-sized proteins and nucleic acids.[19a,22]  The selected-area electron diffraction pattern (Figure  1e) and high resolution TEM (inset of Figure  1e) demonstrated obvious polycrystalline rings of the trimetallic alloys.[20] The crystalline structure and periodic spacing were verified using wide-angle X-ray powder diffraction (Figure 1f) analyses, yielding two distinct sets of diffraction peaks which were indexed to fcc crystal structure of both PdPt (JCPDS: 46-1043 for Pd and JCPDS: 04-0802 for Pt) and Au (JCPDS: 04-0784).[20,23] Notably, the atomic edge and kink sites were observed on the rough surface (inset of Figure 1e), suggesting an unsaturated Au and Pt atomic coordination. Further considering the crystalline nature, we hypothesized that the as-prepared trimetallic alloys exhibited high activity and stability, which are advantageous for the next step LDI enhancement.[24]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To discover the specific metabolic phenotype of GC, we subjected 292 plasma samples from 149 GC patients and 143 normal controls (NC, consisted of 3 healthy controls (HCs) and 140 chronic superficial gastritis (CSG) patients) to PdPtAu-3-assisted LDI MS analysis. Most of the controls were CSG, due to both the clinical prevalence of CSG patients $(\approx85\%$ in China and even higher as ${\approx}100\%$ in adult populations)[55] and their similar metabolism with HCs with spectral similarity scores $>0.9$ reached $99.3\%$ (Figure S23, Supporting Information).[9c,26,56] To adapt to the real-case practice, CSG was universally introduced as controls to screen out biomarkers for GC diagnosis.[57] The summaries of the sample demographics are provided in Table S4, Supporting Information. It was worth noting that all the subjects included in this study had been verified by gastroscopic and histological findings, based on the following criteria from Union for International Cancer Control Tumor Node Metastasis Classification of Malignant Tumors: 1) HCs (no remarkable endoscopic findings, $n=3$ ); (2) CSG patients $'n=140)$ ; 3) stage I GC (SI, $n=46$ ); 4) stage II GC (SII, $n=42$ ); 5) stage III GC (SIII, $n=50$ ), and 6) stage IV GC (SIV, $n=11\mathrm{{\AA}}$ ).[58] Medical history and physical examination were recorded at enrollment, and the corresponding plasma samples were obtained at the same time. All samples used in biosignature discovery and classification model development were divided into the non-overlapped discovery $(n=108/109$ ; GC/NC) and validation set $[n=41/34;$ GC/NC), as described in the study design (Figure 3a). No significant difference in age and sex was observed between GC patients and NC individuals in the discovery cohort $(p>0.05$ , Table S4, Supporting Information). Metabolites with a mass-to-charge $(m/z)$ ratio of 100–1000  Da (Figure  3b, Figure S24, Supporting Information) were measured using PdPtAu-3-assisted LDI MS, consuming $500\mathrm{nL}$ of native sample in several seconds. Out of $100000$ signals extracted from the raw MS spectra, 760 features (i.e., $m/z$ features) with the localized highest intensity for each sample were identified as a plasma metabolic fingerprint (MF) for the disease classifier. We built the blueprint of plasma MFs for all samples, showing signal uniformity for each sample (Figure 3c). Traditionally, plasma metabolites that differed among various groups were identified by commercial molecular tools, including organic matrix-assisted LDI MS,[59] liquid chromatography (LC) MS,[11b,44] and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR),[60] etc. Notably, PdPtAu-3-assisted LDI MS extracted the most metabolic information from the complex plasma samples, compared to blank (Figure S25a, Supporting Information) or commercial organic matrix (e.g., 2,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid, Figure S25b, Supporting Information). In parallel, both LC MS and NMR require tedious sample pretreatment (≈hours) to address sample complexity and large sample consumption $\scriptstyle(\approx\mathrm{mL})$ to address metabolite abundance.[61] In contrast, PdPtAu-3-assisted LDI MS featured little sample pretreatment (only protein precipitation needed), enhanced analytical speed (≈seconds), and reduced sample volume $\mathrm{[500nL}$ of native plasma), due to the enhanced sensitivity through unique opto-electronic/thermal properties and in-situ preconcentration via porous structure. The above results demonstrated reliability and application potency of direct plasma metabolic profiling for biomarker discovery.  We hypothesized that GC patients represented distinct metabolic states that would be reflected in a comparison of MFs′ relative abundances to those of NC individuals. Therefore, a sparse classification model was constructed by elastic net (a method that involves only a subset of $m/z$ features), trained with MFs from the discovery cohort only (Figure S26, Supporting Information). Validation cohort was not used for MF selection or to train the classification models. A receiver operating characteristic curve was plotted to demonstrate the sparse learning performance for differentiating GC from NC individuals, yielding an averaged area under the curve (AUC) of 0.996 with $95\%$ confidence interval (CI) of 0.991–1.000 (sensitivity: $97.2\%$ , specificity: $99.1\%$ ; Figure  3d). A comparable $(p>0.05$ , DeLong test) performance was observed for the independent validation cohort (Figure 3d), showing AUC of 0.983 with $95\%$ CI of $0.949{-}1.000$ (sensitivity: $92.70\%$ , specificity: $100.0\%$ . The classifier was robust with $p=0.233$ (averaged AUC of 0.988 and standard deviation of 0.0058) from the distribution of the AUC calculated by random permutation (1000 times) of samples to different cohorts, demonstrating little influence from cohort composition (Figure S27, Supporting Information).[62] The sparsity of classification model comes from both the penalizing conditional likelihood with an L1-type penalty from elastic net regression and serum metabolic fingerprint from MS data. The sparsity of elastic net algorithm comes from the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (L1 in Equation (S1), Supporting Information) by setting the weight of 0, according to the literature.[13d,63] In parallel, the sparsity of serum metabolic fingerprint comes from the sparsity of $m/z$ features from MS data. MS data are highly dimensional and only small subset of $m/z$ features are both statistically and biologically informative, thus requiring careful statistical treatment to extract meaningful results.[64] Consequently, the sparsity of the elastic net model using the $m/z$ features only with weight over 0 was compatible with the sparsity of MF from MS data, to improve the accuracy of prediction.[18,65] As a result, the sparse learning model selected $205~m/z$ features with statistical weight $\neq0$ (Figure 3e) for classification of the GC and NC populations.  To rank biomarkers, we aggregated multiple feature selection algorithms to define the top $12~m/z$ features as the metabolic signature panel (Figure ${}^{3\mathrm{f},}$ Table S5, Supporting Information), avoiding biases associated with individual feature selection algorithms.[66] The top-ranking $12~m/z$ features encompassed 6 structurally confirmed metabolites according to standard reference and human metabolome database (http://www.hmdb.ca/), including dihydrothymine, glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate, aconitic acid, 5-hydroxytryptophol, 6-succinoaminopurine, and cyclic $N$ -acetylserotonin glucuronide (Figures S28–S33, Supporting Information).[9a,67] Particularly, the abnormal regulation of aconitic acid, a tricarboxylic acid cycle intermediate, fits with the current theory of typical aerobic glycolysis (Warburg effect) in cancer.[9c,68] In parallel, cyclic $N$ -acetylserotonin glucuronide is involved in the circadian rhythms of biological functions, the disruption of which suggested close association with GC development.[9a,67] Especially, the $12~m/z$ features showed significant differences $(p<0.0001)$ , with non-overlapped $95\%$ CI between GC and NC group (Figure S34 and Table S5, Supporting Information).[69]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): We further applied the defined metabolic signature panel to predict GC from NC populations towards simplified clinical applications. The blueprint built on the biomarker panel allowed a clear differentiation between patients and controls (Figure 4a), providing evidence for the promising metabolic separation of GC and NC samples. Importantly, the combination of 6 biomarkers together outperformed any single one of these biomarkers ( $\mathrm{AUC}\leq0.960$ , Table S5, Supporting Information), accounting for an enhanced AUC of 0.985 $(95\%$ CI: 0.973–0.998) for discovery cohort, and 0.968 ( $95\%$ CI: 0.915–1.000) for validation cohort (Figure  4b). The diagnostic performance of the biomarker panel was assessed strictly following the same protocol, including the sparse learning and cross validation parameters and showed no significant difference $(p>0.05$ , DeLong test) with classifier built on all $760~m/z$ features with AUC of 0.996 for discovery cohort and 0.983 for validation cohort. In addition, we further applied other simple classification algorithms for classification, including linear discriminant analysis (LDA), support vector machines, and random forest. As a result, it was feasible to separate the population by “drawing” a diagonal line (i.e., a simple LDA) in the variance plane (Figure S37, Supporting Information). Therefore, the comparable accuracy can be achieved through simpler algorithms. Since the diagnostic model was built with most of controls as CSG, we constructed a new cohort (Table S7, Supporting Information) with 48 endoscopically validated HCs. Importantly, we obtained the AUC of 0.999 with diagnostic sensitivity of $100.0\%$ and specificity of $97.9\%$ to differentiate GC from HC (Figure S38, Supporting Information), consistent with the previous results (AUC of 0.996) from GC/NC classification. Therefore, those chosen biomarkers were generally applicable to HCs, not only limited to differentiating between CSG and GC. Many well-documented GC-associated protein biomarkers, such as CEA and carbohydrate antigen 19-9,[74] lack sufficient sensitivity and specificity to enable early detection.[7,74b] We failed to detect the protein biomarkers using the PdPtAu alloys with laser intensity adjusting from $40\%$ to $100\%$ (Figure S39, Supporting Information), due to size-exclusive effect from mesoporous structure. Considering the molecular weight of protein biomarkers ranging $20{-}1000~\mathrm{kDa}$ , their molecular sizes are estimated as ${\approx}5{-}100\mathrm{nm}$ , which were shielded from the mesopores on the surface of PdPtAu alloys.[75] Therefore, we collected plasma CEA of all samples using clinically approved enzyme linked immunosorbent assay for comparison. Consequently, the AUC reached 0.942 with $95\%$ CI of 0.868–1.000 (Figure  4c) in the detection of patients with SI GC from NC, which was superior $(p<0.05$ , DeLong test) to the results acquired using CEA in the current dataset $\vert\mathrm{AUC}~=~0.545$ , Figure  4c) as well as the reported value $(\mathrm{AUC}=0.576)$ .[7] The outstanding diagnosis at early stage was further verified by the corresponding scatter plot (Figure 4d). Apart from the clinically approved biomarkers (e.g., CEA), it was also difficult to differentiate between benign and malignant lesions at an early stage (e.g., SI GC) by other reported non-invasive approaches, such as exhaled breath analysis of volatile organic compounds showing a sensitivity of $73\%^{\left[76\right]}$ and sequencing analysis of ctDNA methylation showing a sensitivity of $62\%$ .[77]  Note that the detection of precancerous lesions via gastric endoscopy is the current “gold standard” for the prevention of GC, which showed a very promising effect in improving GC patients’ mortality.[1a] However, endoscopists are generally required with considerable amounts of clinical experience for detecting early GC. The independent validation set of $50~\mathrm{MFs}$ from early GC $(n=25$ ) and NC $(n=25$ ) was used to compare the referral decisions via our approach with those made by clinical experts. 3 experts with significant clinical experience in an academic gastroenterology center were introduced to make referral decisions on each patient, using only the patient’s endoscopic images. The diagnostic performances of the experts are summarized in Figure  4e, showing sensitivities ranging from $60.0\%-64.0\%$ and specificities from $84.0\%-92.0\%$ agreeing to the previous reports.[55b] In contrast, the MF-guided approach outperformed the human experts with sensitivity of $92.0\%$ and specificity of $92.0\%$ , to distinguish early GC from NC populations. In detail, two typical endoscopic images were collected to demonstrate the exact breakdown of each expert’s performance regarding the correlation of their predicted labels with the true labels. For case 1 (Figure S40a, Supporting Information), the mucosal surface was rough, and the color was slightly worse than the surrounding tissues. Based on these observations, two out of three experts diagnosed with NC. For case 2 (Figure S40b, Supporting Information), although the gastric mucosa was shallowly depressed; the surrounding mucosal surface was still smooth and no obvious signs of mucosal wrinkles were observed. Based on these observations, all experts diagnosed with NC. To compare with, the MF-guided approach diagnosed both cases as early GC, which was verified by biopsy results (postoperative pathology of pT1N0M0 for both cases, Table S8, Supporting Information). While gastric endoscopy is the main approach to screen GC, the effective detection varies significantly among endoscopists with an estimated adenoma miss rate of $9.4\%$ .[1b,78] In addition, according to the recent guidelines, most gastric endoscopy screenings were conducted in people roughly selected by non-specific risk factors such as age, family history, and Helicobacter pylori infection.[1a] Inefficient pre-selection of high-risk individuals resulted in low cost-efficiency and unsatisfied compliance, which largely weakened the application of endoscopy screening in developing countries. The proposed test of plasma MFs by PdPtAu-3-assisted LDI MS laid the conceptual and practical foundation for a multi-analyte blood test for early GC detection, with enhanced performance (sensitivity of $92.0\%)$ , improved compliance (noninvasive plasma collection), and rapid detection ${[\approx]}$ min for the whole diagnosis procedure). We estimated the cost of the test to be less than $\displaystyle\mathbf{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{{\epsilon}}}^{\mathbf{{\epsilon}}}$ , which was also lower than gastric endoscopy towards large-scale clinical transformation.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The confinement-free PRI method to fabricate PMPs was demonstrated on a super-liquid-repellent surface, i.e., SAPS, which was constructed on a sodium glass slide coated with about $20~\upmu\mathrm{m}$ -thick nanoporous hydrophobic $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ layer to realize ultralow surface energy (for details, see Supporting Information).[13] Poly(ethylene oxide) (PEO, $M_{\mathrm{w}}\approx5{,}000~\mathrm{kDa})$ aqueous solution was selected as the example materials to form the liquid columns because it has excellent thread forming capability. In practice, to generate a single steady polymer solution column, one can simply draw out a filament from the solution using a glass rod and then guide it onto an SAPS. Figure 1a demonstrates the overall formation of PEO droplets from a cylindrical column via PRI on the SAPS at millimeter length scale. At first, the column was intact. Then, sinusoidal fluctuations of the column interface are developed due to interfacial effects, which leads to an inhomogeneous mean curvature,  $H(\gamma)$ , and Laplace pressure, ${\cal P}(\gamma)~=~2\gamma H(\gamma)$ , where γ is the liquid–vapor interface tension, along the column axis $\gamma$ -direction. The pressure gradient then results in the transport of liquids and PRI. Specifically, the mean curvature $H(\gamma)=[\kappa_{1}(\gamma)+~$ $\kappa_{2}(\gamma)]/2$ , where $\kappa_{1}(\gamma)$ and $\kappa_{2}(\gamma)$ stand for the two principle curvatures corresponding to the two principle directions, namely, the radial direction in the cross section $(x,z)$ -plane and the column axis $\gamma$ -direction, respectively. Comparing $\kappa_{1}(\gamma)$ and $\kappa_{2}(\gamma)$ at the bulge and neck positions finds that $\kappa_{1}$ is larger at the neck while $\kappa_{2}$ is larger at the bulge, therefore the principle curvatures lead to opposite gradient direction of the Laplace pressure and then the liquid flow direction. For the sinusoidal fluctuations with wavelength longer than approximately the perimeter of the solution column, the effect of $\kappa_{1}$ wins leading to the PRI driving column to break into a series of spherical droplets of uniform size[14] in less than 3  s (Movie S1, Supporting Information). The droplets were nearly spherical because their radius $(R)$ was much shorter than the water capillary length $L\approx2.7$ mm. As the solvent evaporated, the spherical shape became more precise rendering a PMP (Figure  1a; Figure S2, Supporting Information).[15] While the instable criterion of the capillary fluctuation is determined through balancing the two principle curvatures, specifically, requiring $\kappa_{1}\geq\kappa_{2}$ , where, $\kappa_{1}\cong R^{-1}$ and $\kappa_{2}\cong q=2\pi/\lambda,$ with $q$ and $\lambda$ respectively denoting the wavenumber and the wavelength of the fluctuation mode, leading to the instable criterion $\lambda\ge2\pi R_{0}=\pi D_{0}$ , here $D_{0}$ is the diameter of initial solution column. The diameter $D$ of the ultimately formed spherical droplet is identified as, through analyzing the hydrodynamics of the fluctuations and assuming incompressibility of the viscous flow, the wavelength $\lambda^{*}$ of the fastest instable mode [11a]  By adjusting the drawn filament length, the initial solution column diameter on the SAPS, $D_{0}$ , can be tuned, i.e., the longer the filament, the smaller the column diameter. In this manner, the resulted PMP size ranging from hundreds to a few micrometers was obtained via PRI as shown in Figure  1b. To fully describe the ultrafast dynamic process of solution column break-up and droplets formation at micrometer length scale, ANSYS[11a] Fluent (Version 5.6) was used for computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulation of a viscous solution column with diameter of $10~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ , wherein the volume of fluid (VOF) method was employed to trace the interfaces.[10b] Simulation results showed that the break-up process of solution column with diameter of $10~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ matched the observed phenomenon at macro-dimension very well and was completed in less than one millisecond (Figure 1c; Movie S2, Supporting Information).  In general, this facile PMPs fabrication strategy can be applied to most viscoelastic fluids. Figure  1d,e exhibited the spontaneous formation of particles on SAPS from various polymer solution columns with various glass-transition temperatures. These polymers cover most frequently used materials in the fields like pharmacy,[16] electronics,[6] and photonics.[6,17]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): to maximize $\gamma_{\mathrm{LS}}$ , we need to minimize the $\gamma_{\mathrm{SV}}$ value, where $\gamma_{\mathrm{SV}}$ is the interfacial tension at the solid/air interface. Thus, SAPS can be used to tackle this issue, since its surface energy is only $0.41\mathrm{mN}\mathrm{m}^{-1}$ . In contrast, surface energy of a typical hydrophilic surface is 100 times larger, about $41~\mathrm{mN}~\mathrm{m}^{-1}$ (surface energy values from various types of surfaces are displayed in Table S2, Supporting Information). The polymeric solution columns are in “Cassie state” on SAPS and contact only the nanotips of rough topography leading to ultrasmall liquid–solid contact area $A_{\mathrm{LS}}$ as shown in Figure 2a.[13a] In Figure 2b, by using Equation (2), the $\gamma_{S}$ values from various surfaces were calculated and displayed as a function of $\gamma_{\mathrm{{LV}}}$ . The SAPS leads to the $\gamma_{\mathrm{LS}}$ value being very close to the maxima, which is equal to the value of $\gamma_{\mathrm{{LV}}}$ for the same liquid, thus favoring the growth of PRI, while other surfaces not having low enough surface energies tend to maintain the original shape of solution column. This was further validated in Figure  2c. Only on SAPS, the formation of PMPs was observed, while on superhydrophobic and hydrophobic surfaces, it was merely a thread with spindles. On hydrophilic surface, PRI was not observed at all.  As shown previously in Figure  1, under specific conditions, satellite particles were observed between each main particle. They are negative to the desired narrow size distribution of PMPs, which should be avoided. Previous study showed that the formation of satellite particles depended on whether inertial or viscous force dominant in the solution column break-up dynamics.[19,20] A concise way for understanding is to use the dimensionless Ohnesorge number, which represents the ratio of viscous to inertial force in the form of $\stackrel{\cdot}{O h}=\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{\rho d\gamma}}.$ , where $\eta,\rho_{:}$ , and $\gamma$ are viscosity, density, and surface tension of the solution, d solution column diameter. Hence, small $O h$ value means inertial force domination, while large $O h$ value means viscous force domination. Since the concentrations of PEO solutions used in our work were pretty small, 1.5, 2, 2.7, and $3.3\mathrm{wt}\%$ , so all solution density and surface tension values were treated close to the values of water— $\cdot1\mathrm{gcm}^{-1}$ and $72\mathrm{mN}\mathrm{m}^{-1}$ . In this study, for simplicity, the zero-shear viscosity is adopted to determine the dimensionless number. Then the $O h$ values were estimated based on $d$ and $\eta$ , which were measured sample by sample. Figure 2d covers a wide range of $d$ and $\eta$ values to study the conditions for the solution column break-up and satellite particles formation and its relationship to $O h$ values (see diameter measurement method in Figure S3, Supporting Information for more details). The break-up was not observed when $d$ and $\eta$ were large at the same time, which agreed with the common PRI mechanism based on the competition between surface tension and inertia/viscous forces.[10a] Under the conditions of successful solution columns break-up, three regimes are presented based on the number ratio of satellite/total particles, $\varphi,$ i.e., $\geq50\%$ , $25-50\%$ , and $\leq25\%$ , which more or less correspond to $O h$ values falling within the range of $0.04-$ 0.22, 0.22–0.42, and 0.42–1 (Figure  2c; Figure S4, Supporting  Information). For example, three points with significant differences in $O h$ values—A ${Q h}=0.06)$ , B ( $:O h=0.35;$ , C ( $\mathrm{\mathit{\Omega}}_{[O h]}=$ 1.41) can be identified at approximately $d\approx390,170,58~\upmu\mathrm{m}$ and $\eta~\approx~10,~39.2,91~\mathrm{Pa}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ , respectively (Figure  2c). The corresponding $\varphi$ is ${\approx}61.5\%$ , $37.5\%$ , 0. Specifically, satellite particles were hardly seen when $O h>1$ . Therefore, to suppress the satellite particles formation, one needs to set a high solution viscosity, $\eta$ and small column diameter, $d$ on the SAPS to weaken the influence of inertial force in break-up dynamics. Yet, it should be noted that, for material of interest, specific parameters need to be determined by experiments beforehand.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To produce PMPs at large scale, we used a custom-built microfluidic spinning instrument to collect PEO solution columns on SAPS as shown in Figure 3a and Movie S3 (for details, please see Experimental Section in Supporting Information).[21] In this way, multiple solution columns were captured in seconds by the rapid rotation of SAPS. Then the PMPs were generated via PRI ready to be collected. During the substrate rotation, the solution columns and resulted droplets were stuck on the SAPS which may seem counterintuitive. We argue that this is because the size of generated droplets much smaller than the capillary length, so the gravitational force is negligible compared with the adhesion force acting on the interface between droplet and SAPS (Figure S5, Supporting Information). Figure  3b,c presented the as prepared microdroplets on an SAPS and corresponding PMPs after solvent evaporation. As mentioned above, the size of the particles can be tuned by adjusting the diameter of solution column as seen in the inset SEM images from about $1~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ up to $1~\mathrm{mm}$ , which is readily achieved by adjusting the instrument rotation speed (Figure S7 and Table S3, Supporting Information). The fundamental lower limit of particle size is given by the spacing between neighboring protrusions of SAPS. This is because particles smaller than the spacing between protrusions penetrate the superamphiphobic layer.[13b] Especially, narrow size distributions were obtained indicating excellent PMPs size controllability of this PRI enabled strategy with coefficient of variant $\mathrm{CV}{<}10\%$ .[22] The productivity of our approach is comparable to two-phasebased microfluidic technology, especially for particles diameter smaller than $20~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ . Under specific experimental condition $(0.1\mathrm{mL}\mathrm{min^{-1}}$ , 30 rad $\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ , and $200\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{min^{-1}}$ as flow velocity of feeding solution, rotate speed, and stepper speed, respectively), $10^{5}$ PMPs of $5\upmu\mathrm{m}$ can be generated per second (Table S4, Supporting Information). For large-scale manufacture of PMPs, one can consider building a large chamber equipped with a SAPS substrate with desired dimension. These particles can be peeled off from the SAPS by gentle nitrogen flow or by rolling a big droplet as a cargo to carry them into the storage solution. The particle size distribution remained at a good level (the coefficient of variation increased from $3.68-4.71\%)$ and the SAPS maintained excellent reusability even after 30 peel-off cycles (Figures S9 and S10, Supporting Information).  This facile confinement-free PRI-based PMPs fabrication strategy also shows great flexibility for a broad range of raw materials. This was confirmed by successfully fabrication of microspheres and microgels from quite a few polymers, including synthetic ones, like polyacrylate sodium (PAAS), poly(vinyl alcohol) (PVA), poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA), polystyrene (PS), and natural ones, like sodium alginate (NaAlg), hyaluronic acid (HA) (Figure S11, Supporting Information). As Figure 3e illustrates, after the production of microdroplets, PVA microgels were obtained by repeated freezing and thawing, Na-Alg microgels were obtained by $\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}$ crosslinking (for details, see Experimental Section in Supporting Information). This method also allows particles to be functionalized by simple solution blending with desired functional substances. For instance, by incorporating quantum dots (CdTe) into the precursor solution, the produced particles obtained fluorescent property, which is difficult to achieve by conventional methods such as self-assembly and chemical bonding,[23] shown in Figure  3f (Figure S14, Supporting Information). Another example is by adding $\mathrm{Fe}_{3}\mathrm{O}_{4}$ nanoparticles to obtain magnetic  MPs. We confirmed that magnetic nanoparticles are uniformly distributed in the whole MPs by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of the MP surface and energy-dispersive X-ray diffraction (EDX) spectral images of the MP cross-section (Figure S15, Supporting Information). The vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM) measurement revealed that the particles magnetic behavior was almost linear without significant hysteresis because of the negligible coercive field (Figure 3g).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): We further demonstrated the general applicability of this strategy by producing core–shell structural PMPs. To generate solution columns with core–shell structure, a coaxial needle is applied to feed two raw materials simultaneously as shown in Figure 4a. Previously, for the in-fiber PRI enabled strategy to produce composite particles, one needs to carefully match the surface tension and viscosity among core, shell and polymer cladding.[10b] Absence of polymer cladding in this confinement-free PRI endows one more flexibility and convenience to choose raw materials and prepare feed solutions without considering a third component.  As an example, PAAS and PEO were used as core and shell materials respectively to fabricate composite particles. We confirm the core–shell structure of the particle by fluorescence images that exhibit that the two materials combined in the particle remain compartmentalized (Figure 4a; Figure S18, Supporting Information) and correlating the SEM images with energy-dispersive X-ray diffraction (EDS) spectral images that identify carbon, oxygen and sodium (Figure 4b). The measurements confirm the three-dimensional, two-compartment structure of the core–shell particle. Another example is the design of soft capsule encapsulating oily solvent (glycerin) as shown in Figure  4c. The capsule material was gelatinized calcium alginate (Ca-Alg). The shell thickness was tuned by adjusting the inner and outer diameters of coaxial needle as seen in Figure  4d. This confinement-free PRI enabled strategy is very promising to make soft capsules for medicines that are sensitive to light, unstable when exposed to damp and heat, or easy to oxidize, such as vitamin and cholecalciferol.[24] Moreover, various liquids can be packaged in the capsules for different functions, such as switching fluorescence,[25] antibacterial,[26] or drag reduction.[27]  Furthermore, as previously mentioned in Figure 2d, with the increase of the viscosity of the solution columns, the PRI tends to diminish. In this case, stable and smooth fibers appear after solvent evaporation. Thus, we can tune the viscosity of the solution column to get fiber or particle at will. Specifically, we successfully fabricated necklace-like microfibers with controllable microbead size. As one example, $45\mathrm{wt}\%$ PMMA DMF solution (high viscosity, without PRI) was spun to form one-dimensional parallel fibers arrays. $2.5~\mathrm{wt}\%$ PEO aqueous solution columns (low viscosity, with PRI) constructed 2D droplets array by using the same substrate with a $90^{\circ}$ rotation (Figure S19, Supporting Information). Since PEO solution is much more likely to be adhered on PMMA than SAPS, after break-up, PEO beads were spontaneously adhered on PMMA fibers then fused into them (Figure  4e). Different PMMA fibers spacing leads to different PRI wavelength, $\lambda$ , of PEO solution columns, thus determining the PEO microbeads size (Figure 4f). This fluorescent microarrays with microreactors have a great potential for optical microsensors and optical pattern display devices.

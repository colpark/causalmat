- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In this work, we have developed a fabrication method for the blue perovskite LEDs with the bulk inorganic halide perovskite with anion exchange method. Because the inorganic chlorine source is hardly soluble in most of the solvents, we applied anion exchanging technique to obtain the chlorine-containing blue emissive perovskite film. The halides of bulk film were exchanged maintaining the genuine properties of perovskites. The anion-exchanged bulk film showed nice spectral stability from the suppressed halide segregation. We have successfully fabricated blue PeLEDs with these films and achieved 1468 and 494 cd $\mathrm{m}^{-2}$ of maximum luminance in 490 and $470~\mathrm{nm}$ emission conditions, respectively.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): We developed pathway to fabricate blue color emissive halide perovskite film with the anion exchange method. The overall procedure is shown in the Figure 1a. In detail, the bulk ${\mathrm{Cs}}{\mathrm{Pb}}{\mathrm{Br}}_{3}$ perovskite films were fabricated by the typical spin coating method with assistance of trifluoroacetate (TFA) anions.25 The fabricated films were dipped into the reaction bath, chloroform (CF), and tributylphosphine (TBP) mixed solution to exchange the halides. The other nucleophiles (trioctylphosphine (TOP), trihexylphosphine (THP), tributylphosphine (TBP)) were also tested, however, fast and highly reproducible anion exchange process was enabled only by the TBP, therefore the TBP was chosen for overall experiments (Figure S1). The chlorines were detached from the CF by the TBP and exchanged with the bromines in the perovskite. The detailed exchange procedure is described below  The anion exchanged bulk perovskite films shows blueshifted photoluminescence (PL) peaks (Figure S2). As the reaction proceeds with additional reaction time, the more halides in the perovskite were exchanged and the optical property of the perovskite could be tuned to near UV region. Optical properties of the anion exchanged perovskite films could easily be adjusted by reaction condition, and we focused on perovskites with emission wavelength at 490 and $470~\mathrm{nm}$ for blue color emissive application (A.E. 490 and A.E. 470)  with control of reaction time and concentration of the TBP. The optical properties, such as absorption and PL spectra, and Tauc plot of anions exchanged with targeted emission spectra are shown in Figure 1b and Figure S3. In particular, the emission widths of the perovskite films were also maintained after the halide exchange treatment.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To confirm the exchange of the halides in the bulk perovskite films, XPS measurements were conducted. The  XPS spectra of anion-exchanged bulk perovskite films are shown in Figure 2a,b. The peaks of anion-exchanged perovskite films in the $\operatorname{Br}3\mathrm{d}$ region were weaker than the reference ${\mathrm{Cs}}{\mathrm{Pb}}{\mathrm{Br}}_{3}$ films, and the A.E. 470 condition has the weakest result, as expected. In the $\operatorname{Cl}2\mathfrak{p}$ peak region, the A.E. 470 condition showed the strongest peak which indicates more chlorine content in the films than the A.E. 490 condition and reference films. The halide ratio was summarized in the Figure 2c. The chlorine contents in the films were increasing with more reaction time, exhibiting the more blue-shifted emission result. The TBP could be attached on the surface of the perovskite film and might passivate the A site vacancies27 or might disturb the current flow. We confirmed that the TBP was clearly removed with rinsing steps with XPS measurement. Figure S8 displays the ${\mathrm{~P~}}2{\mathrm{p}}$ region of XPS spectra, and anionexchanged samples did not show any signal of phosphine elements, ensuring no obstacles to inject current to the perovskite emissive layer.  To prove the anion exchange, TOF-SIMS measurement was conducted evenly over the film. Figure 2d−f shows the TOFSIMS tomography result of the anion-exchanged bulk perovskite films for the chlorine contents; blueish color indicates the lower contents and yellowish color indicates the higher contents. The result clearly shows the chlorines were exchanged evenly over the surface. This result was also supported by the EDX measurement (Figure S9). Moreover, the halides were fully exchanged into the deep films and not only on the surface of the films. Seeing the depth profile result of the TOF-SIMS measurement, the chlorines were evenly exchanged into the films and showed similar chlorine content regardless of depth (Figure S10). Considering the mechanism of the anion exchange, the anion exchange occurs at the surface of the perovskite film. Then, the exchanged halides are easily mixed into the perovskite crystals, and these phenomena were reported with the nanocrystals.26,28−31 O ur result ensures that the exchanged halides were easily mixed into the inside of the crystals even in the solid film states.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): With the fine control of the anion exchange treatment, the narrow blue emission was obtained with inorganic bulk perovskites. Before applying the anion-exchanged perovskites to the devices, the effect of anion exchange treatment to the substrate was scanned. Figure S16 is the AFM images of the PEDOT:PSS layer before and after anion exchange treatment. There was no significant change in film morphology and thickness of the layer, which suggests that the anion exchange method could widely be applied to various purposes. With the inorganic bulk blue emissive perovskite films, the PeLEDs were fabricated (Figure 4). The band diagram of the LED was shown in Figure 4a, the electronic energy levels were confirmed with the UPS measurement (Figure S17 and Table S2). The electroluminescence (EL) spectra of the PeLEDs are shown in Figure 4b. As shown in the PL spectra, the PeLEDs exhibited targeted 490 and $470~\mathrm{nm}$ emission peaks with A.E. 490 condition and A.E. 470 condition. The EL spectra showed highly saturated colors with narrow fwhm of 17.86 and $19.06~\mathrm{nm}$ for A.E. 490 and A.E. 470 condition, respectively. The color coordinates of the PeLEDs are marked in the CIE color space (Figure $4{\mathfrak{c}}$ ) which satisfies the NTSC standard. On the basis of the coordinates on the color space, the color purities of the devices were calculated. Considering the center position with the CIE standard illuminant D65, the color purity of PeLEDs with A.E. 470 was $97.29\%$ and with A.E. 490 was $94.07\%$ . The device characteristics are shown in Figure 4d, Figure S18, and summarized in Table S3. The blue color PeLEDs achieved $1.18\%$ and $0.32\%$ of $\mathrm{EQE}^{36}$ and 1468 and $495~\mathrm{cd}/\mathrm{m}^{-2}$ of maximum luminance for the A.E. 490 and A.E 470 condition, respectively, which is the highest value for Cs only bulk perovskite blue LEDs. The uniformity of the PeLEDs was tested over whole device (Figure S19). As shown in Figure 2, the anion exchange treatment was evenly conducted over the film, and the result showed very similar values and peak position.  Operational spectral stability was also tested (Figure 4e,f) to confirm the suppressed halide segregation. As shown in the thermal stability result, the halide segregation was successfully suppressed and maintained the original peak position at the high level of current injection $(\sim10\mathrm{{0}m A}\mathrm{cm}^{-2})$ ). For comparison, the devices without the TFA anions were fabricated and operational spectral stability is shown in Figure S20. Unlike the devices with assistance of TFA anions, the devices without TFA anions showed severe halide segregation and peak shifts even at the very low level of the current injection. Additionally, the operational stability of devices for time was also tested and is shown in Figure S21. The devices showed nice operational lifetimes, and $T_{50}$ values for 490 and $470~\mathrm{nm}$ emission peaks were 7.74 and $3.06\mathrm{min}$ , respectively, at a high level of current injection $(\sim20\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2})$ . The suppressed halide segregation could originate from the passivated surface halide defects by the anion exchange method and the TFA passivated grain boundaries.

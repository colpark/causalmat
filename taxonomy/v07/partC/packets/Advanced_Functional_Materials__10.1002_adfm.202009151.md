- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To investigate the effect of Mo modification on the structure of PTA, DRIFT spectra were conducted and shown in Figure 1a. The classical Keggin structure of PTA is remained after modification with different Mo species proportions since the vibration–absorption bands center at 1096, 1019, 918, and $866~\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ are associated  with $\mathrm{P{-}O_{a}}$ , ${\mathrm{W}}{=}{\mathrm{O}}_{\mathrm{d}}.$ , $\mathbb{W}{-}\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{c}}{-}\mathbb{W}_{\mathrm{~}}$ , and $\mathbb{W}{-}\mathrm{O_{e}}^{-\mathrm{W},}$ respectively. As shown in Figure 1e, for distinguishing the different locations of oxygen atoms, the abbreviation $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{a}}$ , $\mathrm{O_{d}}$ , $\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{c}},$ and $\mathrm{O_{e}}$ represents the exact positions of oxygen atoms which internally coordinates with central $\mathrm{P}$ , terminally connected with Mo, shared $\mathrm{WO}_{6}$ octahedra on the corner and the edge, respectively.[34] Compared with pure PTA, it is worthy to note that an apparent redshift can be observed for ${\mathbb{W}}{-}\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{c}}{-}{\mathbb{W}}$ and $\mathrm{W{-}O_{e}{-}W}$ bands with the increase of Mo content, clarifying the interaction between Mo species and the oxygen on the corner and the edge of shared $\mathrm{WO}_{6}$ octahedral. Hence, we propose that Mo species are inclined to anchor on the 4-H site consisting of ${\mathbb{W}}{-}\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{c}}{-}{\mathbb{W}}$ and $\mathrm{W-O_{e}{-}W}$ bands from Mo-PTA to form a relatively stable structure.  To reveal the surface chemical states of the samples, high-resolution XPS analyses are carried out in Figure 1b–d. As shown in Figure  1c, the W 4f region in PTA spectra shows the spinorbit split doublet $4\mathrm{f}_{7/2}$ and $4\mathrm{f}_{5/2}$ located at 35.86 and $38.0~\mathrm{eV,}$ which could be ascribed to $\mathbb{W}^{6+}$ , respectively.[35] After the Mo species modification on PTA, two newly emerged peaks of W 4f at higher binding energies (36.65 and $38.80~\mathrm{eV}_{.}$ ) suggest the decreased electron density of W element, which proves a robust electronic interaction between Mo species and PTA. Furthermore, the O 1s spectra for PTA in Figure 1b show the $\mathrm{w{-}O{-}W}$ peak centered at $531.15~\mathrm{eV}$ shifts to the higher binding energy $(531.23~\mathrm{~eV})$ after anchoring Mo species, while other peaks belonging to ${\mathrm{W}}{=}{\mathrm{O}}_{\mathrm{d}}$ , $\mathrm{PO}_{4}{}^{3-}$ and $\mathrm{\DeltaW{-}O{-}P}$ are unchanged.[36–38] It illustrates that the electrons outflow of O atoms in the $\mathrm{w{-}O{-}W}$ bond leads to a decreased electron density of O. As for the spectra of Mo 3d, the peaks of Mo $3\mathrm{d}_{5/2}$ and Mo $3\mathrm{d}_{3/2}$ are located at binding energies of 232.0 and 235.17 eV, respectively, attributing to ${\mathrm{Mo}}^{5+}$ originated from the Mo precursor. Besides, the two peaks appeared at 232.73 and $235.83~\mathrm{eV}$ are assigned to ${\mathrm{Mo}}^{6+}$ . The formation of high-valence-state Mo species inevitably results from the surface oxidation of $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA catalyst by air. Moreover, the lower binding energies of the Mo $3\mathrm{d}_{5/2}$ and Mo $3\mathrm{d}_{3/2}$ could be observed at 231.13 and $234.32~\mathrm{eV},$ which are ascribed to ${\mathrm{Mo}}^{4+}$ , revealing that the increased electron density of Mo species is originated from W and O elements. Thus, we conclude that the electron transfer phenomenon occurs after the decoration of Mo species on the 4-H site of PTA, which leads to an electron-rich state of active Mo species, further verifying the potentiality and rationality of Mo species for stimulating the dissociation of inert $\Nu_{2}$ .

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To address the issue of low nitrogen concentration on the electrode’s surface in an aqueous eNRR system, we introduce the super-hydrophobic CNT on the catalyst as gas storage to improve the enrichment ability of $\Nu_{2}$ . Besides, that it could be used as a protective layer to immobilize Mo-PTA for preventing the dissolution. To better observe the morphology of catalysts, we prepared electrodes by loading Mo-PTA $@$ CNT on carbon paper (Figure S3j, Supporting Information). We can find the catalyst layer decorates on the carbon substrate. Furthermore, the morphology of Mo-PTA, CNT, and Mo-PTA $@$ CNT dripped on the carbon paper are represented from the side view. Figure S3, Supporting Information also shows the SEM images of the electrodes from top and side views. Meanwhile, Mo-PTA changes from the original superhydrophilic to the super-hydrophobic material after the modification of CNT, which may effectively restrain HER to enhance the electrons selectivity in the aqueous eNRR.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To testify the enrichment of $\Nu_{2}$ after the introduction of CNT, the $\Nu_{2}$ adsorption performances of the as-prepared catalysts were measured. As shown in Figure 3a, the adsorption capacity of pure PTA and Mo-PTA toward $\Nu_{2}$ is negligible. After the participation of CNT, the gas adsorption capacity of Mo-PTA $@$ CNT is higher than that of CNT and Mo-PTA. The results disclose the introduction of CNT is favorable to the $\Nu_{2}$ gathering on the surface of Mo-PTA $@$ CNT. Furthermore, we carried out the molecular dynamics simulations of Mo-PTA $\textcircled{a}\mathrm{CNT}$ (Figure  3b–d; Figure S5, Supporting Information) to determine the distribution of $\Nu_{2}$ and $\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ molecules at the electrode interface. As shown in Figure $_{3\mathrm{b-e}}$ , the nitrogen distribution density of Mo-PTA at the interface between the catalyst and the electrolyte is about $0.19\#/{\mathrm{nm}}^{3}$ , while pure CNT has a larger concentration $(0.50\#/\mathrm{nm}^{3})$ . This indicates that CNT may facilitate nitrogen enrichment at the interface. Thus, we prepare the catalyst by introducing CNT into Mo-PTA, in which the nitrogen distribution density value is significantly increased to $0.93\:\#/\mathrm{nm}^{3}$ . The hydrophobic CNT contributes to the rearrangement and reduced concentration of interfacial $\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ molecules, thereby providing more storage space for $\Nu_{2}$ molecules. Consequently, the introduction of CNT is conducive to the enrichment of $\Nu_{2}$ on the surface of the catalyst, displaying the promising application potential in eNRR.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To evaluate the eNRR performance of $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA $@$ CNT, first, linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) curves were investigated in $0.1\textbf{M}$ ${\mathrm{K}}_{2}{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}$ solution at $\mathrm{pH4}$ (Figure S6, Supporting Information). Preliminarily, $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA $@$ CNT obtains a higher current density under $\Nu_{2}$ atmosphere than that of Ar atmosphere, implying that it has the potential for electrocatalytic $\Nu_{2}$ reduction. Subsequently, the eNNR activities of $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA $\textstyle{\widehat{\underbrace{a}}}\mathrm{CNT}$ were evaluated in an H-type reactor. As shown in Figure S7, Supporting Information, the absorption peaks of $\mathrm{NH_{4}}^{+}$ were detected in the UV–vis absorption spectra, revealing the production of ammonia during the electrocatalytic process at the different applied potentials. The corresponding current densities at a series of selected voltages suggest the longterm stability of the catalyst for electrocatalysis in Figure  S8, Supporting Information. It is found that the average production rates of ammonia $\left(\mathrm{FE}\left(\mathrm{NH}_{3}\right)\right)$ of $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA $@$ CNT are significantly higher than that of PTA $\textstyle{\widehat{\underbrace{a}}}\mathrm{CNT}$ at the same potentials (Figure  4a), with the highest $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ yield rate of $51\pm1\upmu\mathrm{g}\mathrm{h}^{-1}\mathrm{mg}_{\mathrm{cat}}.^{-1}$ at $-0.1\mathrm{~V~}$ as estimated by the indophenol blue method (Figure S9, Supporting Information). The highperformance is also confirmed by ion chromatography (IC) method at each given potential (Figure S10, Supporting Information). The electrochemical surface area was estimated by the cyclic voltammetry method in Figure S11, Supporting Information. The surface area-normalized yield rate of $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ on $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA $\textcircled{a}\mathrm{CNT}$ is over six times higher than that of ${\mathrm{PTA@CNT}}_{:}$ which accords with that of the mass-normalized activity. Furthermore, as shown in Figure  4c, when CNT is introduced into Mo-PTA catalyst, the eNRR performance has been obviously improved. Significantly, the Faradaic efficiency of Mo-PTA $@$ CNT reaches up to $81\pm1\%$ at $-0.1\mathrm{V}$ versus RHE, which is higher than that most of reported aqueous-based eNRR electrocatalysts (Figure 4b), including bismuth nanocrystals $(66\%)$ ,[39] hollow gold nanocages $(30.2\%)$ ,[40] and single Mo atoms anchored to N-doped porous carbon $(14.6\%$ .[41] The hydrophobic CNT effectively controls the proton concentration contacting the catalyst, which suppresses the competing HER and improves the electron selectivity. Meanwhile, it can act as a protective layer to inhibit PTA hydrolysis. In addition, $\mathrm{N}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{4}$ as a possible by-product is not detected in our system through the chromogenic method established by Watt and Chrisp (Figure S12, Supporting Information). The above eNRR performance discloses that Mo species anchored on PTA and embedded in CNT could greatly improve the activity and selectivity in electrocatalytic $\Nu_{2}$ fixation.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Based on the above-mentioned analysis, we could conclude that Mo-PTA $@$ CNT has excellent electrocatalytic $\Nu_{2}$ reduction performance. To clarify the reaction mechanism, we determine the interaction between $\Nu_{2}$ and the catalyst. First, considering that the chemical state of $\Nu_{2}$ in the aqueous electrolyte system is the gaseous state, the interaction between $\Nu_{2}$ and the catalyst in the electrolyte is very similar to that of the gas-solid system. Therefore, $\Nu_{2}$ -TPD was carried out to investigate the $\Nu_{2}$ adsorption on $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA. As shown in Figure  5a, $\Nu_{2}$ is desorbed from $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA at different programmed heating rates. It is worth noting that the chemical absorbed $\Nu_{2}$ of Mo-PTA began to desorb from 191 to $238~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ with the increase of heating rate, indicating a strong chemical adsorption capacity of $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA toward $\Nu_{2}$ molecule for further activation. According to the method proposed by Cvetanovic and Amenomiya, the nitrogen desorption energy of the catalyst is estimated at about $50.46~\mathrm{kJ~mol^{-1}}$ (Figure S20a, Supporting Information), manifesting that $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA could facily adsorb $\Nu_{2}$ molecules. Moreover, $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ -TPD with different heating rates exhibits a weak adsorption capacity of $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ on $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA (Figure 5b), implying that the produced $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ molecules are prone to desorb from the surface of the catalyst, which contributes to the eNRR process. Figure  5c shows the increased activities of the catalyst with increasing reaction temperatures. As determined by the Arrhenius equation, the activation energy of $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA during eNRR is about $21.36\mathrm{kJ~mol^{-1}}$ , revealing that the catalyst is favorable for electrocatalytic nitrogen reduction at ambient conditions. To further explore the interaction between $\Nu_{2}$ and $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA, in situ DRIFT spectra were employed to examine surface functional groups’ changes. As shown in Figure 5d, the as-prepared $0.7\%$ Mo-PTA is successively treated in vacuum, helium, and nitrogen atmosphere by a self-designed reaction cell. Negligible differences could be observed within the vacuum and He atmosphere. However, after the treatment of $\Nu_{2}$ , the ${\mathbb{W}}{-}{\mathrm{O}}_{\mathrm{c}}{-}{\mathbb{W}}$ and $\mathrm{W-O_{e}{-}W}$ bands of 4-H site shift to higher wavenumbers, while two other peaks remain constant. This result indicates a decreased electron cloud density of 4-H site and the electron transfers to Mo species. This result consists of that of XPS. The abundant electrons on Mo species could migrate to the inert nitrogen molecules for activation, thus promoting the eNRR process.  Therefore, we propose a probable eNRR mechanism over Mo-PTA $@$ CNT in Figure  5e. First, the introduction of CNT reduces the $\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ concentration and enriches dissolved $\Nu_{2}$ on the catalyst surfac. Due to the interface concentration difference, the $\Nu_{2}$ migrates along the CNT to the Mo-PTA. The Mo species anchored on the 4-H site of PTA adsorb and activate the stable $\Nu_{2}$ molecules into the active ${\mathrm{\ddot{\omega}}}\mathrm{N}_{2}$ . Subsequently, the electrocatalysis efficiently reduces nitrogen into ammonia with high electron selectivity powered by electric energy.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To tackle the above challenge, in this work, we design and fabricate dense NiFe nanocone arrays with sharp cone tips and optimized alloy composition as an advanced OER catalyst electrode (Figure  1a,b). The role of the local electric field in enhancing reactant transfer and the alkaline OER rate has been identified by both finite element analysis (FEA) simulation and carefully tuning the apex curvature of NiFe nanocones. FEA simulation confirms a three times stronger local electric field strength and corresponding one order magnitude larger average current density for the sharp tips at $1.5~\mathrm{~V~}$ compared to quasi-planar surfaces. The tip enhances the local hydroxide ions $\left(\mathrm{OH^{-}}\right)$ concentration at the reactive sites, and thus lowers the OER overpotential. Similar to previous studies,[20] the OER catalytic active sites are contributed mainly by the in situ formed surface thin layer $\mathrm{NiO/NiFe(OH)}_{2}$ ; but the local field enhancement effect is found independent on the surface composition (Figure S1, Supporting Information). Our array nanocone electrode show stable operation at large current densities $\langle200{-}500\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2}\rangle$ .  Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images show that dense NiFe nanocones with sharp tips are quasi vertically rooted on the nickel foam (Figure  1b; Figures S4 and S5, Supporting Information). And the surface amorphous layer $(\approx2{\mathrm{~nm}}$ thickness) is identified clearly by high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM). This thin layer is not visible in X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns which is reasonable (Figure S6, Supporting Information). According to the HRTEM image and selected area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns, the lattice parameters of NiFe nanocones are close to that of nickel (JPCDS No. 04-0850) (Figure  1d–f). The TEM energydispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) result presents a uniform distribution of Ni and Fe on the surface of NiFe nanocones (Figure  1g). Hence, we deduce that the NiFe cones are nickelbased NiFe solid solution alloy. EDX mapping also shows the strong oxygen signal due to the surface NiFe oxide/hydroxide layers after electrochemical activation (Figure 1g).

As shown in Figure 2a, all the peaks of NiFe-ONCAs shift toward lower diffraction degrees when comparing to pure nickel patterns (JPCDS No. 04-0850). Moreover, when the atom ratio of Fe increases from $0\%$ to $18\%$ , the position of (111) peak shifts from $44.524^{\circ}$ to $44.296^{\circ}$ , corresponding to a lattice distance increase from 2.035 to $2.045\mathrm{~\AA~}$ . The fine scanning $\mathrm{Ni}~2\mathrm{p}$ spectrum from the activated NiFe-ONCAs surface (Figure  2c) consists of two major peaks of oxidized Ni (the spin–orbit doublets of Ni $2{\tt p}_{3/2}$ at $855.5~\mathrm{eV}$ and Ni $2\mathrm{p}_{1/2}$ at $873.5~\mathrm{eV}.$ ) and two satellites (abbreviated as “sat.”), which can be indexed as NiO or $\mathrm{Ni}(\mathrm{OH})_{2}$ (Figure  2c).[14a] However, the strong metallic Ni signals (852.2 and $869.3~\mathrm{eV},$ ) emerge after $10\mathrm{nm}$ argon ion beam etching and the oxidized Ni signals become weaker (Figure 2c). When the etching depth increases, the signals of $\mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ gradually weaken and finally disappear, only metallic Ni signals exist (Figure  2c). The Fe $2\mathrm{p}$ profile shows a similar trend that the signals of $\mathrm{Fe}^{3+}$ , which supposedly come from Fe-doped $\mathrm{Ni}(\mathrm{OH})_{2}$ [short as $\mathrm{NiFe(OH)}_{2}]$ ,[23] gradually weaken and those of the metallic Fe (peaks at 706.6 and $719.8~\mathrm{eV}_{\cdot}$ ) keep increasing from the surface to the interior of activated NiFe-ONCAs.[14a] The broad peak at around $711.5~\mathrm{eV}$ can be attributed to the Auger peak of Auger Ni LMM.[14a] The O 1s spectrum (Figure  2e) shows typical metal–oxygen bonds $(529.3\mathrm{eV})$ , hydroxide groups $(531.2~\mathrm{eV})$ , and chemisorbed molecular water $(533.0~\mathrm{eV})$ .[14a] Correspondingly, the intensities of peaks of metal–oxygen bonds and oxygen in the hydroxide group decrease dramatically with increasing etching time. The depth profile results indicate that a thin NiFe oxide/hydroxide layer $(<10\mathrm{nm})$ ) dominates on the surface of the nanocones after the electrochemical activation under OER potential. For the asprepared sample without activation, the XPS spectra verify that the NiFe nanocones are of metallic NiFe except for slight oxidation on the surface (Figure S7, Supporting Information).  In situ Raman measurements were also conducted to check the composition change during the OER test. (Data are presented in Figure  2f and Figure S8 (Supporting Information).) Details of Raman peak assignment are presented in the Supporting Information; here we only briefly mention the conclusion. The in situ Raman spectra show that the surface material of as-prepared NiFe nanocone arrays is first oxidized into $\mathrm{NiFe(OH)}_{2}$ and partly into NiO, and then transfers to Fe-doped NiOOH $\mathrm{[Ni(Fe)OOH]}$ when the applied potential during increases. The formed Ni(Fe)OOH serves as the active sites for OER, and will return to and maintain $\mathrm{NiO/NiFe(OH)}_{2}$ below the OER potential. It is also confirmed that the Fe atoms are doped into Ni host without formation of additional Fe or iron oxide phase.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The as-prepared electrodes were directly applied to a typical three-electrode cell as the working electrode in a 1 m KOH electrolyte for OER electrocatalysis. As mentioned earlier, before recording electrochemical data, all the samples were preactivated by 40 repetitive CV scans to stabilize the surface (namely, in situ oxidization). All the polarization curves were recorded from high initial potential to low potential at a scan rate of $5\mathrm{mV}\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ to avoid signal overlap between $\mathrm{Ni}^{2+}/\mathrm{Ni}^{3+}$ oxidation (see the Experimental Section of the Supporting Information for details). A commercial $\mathrm{IrO}_{2}$ catalyst loaded on nickel foam and bare nickel foam samples were also measured as controls. As presented in Figure  3a, the $\mathrm{Ni}_{x}\mathrm{Fe}_{1-x}\mathrm{-ONCAs}$ show significantly enhanced catalytic activity compared to other control electrodes employed and $\mathrm{Ni_{83}F e_{17}{\cdot}O N C A s}$ shows the best OER performance among these samples, which has ultralow overpotentials of 255 and $266~\mathrm{mV}$ at 500 and 1000 mA $\mathrm{cm}_{\mathrm{geometric}}{}^{-2}$ , respectively. At the same overpotential, the current densities of Ni-ONCAs, $\mathrm{IrO}_{2}/\mathrm{NF}$ , and NF are 36, 12, and 2 mA $\mathrm{cm}_{\mathrm{geometric}}{}^{-2}$ and 50, 16, and 3 mA $\mathrm{cm}_{\mathrm{geometric}}{}^{-2}$ , respectively. In particular, at the overpotential of $250~\mathrm{mV},$ the $\mathrm{Ni_{83}F e_{17}{\cdot}O N C A s}$ catalyst exhibits a current density up to $371\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , which is larger than most recently reported OER catalysts at the same overpotential (Figure  3d; Table S4, Supporting Information), manifesting the superior catalytic activity at large current densities. $\mathrm{Ni}_{x}\mathrm{Fe}_{1-x}\mathrm{-ONCAs}$ are also highly active at small current densities. The $\mathrm{Ni_{83}F e_{17}{\cdot}O N C A s}$ needs only an overpotential of $190~\mathrm{mV}$ to reach $10~\mathrm{mA}$ $\mathrm{cm}_{\mathrm{geometric}}{}^{-2}$ which is among the lowest values (Figure  3c). Besides, $\mathrm{Ni}_{x}\mathrm{Fe}_{1-x}{\cdot}\mathrm{ONCAs}$ have low Tafel slopes ranging from 39 to $48\mathrm{mV}\mathrm{dec}^{-1}$ at a wide range of current densities, suggesting fast OER kinetics even at large current densities (Figure 3b).  The intrinsic catalytic activity is also investigated by the efficient electrochemical surface area (ECSA) normalized linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) curve. The ECSA values were estimated using an electrochemical capacitance measurement method (Figure S9, Supporting Information). The $\mathrm{Ni}_{83}\mathrm{Fe}_{17}\mathrm{-ONCAS}$ needs only $215~\mathrm{mV}$ overpotential to reach 1 mA $\cdot\mathrm{cm}_{\mathrm{ECSA}}{}^{-2}$ , much less than Ni-ONCAs and other NiFeONCAs. This is, to the best of our knowledge, the most-efficient OER catalyst in basic media in terms of the overpotential required at $1~\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-1}\mathrm{_{ECSA}}^{-2}$ . The detailed comparisons of intrinsic OER catalytic performance with recently reported advanced catalysts are shown in Figure 3f and Table S4 (Supporting Information).  Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurement shows that $\mathrm{Ni}_{83}\mathrm{Fe}_{17}$ -ONCAs exhibits the smallest charge transfer resistance $\left(R_{\mathrm{ct}}\right)$ among all investigated samples, further confirming its high catalytic activity (Figure S10, Supporting Information). It has been reported that the binding energies of Ni and Fe 2p shift when the Fe content varies in the NiFe oxyhydroxide/hydroxide and correspondingly the electronic structure changes.[14a] Thus we deduce that the highest intrinsic catalytic activity of $\mathrm{Ni_{83}F e_{17}{\cdot}O N C A s}$ is due to the most favorable electronic configuration among all samples.  For practical application in large scale alkaline water splitting, the catalyst must survive over a long period under large currents (higher than $200\mathrm{mAcm}_{\mathrm{geometric}}{}^{-2})$ .[19] Therefore, we evaluated the long-term stability of the $\mathrm{Ni}_{83}\mathrm{Fe}_{17}$ catalyst by chronopotential test at five current densities (10, 200, 300, 500, and $10\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}_{\mathrm{geometric}}{}^{-2})$ for a total of $120\mathrm{~h~}$ (Figure 3g). It is found that the real-time potential has a negligible increase during continuous long-time operation. The potential at 10 mA $\mathrm{cm}_{\mathrm{geometric}}{}^{-2}$ only increases by $0.5\%$ after long-term large current density testing, suggesting excellent durability of the $\mathrm{Ni}_{83}\mathrm{Fe}_{17}$ catalyst. The SEM images and XPS depth profiles spectra prove that the $\mathrm{Ni}_{83}\mathrm{Fe}_{17}$ -ONCAs preserve their original morphology and composition after long-term and large-current density OER testing (Figures S11 and S12, Supporting Information).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To explore the origin of high OER activity of $\mathrm{Ni}_{x}\mathrm{Fe}_{1-x}$ ONCAs in the alkaline solution, we prepared $\mathrm{Ni}_{83}\mathrm{Fe}_{17}$ nanostructures with different tip curvature radii considering the possible effect of local electric fields on OER kinetics and/or thermodynamics. The as-prepared $\mathrm{Ni_{83}F e_{17}{\cdot}O N C A s}$ have been annealed under different temperatures at the argon/hydrogen gas atmosphere to avoid oxidation. The annealed samples showed a negligible change in phase (Figure S13, Supporting Information) or surface composition (Table S2, Figure S14, Supporting Information). The $\mathrm{Ni}_{83}\mathrm{Fe}_{17}$ nanocones keep an almost unchanged shape except for the increased tip curvature radii after annealing for $0.5\mathrm{~h~}$ at $500~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ (Figure  4a,b). After electrochemical activation, the in-situ-formed oxide/hydroxide layer smoothed the rough surface of the original sample and thus removed kinks, edges, and steps that could be active sites for OER (Figure  4d,e). The statistical data from the SEM images show the tip curvature radius is 5.6 and $37.9~\mathrm{nm}$ for the as-prepared and $500~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ -annealed $\mathrm{Ni_{83}F e_{17}{\cdot}O N C A s}$ samples, respectively (Figure S15, Supporting Information). Electrochemical test shows that the ECSA of the $500~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ -annealed sample becomes half that of the as-prepared $\mathrm{Ni_{83}F e_{17}{\cdot}O N C A s}$ , and correspondingly the apparent OER catalytic activity is obviously reduced (Figure  4g,h; Figure S16, Supporting Information). Notably, the ECSA normalized polarization curve also shows decreased OER catalytic performance for the sample with reduced tip curvature radius (Figure  4i), indicating that some factors related to tip curvature affect its intrinsic activity. After annealing at $700~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , the ONCAs were smoothed to a hemisphere-like structure with a radius of ${\approx}170~\mathrm{nm}$ (Figure 4c). Both the ECSA and apparent OER activity drop dramatically (Figure  4g,h).  Again, the intrinsic OER catalytic activity of $700~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ -annealed sample, according to the ECSA normalized polarization curves (Figure 4i), significantly lower than the as-prepared and $500~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ -annealed samples. EIS result also indicates that the charge transfer of the NiFe nanocones at the overpotential of $300~\mathrm{mV}$ in OER becomes worse as the tip curvature radius increases (Figure S16, Supporting Information). Considering the large difference in tip curvature radius between the three samples, we propose that the local electric field distribution affected by nanocone curvature can critically influence their intrinsic OER activities.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To prove this assumption, we employed FEA simulation to explore the effect of electric fields on the distribution of anion concentration and the corresponding reaction current density of $\mathrm{Ni_{83}F e_{17}{-O N C A s}}$ s at nanometer scale. According to the morphology, size, and phase composition of the as-prepared, $500~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ -annealed, and $700~^{\circ}C$ -annealed samples, we built three models. The calculation suggests that a locally enhanced electrostatic field is generated by, and starts from, the locally concentrated positive charge density on the electrode surface (indicated by arrows in Figure  5a–c). The electrostatic repulsion will drive the migration of free electrons to the regions of the sharpest curvature on a negatively charged metallic electrode.[18] We deduce that the “positive charge” will aggregate at the regions of the sharp curvature region on a positively charged metallic electrode. Tip sharpening from a radius of $170~\mathrm{nm}$ ( $700~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ -annealed) to $5.6\mathrm{nm}$ (as-prepared) enhances its positive charge density by 4.2 times and leads to its increased electrostatic field intensity by three times magnitude at the potential of $1.5~\mathrm{~V~}$ (Figure  5a–c; Table S3, Supporting Information). To estimate the quantitative impact of the electric field on the surface-adsorbed anion concentration, we used a Gouy–Chapman– Stern model to map the surface adsorbed $\mathrm{OH^{-}}$ ion density in the Helmholtz layer of the electric double layer directly adjacent to the electrode surface (Figure  5d–f). This indicates a ninefold increase in surface-adsorbed $\mathrm{OH^{-}}$ ion concentration at the $\mathrm{Ni}_{83}\mathrm{Fe}_{17}$ ONCAs tip due to locally enhanced electrostatic field (Table S3, Supporting Information; Figure  5d,f). It is known that $\mathrm{OH^{-}}$ ions are the important reactants in the reaction steps of the whole OER in alkaline solution and the reaction rate of the forward process is proportional to reactants concentration.[3] A higher $\mathrm{OH^{-}}$ concentration means a higher $\mathrm{pH}$ value, which will lead to a more “negative” free energy change (ΔG) for each OER substep according to the DFT calculation (Figure S17,  Supporting Information), indicating a more thermodynamically favorable OER process. Hence, the aggregation of $\mathrm{OH^{-}}$ ions at the tip leads to an increase of reduction current by three times at this site (Figure  5d,f). Furthermore, the base area normalized current density of the as-prepared sample is nearly 2 times and 12 times that of 500 and $700~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ -annealed ones. This trend basically matches with the experimental results where the geometric area normalized current density of the sample is nearly 6 and 25 times that of 500 and $700~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ -annealed ones at $1.5~\mathrm{V}$ (Figure  4e; Table S3, Supporting Information). When normalized to the surface area (equivalent to ECSA normalization), the current density is $40\%$ and $67\%$ higher than that of the 500 and $700\phantom{.000}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ -annealed samples, respectively. Again, this trend also matches with the experimental values of ECSA normalized currents (three and nine times). Therefore, it is demonstrated that higher tip curvature of the NiFe-ONCAs causes stronger electric field strength and corresponds to higher $\mathrm{OH^{-}}$ concentration, which leads to overall larger OER current density for the electrode.  It is noticed that the experimentally the as-prepared $\mathrm{Ni}_{83}\mathrm{Fe}_{17^{-}}$ ONCAs shows evidently better OER performance than that in the FEA calculation results. This difference may be caused by the effect of mass transport that is not reflected in the numerical computation. The tip has the shortest transporting distance of reactants and products of the reaction compared to flat parts. Furthermore, the bubbles formed at the tips have a smaller adhesive force with the electrode and can release from the surface more easily than those formed in the middle or bottom of nanocones.[24] Hence, the tips can be seen as the most reactive site of the nanocone arrays and their advantages in the mass transfer are manifested at large current densities (as high as $1000\mathrm{mAcm^{-2}}$ at 1.5 V).[3] Correspondingly, the concentrating of $\mathrm{OH^{-}}$ at the tip would lead to a faster actual reaction rate compared to the simulated results in which the reactivity is assumed the same for the whole electrode surface.

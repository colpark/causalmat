The GOS of a grain is defined as the mean value of misorientations between the average orientation of the grain and all pixels of the grain, while the GOS for a set of grains is the mean value of the GOS values of all grains [23]. Fig. 8c displays the quantification of grains against the various GOS angle ranges. As shown in Fig. 8c, the BZ has a higher fraction of grains $( 2 6 \% )$ with a high angle GOS $( 3 ^ { \circ } - 5 ^ { \circ } )$ compared to the DZ, which indicates that most of the grains at the BZ were highly strained. Importantly, $3 0 \% { - } 3 3 \%$ fraction of grains showed both low and high angle GOS in the interface zone. This indicated that the interface is an inflection zone between the low and high angle GOS when compared to the DZ and HAZ.  On the other hand, residual stress and strain can be quantified by the grain average image quality (IQ), a parameter used in the EBSD technique that measures crystallographic imperfection of the diffraction volume [27,31]. The IQ is a scalar parameter and proportionally related to the sharpness of the Kikuchi Pattern, which is affected by the presence of crystalline defects such as dislocation, strain, and distortion [32]. - Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Fig. 10 shows the grain average Image Quality (IQ) maps deduced from the Electron Backscatter Diffraction (EBSD) in the three OIM scans at the three selected areas as defined earlier. It quantifies the quality of the corresponding diffraction pattern in the diffraction volume of the cross-section of BZ(C), DZ (C), DZ (I), IZ, and HAZ, respectively. It is reported [24–26] that the IQ value is affected by the induced residual stress or strain in the diffracting volume. As the strain increases the image quality (IQ) decreases. The residual stress causes lattice distortion and thus, the EBSD's ability to index decreases since it is based on the ideal bcc crystal structure. The highly strained or stressed areas appear in a darker color (towards the blue color, low IQ value) compared to the less strained or stressed areas. The lighter the color contrast (towards the red color), the less the strain and stress and the higher the IQ value. Although this color coding is arbitrary, this norm is well practiced in the earlier publications [25,27]. Fig. 10a shows the top of the BZ(C) has a darker blue color with low IQ value (note the labeled orientations in the figures). When moving from the top to the bottom of the BZ(C) the IQ value is increased, and the grains become a lighter green color. The quantified IQ values are plotted against the depth (where the top is $0 ~ { \mu \mathrm { m } } .$ , and the bottom is $3 0 0 ~ { \mu \mathrm { m } } )$ of the bead zone (Fig. 10d). Fig. 10b shows that the top of the DZ(C) has a lighter contrast diffraction pattern (mostly yellow and light green) and the bottom of the DZ(C) has a darker pattern (mostly green and yellow). The IQ values are plotted in Fig. 10e. It indicates that the top of DZ has a high IQ value compared to the bottom of this zone. When moving towards the bottom of the DZ, the IQ value is increased. Fig. 10c shows IQ maps of the three interface regions, namely DZ (I), IZ, and HAZ. The IQ values are plotted in Fig. 10f, g. When moving from the DZ (I) to the interface and interface to the HAZ, the IQ value is increased. However, when moving away from the interface to the rest of the substrate, the IQ value is decreased.  On the other hand, residual stress and strain can be quantified by the grain average image quality (IQ), a parameter used in the EBSD technique that measures crystallographic imperfection of the diffraction volume [27,31]. The IQ is a scalar parameter and proportionally related to the sharpness of the Kikuchi Pattern, which is affected by the presence of crystalline defects such as dislocation, strain, and distortion [32]. A highly dislocated or elastically distorted lattice will have a weak and blurry Kikuchi Pattern and would be expected to have a low IQ [32]. As can be seen in Fig. 10a and d, the IQ value was very low at the beginning and increased to a higher value at the end of the bead zone. The range of the IQ value is within 142–485. This indicated that a high level of stress developed at the beginning of this zone and steadily decreased towards the end of the zone due to variations of the dislocation density and the grain sizes of the martensitic substructures. Subsequently, in the dilution zone (Fig. 10b and e), the IQ value gradually decreased from a higher value to a lower value in the range of 145–450, which indicated an increase of RS from the beginning to the end of this zone.  Interestingly, the IQ value further increased from the end of the DZ (I) towards the interface zone (Fig. 10c) and then decreased when moved away from the interface to the rest of the HAZ (Fig. 10c, f, g). It indicated that the RS value decreased from the end of DZ (I) towards the interface and then increased from the interface to the rest of the HAZ in the substrate. The above-mentioned variations of IQ value throughout those zones of a single bead cladding has similarities with the status of RS shown in Fig. 11 and supported by the earlier TEM study [11] that revealed a variation of martensitic phases and dislocation density in those zones. However, the GOS value observed in Fig. 9 contradicts with the above IQ values (Fig. 10c and Figs. 10f, g). It is because the Grain Orientation Spread (GOS) is dependent on various parameters that makes the obtained results challenging to correlate and interpret [25]. The two most important parameters are scan step size and grain size. Since GOS is grain-based parameter, the definition of a “grain” is extremely vital. If the grain size is changed, this would result in including or excluding parts of the scanned material and thus would interfere with the GOS calculations resulting in an increase or decrease in the average value within the defined grain. On the other hand, when the step size is increased the misorientation between two neighbouring pixels is increased, leading to an increase in the GOS average value [25]. Laser cladding results in an extremely heterogeneous microstructure as shown in this research, with different regions having different grain sizes, morphologies, and texture. Therefore, the “ideal” parameters for scan step size and grain size for one region might not necessarily work for another region, leading to ambiguities in interpreting and correlating the GOS measurements with the IQ value. Lassen et al. first proposed degradation of the diffraction pattern due to plastic strain [33]. Though the IQ is affected by the presence of other factors such as the dislocation density, chemical composition, and solidstate martensitic phase transformation, the synergistic effect of these factors are directly related to the development of RS and IQ value. It is also vital to note that these factors even though are important in this discussion, have a much less effect on IQ values in comparison with the strain and stress [25]. The RS then decreased towards the interface with HAZ. When moving away from the interface to the HAZ, the RS increased again and transformed to tensile stress. Regardless of the type of RS, a high RS was observed in the DZ. This trend for the RS was found to be very similar to the trend for the reverse IQ mapping in all those zones, as shown in Fig. 10. Wardle et al. suggested that the quality of the diffraction pattern or the image quality (IQ) parameter can be used to indicate the presence of residual strain distribution in the material [24]. Lassen et al. first proposed degradation of the diffraction pattern due to plastic strain [33]. However, it is well known that the IQ value also depends on the surface topology, grain boundaries, second phase, beam conditions, camera settings, crystal orientation and sample preparation [25]. Though the IQ is affected by the presence of other factors such as the dislocation density, chemical composition, and solidstate martensitic phase transformation, the synergistic effect of these factors are directly related to the development of RS and IQ value. It is also vital to note that these factors even though are important in this discussion, have a much less effect on IQ values in comparison with the strain and stress [25]. Thus, the IQ parameters are found to be very significant in providing crucial information about the crystal structure with an indication of the distribution of the residual stress and strain in the selected zones of single bead laser cladding of 420 MSS. Moreover, this qualitative and quantitative analysis revealed that the IQ value is inversely proportional to the RS and found to be significantly effective in evaluating the RS distribution in those three zones when compared to the experimental RS values measured by the XRD technique. A new method in this regard will be initiated to convert the IQ value into an RS value in a future study.  • The GOS approach revealed that most of the grains at the BZ and HAZ were highly strained and $2 6 { - } 5 9 \%$ fraction of the grains was at a high angle of GOS $( 3 ^ { \circ } - 5 ^ { \circ } )$ indicating highly deformed grains in both regions. This could be one of the reasons for the devolvement of tensile stress in those regions.   
• The IQ value was found to be inversely proportional to the RS value found in the earlier study. This IQ approach is an effective and helpful tool to evaluate the RS distribution in those zones of lasercladded AISI 420 MSS.   
• This EBSD study revealed the microstructural evolutions occurred in the laser cladded 420 MSS from a crystallographic point of view and thus expected to create new information on the orientation of prior austenite grain sizes, their aspect ratio, grain misorientation and the image quality of the diffraction pattern of the martensitic substructure observed in the three regions of a single bead cladding. These results are expected to be used as a foundation for the laserbased additive manufacturing application of 420 martensitic stainless steel.

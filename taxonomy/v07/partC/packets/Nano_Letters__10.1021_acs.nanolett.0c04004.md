- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Results. The $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NB perovskites were prepared by electrospinning followed by calcination at $700~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $2\textrm{h}$ under air atmosphere (Figure 1a). The initial electrospun nanofibers exhibit an interconnected three-dimensional (3D) network structure with a smooth surface (Figure S1). Interestingly, NB morphology was observed by simply tuning the heating rate to $1^{\circ}\mathrm{C}/\mathrm{min}$ during the calculation processs, and the $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NBs exhibit a rugged surface and still remain the 3D network structure (Figure 1b and Figure S2a). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images reveal that the $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NBs consist of a single grain connected by distinct GBs, forming a bamboo-like morphology (Figure 1c and Figure S2b). The atomic ratio of $\mathrm{La}/\mathrm{Cu}$ is about 2:1, as determined by scanning electron microscopy energy-dispersive $\mathrm{X}$ -ray spectroscopy (SEM-EDS) and inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectrometry (ICP-AES) (Figure S3 and Table S1). It is found that the elements of La, Cu, and O distribute homogeneously on the $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}\mathrm{NBs},$ , as revealed by the STEM-EDS elemental mappings (Figure 1d). As shown in Figure 1e, all of the diffraction peaks match well with orthorhombic-type perovskites of $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD)  analysis. The crystal structure of $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ is displayed in Figure 1f, where the ${\mathrm{CuO}}_{6}$ octahedra regularly locate in the structure and La atoms are inserted into octahedral space. The unit cell parameter was obtained from PXRD using the Rietveld refinement (Table S2). For comparison, $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ nanorods $\left(\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}\right.$ NRs) and bulk $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ were obtained by tuning the heating rate from 1 to 5 and $10^{\circ}\mathrm{C}/\mathrm{min},$ respectively (Figures S4−S6). Compared with $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NBs, the length of $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NRs is shorter and the 3D network structure is destroyed because the higher heating rate during the calcination process results in the fracture of the nanostructure. Therefore, it is speculated that the lower heating rate provides a suitable surface energy, thereby obtaining the La2CuO4 perovskite with GBs.28,29 However, the higher heating rate can accelerate the decomposition of polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) and growth of the crystal, resulting in a bulk ${\mathrm{La}}_{2}{\mathrm{CuO}}_{4}$ perovskite.30 For bulk $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4},$ single grains of various sizes stack disorderly with no distinct GBs. SEM-EDS, ICP-AES, STEM-EDS elemental mapping, and PXRD analysis of $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NRs and bulk $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ show results similar to those of $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NBs, indicating that the heating rate only changes the morphology and GBs.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Discussion. Since electrochemical $\mathrm{CO}_{2}\mathrm{RR}$ generally occurs at the gas−solid−liquid three-phase interface, the adsorption of $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ on the catalyst is a critical prerequisite; therefore, $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ temperature-programmed desorption (TPD) was carried out. $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NRs show the weakest adsorption of $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ as the temperature of $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ chemical desorption peak is the lowest, leading to low activity toward $\mathrm{CO}_{2}\mathrm{RR},$ which is consistent with the experimental results (Figure 4a). In addition, bulk $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ exhibits a distinct $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ desorption peak in a wide high-temperature range, indicating higher $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ adsorption capacity. Electrolyzing $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ to a CO intermediate and maintaining strong binding energy of CO on the active site are significant for promoting $_{\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{C}}$ coupling. To this end, COTPD was further used to evaluate the adsorption behavior of CO on the catalyst (Figure 4b), where the lowest temperature of the CO adsorption peak of bulk $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ indicates the strong desorption ability of CO-related intermediates, and consequently, CO is the main detected product.34 The significant increase in CO desorption peak temperature indicates a stronger adsorption affinity for CO-related intermediates on $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NBs and $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NRs, explaining the enhanced selectivity of $\mathrm{C}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{4}.$ . Subsequently, XAS was used to evaluate the local atomic structure of $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ perovskites. The feature and position of Cu L-edge soft X-ray absorption near-edge structure (XANES) spectra for $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ perovskites are similar to that of standard $\mathrm{CuO},$ , and no obvious signal of ${\mathrm{Cu}}^{0}$ and $\mathrm{{Cu}^{+}}$ can be observed. The results were confirmed by Cu K-edge XANES spectra, demonstrating that the ${\mathrm{Cu}}$ in all catalysts is in the state of $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$ , consistent with the results of Xray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) (Figure 4c and Figures S26 and S27). The FFT of Cu K-edge extended X-ray absorption fine structure (EXAFS) shows that the peaks of $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ perovskites are approximately $1.9\mathring\mathrm{A},$ attributed to the ${\mathrm{Cu}}{-}\mathrm{O}$ bonds (Figure 4d). Compared with standard ${\mathrm{CuO}}_{;}$ , obvious peaks of $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ perovskites at $3.2\textrm{\AA}$ can be assigned to the $\mathrm{Cu-La}$ bonds. Likewise, no ${\mathrm{Cu}}{-}\mathrm{Cu}$ bonds can be observed for all catalysts, consistent with the results of XANES. To acquire detailed structure information, the EXAFS spectra were fitted and the results are summarized (Figure S28 and  Table S4). Compared with $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}\mathrm{NRs}$ and bulk $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4},$ a slight increase of the ${\mathrm{Cu}}{-}\mathrm{O}$ and Cu−La bond length can be observed for $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NBs, demonstrating the presence of strain in TBs, which was confirmed by the results of HRTEM. Based on the above analysis, it is proposed that the presence of strain in TBs will increase the bond length of ${\mathrm{Cu}}{-}\mathrm{O}$ and $\mathrm{Cu}-$ La, resulting in improved intermediate adsorption and enhanced $\mathrm{FE}_{\mathrm{C_{2}H_{4}}}.$ The wavelet spectra of $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ perovskites contain two distinct intensities of ${\mathrm{Cu}}{-}\mathrm{O}$ and ${\mathrm{Cu-La}}$ coordination, consistent with the curve-fitting results (Figure 4e and Figure S29). To evaluate the evolution of $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NBs during the electrocatalytic process, in situ XANES spectra were conducted under the measurement conditions (Figure 4f). Obviously, the Cu K-edge XANES spectrum of $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NBs at the open-circuit potential in $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ -saturated $0.1~\mathrm{~M~}$ ${\mathrm{KHCO}}_{3}$ solution shows an outline and position similar to that of the conventional test, indicating that the electrolyte and $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ do not change the valence state of the catalyst. Interestingly, the Cu K-edge XANES spectrum is still well maintained when a voltage of $-1.0\mathrm{V}$ vs RHE was applied. The above results demonstrate that the catalyst is relatively stable during the catalytic process, which can also be confirmed by Cu L-edge soft XANES spectra (Figure S30).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): For a more detailed look, both surfaces of (113) and (111) are investigated regarding both electronic environment and energetic physicochemical trend. For the (113) surfaces, the bonding and antibonding orbitals near the Fermi level $\left(E_{\mathrm{F}}\right)$ demonstrate that the highly electroactive region dominantly concentrates on the surface Cu sites. We also identify the existence of gap states near La sites and O sites (Figure 5a). For the (111) surfaces, the Cu site is still the electroactive region, whereas the gap states become narrower than that of (113) surfaces. The evidently different electronic distribution indicates the distinct selectivity of the surface for the $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ reduction (Figure 5b). With a further look into the partial projected density of states (PDOSs), the different subtle electronic structure is revealed. For the (113) surface, $\mathrm{Cu}3\mathrm{d}$ orbitals display a sharp peak near $\mathrm{EV}{-}1{.}5~\mathrm{eV}$ $\mathrm{(EV~=~0~eV)},$ ), whereas the $\mathrm{~O~}2\mathfrak{p}$ orbitals exhibit a broad coverage from EV to $\mathrm{EV}{-}6.0~\mathrm{eV}.$ . The main contribution of La 5d orbitals lies in the conduction band. Notably, the gap states are constructed by the joint contribution of La, Cu, and O sites, which significantly promote the electron transfer by the annihilation of the barriers (Figure 5c). Although the electronic contribution on the (111) surface also shows the main contribution of ${\mathrm{Cu}}$ and La for both valence band maximum (VBM) and conduction band minimum (CBM), respectively, the electron transfer barrier still exists, which cannot be alleviated by the gap states. Such a barrier determines that the electron transfer efficiency on the (111) surface is consequently much lowered, especially for the $_{\mathrm{C-C}}$ coupling. The strain induced in the interfacial region of ${\mathrm{La}}_{2}{\mathrm{CuO}}_{4}$ NBs and $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NRs facilitates the electron transfer, which explains why only the $\mathrm{{C}_{1}}$ product is obtained for bulk $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ (Figure 5d). For the complicated $\mathrm{C}_{2}$ route of $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ reduction, the PDOSs of the key adsorbates are also illustrated. On the (113) surface, from the adsorption of the initial reactant to the final product $\mathrm{C}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{4},$ we notice a linear correlation on the dominant peak of the PDOSs, which guarantees the efficient electron transfer in the reaction coordinates. Meanwhile, Cu 3d orbitals demonstrate a highly stable valence state, which is consistent with the experimental characterizations (Figure 5e). On the contrary, such a linear correlation has been obviously weakened on (111) surfaces, which is attributed to the $_{\mathrm{C-C}}$ coupling with a larger electron transfer barrier induced by the overbinding of CO. In addition, a slight downshifting of the Cu 3d band center has been noticed, indicating that the local electroactivity on the surface is affected by the adsorption of intermediates (Figure 5f). By comparing the PDOS of both adsorbed CO, the (113) surface shows a more evident reduction trend due to the larger downshifting of CO orbitals, facilitating further $_{\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{C}}$ coupling. Meanwhile, the (111) surface demonstrates the stronger electron transfer with a proton, which leads to the high production of $\mathrm{H}_{2}$ in the experimental results (Figure 5g).  We also compare the energetic reaction trend of $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ reduction on both surfaces. For the (113) surface, the ratedetermining step (RDS) is the $_{\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{C}}$ coupling, which is the key step for the $C_{2}$ route in $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ reduction. The RDS shows a slight barrier of $0.38~\mathrm{eV}.$ . The strain induced within TBs in $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NBs largely promotes the $_{\mathrm{C-C}}$ couplings of the $C_{2}$ route. A strong reaction trend is noted for the (113) surface by releasing overall energy of $2.19~\mathrm{eV}_{;}$ , supporting the high FE of $\mathrm{C}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{4}$ formation (Figure $5\mathrm{h}$ ). On the contrary, we notice a much larger energy barrier of the RDS $_{(0.76~\mathrm{eV})}$ for the (111) surface, which is induced by the overbinding of $\mathrm{CO^{*}}$ . The 2- fold higher RDS barrier leads to the distinct selectivity of the surface, which is trapped in the formation of the $\mathrm{{C}_{1}}$ product of CO. Moreover, the overall reaction energy also supports the enhanced selectivity of the (113) surface on the $\mathrm{C}_{2}$ product of $\mathrm{C_{2}H_{4}}$ (Figure 5i). Therefore, the different selectivity of $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NBs, $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}\mathrm{NRs},$ and bulk $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ is attributed to the intrinsic electroactivity of the different surfaces. For $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NBs with high FE of $\mathrm{C_{2}H_{4}},$ the main contribution comes from both highly electroactive (113) surfaces and the facilitation of strain induced between TBs. Meanwhile, the $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4}$ NRs show less selectivity to the $C_{2}$ product due to the less electroactive (111) surfaces. A minute amount of $\mathrm{C}_{2}$ product is achieved by the strain of GBs, which lowers the energy barrier of the RDS and increases the electron transfer efficiency. For bulk $\mathrm{La}_{2}\mathrm{CuO}_{4},$ the lack of strain cannot overcome the RDS barrier of $_{\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{C}}$ coupling in the $C_{2}$ route, which leads to the formation of CO and $\mathrm{H}_{2}$ in the product. Therefore, our calculations have confirmed the contributions of both high-index facets and the twin boundary to the superior performance of $\mathrm{CO}_{2}\mathrm{RR}$ in this work.

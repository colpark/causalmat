- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): While $Z\mathrm{nO}$ NWs are widely used as an electron transporting layer (ETL) for the fabrication of efficient organic and PbS QDs solar cells, there is a comparatively limited attention in their use in PSCs due to often encountered chemical instability of $Z\mathrm{nO/}$ perovskite interface.[37,38] In this study, we deposit doubleA cation perovskite (methylammonium (MA)/formamidinium (FA)) on $Z\mathrm{nO}$ NWs ETL grown by hydrothermal technique (Figure S1, Supporting Information). Figure 1a,b show the scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of the decomposed perovskite film annealed on top of ZnO NWs with different magnifications. As seen, there are three different regions in the film containing only $\mathrm{PbI}_{2}$ flakes, perovskite crystals, and mixed $\mathrm{Pb}\mathrm{I}_{2}$ and perovskite phases. Ultraviolet-visible (UV)–visible spectrum (Figure 1c) and X-ray diffraction pattern (XRD) (Figure 1d) indicate the presence of $\mathrm{PbI}_{2}$ phase in the perovskite film, which is in good agreement with the SEM images.[39] Notably, we may have some $\delta$ phase after annealing in the perovskite film. There are two approaches to tackle with this stability issue, that is, annealing of perovskite film at low temperature $(70~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ or applying an interface layer between $Z\mathrm{nO}$ NWs and perovskite.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In this context, we passivate the surface of $Z\mathrm{nO}$ NWs with a thin layer of amorphous $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ $(4\mathrm{nm})$ , deposited by atomic layer deposition (ALD). Figure  2a–c shows the schematic of device fabrication on the ZnO $\mathrm{NWs}/\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ ETL. As seen, the $Z\mathrm{nO}$ NWs were grown on the indium-doped tin oxide (ITO) glass coated by a $Z\mathrm{nO}$ seed layer (Figure 2a). Then, a conformal layer of $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ was deposited on the $Z\mathrm{nO}$ NWs array using ALD method (Figure 2b). Transmission electron microscopy images shown in Figure S2, Supporting Information confirms the presence of uniform amorphous $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ layer around the ZnO NW with an interplanar d-spacing of $0.26~\mathrm{nm}$ (corresponded to the (002) planes).[39] We also measured the photoluminescence spectra of the NWs and NWs/ $\mathrm{{\dot{S}n O}}_{2}$ samples and observed a near band emission peak located at ${\approx}380\mathrm{nm}$ and a broader green emission peak (defect peak) at the visible region for both samples (Figure S3, Supporting Information). The enhanced PL peak at $380~\mathrm{nm}$ and the quenched green emission peak for the NWs $\mathrm{{\sf~SnO}}_{2}$ sample clearly shows that the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ layer passivates the surface defects of the NWs array,[12] which is also consistent with the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) results (Figure S4, Note S2, Supporting Information). The XPS results indicate that ALD deposition of $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ on the surface of $Z\mathrm{nO}$ NWs array can passivate the surface defects of NWs and reduce the presence of oxygen vacancies and especially hydroxyl groups, resulting in high quality $Z\mathrm{nO}$ NWs array.[40]  Figure 2d shows SEM image of the perovskite film deposited on the NWs/ $\mathrm{{\dot{S}n O}}_{2}$ ETL (annealed at $150~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ ), indicating a pinhole-free morphology and great crystallinity of the perovskite film. Moreover, the perovskite film has an average grain size of ${\approx}420~\mathrm{nm}$ , as confirmed by atomic force microscopy analysis as well. As shown in Figure S5, Supporting Information, the perovskite film has a smooth surface with a roughness of $23\pm5$ nm. XRD pattern of the perovskite film, shown in Figure  2e, indicates excellent crystallinity of the perovskite film with a small peak of ${\mathrm{PbI}}_{2}$ originated from excess $\mathrm{Pb}\mathrm{I}_{2}$ in the perovskite composition. In order to study the effect NWs array on optical properties, we have measured the transmittance spectra of the $Z\mathrm{nO}$ NWs/ITO glass, and $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}/\mathrm{ZnO}$ NWs/ITO glass samples as compared to the ITO glass and planar $Z\mathrm{nO/ITO}$ glass, as shown in Figure S6a, Supporting Information. NWs array generally causes optical scattering, which results in a longer optical path length in the absorber layer and increases the probability of the optical absorption in the perovskite film.[41,42] As seen in Figure S6a, Supporting Information, the NWs based sample has $6-10\%$ more transmittance over the entire wavelengths as compared to the planar sample, indicating their effect on light trapping properties. This can drastically enhance the perovskite absorption and thus increase the current density in the device. Figure S6b, Supporting Information depicts UV–visible spectra of the perovskite films on NWs and $\mathrm{NWs}/\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ ETLs indicating similar bandgap of ${\approx}1.55~\mathrm{eV}$ and higher absorption of perovskite film on NWs/ $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ sample due to its better crystallinity. Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy reveals that perovskite film on $\mathrm{NWs}/\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ sample has stronger quenching effect as compared to the NWs sample (Figure 2f). Thus, a better charge transfer at the interface of perovskite/ $\mathrm{NWs}/\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ is expected as compared with the perovskite/ZnO. When we have a stronger quenching effect, it means that the electrons can quickly transfer from the perovskite layer to the ETL before they recombine with holes. For the case of $Z\mathrm{nO/}$ $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ /perovskite sample, the electrons transfer from perovskite to the ETL faster than the $Z\mathrm{nO}_{I}$ /perovskite one. Therefore, in $\mathrm{ZnO}/\mathrm{SnO}_{2}/$ perovskite case, the chance of non-radiative recombination is lower than the $Z\mathrm{nO}/$ perovskite sample and we have a better charge transfer.[10,12] We also performed time-resolved PL (TRPL) spectroscopy for the corresponding samples, as shown in Figure 2g. Biexponential model was used to fit these data as summarized in Table S1, Supporting Information. The perovskite film on NWs/ $\mathrm{{\sf~SnO}}_{2}$ ETL shows shorter lifetime than the perovskite on pure NWs, which is in good agreement with the PL results. In addition to the above results, we also compared the optical properties of perovskite films deposited on planar ${\mathrm{ZnO/SnO}}_{2}$ and ZnO NWs/ $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ ETLs, as illustrated in Figure S7, Supporting Information. We find that the NWsbased sample shows higher absorption due to better light trapping properties of NWs and stronger quenching effect due to larger surface area as compared to the planar sample.[5]

We found that the $\mathrm{NWs}/\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ device shows lower series resistance $\left(R_{\mathrm{s}}\right)$ and larger recombination resistance $(R_{\mathrm{Rec}})$ as compared to the NWs PSCs, which can explain the observed higher FF and $V_{\mathrm{OC}}$ in the $\mathrm{NWs}/\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ device, respectively. Additionally, we performed ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurements for the NWs and NWs/ $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ ETLs and found that the band offset between the conduction band of perovskite film and the $\mathrm{NWs}/$ $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ ETL is less than that of NWs ETL, resulting in a better electron transfer at the interface of perovskite/ETL (Figure S11, Supporting Information). As seen, the average values of PV parameters for the NWs/ $\mathrm{{\dot{S}n O}}_{2}$ PSCs are higher than those of the $Z\mathrm{nO}$ NWs ones, which indicate the same trend as the best performing devices. We find that the average value of HI for the NWs/ $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ based PSCs $(1.09\%)$ is lower than that of $(1.46\%)$ the NWs devices, which can be correlated to the stronger quenching effect in the device with NWs/ $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ ETL. Besides, the operational stability of the PSCs on NWs and NWs/ $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ ETLs was monitored over $200\mathrm{~h~}$ under continuous illumination and nitrogen environment (Figure S14, Supporting Information). Interestingly, the $\mathrm{NWs}/\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ device retains $92\%$ of its initial PCE after $200\mathrm{~h~}$ , whereas the NWs cell degrades quickly, possibly due to the light activated chemical reaction at the ETL/perovskite interface.

To further increase the $V_{\mathrm{OC}}$ of the low-bandgap PbS QDs, we reduced the surface defects of $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{PbS}}$ QDs by atomic passivation technique. Therefore, there is a trade-off between $J_{\mathrm{SC}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{OC.}}$ Although the PCE of the PbS QDs devices is dropped by decrease of the bandgap, the current match between top and bottom solar cells in a tandem configuration is a key parameter and need to be improved for further increase in the PCE of tandem devices. This could be beneficial for improving the stability of the PSC devices in a monolithic tandem device architecture using the QDs device as a back cell.  In order to fabricate perovskite/PbS QDs tandem devices, we fabricated semi-transparent PSCs using ITO glass as top electrode and copper (II) phthalocyanine (CuPC) as an hole transporting layer (HTL). After proper optimization, we fabricated semi-transparent PSCs as front cells in a 2T-tandem design by changing the HTL and top electrode of front cells. In particular, we employed CuPC and Au $(1\mathrm{nm})/\mathrm{MoO}_{3}/$ sputtered ITO as HTL and recombination layer, respectively, and optimized the device performance and the thickness of perovskite active layer, in order to match the current density in both subcells. In fact, spiro HTL due to having some dopants and high optimum thickness reduces the stability of the tandem device and increases the parasitic absorption. Since CuPC is a stable HTL, does not require any dopant and can be deposited uniformly with low thickness by thermal evaporation, it would be a great replacement for the spiro HTL.[44] As reported in the literature,[45] Au (1 nm) gold nanocluster is an excellent recombination layer, which improves the FF and $V_{\mathrm{OC}}$ of the tandem device. Moreover, the purpose of the ${\mathrm{MoO}}_{3}$ thin layer is band alignment and protection of the underneath layers from the damages caused by ITO sputtering.[45] We used different thicknesses of perovskite layer in 2T and 4T tandem devices by changing the concentration of solution in order to improve the efficiency. Stability is one of the main challenges in the PSCs field, which is compulsory step for commercialization of PSCs.[46] Consequently, we measured the stability of our 2T-tandem device at maximum power point, under continuous illumination and nitrogen flow. We first tracked the $V_{\mathrm{OC}}$ of this device over $200~\mathrm{s}$ , as shown in Figure 4j. As seen, the $V_{\mathrm{OC}}$ is stable over time with a value of over $1.55\mathrm{V}.$ Figure 4k depicts the operational stability results of this device over $500\mathrm{h}$ under continuous illumination. From the results, the 2T-tandem device retains $94\%$ of its initial PCE value, which is a great stability for such design. In fact, PbS QDs device has excellent stability in ambient condition (Figure S21, Supporting Information), even in humid environment.[30] As can be observed in the inset image of Figure 4k, the contact angle of water droplet on the surface of PbS QDs layer is ${\approx}97^{\circ}$ , which can effectively protect the PSCs front cell from humidity. Consequently, combining PSCs and PbS QDs solar cells in a tandem device is a great advantage not only to improve the PCE and $V_{\mathrm{OC}}$ of the device, but also to enhance the ambient stability of the PSCs. Figure S23, Supporting Information shows the ambient stability results of a single junction PSC and a perovskite/PbS QDs 2T tandem device in a humid environment with $65\%$ RH. As can be observed from the results, the tandem device shows no PCE loss after 72 days in these conditions, whereas the single junction PSC degraded very fast in the same condition with $37\%$ PCE loss. This result highlights the role of PbS QDs as a great protection layer on ambient stability for the perovskite/PbS QDs tandem design.

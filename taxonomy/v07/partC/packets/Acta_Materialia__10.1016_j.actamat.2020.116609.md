- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Fig. 4(a) and (b) present the typical Tafel plots of all samples after PD tests in 0.6 M NaCl aqueous solution at room temperature.  The measured $\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{corr}}$ and $\mathrm{i}_{\mathrm{corr}}$ are summarized in Tables 2 and 3. It is noted here while current rather than current density was preferred for Tafel plots of galvanic couples, in the present work, each NMM sample is treated as a ‘composite’ material and the measured current density is calculated by normalizing the corrosion current by the exposed sample area. In Fig. 4(a), $\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{corr}}$ of monolithic Ti and Cu are more positive while that of $\mathsf{M g}$ more negative than that of Al, which is in agreement with previous results [48-50]. The values of $\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{corr}}$ increase in the order of Mg, Al, Ti, and Cu, in consistent with their reduction potential trend from the EMF series under the standard state. $\mathrm{i}_{\mathrm{corr}}$ is the smallest for pure Ti and highest for Mg. Among all monolithic samples, Al exhibited active corrosion in the anodic branch, Cu and Mg exhibited pitting behavior at $\mathrm{E_{pit}}$ ,  while Ti exhibited an active-pseudo passive behavior, with a transition potential around $0.4~\mathrm{V}$ vs. Ag/AgCl.  Fig. 4(b) shows that the $\mathbf{i}_{\mathrm{corr}}$ of all NMM samples increases in the order of $\mathsf{A l/T i,A l/C u},$ , and Al/Mg while $\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{corr}}$ increases in the order of $\mathsf{A l/M g,A l/T i}$ , and $\mathsf{A l}/\mathsf{C u}$ . The anodic polarization branches of all NMMs are characteristically different. For both Al/Mg and $\mathsf{A l}/\mathsf{C u}$ NMMs, an active-passive type of behavior was observed, where the anodic corrosion current density first increased with potential from $\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{corr}}$ ,then reversed to a lower value over a range of more positive potentials. Interestingly, for both samples, two primary passive potential $\mathrm{E_{pp}}$ was observed, at $\sim-1.62\mathrm{~V~}$ and \~ -0.74 V vs. $\mathrm{Ag/AgCl}$ for Al/Mg NMMs, and $\sim0.03\mathrm{~V~}$ and 0.57 V vs. Ag/AgCl for Al/Cu NMMs. Such behavior is likely due to the switching of corrosion mode between a passive state, contributed mainly by the outmost Al layer, and an active corrosion state by the underneath Mg and Cu layers as corrosion progressed through the thickness of the NMMs. On the other hand, the anodic branch of $\mathsf{A l/T i}$ exhibited a stable passive state over the majority of the anodic potential with $\mathrm{i}_{\mathrm{corr}}$ of $\sim0.042\mu\mathrm{A}/\mathrm{cm}^{2}$ ,  smaller than that of either Al or Ti. This $\mathrm{i}_{\mathrm{corr}}$ value is around two orders of magnitude smaller than those reported of $\mathsf{A l/T i}$ bilayers and multilayers with micrometer-sized individual layer thickness [51,52].  Mott-Schottky (MS) results are shown in Fig. 4(c-f). The capacitance of the Helmholtz layer is assumed to be much bigger than the semiconductor capacitance of the passive layer, hence the measured capacitance C is taken as the semiconductor capacitance of the passive layer to calculate the defect densities according to [53,54] $\begin{array}{r}{\frac{1}{c^{2}}=\frac{\pm\bar{2}}{\varepsilon\cdot\varepsilon_{0}\cdot\mathsf{q}\cdot\mathsf{N}}\times\mathsf{\Omega}(\mathsf{E}-\mathsf{E}_{\mathrm{fb}}-\frac{\mathrm{kT}}{\mathsf{q}})}\end{array}$ , where $\varepsilon$ is the passive film dielectric constant (10 for $\mathsf{A l}_{2}\mathsf{O}_{3}$ $\varepsilon_{0}$ is the permittivity of vacuum $^{\prime}\varepsilon_{0}{=}8.85\times10^{-14}\mathrm{F/cm})$ , N is the donor or acceptor density, $\mathrm{E_{fb}}$ is the flat band potential, k is the Boltzmann constant $(1.38~\times~10^{-23}~\mathrm{JK^{-1}})$ , T is the absolute temperature, and $\mathbf{q}$ is the elementary charge $\phantom{0}{(1.602\times10^{-19}}\phantom{x}\mathrm{C})$ . According to this relationship, a positive slope of the MS curve indicate the presence of ntype defect such as oxygen vacancies and cation interstitials (e.g. ${\sf A}{\sf I}^{3+}$ ), while a negative slope indicates $\mathsf{p}$ -type defect such as cation vacancies. Fig. 4 shows that while only n-type defects are present in $\mathsf{A l/T i}$ over the whole potential range, both $\mathsf{A l/M g}$ and Al/Cu experiences a transition from n-type to p-type when the potential increases above OCP. It is likely the active corrosion of $\mathsf{M g}$ and Cu in the later lead to the dissolution of metallic ions at the surface, leaving behind cation vacancies. Fig. 4(c) shows the n-type defect density of all NMMs measured from the cathodic potential region (between the dashed lines in Fig. 4(d-f), hence corresponds to that of the intact surface Al layer. It can be seen that the defect density of all samples is \~ $10^{20}/\mathrm{cm}^{3}$ ,  with $\mathsf{A l/T i}$ slightly higher than the rest. These values are about two orders of magnitude lower than those measured in pure Al and Al 3003 alloy [53].

On the other hand, the small potential shift of $\mathsf{A l/M g}$ and $\sf A l/C u$ indicates a lack of surface passivity. Hence depassivation by the counter body has small effect on OCP.

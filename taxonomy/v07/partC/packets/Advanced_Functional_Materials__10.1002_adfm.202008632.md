- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Boron element located in the period 2 group 13, as the only non-metal element in group 13, boron is unique and fascinating. From the perspective of hybrid orbital theory, boron has two types of hybridization forms, $\operatorname{sp}^{3}$ hybrid and $\operatorname{sp}^{2}$ hybrid (Figure  1), and forms four covalent bonds and three covalent bonds respectively, thus resulting in anionic tetracoordinate boron centers or neutral tricoordinate boron centers in organoboron compounds. Hybridization form determines the electronic structure around boron atom. In $\operatorname{sp}^{2}$ hybrid form with an unoccupied p-orbital available, boron exhibits electron deficiency property. This means that compounds containing $\operatorname{sp}^{2}$ boron atom will act as electron acceptors or Lewis acids. In contrast, boron atom in $\operatorname{sp}^{3}$ hybrid form is at the state of full octet of electrons structure with a negative charge. As a result, boron will behave like an electron donor or Lewis base. This flexible hybridization form enables boron to generate diverse compounds with adjustable physicochemical properties. The structural uniqueness of boron compounds brings about enhanced lithium-ion transportation, better thermal stability, and more stable solid electrolyte interface to the electrolytes for lithium batteries.[29] Thus far, various boron compounds have been extensively explored in lithium batteries.  In this review, we focus on organoboron compounds employed in PEs. Some other widely used inorganic borides such as lithium tetrafluoroborate, boron nitride nanomaterials, boron-doped carbon materials, and borohydride-scaffolded Li fast ionic conductors are not covered in this work. To the best of our knowledge, no critical reviews dedicated to organoboron-containing PEs have been systematically summarized yet. Here, depending on their ways to be introduced into PEs, organoboron compounds can be classified as follows: one type is boron moieties covalently linked to polymer matrixes, and the other type is boron moieties added to PEs directly. Each class of boron moieties that include $\mathrm{sp}^{3}$ and $\operatorname{sp}^{2}$ structures will be discussed within the scope of PEs utilized in lithium batteries, including their physicochemical and electrochemical properties (Figure  1), and selected examples of advanced organoboron containing PEs are presented in detail. This review aims to highlight the diverse functions and great potentials of boron element applied in PEs. Besides, it is anticipated to offer some insights into the problems confronting in organoboron-containing PEs, and thus to facilitate the exploration of organoboron containing PEs towards practical applications.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In order to accelerate Li-ion transportation, the ion-association between anions and Li cations should be diminished through expanding the charge distribution of anion moieties. The incorporation of aromatic conjugated groups can lead to the delocalization of the negative charge of the anion moieties and increase ionic conductivity. Cheng et  al.[45] synthesized a borate linked PE using bisphenol-A (BPA), where boron atoms are connected to aromatic conjugated groups (Figure  2a). The electron delocalization of boron atoms through $\pi$ conjugation can promote the dissociation of $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ and increase the ionic conductivity. At $60~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , when the molar ratio of $\mathrm{Li^{+}},$ /ethylene oxide was $1/8$ , the prepared PE exhibited the highest ionic conductivity of $1.7\times10^{-4}\mathrm{Scm^{-1}}$ and a $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ transference number value of 0.92. The polymer cell based on this electrolyte showed excellent capacity retention after 90 cycles, in the temperature range of $50{-}80~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ at various C rates (Figure 3a). Specifically, an initial discharge capacity of $147\mathrm{mAhg^{-1}}$ was delivered at $80~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , and about $100\mathrm{mAhg^{-1}}$ was realized at $50~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ (Figure 3b).  Raising the flexibility of polymer chain is another effective strategy, which has been accepted to promote the transport of the charged species. Ignacio González et  al. prepared a series of SICPE on the basis of anionic tetracoordinate borate and poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG) bridges (Figure 2b). The ionic transportation can be facilitated through boosting the chain mobility as the chain length was increased (Figure 3c).[46] After optimization, the highest ionic conductivity was $5\times10^{-6}\mathrm{Scm^{-1}}$ . The $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ transference number of SICPE membranes at $25~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ decreased from 0.97 to 0.88 when the PEG bridge length between tetracoordinate borate was increased.  In order to further enhance the ionic conductivity of SICPE based on tetracoordinate borate linkers and flexible PEG chains, partial crosslinking and adjusting the electron-withdrawing substituents attached to the $\mathrm{BO_{4}}^{-}$ groups are employed.[47] Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations show that for $\mathrm{BO_{4}^{-}{-}L i^{+}}$ salt substituted with $-\mathrm{CH}_{3}$ and $-\mathrm{CF}_{3}$ groups (Figure 3d), $-\mathrm{CF}_{3}$ groups have an essential effect on decreasing the charge associated to the $\mathrm{BO_{4}}^{-}$ , enlarging the $\mathrm{O-Li^{+}}$ distance and reducing the ${\mathrm{BO}}_{4}{}^{-}{-}{\mathrm{Li}}^{+}$ interaction energy (Figure  3e). The calculation results were also confirmed by nuclear magnetic resonance analysis, where the chemical environment of B and Li changes substantially. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy results revealed that the substituents are insufficient to significantly improve the ionic conductivity, due to the limited chain mobility at room temperature. In other words, in addition to the improved mobility of the chains, the effect of electron-withdrawing group substituents should be further emphasized to achieve high conductivity in boron-containing polymers.

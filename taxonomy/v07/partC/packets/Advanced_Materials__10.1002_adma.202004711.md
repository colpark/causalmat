- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The ring-opening polymerization between the mixed DOL-DME solvent and Li alloys is the key step for the formation of artificial SEI layer, which was verified by Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR) and given in Figure 2a. The assignments of the FT-IR spectra are summarized in Table S1, Supporting Information. As shown in the spectrum of the $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ , the existence of peaks at $879~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ , related to the formation of ROLi or RCOOLi; at $1083\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ , related to the $\scriptstyle\nu\mathrm{{C-O}}$ bond, clearly indicates the constituents of SEI layer according to the previous studies related to DOL-DME liquid electrolyte systems.[39,43,44] Those peaks are also found in the FT-IR spectrum of $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Ge}_{5}$ and $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Sn}_{5}$ (Figure S4, Supporting Information) but not found in Si and $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ , indicating that the special layer forms on the surface of Li alloys and is different from those on the Si and $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ due to the ring-opening polymerization.  Figure  2b shows the XRD patterns of the $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}$ , $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Si}$ , $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ , and $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ electrolyte membranes prepared with the process mentioned above. The $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}$ exhibits two high intensity diffraction peaks at $19.2^{\circ}$ and $23.5^{\circ}$ (the normalized intensity listed in Table S2, Supporting Information), which reflect the crystalline chain structure in the PEO membranes. However, these two characteristic peaks of crystalline PEO chains show lower intensity in the $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Si}$ , $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ , and especially in $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ , indicating that the Si, $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ , and $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ fillers, as the obstacles, prevent the PEO chains from the ordered arrangement.[45–47] According to the intensity change, the addition of $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ exhibits strongest effect on decreasing the crystallinity of PEO matrix. The differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) results of those electrolyte membranes and pure PEO powder, as shown in Figure  2c, further reveal the lowering of crystalline stability of PEO matrix after adding Si-based filler particles. The endothermic peak of the pure PEO powders, representing the crystal-amorphous transition, appeared at $60~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{i}}$ and the peaks decreased to $48~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}$ electrolytes due to the addition of LiTFSI. Benefiting from the adding of inorganic and metallic particles, the crystallization peak of PEO matrix decreases further for $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Si}$ , $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ , and $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ , and $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ membranes exhibit the lowest endothermic peak at $43~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . This means that the irregular arranged chains in PEO matrix could maintain at lower temperature owing to the stronger interaction between $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ and PEO matrix compared with those added with Si and $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ fillers. Similarly, adding other Li-based alloy fillers, such as Li– Ge and $\mathrm{Li}{-}\ensuremath{\mathrm{Sn}}$ alloy, as shown in Figure S5, Supporting Information, also inhibits crystallization of the PEO. Nevertheless, the $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ exhibits the lowest amorphous to crystal transition peak compared with those added with $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Ge}_{5}$ and $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Sn}_{5}$ fillers, which may be due to the higher volume fraction of $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ at the same mass ratio.  The Li 1s, C 1s, O 1s, and $\mathrm{F}$ 1s of the $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}$ , $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Si}$ , $\mathrm{{PEO}_{\mathrm{{m}}}.}$ $5\%\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ , and $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ electrolyte membranes were characterized by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) to distinguish the chemical state changes after adding those fillers. As shown in Figure 2d, the XPS results of the $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Si}$ and $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ are similar compared with $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}$ , which means that the addition of Si and $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ has little effect on the chemical states of Li, C, O, and F. However, the chemical states of these elements changed in the $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ and the appeared peaks are: 1) RCOO-Li at Li 1s spectra (55.4 eV), C 1s spectra $(287.8~\mathrm{eV})$ and O 1s spectra $(531.2~\mathrm{eV})$ ; 2) LiF at Li 1s spectra (56.7  eV), and F 1s spectra (684.9 eV).[48–53] Since the RCOOLi and LiF are the typical components of SEI layer contributed from the ring-opening polymerization, this result strongly indicated that the artificial SEI layer was successfully constructed in the PEO matrix, although some characteristic signals of the SEI layer, such as $_{\mathrm{R-O-Li}}$ , were covered by the strong $_{\mathrm{C-O}}$ signals from PEO matrix. Besides, the existence of a small amount of LiOH should be due to the oxidation of $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ , and the $\mathrm{Li}_{\gamma}\mathrm{SiO}_{x}$ could also be found in the $\mathrm{Si}~2\mathrm{p}$ spectra, as shown in Figure S6, Supporting Information.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The distribution of Li and Si from the $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ particles to the PEO matrix in $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}\cdot\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ electrolyte membranes were further characterized by scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM). Figure 4a displays the ADF-STEM image obtained in the typical region of SEI layer. The content changes of Li and Si from $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ particle to PEO matrix were analyzed by the electron energy loss spectroscopy (EELS) spectra of Li K-edge and Si $\mathrm{L}_{2,3}$ edge, marked from 1 to 8 and were shown in Figure S7, Supporting Information. Although the curves indicate that the Li and Si elements are oxidized partly due to the radiation damage caused by beam effects,[54,55] the signal intensities of Li and Si elements show completely different trends, as shown in  Figure 4b: For the Si element, the signal intensity drops rapidly in PEO matrix from the edge of $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ particle, which indicates that Si does not diffuse from the alloy filler to the PEO matrix. On the contrary, however, Li presents gradient distribution from high to low in the region between the $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ particle and the PEO matrix. This feature is also obvious from the restructured EELS mapping of Si (Figure 4c) and Li (Figure 4d), respectively.  The above result confirms solidly the formation of artificial interface layer, which is composed of the SEI characteristic components such as RCOO–Li and LiF formed in liquid electrolyte, on the surface of $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ particle by the reaction of $\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ with DOL-DME solvent and PEO matrix in the prepared $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}\mathrm{-Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ composite polymer electrolyte. This artificially constructed SEI interface layer is expected to possess the ability of fast $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ transport, which could finally promote the ionic conductivity of the electrolyte. It should be noted that the thickness of the gradient distribution of Li is about ${\approx}50{-}75~\mathrm{nm}$ , which is thicker than the layer observed by the TEM, indicating that the Li would diffuse further into the PEO matrix. In other words, the real interface region for fast $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ transport is wider than that observed by TEM, and would definitely be beneficial to the ionic conductivity in the polymer electrolyte. Moreover, this is the first time the reliable thickness of the interface has been directly measured rather than estimation.

This result indicates that the $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ could still maintain stability in all-solid-state lithium metal batteries during cycling in a wide temperature range. As shown in Figure  7d, the midpoint voltage of the discharge curve is $3.39\mathrm{~V~}$ at $60~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , and when the temperature decreased to 45 and $30~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , the midpoint voltage decreased very small. Moreover, when the temperature was reduced to $30~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ the cells with $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ electrolyte could still provide a rather high discharge plateau of $3.35\mathrm{~V~}$ and exhibited a reversible capacity of $135.4\operatorname*{mA}\mathrm{h}\mathrm{g}^{-1}$ . This is a great breakthrough in comparison with $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}$ which cannot provide capacity at this temperature at all. It is worth mentioning that, at $30~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , the capacity would increase at the initial cycles. This is because, at the initial cycle, a homogenization process of $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ would occur in most composite electrolyte, and the soft and viscoelastic polymer electrolyte would promote the uniform deposition of Li and permeate into the pores of the electrode materials. This process would lead to the formation of $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ transport pathways and improve the contact between the electrolyte and electrode.[62,63] Usually, it would not be noticed when the electrolyte working higher than the temperature of crystal–amorphous transition because the polymer is very ductile. However, at $30~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , there are a large number of crystalline PEO chains in the electrolyte, and the ductility decreases rapidly, and it would take one or more cycles to form the stable and wellcontacted electrode–electrolyte interface. Therefore, the capacity is relatively low in the initial cycles. Moreover, in general, the capacity of lithium metal batteries using polymer electrolytes will decrease rapidly at high current density due to the low ionic conductivity. On the contrary, by constructing artificial SEI layer in PEO matrix, the $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ exhibits excellent rate performance at $45~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}_{:}$ , as shown in Figure 7e. The $\mathrm{LiFePO_{4}}$ | $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.$ - $5\%\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}\mid$ Li cells could provide capacity of 164.4, 152.8, 136.8, 116.3, and $84.5~\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ at 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 1, and $2\mathrm{C}$ , respectively. Even if the current density increases to $4\mathrm{C}$ the cells still deliver a reversible capacity of 47.7  mA  h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ . The discharge/charge curves of rate performance at $45~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ are shown in Figure  7f, and the midpoint voltage decreases from 3.39 to $3.09\mathrm{~V~}$ when the rate increases from 0.05 to $4\textrm{C}$ . This rather small decrease in voltage indicates that the $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5}$ still exhibits the ability of fast ion transport at high current density. In addition, as the rate performance at $30~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ shows in Figure S14, Supporting Information, the cells could still maintain a stable capacity of ${\approx}120~\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ at the rate of $0.3~\mathrm{~C~}$ . The long-term cycling performance of the cells with current density of $0.5\mathrm{~C~}$ at $45~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ is shown in Figure $7\mathrm{g}$ , and the high reversible capacity of $111.3~\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ could be obtained even after 200 cycles with high Coulombic efficiencies of near $100\%$ and high capacity retention of $79.5\%$ under this rather high rate. The cycling and rate performance of the cells assembled with $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}.5\%\mathrm{Li}_{21}\mathrm{Si}_{5},$ as shown in Figure $\mathrm{7h}$ and Table S4, Supporting Information, are superior to that of the previous typical $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}$ -inorganic fillers, $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}$ -inorganic electrolytes and $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}$ -metal organic frameworks composite electrolytes.[16,22,23,64–70] In all, constructing the artificial SEI interface layer by adding Li-based alloy fillers promotes the ionic conductivity and reduces the working temperature of composite electrolyte of $\mathrm{PEO}_{\mathrm{m}}$ matrix significantly and it can achieve excellent electrochemical performance even when its working temperature is ${\approx}15{-}20{}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ lower than the composite electrolytes with other fillers.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To fulfill all the desired properties of hydrogel wound dressings for chronic diabetic wounds, herein, we facilely designed a multifunctional “smart” hydrogel utilizing pre-assembled complex between EGCG and APBA (E-A complex) as dynamic cross-linker to copolymerize with acrylamide (AM) (Figure 1a). Owing to the dynamic nature of the boronate ester bonds, the hydrogel features moderate tissue-adhesive capability to facilitate ease of application, and can be easily peeled off from skin tissues without any residuals (Figure 1b); the two-cut hydrogel species can self-heal efficiently to withstand 3 times elongation after contacting for only 3  min (Figure  1c); these outstanding performances can ensure the efficient sealing of the wound site and prevent the loss of protection upon normal body action and local stress. Additionally, EGCG can be released substantially in a controlled manner in situ to exert antimicrobial, anti-oxidative, pro-angiogenic, and anti-inflammatory effects and accelerate the healing process in a clinical desired time frame (Figure  1d). Importantly, with the release of EGCG, the hydrogel network gradually dissociates and can be totally removed through wiping or showering the wound site with wound cleanser for dressing change, which can prevent the damage of the newly-formed tissue and improve the care quality compared to the reported stimuli-triggered dissolvable hydrogel dressings.[17]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The synthetic procedures of E-A complex-based polyacrylamide  hydrogel (EACPA hydrogel) and further applications in diabetic chronic wound dressing are depicted in Figure  1. As shown in Figure S1a, Supporting Information, a significant increase of the fluorescence intensity at $595~\mathrm{nm}$ was observed when the molar ratio of APBA and EGCG was higher than 2 in the presence of Alizarin Red S (ARS). Theoretically, one molecule of EGCG can react with two molecules of APBA to form boronate esters as crosslinkers (E-A complex). Based on the standard curve of the free APBA with ARS solution (Figure S1b, Supporting Information), it was calculated that there were 1.8 molecules of APBA linked with 1 molecule of EGCG when the ratio of APBA to EGCG was 2, and the further increase of APBA content did not result in obvious increase of reaction efficiency. Hence, the ratio of 2 for APBA to EGCG was selected in this work to form the E-A complex acting as dynamic crosslinkers. The formed E-A complex was directly processed for the polymerization with AM in the presence of N, N, N, N-tetramethyl ethylene-1,2-diamine (TEMED) and ammonium persulfate (APS) to afford the hydrogel formation. As shown in Figure 2a, the mixture of the prepared E-A complex and AM aqueous solution (2 m) presented in the sol state at room temperature, and experienced sol–gel transition upon the addition of APS and TEMED after purging with nitrogen for 5 min. We found that the hydrogel formed with 3 mm E-A complex was too soft, and there were precipitates observed in the mixture with the E-A complex of $11~\mathrm{mM}$ due to its limited water solubility. Hence, the E-A complex with the concentration of 5, 7, and $9\mathrm{m}\mathrm{\mathbf{M}}$ was selected in the hydrogel preparation. The tube inversion method was adopted to investigate the gelation time for different systems. As the concentration of the E-A complex increased from 5 to $9\mathrm{mm}$ , the gelation time of EACPA hydrogel increased accordingly from 8 to $15\mathrm{min}$ (Table S1, Supporting Information), which may be attributed to the more free phenol groups with the higher EGCG content.[14c] To monitor the sol–gel transition and evaluate the moduli of the hydrogels, rheology experiments were conducted at a fixed frequency (10 rad $\mathrm{~s^{-1}}^{\phantom{-1}}$ ) and $1\%$ strain. As present in Figure S2, Supporting Information, the storage moduli $\left(G^{\prime}\right)$ of the three systems were very low in the initial stage, and a significant increase in $G^{\prime}$ was observed with increased polymerization time, indicating the sol–gel transition. The higher crosslinking density led to the enhanced modulus, which was 320, 790, and 1160 Pa at the plateau for E-A complex content of 5, 7, and $9\mathrm{mm}$ , respectively. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) results showed that all the EACPA hydrogel samples presented an irregular and porous network structure with the pore size of several hundreds of micrometers (Figure  2b; Figure S3, Supporting Information), which may endow the hydrogel sufficient air permeability. The increase of the E-A complex content led to the smaller pore size owing to the higher crosslinking density.  Degradation behavior is a pivotal performance parameter for the dressing change in the wound healing process since the frequent change and hard removal of the dressings may increase the cost and cause secondary injury to the wound. The in vitro degradation studies demonstrated that the mass of all the three EACPA hydrogels increased initially due to the swelling behavior at $\mathrm{pH}7.4$ (citric acid– $\mathrm{\cdotNa}_{2}\mathrm{HPO}_{4}$ buffer solution), which is beneficial for the absorption of excessive tissue exudate. Since the boronate ester bond is reversible, the mass of the hydrogels gradually decreased with the longer incubation time. The higher E-A complex concentration led to a slower degradation rate due to the enhanced crosslinking density (Figure  2c). The release of EGCG could lead to the gradual dissociation of the hydrogel network, by which the dressings can be easily removed with routine dressing change procedures (showering the wound site with lukewarm water or wound cleanser and wiping). The formation of boronate ester bonds is sensitive to the $\mathrm{pH}$ of the solution, and the pH-dependent degradation performance of the hydrogels was also evaluated at $\mathrm{pH}4.6$ , 5.6, and 6.8, respectively. It was found that the loss rate of the hydrogel mass was accelerated at lower pH values, and the hydrogels can be completely cleared within $24\textrm{h}$ at $\mathrm{pH}4.6$ (Figure S4, Supporting Information). In general, the lower the pH condition is, the faster degradation was observed. In vitro release of EGCG from the hydrogels was also performed in PBS buffer solution at $\mathrm{pH}7.4.$ . As shown in Figure S5, Supporting Information, sustained release of EGCG was detected in the tested periods, and the release rate was reduced when the higher concentration of E-A complex was used, which was attributed to the enhanced crosslinked density of the hydrogel networks.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Materials based on boronate ester bonds constantly undergo dynamic exchange between the reactants (boronic acid derivatives and catechols) and the products (boronate ester) under wild conditions, by which the materials usually feature excellent self-healing performance. As depicted in Figure  3a, when the separated hydrogel surfaces contact, the rearrangement of the boronate ester bond could promote the healing of the hydrogels immediately. For practical application, the fast selfhealing of the dressing after break by normal body movement and local stress could prevent the external infection and maintain a sterile environment for the wound healing. As shown in Figure 3b, a hydrogel piece adhered to the author’s finger was cut into two parts to mimic the break of the dressings. After contacting the two parts for $30~\mathrm{s}$ , the healed hydrogel can withstand the back and forth bending of the fingers (Movie S1, Supporting Information), indicating the outstanding practicability. Rheology study was then performed to evaluate the self-healing behavior of the EACPA hydrogel (Figure  3c). Based on the strain amplitude sweep test in Figure S7, Supporting Information, $100\%$ strain was selected for the cyclic testing. When the large strain was processed, the storage modulus $\left(G^{\prime}\right)$ was significantly declined and lower than the loss modulus $\left(G^{\prime\prime}\right)$ , indicating the disruption of the hydrogel network. As the strain was switched to $1\%$ , the network can be restored revealing by the recovery of the storage and loss moduli. Even after five cycles, there was no obvious change of the moduli observed, demonstrating the good healability of the EACPA hydrogel.  The stress–strain tests were further performed to qualify the self-healing efficiency. The EACPA hydrogel was first cut into two parts using a razor blade and then brought together at the fracture surface for different times to process the tensile experiment. Figure 3d showed the stress–strain curves of the EACPA hydrogel (9  mm E-A complex) healed without any stimuli at room temperature for 30 s, 3 min, and $10\mathrm{min}$ . We can find that the increase in contacting time led to the more rearrangement of the boronate ester bonds at the interface, which was revealed by the improved tensile stress and elongation at break. The healing efficiencies were $35.1\%$ , $46.0\%$ , and $100.0\%$ for $30\mathrm{~s~}$ $3\mathrm{min}$ , and $10\mathrm{min}$ attaching, respectively, and the details for the self-healing efficiency was present in Figure  3e. Although the tensile fracture strain was slightly lower than the initial hydrogel, the tensile strength could completely recover after $10\mathrm{min}$ incubation. Interestingly, the healed hydrogels $(10~\mathrm{min}$ contacting) broke from a new site when it was subjected to the tensile testing (Figure  3f), indicating that a firm healing interface was formed. The self-healing capabilities of EACPA hydrogels with 5 and 7  mm E-A complex were also evaluated as shown in Figure S8, Supporting Information, both of which exhibited outstanding self-healing behaviors.

The production of excessive ROS in wound sites is the main reason for chronic wound healing. For diabetic wounds, persistent hyperglycemia and infection can lead to excessive ROS accumulation, and the activated immune system can also produce a large number of ROS, all of which cause a significant increase in oxidative stress in the wound environment.[11a,19] The integration of antioxidants into wound dressings can effectively scavenge the ROS species, promote enzymatic repair and metabolic improvement, which is of importance for chronic wound healing.[2c,20] Owing to the free phenol groups and dynamic nature of boronate esters in our system, EACPA hydrogels are expected to exhibit promising anti-oxidation effect for diabetic wound treatment. In this case, the in vitro antioxidative efficiency of the EACPA hydrogel was investigated by DPPH free radicals scavenging test and ROS scavenging test. It was found that the DPPH scavenging efficiency showed an EACPA hydrogel concentration-dependent manner, and the sample hydrogel presented obvious oxidation resistance with $80\%$ radical scavenging efficiency while the hydrogel concentration was more than $3~\mathrm{mg~mL^{-1}}$ (Figure  4c). Notably, the EACPA hydrogel exhibited similar antioxidant activity with EGCG once the hydrogel concentration was over $5~\mathrm{mg~mL^{-1}}$ , indicating the outstanding radical scavenging property. Furthermore, to test the intracellular ROS scavenging capability, the EACPA hydrogel was incubated with L929 fibroblast cells treated by the Rosup agent, which can stimulate the cells to produce excessive intracellular ROS. Meanwhile, dichlorodihydro-fluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) was used as a probe to detect the ROS level, of which the fluorescence can be activated through the oxidation of DCFH-DA by the intracellular ROS. As shown in Figure 4d, obvious fluorescence quenching was observed in L929 cells treated with $10\mathrm{mg}\mathrm{mL^{-1}}$ hydrogel, and the intensity of the fluorescence was similar to the control group, which was comparable to the Rosup mediated positive group. In our system, after the formation of the E-A complex, there are still free phenol groups left to scavenge ROS. Moreover, the dynamic arrangement of the boronate ester bond and the release of EGCG also can be attributed to the excellent anti-oxidation performance. These results demonstrated that the advanced EACPA hydrogel may be a good candidate as a wound dressing to address the issue of excessive ROS in chronic diabetic wounds.  Bacterial infection often appears in the process of wound healing, especially for chronic wounds, which may delay the wound repair. Hence, wound dressings with antibacterial ability are of great value during clinical applications.[3a,9c] EGCG has been proved to exhibit strong antimicrobial properties against resistant microorganisms, and it can affect the expression of proteins involved in the bacterial cell wall/membrane synthesis and inhibit the biosynthesis of the cell constituents, cell signaling or DNA damage.[21] The antibacterial properties of the EACPA hydrogel with different concentrations of EGCG were investigated using both Escherichia coli (E.coli) (Gram-negative bacterium) and Staphylococcus aureus (S.aureus) (Gram-positive bacterium). Bacteria were first cocultured with EACPA hydrogels with different concentration of E-A complex for $6\mathrm{~h~}$ at $37~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}_{:}$ , and the medium containing the treated bacteria were subsequently spread on the agar plate and incubated for $^{12\mathrm{~h~}}$ . Afterward, the corresponding colony-forming units (CFU) on the Petri dish was photographed to assess the antibacterial activity of EACPA hydrogels. As expected, our EACPA hydrogels showed favorable antibacterial ability toward S. aureus and E. coli (Figure  4e). EACPA hydrogels with 5, 7, and 9 mm E-A complex exhibited $98.2\%$ , $97.8\%$ , and $96.6\%$ decrease of $E$ . coil colonies, respectively, and the killing ratios against S.aureus were $98.0\%$ , $97.3\%$ and $95.6\%$ , respectively (Figure  4f). Although EGCG has been reported to be more effective against Gram-positive than Gram-negative bacteria, there is no significance in the kill ratio in the present test.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The above results proved that EACPA hydrogel possessed all the desired properties as a powerful wound dressing for diabetic chronic wounds. Therefore, we evaluated the treatment efficiency of the EACPA hydrogel $(9\mathrm{mu}\mathrm{E}{\cdot}\mathrm{A}$ complex) on a full-thickness diabetic wound model. The diabetic mice model was established on $\mathrm{C57BL}/6$ mice via intraperitoneal injection of streptozotocin (STZ) for 5 consecutive days, as STZ-induced diabetes animal models have been widely used for investigating diabetic-impaired wound healing.[24] The mice whose blood glucose was substantially over $300~\mathrm{mg~dL^{-1}}$ ( $16.65\mathrm{mmol}\mathrm{L^{-1}})$ for 4 weeks were applied for the treatment of the EACPA hydrogel dressing. Full-thickness dorsal skin punch wounds with $8{\cdot}\mathrm{mm}$ diameter were inoculated and subsequently treated with Tegaderm film, EACPA hydrogel dressing, or left undressed (untreated control group), respectively. The details of the treatment were summarized in Figure 5a. First, we tested the adhesive capability of EACPA hydrogel in the wound site to evaluate the practicability. As shown in Movie S4, Supporting Information, after contacting for $3\mathrm{min}$ , EACPA hydrogel can firmly stick onto the wound site to withstand pulling by a tweezer, making ease of application. According to the instruction of Tegaderm film, the dressing is suggested to leave on as long as possible until the dressing becomes compromised (i.e., edge lift, rolled, or soiled) or routinely at 7 days. In this case, both the EACPA hydrogel dressing and Tegaderm film were changed every 7 days in the evaluation. Representative pictures of the wounds in different groups at scheduled time intervals were shown in Figure 5b. Macroscopically, we found that the healing efficiency by EACPA hydrogel was much higher than that by the non-treated group, and there was still dry scab observed in the non-treated mice after 18 days observation. Furthermore, the quantitative wound area in the experimental time frame was measured based on the wound photos (Figure  5c). Compared to the control group and Tegaderm dressing, EACPA hydrogel reduced the wound area to $85.4\%$ after 3 days. On the seventh day, the wound areas in the untreated control group remained as high as $91.3\%$ , but $53.3\%$ of the contraction in the wound area was achieved with the EACPA hydrogel dressing, which was also smaller than that of Tegaderm dressing group $(71.0\%)$ . According to the following observation, the average wound area of EACPA hydrogel was around $18.4\%$ on day 11 and reduced to $11.7\%$ on day 14, much smaller than that of the Tegaderm dressing group $(51.3\%$ and $21.8\%$ , respectively). On the 18th day, the wound area in the untreated control group was still $19.1\%$ , indicating the delay of the wound healing process. And the EACPA hydrogel was still superior (about $2.0\%$ in wound closure rate) to commercial Tegaderm dressing. Notably, we found that the EACPA hydrogel dressing can be easily removed 7 days after treatment through cleaning the wound site with saline solution and cotton swabs, which is attributed to the gradual release of EGCG and the decline of the tissue adhesiveness (Movie S5, Supporting Information). This advantage can avoid secondary injury of the wound site and greatly facilitate dressing change. These results demonstrated the desirable physical and chemical  characteristics endow the EACPA hydrogel with the best chronic diabetic wound healing efficiency.  The healing of the wound is a specific biological process that requires a well-orchestrated integration of the complex biological and molecular events: hemostasis, inflammation, cell migration, cell proliferation, extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition, and remodeling.[25] To evaluate the healing effect of the EACPA hydrogel dressing in histological view, hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and Masson trichrome staining (MTS) were performed on the regenerated skin tissues which were collected on day 18. The histological results were consistent with the wound residual areas observed in the digital images. As shown in Figure  5d, after 18 days of treatment, residual scabs (crusts of dried blood, serum, and exudate) were still observed in both Tegaderm film and untreated control groups, which demonstrated the delayed healing process of the chronic wounds. Comparatively, we did not find any intermediate scab in the EACPA hydrogel group. Besides, a mass of dead inflammatory cells encapsulated in scab can be observed in the untreated control group, suggesting that inflammation occurred at the early stage of healing (Figure 5d, white dotted circle). Both the EACPA hydrogel and commercial dressing group maintained a mitigated inflammatory environment favorable to wound healing. Granulation tissue formed at the wound beds was also significantly enhanced in the EACPA hydrogel group on day 18, which exhibited approximately $120\upmu\mathrm{m}$ thicker than commercial film dressing group (Figure  5e). Besides, the collected tissues in the EACPA hydrogel group also exhibited better-organized structure than control and Tegaderm film groups, of which hair follicles, sebaceous glands, ducts of sweat gland, and squamous epithelium can be clearly observed in EACPA hydrogel group (Figure 5d). It was found that both the commercial dressing and EACPA hydrogel groups exhibited obviously higher collagen deposition than that of the control group after 18 days. It is worth noting that the EACPA hydrogel group presented the densest collagen deposition, indicating that the functional hydrogel accelerated the wound recovery by the promotion of collagen deposition.  Blood vessels can provide nutrition to the cells associated with healing and sustain the growth of the newly-formed granulation tissue, and thus the angiogenesis process is also crucial for chronic wound repair.[26] In this work, the new vessels formed at the wound area were stained by CD31 with red fluorescence. As depicted in Figure 5g, there was little fluorescence observed in the untreated group, and the intervention of wound dressings led to the obvious enhancement in the fluorescence intensity, of which EACPA hydrogel mediated the highest CD31 expression. Quantitative results in Figure 5h demonstrated that the relative coverage area of CD31 in the hydrogel dressing group was 6.63- and 4.66-fold higher than the untreated and commercial dressing groups, respectively. The immunohistochemical (IHC) staining results of CD31 at days 18 were also displayed in Figure S12a,b, Supporting Information. The strong positive staining of CD31 was observed in the EACPA hydrogel group, which was higher than the other two groups. These macro­ scopic and microscopic observations for in vivo assessments suggested that the application of EACPA hydrogel dressing has a favorable influence on the various phases of diabetic wound healing and thus facilitates the healing process.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): It is now well-established that inflammation microenvironment is pivotal during the process of wound healing and tissue regeneration. The inflammation phase in chronic wounds is severely prolonged or even unable for transition into the proliferation phase.[2e,27] As a result, macrophages mainly display in pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype and produce a series of inflammatory cytokines (i.e., IL- $\cdot1\beta$ and interleukin-6 (IL-6)) to induce tissue destruction and organ dysfunction; the number of M2 phenotype macrophages, which secrete high levels of anti-inflammatory cytokines and reparative growth factors (i.e., vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), epidermal growth factor (EGF)) to reduce inflammation, regulate granulation formation, and promote tissue regeneration, is significantly  reduced.[28] As these cytokines and growth factors are highly involved in different phases of the wound healing process, to further explore the mechanisms of EACPA hydrogel dressing on reducing the inflammatory response and promoting tissue healing, the tissues around the wound area were harvested three days after different treatments (Figure 6a) and the contents of chemokines in wound tissues were first detected by ELISA assays (Figure 6b). Compared to the control group, the content of pro-inflammatory chemokines $(\mathrm{IL}{-}1\beta$ and IL-6) was significantly reduced after EACPA hydrogel application, indicating the effectively promoted transformation effect on macrophages from inflammatory M1 to reparative M2 phenotype during the early inflammation phase. Among them, the difference in IL- $1\beta$ content was significant $(195.4~\mathrm{pg}~\mathrm{mg}^{-1}$ for control group, $182.3~\mathrm{pg}~\mathrm{mg}^{-1}$ for Tegaderm dressing group and $128.2~\mathrm{pg}~\mathrm{mg}^{-1}$ for EACPA hydrogel group) $(p<0.001)$ . In addition, the content of anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-4 and IL-10) was also enhanced in the experimental group compared to the untreated group. It is worth noting the content of IL-10 in the EACPA hydrogel group was 1.5-fold to that of the untreated control group, demonstrating that EACPA hydrogel can inhibit inflammatory responses and accelerated the healing process by promoting anti-inflammatory cytokines secretion.  TGF- $\beta1$ , as a well-known immunosuppressive factor, participates in the amelioration of excessive inflammatory responses via inducing macrophage M2 polarization and plays a key role in stimulating endothelial cell migration and angiogenesis.[29] The EACPA hydrogel treatment intensely increased the expression level of TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ to $307.9~\mathrm{pg}~\mathrm{mg}^{-1}$ significantly higher than that in the untreated control group $(158.6~\mathrm{pg}~\mathrm{mg^{-1}})$ and the commercial dressing group $(240.9~\mathrm{pg}~\mathrm{mg}^{-1})$ (Figure  6b). Additionally, M2 macrophages can further secrete reparative growth factors such as VEGF for neovascularization. As expected, the content of VEGF protein $(167.1~\mathrm{pg}~\mathrm{mg^{-1}})$ in EACPA hydrogel group was about 1.6 times of Tegaderm dressing group $(102.4~\mathrm{pg~mg^{-1}})$ $(p<0.001)$ and 1.5 times of the untreated control group $(114.0~\mathrm{pg}~\mathrm{mg^{-1}})$ $(p<0.001)$ , demonstrating the up-regulated angiogenesis of regenerated skin mediated by EACPA hydrogel in the pro-healing process on diabetic chronic wounds. With the increase of neovascularization stimulated by EACPA hydrogel dressing, the tissue regeneration was promoted, leading to faster and better-organized healing of the diabetic chronic wound. These results indicated that, for diabetic chronic wounds, EACPA hydrogel could alleviate inflammatory responses in the early wound phase through the sustained release of EGCG and accelerate the transition from the inflammation phase to the proliferation phase by promoting the transformation of macrophages from proinflammatory M1 to reparative M2 phenotype, and keep the wound into a steady state of remodeling.  The immunofluorescence staining of $\mathrm{IL}{\cdot}1\beta$ of skin tissue samples was also performed on day 3 to investigate whether the anti-inflammatory effect was inhibited in diabetic wounds in vivo. As presented in Figure  6c, the lowest fluorescence intensity in the EACPA hydrogel revealed that the expression of $\mathrm{IL}{\cdot}1\beta$ was obviously inhibited. The relative coverage area of IL- $1\beta$ was 20 and 12 times lower than that in the untreated and Tegaderm dressing groups (Figure 6d). Both of these results are consistent with the ELISA experiment, suggesting that EACPA hydrogel is indeed efficient in reducing inflammation.  To further validate the transformative effect of EACPA hydrogel on macrophages in vivo, the immunofluorescence staining of F4/80 and CD163 was conducted. As shown in Figure  6e, most macrophages stayed in the M1 phenotype $(\mathrm{F}4/80^{+}\mathrm{CD}163^{-})$ ) without treatment, and after EACPA hydrogel application, a significant increase of M2 phenotype macrophages $(\mathrm{F4}/80^{+}\mathrm{CD206^{+}})$ was observed in the collected tissues. Although red fluorescence for M2 phenotype macrophages was also detected in the Tegaderm film group, the intensity and relative area were much lower than those in the EACPA hydrogel dressing group.

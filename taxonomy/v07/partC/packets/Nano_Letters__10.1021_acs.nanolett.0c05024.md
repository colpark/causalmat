- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Results and Discussion. Dissolution of the perylene monoimide-based chromophore amphiphile 1 (see Scheme S2 for molecular structure, $11.5~\mathrm{mM}$ ) by neutralization with equimolar sodium hydroxide $\mathrm{(NaOH)}$ yielded a deep red solution containing noncrystalline aggregates as reported earlier.2 This red solution showed a $\mathsf{p H}$ of 8.5, consistent with the complete deprotonation of the carboxylate groups, and the supramolecular polymerization experiments were performed at this $\mathrm{pH}$ unless otherwise noted. The addition of $300~\mathrm{mM}$ sodium chloride aqueous solution to the solution of 1, such that the final salt concentration became $50\mathrm{mM}$ caused the color of the solution to lighten significantly, as confirmed by UV−vis absorption spectroscopy (Figure S1). Allowing this solution to rest led to an increase in viscosity and formation of physically cross-linked gel-like materials;2 however, vortexing of this weak gel-like material quickly restored the sol-like behavior. As observed by CLSM, transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and atomic force microscopy (AFM), the resulting ribbon-like supramolecular polymers were approximately $50{-}200~\mathrm{nm}$ in width, $0.5{-}1\mu\mathrm{m}$ in length, and ${\sim}2\mathrm{nm}$ thick (Figure $\mathrm{1A,C,E}$ ), and retained their dimensions upon storage in solution at room temperature (referred to as “control samples”). In contrast, samples subjected to thermal annealing at $95^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for 1 h contained supramolecular polymers, which grew into extra-large dimensions with widths in the range of $0.5{-}3~\mu\mathrm{m}$ and lengths of ${\sim}10\mu\mathrm{m}$ (see Figure 1B,D,F). We also observed that the thickness of the supramolecular polymers remained constant at ${\sim}2\mathrm{nm}$ after the annealing procedure, as measured by AFM (Figure 1E,F, Figures S2−4). While TEM and AFM were performed on dry samples, which can introduce sample preparation artifacts, the good agreement in size distribution with solution CLSM images indicates that the morphological trends are not fundamentally affected by such procedures.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In situ annealing studies were performed to probe the growth mechanism of the CA crystalline supramolecular polymers at elevated temperatures. Variable-temperature (VT) UV−vis spectroscopy revealed upon heating to $95~^{\circ}C$ for $30\mathrm{min}$ the loss of absorption bands at 447 and $563~\mathrm{nm}$ and the appearance of a single band at $477\mathrm{nm}$ (Figure 2A). We previously found that this change in the absorption spectrum can be attributed to a change in the excitonic state character, which is correlated to the loss of crystallinity within the supramolecular polymers.2,37 Cooling resulted in an identical absorption spectrum trace indicating the initial crystal phase was retained after the annealing process. Heating and cooling at $1^{\circ}\mathrm{C}/\mathrm{min}$ revealed dramatic changes in the absorbance at $477\mathrm{nm}$ around $77~^{\circ}C$ (Figure 2B), suggesting a simple twostate transformation from a crystalline to a noncrystalline phase. VT X-ray scattering experiments tracked the crystallinity as well as supramolecular polymer morphology during annealing (Figure 2C,D). At low temperatures $(25-75~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ , we observed strong Bragg reflections in the wide-angle X-ray scattering (WAXS) region corresponding to the 2D oblique lattice of 1. We also observed a Porod slope of $^{-2}$ in the smallangle X-ray scattering (SAXS) region, characteristic of ribbonlike assemblies. Upon reaching $85^{\circ}\mathrm{C},$ a significant decrease in the intensity of the WAXS reflections was observed, coupled with a progression of the Porod slope to $-3$ and the appearance of layered assemblies with $15\mathrm{nm}$ spacing. At 90 ${}^{\circ}C,$ , the Bragg reflections in the WAXS region were completely replaced by a broad scattering feature at $q=1.8\mathrm{{\AA^{-1}}}$ which corresponds to $\pi{-}\pi$ stacking within the noncrystalline assemblies. Here, the reciprocal space vector $q$ is defined as $q=4\pi\sin\theta/\lambda,$ where $\theta$ is half of the total scattering angle and $\lambda$ is the X-ray wavelength. The increase in the Porod slope and the appearance of stacking order further corroborated the existence of supramolecular polymers at elevated temperatures. After cooling, we again observed the Bragg reflections corresponding to crystalline supramolecular polymers (Figure S5,) and the periodic interlayer spacing disappears completely. We attribute the formation of the layered structure to the emergence of electrostatic repulsion upon entropically driven dehydration of the supramolecular polymers.38 Upon cooling, this electrostatic repulsion is largely screened by bound water molecules due to an increase in the dielectric constant.

We observed that the (02) planes in real space ran perpendicular to the long axis of the supramolecular polymer. On a molecular level, the (02) planes repeat in the $\pi-\pi$ stacking direction, indicating this interaction is the preferred mode of growth in the assemblies. An increase in polymer width occurred along the (20) planes driven by edge-to-face and dipolar interactions. The sharpness of the diffraction spots and the lack of reflections at the same radii also indicate that the supramolecular polymers are single-crystal-like in nature and that domains align along the same axis. This is, in fact, an important feature for efficient excitonic transport within these assemblies.37 Interestingly, SEM images revealed aligned rectangular holes throughout the supramolecular polymers, where monomers were chemically extracted with ethanol and supercritical carbon dioxide during the critical point drying sample preparation process (Figure 4). This observation supports the single-crystal-like nature of these supramolecular polymers.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The supramolecular polymers investigated here had been previously explored as visible light photosensitizers for solar hydrogen fuel production in an aqueous environment. $2,5,8,9,45-47$ Thus, with the newly discovered ability of these systems to generate extra-large crystalline supramolecular polymers, we probed their performance as scaffolds for photocatalytic proton reduction using molybdenum sulfur clusters $([\mathrm{\dot{M}o}_{3}\bar{S_{13}}^{2-}])$ as the catalyst. Since acidic conditions required for hydrogen evolution would likely protonate the carboxylate groups and lead to macroscopic precipitation,2 the supramolecular polymers were first complexed with the cationic polyelectrolyte poly(diallyldimethylammonium) chloride (PDDA) at the initial $\mathsf{p H}$ of ${\sim}9$ before the addition of the sacrificial electron donor ( $1.7~\mathrm{M}$ ascorbic acid solution, with $\mathrm{pH}$ adjusted to 4 by addition of $\mathrm{NaOH}_{\mathrm{aq}},$ ). We found that hydrogels composed of the extra-large anionic crystalline supramolecular polymers produced $70\%$ more $\mathrm{H}_{2}$ gas over 110 h of illumination compared to control samples, reaching turnover numbers of 13,500 while generating $1~\mathrm{mL}$ of $\mathrm{H}_{2}$ with quantum efficiencies as high as $0.13\%$ at ${505~\mathrm{nm}}$ (Figure $\mathsf{S A},$ Table S1). These rates of photocatalytic hydrogen production represent the highest of all the PMI-based aqueous systems studied previously.2,5,8,9 We first considered if changes in optoelectronic properties of the enlarged CA crystalline supramolecular polymers led to the observed enhancement in hydrogen production. However, the extra-large supramolecular polymers of 1 showed equivalent optoelectronic properties to control samples, including absorption spectra (Figure S18) and exciton lifetimes measured by transient absorbance spectroscopy (TA) (Figures S19−25). Thus, we explored other explanations for the improvement in photocatalytic activity when the hydrogel scaffolds contain the extra-large supramolecular polymers.  Scanning electron microscopy images of critical point $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ dried hydrogels showed a densely packed network of small assemblies for control samples and a much more porous network when the extra-large crystalline supramolecular polymers were used to create the photocatalytic materials, consistent with the CLSM images of the same hydrogels in solution (Figure 5B,C). We hypothesized that differences in diffusion kinetics of molecular species in the highly porous structures were responsible for the observed enhancement in hydrogen production. Using 8-hydroxypyrene-1,3,6-trisulfonic acid trisodium salt (HPTS) as an analogue for our molecular reagents, fluorescence recovery after photobleaching (FRAP) experiments were conducted to probe diffusion rates within the hydrogels (Figure 5D). Free HPTS was found to have a fluorescence recovery half-life time of ${\sim}2.9\pm0.3$ s when using the same conditions used in the photocatalysis experiments. The fluorescence recovery time was found to increase to 24.2 $\pm\quad1.7$ s in hydrogel scaffolds containing the control supramolecular polymers, suggesting a significant restriction of diffusion in these hydrogels. Interestingly, when the extralarge crystalline supramolecular polymers formed the hydrogels, the fluorescence recovery time decreased to a significantly smaller value of $11.1\pm0.9\mathrm{~s~}$ . The reduction in fluorescence recovery time corresponds to a 2-fold enhancement in diffusion coefficient within scaffolds containing the extralarge supramolecular polymers. We propose that the greater porosity of hydrogels containing extra-large supramolecular polymers results in the observed enhanced diffusion. This, in turn, results in greater accessible surface area for contact between the reagents and the photosensitizing supramolecular assemblies. Interestingly, specific surface areas obtained from nitrogen physisorption experiments on calcium chloride-crosslinked scaffolds that have been freeze-dried are consistent with this hypothesis (see Figure S26), although we note that such measurements are highly affected by the drying processes and do not necessarily reflect the accessible surface area in hydrogels. We interpret that the porogenic nature of the extra-large supramolecular polymers originates in their inability to pack into dense hydrogels, as observed by SEM and CLSM (Figure 5B). Finally, the large size of the molecularly thin assemblies reduces the edge surface area exposed to the catalyst, and this might be another factor enhancing hydrogen production if the broad faces of the supramolecular polymers are the most productive sites for photocatalysis. We speculate that more structural defects exist at these edges, and this might impact the exciton lifetimes.

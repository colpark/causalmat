- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In this work, a series of mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) and polystyrene nanoparticles (PSs) were used as model materials to systematically investigate the mechanism of SDT enhanced by the modulation of nanoparticles’ wettability (Scheme 1). We also used pulsed spark cavitation method to simulate single-period cavitation to examine the relevant cavitation phenomenon. Cryogenic transmission electron microscopy (cryo-TEM) image showed that the nanoparticles could adsorb and stabilize the bubbles in liquids. In addition, the desorption energy calculation shows that the wettability of nanoparticles can adjust the desorption energy between the nanoparticles and the nanobubbles. A larger desorption energy implies higher nanobubble stability, cavitation effect, and ROS yield. Guided by this finding, we applied wettability-modified MSN (dMSN-2) with the largest desorption energy and a water contact angle (CA) of about $90^{\circ}$ to sonothrombolysis. Compared with unmodified MSNs, the ROS yield of dMSN-2 increased by approximately more than tenfold and the thrombolytic efficiency increased by $50.3\%$ . We believe that this study provides new ideas for design of nanomaterials with efficient SDT properties.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): For exploring the effect of particle surface properties on the cavitation effect, we first developed a pulsed spark cavitation method to effectively mimic single-period cavitation of nanoparticles’ suspension (Figure 2a). Typically, the smallsized periodic cavitation bubbles are barely recognized using optical methods and cavitation nuclei on the wall of container have a significantly influence on the observations (Figure S1, Supporting Information). In addition, cavitation clouds are impacted by periodic cavitation bubbles interactions, which can induce high background interference. Pulsed spark cavitation method reflects the real state of the cavitation nuclei in suspension, eliminating the influence of Bjerknes force on the position and number of the nuclei (due to Bjerknes force, the bubbles go through fast move, split, and merge).[16] In the single-period cavitation progress of pulsed spark cavitation, we used a high-speed camera to capture the shock wave and visualized it by schlieren method (Movies S1, Supporting Information).[17] First, the suspension was degassed by a vacuum pump $(-0.1\mathrm{MPa})$ for 5 min to reduce microbubbles and unstable cavitation nuclei suspended in the suspension due to entrainment or dissolution. Subsequently, a synchronous controller was connected to the high-speed camera and high voltage equipment to ensure that both of them were started at the same time. When the high voltage equipment was activated, a corresponding high voltage (about 800  V) was generated on the copper needles in the sink. The liquid between the two electrodes decomposed to produce plasma and released a large amount of heat, causing the surrounding liquid to vaporize instantly and formed cavitation bubbles. The spark-induced cavitation bubbles grew in size over time along with luminescence and deionization. The collapse of these bubbles then generated shock waves that spread in the container of nanoparticle suspension and further induced cavitation (Movie S2, Supporting Information). The high-speed camera captured the entire cavitation progress (Movies S3–S7, Supporting Information). Figure 2b,c, and Figure S2 in the Supporting Information, are screenshots from Movies S3–S7. It can be seen that the number of cavitation bubbles in water was less than that of nanoparticle suspension, and the number of cavitation bubbles in dMSN-2 suspension exceeded that of other groups. The number of cavitation bubbles is positively corrected to cavitation effect. Therefore, the results indicate that nanoparticles tend to enhance the cavitation effect, and the enhancement effect of properly hydrophobic MSN is better than that of MSN.  To investigate whether the wettability of nanoparticles could affect SDT, electron spin resonance (ESR) was employed to evaluate the ROS generation of nanoparticles, in which 2,2,6,6-tetramethylpiperidine (TEMP) acted as a singlet oxygen $({}^{1}\mathrm{O}_{2})$ trapping agent.[18] Figure  2d demonstrates that the efficiency of ${}^{1}\mathrm{O}_{2}$ generation by different nanoparticles did not change significantly. Additionally, 5,5-dimethyl-1-pyrroline N-oxide (DMPO) was adopted to assess the generation of hydroxyl radical (•OH).[19] Compared with $\mathrm{MSN+US}$ group, the •OH production of the dMSN- $^{\lfloor+}$ US, dMSN- $2+\mathrm{US}$ , and dMSN- $3+\mathrm{US}$ groups greatly increased by $652.9\%$ , $1117.8\%$ , and $1005.8\%$ , respectively (Figure 2e). Furthermore, MSN, dMSN-1, dMSN-2, and dMSN-3 failed to produce •OH in the absence of US (Figure S3, Supporting Information). These results provide solid evidence that dMSN-2, with a CA of $91.5^{\circ}$ , has the strongest ability to enhance SDT among these particles.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The nanobubbles can act as cavitation nuclei to improve cavitation effect.[20] An increase in the number of cavitation nuclei implies an enhanced cavitation effect.[21] However, the mechanism by how nanobubbles are stabilized in water is still uncertain. To determine the interaction between nanoparticles and nanobubbles, we used cryo-TEM to observe the nanobubbles. Interestingly, we observed the adhesion of dMSN-2 on the nanobubbles (Figure S9, Supporting Information), suggesting that the nanobubbles can be stabilized by nanoparticles to some extent, which is similar to Pickering foam stabilization mechanism.[22] No nanobubbles adsorption was found in the MSN suspension, which suggests that the wettability of nanoparticles may affect the interaction between nanoparticles and nanobubbles and play a non-negligible role in the stability of nanobubbles. - Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Thrombus-related diseases have a high mortality and disability rate. Thrombolytic drugs, such as fibrinolytic drugs, are currently widely used in clinical practice, but the risk of fatal bleeding after thrombolysis is a major limitation for safe application.[25] Encouraged by the high ROS yield of the wettability modulated nanoparticles, we attempted to apply dMSN-2 for sonothrombolysis. After incubation with human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) for $24\textrm{h}$ , the cytotoxicity of the nanoparticles was evaluated using standard methyl thiazolyl tetrazolium (MTT) assay. Within 12.5 to $200~\upmu\mathrm{g}~\mathrm{mL}^{-1}$ MSN and dMSN-2 had no significant toxicity (Figure 4a), and their hemolysis rates were less than $5\%$ , indicating a good biosafety (Figure S12, Supporting Information). The blood clots are formed by red blood cells (RBCs), platelets, and fibrin. Therefore, three predominant cause may lead to the lysis of clots: Hemolysis, fibrin degradation, and clot deformation without thrombolysis.[26] Base on this, the hemolysis rates were assessed by using RBCs. The hemolysis rates of MSN, and dMSN-2 at $200\upmu\mathrm{g}\mathrm{mL}^{-1}$ under US irradiation were $33.3\%$ and $51.0\%$ , respectively (Figure  4b). Scanning electron micrograph (SEM) image indicated the changes in the morphology of US-irradiated RBCs (Figure  4c). In addition, ruptured membranes rather than pores were observed in the dMSN- $2+\mathrm{US}$ group, demonstrating that the enhancement of SDT effect was assisted by dMSN-2. Figure S13, Supporting Information, also shows the damaging effect of dMSN-2 and MSN. Compared with control group, some RBCs were shrinking in the US group, whereas RBCs were broken into tiny fragments in $\mathrm{dMSN}{\cdot}2+\mathrm{US}$ group. The destructive effect was further demonstrated by labeling live RBCs with the fluorescent probe, calcein acetoxymethyl ester (calcein-AM) (Figure S14, Supporting Information). Smaller field fluorescence signal could be discerned in the dMSN- $2+\mathrm{US}$ group; conversely, the US group and the $\mathrm{MSN+US}$ group exhibited distinct large-area green fluorescence. Furthermore, to investigate the therapeutic mechanism, ROS productions of each group was indicated by 2,7-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) (Figure S15, Supporting Information).[27] Confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM) revealed a clear fluorescence in the $\mathrm{dMSN{-}2+U S}$ group. In contrast, little fluorescence signal was found in other groups. These results suggested that dMSN-2 generated the higher level of intracellular ROS under US irradiation. Our previous studies have shown that the generated ROS can disrupt phospholipids such as platelet factor 3.[28] Moreover, ROS could disrupt the fibrin skeleton, as assessed by fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-labeled fibrin clots assay. In Figure 4d, amount of collapsed skeleton and fragments were distinguished after incubating fibrin with nanoparticles and US irradiation. For the $\mathrm{dMSN}{\cdot}2+\mathrm{US}$ group, the fragments of fibrin were further degraded. Finally, an artificial thrombus model was established based on our previous report.[28] After irradiating by US, obvious thrombolysis progress was observed with nanoparticles (Figure S16, Supporting Information). Besides, the absorbance of the supernatant at $540~\mathrm{nm}$ was measured as an indicator of hemoglobin, which showed a better thrombolysis efficiency of dMSN-2 under US irradiation (Figure S17, Supporting Information). The thrombolysis rates were calculated by the weight variation of blood clot before and after US irradiation (Figure  4e). The dMSN-2 group was $150.3\%$ as high as MSN group, which showed a better thrombolysis ability of dMSN-2 assisted by US. The rats were randomly divided into seven groups to assess the in vivo therapeutic effects by lower limb thrombus model: 1)  normal group; 2) embolized group; 3) MSN group; 4) dMSN-2 group; 5) US group; 6) $\mathrm{MSN}+\mathrm{US}$ group; 7) $\mathrm{d}\mathrm{MSN}{\cdot}2\mathrm{~+~}\mathrm{US}$ group. The histopathology assessments showed that less thrombus in the blood vessels in the dMSN- $2+\mathrm{US}$ group, which also means a better thrombolysis ability of dMSN-2 assisted by US (Figure 4f).

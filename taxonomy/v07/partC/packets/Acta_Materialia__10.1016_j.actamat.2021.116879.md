- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Four Ni-Al-Y alloys are considered, i.e., eutectic 22Al10Y, hypoeutectic 10Al3Y, hypereutectic 10Al10Y, and minor-Y-doping 10Al0.5Y. A summary of the mentioned microstructures and corresponding chemical compositions is listed in Table 1. SEM, EBSD, and XRD were performed to analyze the as-melted microstructures of the four alloys. The results reveal that 22Al10Y typically possesses lamella-like eutectic structure, composed of NiAl and $\mathrm{Ni}_{5}\mathrm{Y}$ (Fig. 1a and Supplementary martials Fig. S1a). 10Al3Y possesses $\gamma{-}\mathrm{Ni}$ as proeutectic phase, and $(\gamma-\mathrm{Ni}{+}\mathrm{Ni}_{5}\mathrm{Y})$ as eutectic structure (Fig. 1b and Fig. S1b). 10Al10Y possesses $\mathrm{Ni}_{5}\mathrm{Y}$ as proeutectic phase, and $(\gamma-\mathrm{Ni}{+}\mathrm{Ni}_{5}\mathrm{Y})$ as eutectic structure (Fig. 1c and Fig. S1c). Finally, 10Al0.5Y possesses $\gamma$ -Ni as matrix and minor $(\gamma-\mathrm{Ni}{+}\mathrm{Ni}_{5}\mathrm{Y})$ locating mainly in GBs (Fig. 1d and Fig. S1d). According to these results, Al has obvious solubility in $\mathrm{Ni}_{5}\mathrm{Y}$ phase, which is in accordance with our thermodynamic calculations using TC [48,49], as shown in Fig. S2. It is critical to emphasize the solubility of Al in $\mathsf{N i}_{5}\mathsf{Y},$ due to the calculation of actual $\Delta\mathrm{G_{f}}$ later need the support of chemical concentration of specific component. In contrast, there is almost no solubility of Y in Ni. Therefore, the negligible Y content in $\gamma$ -Ni is set to zero in the following thermodynamic calculations.

With the increase of scale thickness, the metal activities near scale/alloy interface gradually decrease (finally approaching the equilibrium value of oxidation reactions), which means that the metal activity gradients in the scale gradually decrease, resulting in the decrease of ion flux and reaction rate (Fig. 4) [40].

- path: /home/aid1/Documents/causalmat/matmech/Bioactive_Materials/j.bioactmat.2020.01.001/images/51076db90eb2145e1065b558dbddd9a97a72ee1fcc5f7d191b2c3c00cf15bcff.jpg
- caption: Fig. 4. XPS patterns of the different samples: (a) survey, (b) Si 2p.
- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In order to further explore the element composites and Si status in hydroxyapatite lattice, HA and Si-HA were detected by XPS, as shown in Fig. 4. HA consists of C, O, Ca and P, and Si-HA additionally contains Si beside the other elements on HA. It is further confirmed by the corresponding surface chemical compositions (Table inserted in Fig. 4(a)). The high-resolution spectrum of Si 2p peak on Si-HA is asymmetric and was deconvoluted into two components centered at 101.8 and 103.1 eV (Fig. 4(b)). 101.8 eV was assigned to the silicon atoms present in the orthosilicate anions $\mathrm { S i O _ { 4 } } ^ { 4 - }$ , which occupied $\mathrm { P O _ { 4 } } ^ { 3 - }$ anions in HA lattice, and 103.1 eV was assigned to three-dimensional silicates [15,19,34]. Based on the obtained results (Figs. 2–4), it is confirmed that substitution of Si does not change nanorod microstructure obviously, and Si replaces some $\mathrm { \bf P }$ in hydroxyapatite lattice successfully, resulting in lattice distortion.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The integration of implant surfaces with skin in vivo for 4 weeks was evaluated, and Fig. 9 shows the histological strained images of different samples. For Ti, the skin migrated significantly along the implant surface (marked with the black squares in Fig. 9), leaving little skin adhered on the surface, and fibrous capsule (marked with the black line in Fig. 9) about $4 0 0 ~ { \mu \mathrm { m } }$ was formed around the implant. For Si-HA, skin migrated a little along the implant surface (marked with the blue squares in Fig. 9) and no fibrous capsule was formed around the implant, indicating a tighter seal between the underlying dermis and the implant surface.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): It has been known that conjugated polymers usually aggregate in solution due to the strong intra- and interchain interactions.[28,29] The multilevel self-assembly from the solution to the solid state of conjugated polymers enables an effective strategy to tune the polymer packing and morphology.[30,31] Herein, we obtain conjugated polymer microwire crystals through a controlled assembly process (Figure 1). A typical conjugated polymer, $\mathrm{F}_{4}\mathrm{BDOPV}{\cdot}2\mathrm{T}_{\cdot}$ was adopted due to its representativeness as an n-type building block. F4BDOPV-2T possesses high thermal stability according to the high-temperature size exclusion chromatography, thermal gravity analysis, and differential scanning calorimeter results (Figures S1 and S2, Supporting Information). In the previous works, thin-film transistors based on $\mathrm{F}_{4}\mathrm{BDOPV}{\cdot}2\mathrm{T}$ exhibited high electron mobilities reached $1~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{V}^{-1}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and stable charge transport.[31,32] A combination of experiments and theoretical simulations allows us to obtain structural information down to the molecular scale. Molecular modeling provided reasonable polymer crystal structures, supported by X-ray experiments and simulations. Polymer microwire transistors were fabricated to evaluate the charge transport properties in the crystallized state. Polymer crystals of $\mathrm{F}_{4}\mathrm{BDOPV}{\cdot}2\mathrm{T}$ exhibited more effective charge transport with higher electron mobilities (over six times) and a lower hopping energy barrier (only one-third of the value) than those of the spin-coated thin films. Our results provide an assembly method to obtain conjugated polymer crystals, which can be regarded as an ideal platform for studying the structure–property relationship.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): It is an urgent need but still a significant challenge to obtain the crystal structure of conjugated polymers.[19,40] The combined approach of X-ray diffraction and molecular simulations has been used to determine the 3D supramolecular structures of the conjugated polymers.[8,23] However, only a few conjugated polymers with precise crystal structures have been reported (Table S1, Supporting Information).[13,33,40,41] Hence, we used this method to obtain the crystal structure to deeply understand the charge transport. First, we gathered enough polymer crystals and performed the powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) experiment with synchrotron radiation. The polymer crystals exhibited strong diffractivity (Figure 3a,b), suggesting highly ordered packing. Furthermore, molecular modeling was adopted to elucidate the possible polymer crystal structures with the help of PXRD (Figure S7, Supporting Information). After density functional theory (DFT) optimization of the polymer repeating unit (Figures S8 and S9, Supporting Information), we built the crystal packing model using a dense $\pi{-}\pi$ stacking (3.45 Å) and interdigitated alkyl chains.[9,40] The initial crystal parameters were set as those valves according to the experimental PXRD profile. The structure was then subjected to the molecular dynamics simulation to obtain reasonable crystal structures. After two quenched molecular dynamics, several possible crystal structures were collected from the lowenergy snapshots for further screen reasonable crystal structures (Figure S10, Supporting Information). The similar dense polymer packing and interdigitated alkyl chains were retained in these possible crystal structures. Therefore, the searched most possible crystal structure (Figure S11, Supporting Information) was finally refined and optimized according to the experimental PXRD profile using the Pawley method. The Pawley refinement gave negligible residual errors, indicating the molecular modeling of the polymer crystal structure is reasonable and reliable (Figure 3b).  The diffraction peaks at $2\theta=1.21^{\circ}$ $(d=30.90$ Å), $2\theta=2.40^{\circ}$ $(d=15.58\mathrm{~\AA~})$ , $2\theta=3.62^{\circ}$ $'d=10.33\mathrm{~\AA~})$ , and $2\theta=11.07^{\circ}$ ( $\'d=3.39\mathring{\mathrm{A}}_{,}$ can be assigned to the (100), (200), (300), and (010) $d$ -spacing with the simulated cell parameters $(a=31.27$ Å, $b=\bar{4}.12\mathrm{~\AA~}$ $c=24.85\mathrm{~\AA~}$ , $\alpha=57.65^{\circ}$ , $\beta=82.15^{\circ}$ , $\gamma=80.05^{\circ}$ , Table  S2, Supporting Information). Toward maximizing electrostatic attraction and avoiding steric hindrance between alkyl chains, the two adjacent polymer chains stacked with a slight dislocation of $2.2\mathring\mathrm{A}$ along the backbone direction (Figure 3c,e and Figure S12, Supporting Information). The slight dislocation will benefit the intermolecular charge transport. We also carried out the focused XRD experiments on a single polymer microwire. However, only a weak (010) diffraction arc was observed along the perpendicular direction (Figure S13, Supporting Information), implying a high orientation of polymer chains and the existence of crystalized domain with finite sizes in the microwire. The focused XRD pattern indicated that the polymer microwire’s radial direction is along the $\pi{-}\pi$ direction, whereas its longitudinal direction is along the polymer backbone. With the polymer crystal structure as the initial model, it is feasible to construct the polymer packing structures in the disordered phase, which is the primary part of spin-coated polymer films. For example, a theoretical work about thermoelectric transport in the disordered packed poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT) has been demonstrated by Abutaha  et  al. with the initial model of the P3HT crystal structure.[21]  We performed the simulation of the grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) pattern (Figure S14, Supporting Information).[42] The simulated GIWAXS pattern showed (h00) peaks along the out-of-plane direction and (010) peak along the in-plane direction. These characteristics were similar in the experimental pattern, suggesting the structural modeling is reasonable. We note that some simulated diffraction peaks are not observed in the GIWAXS experiments. To deeply understand the polymer packing in the solid state, we simulated the GIWAXS and PXRD patterns using the crystal structure with or without side chains (Figure S15, Supporting Information). We found that the side chains mainly contributed to the diffraction signals with the scattering vector ranging from 0.5 to 1.5 Å−1.[13,40] The diffraction peaks from the side chains disappeared in the experimental thin-film GIWAXS pattern compared to the simulated results. However, these signals from the side chains exhibited strong intensity $(2\theta\approx5^{\circ}{-}10^{\circ})$ in the PXRD profile of the microwire crystals (Figure  3b and Figure S15, Supporting Information), implying high ordering of the side chains inside. Therefore, the comparison of X-ray diffraction results between the simulated and experimental data suggests the higher ordering of polymer backbone and side chains in microwire crystals than thin films.[43] Overall, our X-ray experiments and theoretical simulations indicate that our modeling method is useful in obtaining the precise crystal packing structures of conjugated polymers.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Top-contact/bottom-gate transistors (Figure 4a) of polymer microwire crystals were fabricated on the Cytop-modified $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/$ Si substrates via the shadow mask method. The polymer crystals grew directly on the substrates, followed by depositing a gold layer with $30~\mathrm{nm}$ thickness to form the top-contact source and drain electrodes. The transistor channel is along the polymer backbone according to the XRD results. Polymer crystals of $\mathrm{F}_{4}\mathrm{BDOPV}{\cdot}2\mathrm{T}$ exhibited typical n-type carrier transport characteristics in the air (Figure 4b,c). The polymer microwire transistors showed the highest electron mobility of $5.58~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{V}^{-1}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ with the average value of $3.46~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{V}^{-1}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ , which is the highest electron mobility reported in the polymer crystal transistors (Table S1, Supporting Information).[48–51] The reliable factor of the maximum mobility was estimated to be $93\%$ , suggesting the mobility evaluation from the microwire transistor is reliable.[52] In contrast, the spin-coated polymer thin-film transistors exhibited much lower electron mobilities with the average value of $0.85~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{V}^{-1}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ (Table S3, Supporting Information), comparable to the previously reported value of $1.01\mathrm{cm}^{2}\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ reliably extracted from the linear transfer curves.[32] In the polymer crystal transistors, the electron mobility diminished with reducing temperature (Figure  4d). Therefore, a thermalactivated hopping energy barrier $\left(E_{\mathrm{a}}\right)$ can be evaluated using the Arrhenius equation: $\mu=A\mathrm{exp}(-E_{\mathrm{{a}}}/k_{\mathrm{B}}T)$ , where $\mu$ is the carrier mobility, $A$ is the pre-exponential factor, $k_{\mathrm{B}}$ is the Boltzmann constant, and $T$ is the temperature. The $E_{\mathrm{a}}$ in the $\mathrm{F_{4}B D O P V}.$ 2T microwire transistor was estimated to be $35~\mathrm{meV},$ which is smaller than this value of $104~\mathrm{meV}$ in a referential thin-film transistor (Figure  4d). The enhanced electron mobilities and a lower hopping barrier energy in the polymer microwires might derive from the ordered polymer packing with less structural and energetic disorders.[23]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To demonstrate the generality of this growth strategy, another famous n-type conjugated polymer was also adopted for crystal growth, structural elucidation, and mobility measurement (Figure 5 and Figure S18, Supporting Information): poly $\{[N,N^{\prime}$ bis(2-octyldodecyl)-naphthalene-1,4,5,8-bis(dicarboximide)-2,6- diyl]-alt- $^{5,5^{\prime}}$ -(2,2′-bithiophene)} (P(NDI2OD-T2)).[53,54] Distinct optical changes in temperature-dependent absorption spectra of P(NDI2OD-T2) demonstrated that the polymer chains adopted different backbone conformations in the solvated aggregates (Figure S19, Supporting Information).[55] Using a similar approach, microwire transistors of P(NDI2OD-T2) were fabricated on the Cytop modified $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{Si}$ substrates (Figure  5b,c). As expected, P(NDI2OD-T2) microwires exhibited the highest electron mobilities of $2.56~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{V}^{-1}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ , which is much higher than the previously reported mobilities of about 0.06 to $0.85~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{V}^{-1}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ .[53,54] In contrast, the thin film transistors using the same batch of P(NDI2OD-T2) only showed the electron mobility of $0.053~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{V}^{-1}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ (Figure  5d). We note that higher electron mobilities, up to 6.4 and $3.9~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{V}^{-1}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ could be achieved in highly aligned and ordered P(NDI2OD-T2) films.[56,57] Compared to thin-film transistors, several device issues in crystal transistors might limit the observation of higher mobilities, such as long injection pathways and poor contact at dielectric interface.[18,58] Overall, microwire transistors of conjugated polymers, such as F4BDOPV-2T and P(NDI2OD-T2), exhibited better charge transport performance than thin-film transistors, which would be ascribed to the orderly packed structures and large crystalline domains in microwires. Therefore, our growth approach would be practically useful to obtain the crystals of conjugated polymers.  We performed PXRD experiments and Pawley refinement to obtain possible crystal structures of P(NDI2OD-T2). According to the PXRD results, two distinct lamellar peaks and a broad peak around $9\mathrm{-}13^{\circ}$ were recognized, indicating relative lower order in  P(NDI2OD-T2) than in $\mathrm{F}_{4}\mathrm{BDOPV}{\cdot}2\mathrm{T}$ (Figure  5e). First, the stereogenic centers of the branched alkyl chains in P(NDI2OD-T2) had been demonstrated to be nonessential to the crystal structures.[59] Second, two packing polymorphs of P(NDI2OD-T2) were identified where the NDI and bithiophene motifs own segregated or mixed stacking columns. Both polymorphs showed similar reticular distances according to electron diffraction.[60,61] Based on the above knowledge and the simulated crystal structures by Lemaur et al.,[59] we built the possible crystal structure of P(NDI2OD-T2) (Figure 5f,g). The crystal structure of P(NDI2ODT2) accords well with the experimental PXRD; it shows small differences of the cell parameters (Table S4, Supporting Information) against the previously reported model,[59] implying the structural elucidation is reasonable. Pawley refinement of PXRD data also gave negligible difference (Figure  5e). To clarify the effect of chemical moiety on charge transport, the interchain transfer integral between the adjacent P(NDI2OD-T2) dimer is estimated to be $53.9\mathrm{meV}$ (Figure S20, Supporting Information), which is smaller than the value in $\mathrm{F}_{4}\mathrm{BDOPV}{\cdot}2\mathrm{T}.$

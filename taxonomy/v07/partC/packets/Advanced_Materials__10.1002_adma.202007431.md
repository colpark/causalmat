- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Figure 1a shows the molecular structure of HL38 having two triphenylamine moieties as donor unit and thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole4,6-dione as acceptor unit and two thieno[3,2-b]thiophene units linking donor and acceptor units. The synthesis of HL38 is schematically depicted in Figure  1b. Briefly, 4-bromo-N,Nbis(4-methoxyphenyl)aniline is treated with triisopropyl (5-(tributylstannyl)thieno[3,2-b]thiophen-2-yl)silane[51] under Pd-catalyzed coupling reaction followed by the deprotection of triisopropylsilyl group by tetra-n-butylammonium fluoride giving rise to an intermediate-1 in good yield $(90\%$ for two steps). The intermediate-1 thus reacts with $n$ -BuLi, followed by adding of tributyltin chloride and the freshly formed product is immediately subjected to a Stille coupling reaction with 1,3-dibromo-5-octyl-4H-thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6(5H)-dione[52] providing the final target HL38 (overall $42\%$ yield for two steps). The detailed synthetic procedure and structural analytical data (proton nuclear magnetic resonance $[^{1}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{-NMR]}$ , $^{13}\mathrm{C}$ -NMR, $^{13}\mathrm{C}$ DEPT NMR, and HRMS of HL38) are given in Figures S1–S4, Supporting Information. Intrinsic properties of HL38 are measured, which are compared to commonly used spiro-MeOTAD. In dichloromethane solution, HL38 exhibits strong absorption in visible region with absorption maxima at $511\mathrm{nm}$ (molar extinction coefficient $[\varepsilon]=92000\mathrm{Lmol^{-1}c m^{-1}})$ as shown in Figure  1c, which is attributed to its intramolecular charge transfer characteristics. The optical gap of HL38 estimated at the absorption edge ( $\mathrm{^{\prime}}580~\mathrm{nm})$ is $2.14~\mathrm{eV.}$ In solution, HL38 exhibits fluorescent property with the emission band centered at $603~\mathrm{nm}$ with the Stokes shift of $92\mathrm{nm}$ . In the solid-state thin film, a shoulder and a slight redshift of maximum absorption wavelength is observed in absorption spectrum at lower energy (see Figure 1c). It could suggest the presence of a well-ordered molecular assembly in the solid state issued from the enhancement of $\pi{-}\pi$ intermolecular interactions which improves intermolecular charge transfer and hole mobility. Similar effect has been observed for molecular MHT in $\mathrm{PSC}^{[53]}$ or copolymers using the same thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6-dione electron accepting unit in which a vibronic shoulder implies an ordered arrangement of the polymer in the solid film, with strong $\pi{-}\pi$ stacking between the polymeric backbones, a feature that also appears in regioregular poly(3-hexylthiophene).[54] The optical bandgap of the HL38 film is estimated to be $1.98~\mathrm{eV}$ from absorption edge $\mathrm{\mathbf{(625nm)}}$ , which is much smaller than that of the spiroMeOTAD film (2.78  eV). As shown in Figure  1d, the cyclic voltammogram of HL38 (in dichloromethane) shows multiple oxidation processes with the first quasi-reversible oxidation ascribed to the oxidation of the triphenylamine groups. The onset oxidation (0.22  V vs $\mathrm{Fc/Fc^{+}})$ is used to estimate HOMO energy level $(E_{\mathrm{HOMO}})$ of HL38 by assuming that the ionization potential of Ferrocene (Fc) is $5.1~\mathrm{eV}$ versus vacuum[55] which leads to $E_{\mathrm{HOMO}}$ of $_{\mathrm{HL}38}=-5.32~\mathrm{eV}.$ . For the comparison purpose, the onset oxidation potential of the other triphenylaminebased MHTs measured in the similar conditions is ${\approx}0.1$ V versus $\mathrm{Fc}/\mathrm{Fc}^{+[56]}$ and that of the spiro-MeOTAD is $-0.1\mathrm{V}$ versus $\mathrm{Fc/Fc^{+}.}^{!}$ 57] From the optical gap and $E_{\mathrm{HOMO}}$ , the corresponding LUMO energy level $\left(E_{\mathrm{LUMO}}\right)$ of HL38 is determined to be $-3.18~\mathrm{eV.}$

At trap-free SCLC region, $\mu$ is calculated using Mott–Gurney equation, $J=9\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}\mu V^{2}/8d^{3}$ ,[58,59] where $d$ is the thickness of MHT layer on FTO substrate ( $150\mathrm{nm}$ for HL38 and $190\mathrm{nm}$ for spiro-MeOTAD layer in the FTO/MHT/Au devices as measured by the cross-sectional scanning electron microscope [SEM] images in Figure S5, Supporting Information), $V$ the applied bias voltage, $\varepsilon_{0}$ the vacuum permittivity $(8.8542\times10^{-14}\mathrm{Fcm^{-1}})$ , and $\varepsilon$ the dielectric constant of MHT layer. The $\varepsilon$ values are estimated from the relation $\varepsilon=\mathrm{C}d/A\varepsilon_{0},{}^{[60,61]}$ where the geometrical capacitance $(C)$ is obtained from high frequency region in electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) as shown in Figure S6, Supporting Information. From the trap-filled limit voltage $\left(V_{\mathrm{TFL}}\right)$ , $n_{\mathrm{t}}$ is calculated using the relation $V_{\mathrm{TFL}}=e n_{\mathrm{t}}d^{2}/2\varepsilon\varepsilon_{0}$ ,[62,63] where $e$ is the electric charge $(1.602\times10^{-19}\mathrm{~C})$ . HL38 shows $\mu=1.36\times10^{-3}~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{V}^{-1}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ that is 2.8 times higher than spiroMeOTAD $(\mu=4.86\times10^{-4}\mathrm{cm}^{2}\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ that is one-order of magnitude higher than the reported data[64]). $n_{\mathrm{t}}$ of HL38 is estimated to be $2.38\times10^{16}~\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ which is 1.9 times lower than that of spiro-MeOTAD $(4.41\times10^{16}\mathrm{cm}^{-3})$ . Despite better p-type characteristics, photovoltaic performance of the $\mathrm{FA_{0.9}C s_{0.1}P b B r_{0.2}I_{2.8}}$  PSC with HL38 $\left(\mathrm{PCE}=19.60\%\right)$ ) is lower than that of the one with spiro-MeOTAD $(\mathrm{PCE}=20.06\%)$ , mainly due to lower open-circuit voltage $V_{\mathrm{oc}}=1.035~\mathrm{V}$ for HL38 vs 1.101 V for spiroMeOTAD) (Figure S7, Supporting Information). Although the perovskite/HL38 interface forms well physically as shown in the SEM images in Figure  2d, time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) in Figure  2c finds a poor charge extraction property from perovskite to HL38 compared to the perovskite/ spiro-MeOTAD case. It is noted that there is a little deviation in absolute value of MHT thickness $130\mathrm{nm}$ for HL38 and $160~\mathrm{nm}$ for spiro-MeOTAD) in the real devices in Figure  2d,e as compared to those in the devices for SCLC measurement. The PL decay is fitted with the biexponential decay equation and the fitting parameters are listed in Table S1, Supporting Information, where the average PL lifetime $(\tau_{\mathrm{PL}})$ is estimated to be $6.38\mathrm{~ns~}$ for HL38 and 3.67  ns for spiro-MeOTAD based on amplitude weighted lifetime.[65] This underlines that interface between HL38 and perovskite should be further tuned by adequate interfacial engineering to enhance charge extraction efficiency and thereby photovoltaic performance.

Ethyl group in 2-TEAI is expected to induce van der Waals interaction to stabilize the interlayer between perovskite and HL38.[20,66,67] Long alkyl chain may be better for van der Waals interaction, which is however unfavorable for charge transfer. Figure 3a shows electrostatic potential map of $2{-}\mathrm{TEA^{+}}$ ion, where thiophene group has higher electron density than ammonium group. This dipole alignment is expected to facilitate hole extraction from perovskite to HL38. To investigate the interaction between 2-TEAI and HL38, proton nuclear magnetic resonance $(^{1}\mathrm{HNMR})$ of the mixture of HL38 and 2-TEAI (molar ratio $=1{:}1$ ) in acetone- ${\bf\nabla\cdot}\mathrm{d}_{6}$ is measured together with that of HL38 and 2-TEAI (Figure 3b). - Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To explore thermal stability of the HL38-based PSCs, differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) is first studied on the HL38 doped with additives of LITFSI and tBP to determine the $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ because $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ could be affected by additives, impurity, and morphology.[6,7,76] We have measured DSC for two types of MHTs: a dried-powder obtained by evaporating solvent from the MHT solution and a recovered-powder obtained by collecting it from the MHT film deposited on a glass substrate and then dried under high vacuum for $36\mathrm{~h~}$ . The latter is investigated to study the thermal property of the MHTs in a real device system. The DSC results for the dried-powder $(T_{\mathrm{g}}{-}1)$ , the recovered-powder without additive $(T_{\mathrm{g}}{-}2)$ , and the recovered-powder with tBP $(T_{\mathrm{g}}{-}3)$ are shown in Figure S14, Supporting Information, and those for the recovered-powder with tBP and LiTFSI $(T_{\mathrm{g}}{-4})$ are shown in Figure 4a. $T_{\mathrm{g}}\mathrm{s}$ of spiro-MeOTAD are determined to be 127.1, 98.3, 72.8, and $62.3~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}_{}$ , while those of HL38 are measured to be 131.7, 119.0, 107.1, and $92.2~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $T_{\mathrm{g}}{-1}$ , $T_{\mathrm{g}}{-2}$ , $T_{\mathrm{g}}{-3}$ , and $T_{\mathrm{g}}{-4}$ , respectively. $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ is lower for the film form $(T_{\mathrm{g}}{-}2)$ than for the dried-powder $(T_{\mathrm{g}}{-}1)$ . It is noted in the recovered-powder that an inclusion of additive of tBP and LiTFSI has less influence on $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ of HL38 (from 119.0 to $92.2~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ ) as compared to the more decrease in $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ from 98.3 to $62.3~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for spiro-MeOTAD. Moreover, no obvious crystallization temperature $(T_{\mathrm{c}})$ and melting point $(T_{\mathrm{m}})$ are observed for the undoped/doped HL38, as compared to the distinct $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{m}}$ for both the doped and undoped spiro-MeOTAD (Figure 3a and Figure S10, Supporting Information). This implies that HL38 is thermally stable regardless of doping. Thermogravimetric analysis for HL38 in Figure S15, Supporting Information, shows that decomposition starts from $339~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ ( $5\mathrm{~wt}\%$ loss) and is maximized kinetically at $465~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . For further explaining the superior thermal stability of HL38, we examine the $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ ion migration in the PSC device by measuring time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (ToF-SIMS) of the non-aged fresh device and the $85^{\circ}\mathrm{C}{\cdot}1032$ h-aged one. In Figure 4b, ToF-SIMS depth profile of the fresh HL38-based PSC (dashed lines) is compared with that of the $1032\mathrm{~h~}$ -aged one (solid lines), in which ion species of ${\mathrm{Au}}^{+}$ , $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ , $\mathrm{Sn^{+}}$ , $\mathrm{Cs^{+}}$ , and $\mathrm{Pb^{+}}$ are presented. ToF-SIMS profile for the fresh and aged devices with spiro-MeOTAD is also presented in Figure  4c. For the fresh (non-aged) devices, ten times higher $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ signal is detected at perovskite layer for spiro-MeOTAD than for HL38, which might be due to lower $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ affinity of spiro-MeOTAD. Moreover, large portion of $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ signal is detected at $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ layer for both the doped devices as compared to no $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ in $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ for the undoped devices (see Figure S16, Supporting Information), which is likely to result from a deep penetration of $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ ion during spincoating.[77] After aging the HL38-based PSC for $1032\mathrm{h}$ at $85~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , relatively small portion of $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ ion is diffused out from HL38 layer and most of the diffused $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ ions are found at perovskite/ HL38 interface. On the other hand, large portion of $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ ion is diffused out from spiro-MeOTAD layer and reaches the perovskite/ $\mathrm{{\sf~SnO}}_{2}$ interface after aging the spiro-MeOTAD-based PSC. Moreover, large portion of Cs ion is diffused from perovskite to spiro-MeOTAD layer after thermal stability test, which is contrary to the absence of Cs ion in the HL38 layer. ToF-SIMS results imply that better thermal stability from the HL38-based device is related to the suppressed $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ ion diffusion during thermal test at $85~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . This low $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ ion diffusivity is expected to be due to a strong interaction of the $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ ion with carbonyl functional group $\scriptstyle(\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{O})$ in the Octyl-TPD unit in HL38. To further confirm this hypothesis, Fourier transformed infrared (FT-IR) is studied. As shown in Figure 4d, FT-IR peaks of symmetric and asymmetric ${\mathrm{C}}{=}0$ stretching at 1718 and $1689~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ in Octyl-TPD[78] are shifted to lower wavenumber of 1712 and $1687~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ upon mixing Octyl-TPD with LiTFSI (1:1 molar ratio), which indicates that the bond strength of ${\mathrm{C}}{=}0$ is weakened due to interaction with Li ion. Consequently, the aromatic ${\mathrm{C}}{=}{\mathrm{C}}$ stretching $(1515~\mathrm{cm^{-1}})$ is shifted to a higher wavenumber $(1523~\mathrm{cm^{-1}})$ due to the interaction of the carbonyl group with the $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ ion.[79] In addition, asymmetric vibration of S–N–S in LiTFSI $(1055~\mathrm{cm^{-1}})$ is shifted to slightly higher wavenumber of $1059~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ , which indicates that interaction of the TFSI− ion with the $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ ion becomes less strong due to the $\mathrm{C=O-Li}$ interaction in Octyl-TPD/LiTFSI complex (see the schematic structure of the Octyl-TPD/LiTFSI complex in Figure  4e).[80,81] It cannot be ruled out that lower diffusivity of the $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ ion in HL38 than in spiro-MeOTAD might be in part attributed to more reactive (Lewis basic) thiophene in HL38 than benzene in spiro-MeOTAD. Since hole conductivity will be lowered by the diffusion of $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ from MHT to perovskite layer, the HL38- based PSC is expected to show better thermal stability than the device employing spiro-MeOTAD due to lower $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ diffusivity. $J-V$ curves for the HL38- and the spiro-MeOTAD-based PSCs are monitored with aging time at $85~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ in Figure S17a,b, Supporting Information. The HL38-based device shows no degradation in $J_{\mathrm{sc}}$ and FF except for a small decrease in $V_{\mathrm{oc}},$ while a large decrease in FF along with a gradual decrease in $J_{\mathrm{sc}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{oc}}$ for the spiro-MeOTAD-based device. This clearly demonstrates that thermal stability of the HL38-based PSC is better than that of the spiro-MeOTAD-based device, as aforementioned, due to higher $T_{\mathrm{g}}$ and lower Li ion diffusivity.  As a result, the HL38-based PSC maintains $85.9\%$ of its initial PCE (from $21.9\%$ to $18.8\%)$ after aging for $1032\mathrm{~h~}$ at $85~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , whereas a significant degradation in PCE from $21.1\%$ to $5.8\%$ is observed for the spiro-MeOTAD-based device (Figure  4f) (see also statistical stability data in Figure S18 and Table S3, Supporting Information). We have also investigated thermal stability of the doped (additive-containing) HL38 devices without 2-TEAI post-treatment to clearly show the role of HL38 on enhancing the thermal stability, together with that of the additive-free HL38 devices with and without 2-TEAI post-treatment (see Figure S19, Supporting Information). The additive-free HL38 device shows a PCE as low as $10.57\%$ before 2-TEAI post-treatment, while a higher PCE of $15.91\%$ is obtained after 2-TEAI post-treatment. As expected, the PCE of the additive-free HL38 (with 2-TEAI treatment) device maintains $89.0\%$ of its initial PCE after $648\mathrm{~h~}$ at $85~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , which might be due to the absence of the additives and/or intrinsic thermal property of HL38. For the doped HL38 device without 2-TEAI post-treatment, the PCE maintains substantially $87.3\%$ of its initial PCE from $19.63\%$ to $17.15\%$ after aging for $648\mathrm{~h~}$ at $85~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , which strongly indicates that the HL38 play decisive role in thermal stability of PSCs. In Table S4, Supporting Information, the thermal stability of the HL38-based PSC is compared with other hole-transporting materials, where the HL38-based PSC shows comparable or even better PCE and thermal stability at test condition of $85~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for over $1000\mathrm{~h~}$ . This work highlights the importance of complexation of LiTFSI with MHT in order to improve thermal stability by protecting Li ion from diffusion from MHT into perovskite layer.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The key challenge in preparing the highly-ionized CNC/polycation composite was that the colloidal self-assembly of CNCs was very sensitive to ionic strength and EISA could fail using a CNC suspension containing ionic monomers. Our simple approach was to use a swelling process after the assembly of CNCs (Figure 1a). First, CNC films were prepared by EISA from a $3\mathrm{~wt}\%$ suspension in water. The obtained cholesteric film was iridescent and exhibited a fingerprint texture under polarized optical microscopy (Figure  1b). In addition, layered structures were observed in the scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images (Figure 1d), demonstrating the periodic stacking of the cholesteric structure. The methacryloxyethyl trimethyl ammonium chloride (MTAC) mixed with the crosslinker and photo-initiator in ethanol, could effectively swell the CNC film without destroying the cholesteric order. Polymerization was initiated by UV irradiation to produce a CNC/polyMTAC (CNC/PMTAC) composite. The successful incorporation of the chemically-crosslinked PMTAC (Scheme S1, Supporting Information) with CNCs was demonstrated because the composite did not re-disperse in water like pristine CNCs films. Additionally, the FT-IR spectra showed characteristic peaks at $1060~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ , attributed to the stretching vibration of the $\mathrm{C}{-}\mathrm{O}$ bond in cellulose, and at $1730~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ , attributed to the ${\mathrm{C}}{=}0$ stretching of PMTAC (Figure  1g). In addition, the redshifted reflectance maxima (from 399 to $451~\mathrm{nm}$ , Figure  1f) and color (from blue to green) demonstrated the effective incorporation of PMTAC into CNC liquid crystals which increased the pitch of the cholesteric structure. The obtained CNC/PMTAC composite exhibited similar fingerprint textures and periodic structures compared with pristine CNCs film (Figure 1c,e), suggesting that the composite preserved the chiral helix arrangement of CNCs.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The as-prepared CNC/PMTAC composite exhibited high hydrophilicity. When immersed in water, the iridescent film became milky after swelling due to the excess red-shift of the reflectance maxima (due to the increased pitch by swelling) towards the near-infrared, leaving a white appearance caused by scattering between the swollen PMTAC and CNCs. After the counterion of $\mathrm{Cl^{-}}$ was replaced by less hydrated anions of $\mathrm{Br^{-}}$ , $\mathrm{I^{-}}$ , $\mathrm{BF_{4}}^{-}$ , $\mathrm{ClO_{4}}^{-}$ , $\mathrm{PF_{6}}^{-}$ , and $\mathrm{TF}_{2}\mathrm{N}^{-}$ (bistrifluoromethane sulfonyl imide), respectively, the reflectance maxima of these CNC/ PMTAC films (denoted as CNC/PMTAC-X, ${\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{Cl}^{-}}$ , $\mathrm{Br^{-}}$ , $\mathrm{I^{-}}$ $\mathrm{BF_{4}}^{-}$ , $\mathrm{ClO_{4}}^{-}$ , ${\mathrm{PF}}_{6}{}^{-}{}$ and $\mathrm{Tf}_{2}\mathrm{N}^{-}$ in sequence of decreasing hydration) in water underwent blueshift slightly from $841\mathrm{nm}$ of $\mathrm{Cl^{-}}$ to $743.6~\mathrm{nm}$ of $\mathrm{TF}_{2}\mathrm{N}^{-}$ (red line in Figure 2c). Interestingly, when the films were dried and soaked in water again, some of these samples exhibited incompletely reversible swelling (Figure  2b,c). The films containing $\mathrm{Cl^{-}}$ , $\mathrm{Br^{-}}$ , and $\mathrm{I^{-}}$ showed reversible swelling, while the films containing $\mathrm{BF_{4}}^{-}$ , $\mathrm{ClO_{4}}^{-}$ $\mathrm{PF_{6}}^{-}$ , and $\mathrm{TF}_{2}\mathrm{N}^{-}$ showed lower swelling degree when compared with the same films before being dried. The incompletely reversible swelling led to a limited red-shift of the reflectance maxima and retained the chiral optical properties of the films when immersed in water (Figure S1, Supporting Information). For example, CNC/PMTAC-TF $2^{\mathrm{N^{-}}}$ showed a reflectance maximum at $743.6\ensuremath{\mathrm{nm}}$ before the first drying, but exhibited a reflectance maximum at $545~\mathrm{nm}$ in the second swelling. As shown in Figure 2b, the swollen CNC/PMTAC films containing less hydrated counterions exhibits bright colors rather than a milky appearance. The anion exchange in such strong polyelectrolyte systems was highly reversible, as demonstrated by the cyclic exchanges between $\mathrm{I^{-}}$ and $\mathrm{TF}_{2}\mathrm{N}^{-}$ (Figure 2e). The reflectance maxima in water remained nearly unchanged after multiple exchanges, demonstrating the rewritable characteristic of the film. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis of the film also demonstrated the effective and reversible exchange of counterions (Figure S2, Supporting Information). The exchange kinetics of ion-exchanging from highly hydrated to less hydrated counterions were faster than the reverse process because the highly hydrated status exhibited higher swelling degree, promoting the exchange. For example, the equilibrium time for the exchange of $\mathrm{Cl^{-}}$ into $\mathrm{PF_{6}}^{-}$ in 200 mmol $\mathrm{L}^{-1}~\mathrm{NaPF}_{6}$ solution was about $90~\mathrm{s}$ , while the reverse process took 15 min to reach near the equilibrium. The swelling kinetics of the CNC/PMTAC film were rapid regardless of the counterion. As shown in Figure $^{2g,\mathrm{h}}$ , all of the films reach swelling equilibrium within $10~\mathrm{s}$ .  The reason for the controlled wetting dominated by the counterions was that the counterions exhibited various hydration capacities according to their charge density,[19] which could be quantified by the enthalpy of hydration and the hydration shell thickness (Table S1, Supporting Information). The hydration impact from counteranions could be supported by the increasing static contact angles of the PMTAC with different anions as shown in Figure S3, Supporting Information, from $27.63^{\circ}$ of PMTAC-Cl− to $42.53^{\circ}$ of $\mathrm{PMTAC}\cdot\mathrm{Tf}_{2}\mathrm{N}^{-}$ . In extension, as the hydration of anions decreased, the saturated water contents of PMTAC (dried and re-swollen) decreased from $91.89\%$ in PMTAC-Cl− to $12.53\%$ in $\mathrm{PMTAC}\cdot\mathrm{Tf}_{2}\mathrm{N}^{-}$ (Figure  2f) indicating that the counterions significantly affected the swelling degrees of PMTAC, which determined the wettability of the composite film. For extremely low hydrated counterions such as $\mathrm{PF_{6}}^{-}$ and $\mathrm{Tf}_{2}\mathrm{N}^{-}$ , the film exhibited limited swelling rate on the surface, preventing the solution from further swelling the inner layer of the film, leading to incompletely reversible reswelling behavior.

- caption: Figure 3. a) Schematic diagrams and b) optical images of the inkjet printing and erasing process to create invisible patterns on the CNC/PMTAC film. c) To e) hidden and revealed patterns on the CNC/PMTAC film by inkjet printing. Scale bars: $7\:m m$ .
- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Despite the significant differences in the swelling properties of CNC/PMTAC with various counterions, these CNC/PMTACs exhibited nearly the same appearance and reflection wavelengths in the dry state (Figure S4, Supporting Information) due to the limited impact of the counterions on the refractive index of PMTAC and the low PMTAC fraction in the system $\mathrm{(PMTAC:CNC=22wt\%}$ in average). However, distinct contrasts between the regions with different counterions appeared rapidly when the dried film was immersed in water. As shown in Figures 3a,b, the patterns of ${}^{46}\mathrm{SYSU}^{57}$ and Chinese characters, created by inkjet printing with a $200\mathrm{mu}\mathrm{NaPF}_{6}$ solution, appears within seconds when immersed in water (see Video S1, Supporting Information) and reverts to an invisible state when dried. When immersed in water, the background of CNC/ PMTAC- $\mathrm{\cdot\mathrm{Cl^{-}}}$ was milky in appearance because of water absorption as mentioned above, while the CNC/PMTAC- $\mathrm{PF_{6}}^{-}$ patterns preserved the iridescent structural colors. The wetting-drying cycle of the film enabled the reversible decoding and hiding of information. More importantly, the patterned film could be erased for reprogramming, by exchanging $\mathrm{PF_{6}}^{-}$ back to $\mathrm{Cl^{-}}$ via immersion in a $1\mathrm{~M~}\mathrm{{NaCl}}$ solution for $15~\mathrm{min}$ (Figure S5, Supporting Information), indicating that the film was rewritable via the exchange of monovalent anions. As shown in Figure 3c–e, the same film is reprinted into various patterns to demonstrate its reprogrammable property and all of the hidden patterns can be revealed by wetting. As shown in Figure S6, Supporting Information, the printing resolution was about $100\upmu\mathrm{m}$ (printed by a nozzle whose diameter is $20~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ ). Based on the high resolution, an invisible QR code was scanned by a smartphone when immersed in water for a few seconds (see Video S2, Supporting Information).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): As shown in Figure 5b, a badge was printed with a CBS solution onto the film, and then the film was washed with a concentrated NaCl solution. However, only CBS on the surface was exchanged during this process, and the CBS that remained in the inner layer of the film became a permanent fluorescence pattern which could be observed under UV irradiation (excitation maximum $\lambda=375\mathrm{{nm}}$ , emission maximum $\lambda=440\mathrm{nm}_{.}$ ). - caption: Figure 6. Schematic illustrations and optical images showing encryption and decoding of the sandwich design formed by two CNC/PMTAC films separated by a transparent unidirectionally stretched film. The QR codes could only be recognized under right-handed CPL with the bottom layer wetted and the top layer dried.
- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Since the cholesteric self-assembled CNCs were characterized by their polarization-dependent structural colors, a polarization-wettability dual-responsive encoding motif based on a coupled depolarization strategy was demonstrated on the CNC/PMTAC composites. The key to realizing the polarization decoding was a sandwich design formed by two CNC/PMTAC films separated by a transparent unidirectionally stretched film (actually, most commercial transparent polymeric films were stretched). The stretched film in the middle played a crucial role in partially depolarizing the incident light, resulting in asymmetrical optical responses of the two CNC/PMTAC films. As shown in Figure S9, Supporting Information, the partial depolarization of the incident light is demonstrated by the significantly increased transmittance when a unidirectionally stretched PET film was placed in the middle of a right polarizer and a quarter-wave plate with a linear polarizer as an analyzer. Correspondingly, the cholesteric CNC/PMTAC film was transparent under a right-handed CPL, while bright structural colors were still visible when the CNC/PMTAC film was covered by a unidirectionally stretched PET film because the CPL was partially depolarized by the PET film (Figure S10, Supporting Information). The sandwich structure is constructed as shown in Figure 6, in which the top layer is a thick CNC/PMTAC film as a sheltering layer and the bottom layer is patterned with an invisible QR code via inkjet printing. The polarization-dependent coating was based on invisible structural colors of the top shelter layer under right-handed polarized light but visible structural colors of the bottom layer because the stretched film in the middle partially depolarized the incident light. The top layer exhibited iridescent structural colors that concealed the bottom layer, and wetting with water caused an unpolarized milky shelter against the decoding of the bottom layer. Hence, the top layer could only be decoded under right-handed CPL due to the elimination of structural colors (Figure S11, Supporting Information). The bottom layer could be decoded through water wetting, and its structural color did not disappear under right-handed CPL due to the depolarization effect of the stretched film at the center of the sandwich structure. Consequently, a double-encoded system was realized using independent decoding motifs of polarization and wettability based on a single platform of CNC/ PMTAC composite.

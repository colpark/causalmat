- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The GaNP NWs investigated in this work were grown by Ga-catalyzed molecular beam epitaxy on Si (111) substrates;47,48 see Supporting Information, S1 for details. Figure 1a shows a representative scanning electron microscopy (SEM) image of the as-grown GaNP NW transferred onto a $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}(280~\mathrm{nm}$ thick)/Si substrate, which features a slender structural profile. The base region of the wire has a diameter of $100\mathrm{nm}$ , with slight tapering occurred at the center and giving a top part diameter of around $90~\mathrm{nm}$ . The entire $2.2\mu\mathrm{m}$ long NW exhibits smooth side facets, implying good crystalline quality and a well-defined nanoantenna structure. To unambiguously identify the role of N in nonlinear optical processes, pure GaP wires of the same diameter, ${\sim}90~\mathrm{nm}$ , were prepared as control samples; see Supporting Information, S2. To reveal the effect of alloying with $_\mathrm{N}$ on linear optical properties of the GaNP NWs, steady-state microphotoluminescence ( $\mu$ -PL) measurements were performed on single wires transferred onto the $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{Si}$ substrate by using a $488~\mathrm{nm}$ diode laser as an excitation source. Figure 1b displays the representative PL spectra recorded at room temperature (RT). In stark contrast with GaP wire, whose emission is hardly detected due to an indirect bandgap, the PL spectrum of GaNP NW is dominated by a broad asymmetric band centered at around 2.065 eV $\mathrm{\check{(}600n m)}$ , with a long emission tail extended toward the low energy side. The observed PL peak originates from the radiative transitions of localized excitons (LEs) trapped at localized N-related states, $\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{c}}$ below the band continuum.43−46,49,50 The PL cutoff at around $2.26~\mathrm{eV}$ in Figure 1b (indicated by the dashed line) marks the onset of the band continuum. Based on the band anticrossing model for dilute nitrides,40,51 the $_\mathrm{N}$ content, [N], of GaNP NWs was determined to be ${\sim}0.45\%.$ , which falls within the range when the GaNP bandgap undergoes a transition from an indirect to a quasi-direct one according to earlier studies of GaNP thin films.40−42 This crossover is further supported by a change of a radiative lifetime, revealed from time-resolved PL (TRPL) dynamics of LEs at $5~\mathrm{K},$ as shown by the inset of Figure 1b, when nonradiative recombination processes become largely suppressed. By fitting the PL decay with a stretched exponential function for disordered tail states,46,52 we extracted the radiative lifetime, $\tau_{\mathrm{r}}$ of $\mathord{\sim}30$ ns. This value is much shorter than the radiative lifetimes, $\tau_{\mathrm{r}}>200\mathrm{ns},$ , of near-band edge transitions (see open dots in the inset of Figure 1b) in the parental GaP with an indirect bandgap,53 and also in GaNP alloys with much lower N compositions, indicative of an increased dipole oscillator strength upon alloying. This increase is a result of N-induced admixing of all extended CB states and localized $\mathrm{N_{c}}$ states, bringing a sizable $\Gamma$ character to the CB states at the bacnd edge.40−42 The measured $\tau_{\mathrm{r}}$ is, on the other hand, about 1 order of magnitude longer than other $\mathrm{II}{-}\mathrm{V}$ direct gap semiconductors, such as InP and GaAs with a typical radiative lifetime of several ns, see inset of Figure 1b.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Having ascertained the crystalline structure of the GaNP NW antenna, comprehensive SHG measurements were performed on single GaNP and GaP NW antennae to interrogate the effect of alloying with $\mathbf{N}$ on nonlinear optical properties. The utilized measurement geometry is schematically shown in Figure 1c. Both types of the nanowires were placed on $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}\big(280\mathrm{nmthick}\big)/\mathrm{Si}$ substrate and oriented along the $x$ -axis in the lab coordinate system. An $800~\mathrm{nm}$ line from a Ti: Sapphire pulsed laser was used as an excitation source, which was focused at the center of NWs to a spot size of $1\mu\mathrm{m}$ . The excited SHG signal was detected in the backscattering geometry along the $z$ -axis. The polarization angle of the fundamental/SHG light is defined as $\theta_{\omega}/\theta_{2\omega},$ with respect to the $x\cdot$ -axis. Spectra 1 and 2 in Figure 3a display the SHG signal recorded from individual GaP and GaNP NWs, respectively. In this case, the excitation laser was polarized along the NW, i.e., $\theta_{\omega}=0^{\circ}\left(E_{\omega}\|x\right).$ , for optimal light coupling into the subwavelength antenna cavity, and the detection was unpolarized. The SHG emission from both wires can be clearly observed at $400~\mathrm{nm}$ , which is corroborated by its quadratic dependence on a pump fluence shown in the inset of Figure 3b. Noticeably, the SHG signal from the GaNP NW is about five times the intensity from the GaP NW, implying that alloying with N enhances the SHG output. The intense nonlinear radiation of GaNP NW is also visible from the optical micrograph in the inset of Figure 3a, where two symmetric lobes transversely radiated from the central excited SHG dipoles are observed.13 To better quantify the enhancement effect and rule out all possible factors that may influence nonlinear signal strength like wire length, crystalline quality, morphological variation, and so on, statistical measurements were conducted on ${\sim}120\mathrm{GaP}$ and GaNP NWs. The results of these measurements are compiled in Figure 3b as histograms 1 and 2 for the GaP and GaNP NWs, respectively. Fitting these distributions with a normal distribution function yields mean intensity values $(\mu)$ of 130 and 25 for the GaNP and GaP NWs, respectively, rendering an average enhancement factor of 5.2. The standard deviation, $\sigma,$ of the SHG distribution is slightly larger for the GaNP NWs than that for the GaP NWs, i.e., $\sigma\big(\mathrm{GaNP}\big)=70\$ vs $\sigma\big(\mathrm{GaP}\big)=50\$ . Such broadening can be attributed to the fluctuations in the N composition among the NW ensemble. It is known that the SHG response can be expressed in terms of $I_{2\omega}\propto|P_{2\omega}|,^{2}$ where $P_{2\omega}$ is the secondorder nonlinear polarization following $P_{2\omega}=\varepsilon_{0}\chi^{(2)}E^{26}$ . Given the nearly identical pumping condition, the 5.2-fold increase in the SHG intensity indicates an enhancement of the secondorder nonlinear susceptibility, $\chi^{(2)}.$ , by ${\sim}2.3$ times. As an important figure of merit for nonlinear photon generation, we estimated the second harmonic conversion efficiency,13,23 $\eta_{\mathrm{SHG}},$ via $\eta_{\mathrm{SHG}}~=~P_{\mathrm{peak},2\omega}/P_{\mathrm{peak},\omega}^{2}$ . Here $P_{\mathrm{peak},2\omega}$ $\big(P_{\mathrm{peak},\omega}\big)$  represents the peak power of the SHG signal (the fundamental laser). An $\eta_{\mathrm{SHG}}$ of $\hat{\sim}1.2\times10^{-7}\mathrm{W}^{-1}$ was derived for the GaNP NW antenna (see Supporting Information S3 for details), being higher than the reference GaP NWs with $\eta_{\mathrm{SHG}}$ of ${\sim}2.3\times$ $10^{-8}\mathrm{W}^{-1}$ .  The facilitated SHG output in the GaNP NW can be attributed to the strong N perturbation of the global CB continuum states and also to a reduction of the local crystal symmetry upon $_\mathrm{N}$ incorporation. Indeed, since N atoms have an appreciably smaller atomic size and a larger electronegativity in comparison with phosphorus (P) atoms, their incorporation into GaP causes the breaking of translational symmetry and the consequent X-L-Γ valley mixing, as was revealed by both optical and theoretical studies. 57 This leads to a strong modulation of the electronic density of states and dipole oscillator strength in CB, hence contributing to the increased second-order nonlinear coefficient.6,58 Moreover, microscopic lattice relaxation due to the atomic size mismatch, as well as N-induced alloy disorder, and the formation of N clusters can further lower down local crystal symmetry, leading to the more efficient photon conversion process. The SHG enhancement by the localized N cluster states is evident from the performed wavelength-dependent SHG studies. The SHG signal becomes resonantly enhanced when its energy is resonant with that of the localized N states (see the solid green dots in Figure 1b), rendering a spectral dependence of the SHG intensity similar to that of the PL emission. These results are distinct from that of GaP wires (see the open blue dots in Figure 1b), where the resonance peak is absent and, therefore, corroborates the important role of N states in boosting the SHG yield.  The polarized radiation pattern of a nonlinear nanoantenna is crucial for controlling light coupling and routing in chipbased PICs and nonlinear meta-photonic devices. To ascertain the nonlinear polarimetric profile of our subwavelength antenna, especially the influence of nanotwin domains on the SHG radiation, systematic polarization-resolved measurements were performed on single GaP and GaNP NWs on the $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{Si}$ substrate. The obtained SHG patterns under different measurement schemes are given in Figure $_{3\mathsf{c}-\mathsf{e}}$ . First, we look at the configuration where the polarization of the SHG detection is colinear with that of the excitation in the lab coordinate frame, i.e., $\pmb{\theta}_{2\omega}=\pmb{\theta}_{\omega}$ $\left(\boldsymbol{E}_{2\omega}\|\boldsymbol{E}_{\omega}\right)$ . In this case, the polar SHG signal is found to be symmetrical along the NW axis, shown by dots in Figure 3c. The obtained polarimetric pattern is distinct from that of pure ZB phase with asymmetric distribution about wire axis,54 implying the involvement of other factors in modifying the SHG profile, most likely due to nanotwin formation and the optical antenna effect. - Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Motivated by the quest for a higher integration density in chip-scale PIC, miniaturized nonlinear nanoantennae with a deep subwavelength footprint and uncompromised conversion efficiency are highly desired. However, the diffraction loss of the excitation light becomes increasingly important as wire diameters fall far below the pump wavelength. To estimate the optical confinement of the $800\mathrm{nm}$ laser light in the GaNP nanoantenna on a $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ substrate, we calculated the effective indices, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of optical modes against diameter, $d,$ which are shown by the solid and dashed lines in Figure ${^{4}}\mathbf{g}.$ $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of all optical modes decreases progressively as the wire diameter reduces and results in a cutoff $d$ of ${\sim}180~\mathrm{nm}$ for the fundamental $\mathrm{HE}_{11\mathrm{a}}$ mode, below which mode field becomes severely leaked through the $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ substrate, as reflected from Figure 4a,e. To attain tight localization of the light field in our sub- $100~\mathrm{nm}$ diameter NWs, the GaNP nanoantenna was placed on a gold thin film, as illustrated in Figure 1c, to form the hybrid surface plasmonic (SP) cavity .13,23,61−63 Such structure is expected to sustain fundamental SP mode down to ${\sim}90~\mathrm{nm}$ diameter size, as manifested by a solid star in Figure $4g$ and corresponding eigen mode profile in the inset. Besides the merit of light confinement, the hybrid antenna also warrants improved phase-matching conditions for the SHG process, by which light momentum is conversed via the annihilation of counter-propagating SP modes and consequent generation of transverse-emitting SHG photons.23 The intensified light localization in hybrid antenna, under the normal incidence of laser excitation, is visualized in Figure 4b,f for $E_{\omega}\|x$ . It should be noted the total field intensity is contributed by both enhanced fundamental Mie resonance and the SP cavity mode, whose effective mode volume was calculated to be $\sim\left({\frac{\lambda}{2n}}\right)^{3}$ ; see Supporting Information, S5. By substituting the $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ substrate with the Au thin film, an average field enhancement factor in the hybrid antenna is derived to be ${\sim}11$ . The concentration of laser light in the hybrid SP nanoantenna is experimentally manifested by the drastically increased SHG signal 3 and histogram plot in Figure 3a,b, reaching 72-fold enhancement with respect to that of the same GaNP NW on $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}/\mathrm{Si}$ substrate and 380-fold to that for the GaP NW. The $\eta_{\mathrm{SHG}}$ was determined to be $9.4\times10^{-6}\mathrm{W}^{-1}.$ which outperforms the reported hybrid SHG nanoantennae in terms of the efficiency and device size. $^{13,23,63,64}$ It should be mentioned that no SHG signal was detected from bare Au thin film, as depicted by a dotted line in Figure 3a, therefore substantiating the increased optical nonlinearity as arising from the hybrid structures. The SHG enhancement was also observed on silver $\left(\operatorname{Ag}\right)$ thin film-coupled NWs, manifesting the high efficiency of the hybrid metallo-dielectric systems; see Supporting Information, S6. We note that the experimentally derived SHG enhancement ratio in the hybrid antenna is somewhat lower than the theoretical value of 121 times obtained by squaring the excitation enhancement factor. The discrepancy most likely arises from the surface roughness of the Au thin film, which causes imperfect contact with the NWs and hence degrades the SP coupling. This can be hinted at by its much broader normal distribution in Figure 3b, giving a fitted $\sigma$ value of ${\sim}1500$ . An improvement of the dielectricmetal interface in hybrid antennae should further increase the SHG intensity and hence $\eta_{\mathrm{SHG}}$ . Though demonstrated in a prototype device form, the suggested dilute nitride NW-based hybrid antenna clearly represents a high-efficiency SHG source in the subdiffraction regime, offering another promising nonlinear building block for Si-integrated optoelectronic circuitry.

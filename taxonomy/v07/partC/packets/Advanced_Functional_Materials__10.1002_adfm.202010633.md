- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The underlying mechanism of HOR, which is the reverse process of the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER), is similar to that of HER (Figure  1b).[22–24] Therefore, it is not illogical to speculate that electrocatalysts should possess similar activities toward HER and HOR; however, PGMs and non-PGMs behave distinctively toward these two reactions. As illustrated in Figure  1c, PGMs including Pt, Pd, and Ir possess high electrocatalytic activity for both HOR and HER. Some Ni-based electrocatalysts exhibit interesting HOR/HER activity in alkaline media, yet their performances are still much inferior to the state-of-the-art PGM-based materials.[25–28] Notably, a large number of PGM-free electrocatalysts such as transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs), transition metal phosphides (TMPs), and their heterostructures exhibit high HER activity, while most of them show negligible HOR activity.[29–31] In a nutshell, metallic surfaces are more favored by the HOR, and only a few nonmetallic compounds show noticeable HOR activity.[19] Until now, the exact reasons remain unclear.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In alkaline media, the HOR kinetics of PGMs become more sluggish, and many theories have been proposed to explain this phenomenon.[24,41–46] An important model is that the HOR/HER activity is still solely determined by the HBE;[24,41,47] however, PGMs bind hydrogen somehow stronger as the increase of pH, and hence PGMs shift away from the top of the volcano plot in alkaline media (Figure 2a). Durst  et  al. studied the HOR/HER kinetics of carbon-supported Pt, Ir, and Pd electrocatalysts using a PEMFC and a rotating disk electrode (RDE) under the acid and alkaline environment, respectively.[24] The results suggested that the HOR/HER possess identical elementary steps in acid and base media, and therefore the authors proposed using the HBE as the sole descriptor for the HOR/HER activity. Besides, they asserted that the effect of pH might be the origin of the observed activity difference.[24] Later, Yan and co-workers discovered that the HBE of Pt increases monotonically with pH by evaluating cyclic voltammograms (CV)  curves,[41] and HOR/HER activities decrease accordingly owing to the excessively strong HBE in alkaline media. Their results directly supported that the HBE is the sole decisive factor for the HOR/HER activity on monometallic Pt.[41]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Koel and co-workers reported a N-containing Hf oxide $(\mathrm{HfN}_{x}\mathrm{O}_{\gamma})$ by treating hafnium oxyhydroxide with nitrogen plasma.[69] As displayed in the polarization curves in Figure 3b, the diffusion-limited HOR current on $\mathrm{HfN}_{x}\mathrm{O}_{\gamma}$ is similar to $\mathrm{Pt},$ demonstrating its high activity. CV curves show significant differences before and after the nitrogen plasma treatment, indicating that the treatment activates the $\mathrm{HfN}_{x}\mathrm{O}_{\gamma}$ surface, probably by increasing the electric conductivity of the pristine Hf oxide film, but the exact origin remains unclear. Also, the $\mathrm{HfN}_{x}\mathrm{O}_{\gamma}$ film was prepared on an Au foil, and the possible support effects need further exploration. Moreover, Figure 3c demonstrates an apparent hydrogen desorption current on $\mathrm{HfN}_{x}\mathrm{O}_{\gamma}$ in the HOR potential region, which is not usual for non-PGM electrocatalysts and could be a probable reason for the high HOR activity.  Zeng  et  al. reported the activation of ${\mathrm{MoO}}_{2}$ by Ni doping for HOR/HER in acid media. X-ray absorption fine structure (XAFS) measurements indicated that Ni dopants take the position of Mo atoms in $\mathrm{MoO}_{2},^{[70]}$ resulting in electron deficiency on neighboring O atoms that serve as hydrogen adsorption sites (Figure 3d). DFT calculations were used to further explore the hydrogen adsorption behavior of $\mathrm{Ni}\mathbf{-MoO}_{2}$ . The results prove that the $\Delta G_{\mathrm{H}}$ value is dependent on the surface hydrogen coverage. For Pt, the $\Delta G_{\mathrm{H}}$ is about $-0.09\mathrm{eV}$ at a hydrogen coverage of $100\%$ under experimental conditions, while the pristine ${\mathrm{MoO}}_{2}$ possesses a similar $\Delta G_{\mathrm{H}}$ only at much lower coverage. As for the Ni-doped cases, the incorporation of Ni increases the proton affinity, and a close-to-neutral $\Delta G_{\mathrm{H}}$ can be achieved at higher hydrogen coverage (Figure  3e). Figure  3f shows that Ni-doped ${\mathrm{MoO}}_{2}$ exhibits apparent HOR current, which, however, is still much lower than that of $\mathrm{Pt}/\mathrm{C}$ . This work emphasizes the influence of the hydrogen coverage on $\Delta G_{\mathrm{H}}$ , and further theoretical studies should pay more attention to the $\mathrm{HOR/}$ HER kinetics at different hydrogen coverages.  Hydrogenases are natural electrocatalysts for reversible HOR/HER with extremely high turnover frequencies (TOFs) using only Fe and Ni atoms as active sites. Inspired by hydrogenases, Artero and co-workers developed Ni-based arginine-containing derivatives $[\mathrm{Ni^{II}}(\mathrm{P_{2}}^{\mathrm{Cy}}\mathrm{N_{2}}^{\mathrm{Arg}})_{2}]^{7+}$ (NiArg) integrated with graphene acid (GA), as illustrated in Figure  3g.[71] Figure  3h shows the CV curves in $0.5\textbf{M}$ $\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{SO}_{4}$ , where $2~\upmu\mathrm{L}$ of $5\times10^{-3}$ m NiArg are deposited on a gas diffusion layer with different GA loadings. The HOR current increases with GA loadings, and the composite electrode shows a maximum current density of about $40\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2}$ at $0.4~\mathrm{~V~}$ versus RHE with a GA loading of $0.8\mathrm{mg}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ . This result demonstrates the great potential of GA with regard to improving the catalyst loading and managing the porosity within nanostructured electrodes, which is vital to achieving a high catalytic current with PGM-free electrocatalysts.[71]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Alloying and doping is a common approach to improving the HOR activity of Ni-based electrocatalysts.[90,91] Raney Ni is a typical low-cost electrocatalyst for the HOR and HER. Doping Raney Ni with transition metals such as Cr, La, Ti, Cu, and Fe affects its HOR activity significantly.[92] For example, metallic Cu is located at the right side of the volcano plot with weak HBE, and hence alloying Ni with Cu is expected to tune the HBE of Ni.[93] Zhuang’s group systematically studied the HOR/HER activity of NiCu binary alloys, and the maximum activity was achieved in the case with ${\approx}40\mathrm{at}\%\mathrm{Cu}.^{\leftmoon}$ In another example, Yan and co-workers reported a ternary metallic CoNiMo catalyst for the alkaline $_\mathrm{HOR.}$ As shown in Figure  4d, the activity of the optimized CoNiMo ternary alloy outperforms Pt with sufficient mass loading. DFT calculations suggest that the HBE of CoNiMo alloy is much lower than Ni owing to the formation of multimetallic bonds. Similarly, the HBE of Ni can also be lowered by the interstitial N doping in $\mathrm{Ni}_{3}\mathrm{N}$ , and the free energy for hydrogen oxidation is hence decreased (Figure 4e).[76] Recently, Yu’s group reported a bimetallic $\mathrm{MoNi_{4}}$ alloy as the HOR electrocatalyst.[26] Scanning TEM (STEM) image in Figure 4f shows that the $\mathrm{MoNi_{4}}$ alloy possesses interconnected nanosheets morphology. Significantly, the $\mathrm{MoNi_{4}}$ alloy exhibits even higher activity than the commercial $\mathrm{Pt}/\mathrm{C}$ electrocatalyst in $0.1\mathrm{~M~}\mathrm{~KOH~}$ (Figure $4g)$ . At the overpotential of $50~\mathrm{mV},$ the $\mathrm{MoNi_{4}}$ catalyst achieved a geometric kinetic current density of $33.8\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2}$ , which is 2.8 and 105 times that of the commercial $\mathrm{Pt}/\mathrm{C}$ and the as-prepared Ni, respectively. The authors attribute the high HOR activity to the alloying effect that results in the optimized $\mathrm{H^{*}}$ and $\mathrm{OH^{*}}$ adsorption behavior. A similar alloying effect is also demonstrated in $\mathrm{WNi_{4}}$ alloy. Besides, the Ni 2p XPS spectra suggest that $\mathrm{MoNi_{4}}$ and $\mathrm{WNi_{4}}$ alloys possess a higher metallic portion as well as lower binding energy as compared with Ni metal, indicating that the surfaces are more difficult to be oxidized after alloying.[26]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Serov and co-workers synthesized NiMo NPs on Ketjenblack (NiMo/KB) as a non-PGM HOR electrocatalyst, which was evaluated in both a three-electrode system and a fuel cell.[83] The hydrogen diffusion limit current density reaches around $1.0\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ with a catalyst loading of $0.5\mathrm{mg}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ (Figure 5a). - Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Heterostructure engineering is another commonly used strategy to improve the activity and durability of Ni-based electrocatalysts. The most prominent effects of heterostructure engineering include electronic interaction and the possible synergistic effect between different components. As illustrated in Figure 6a, the electron redistribution between different components changes the electronic structure of active sites, usually manifesting as the binding energy shift in XPS spectra. Tuning the electron distribution at the heterointerface is an effective strategy to modulate the electronic structure of active species.[95,96] In Ni-based heterostructured electrocatalysts, notably, Ni usually serves as the electron donor owing to the relatively small electronegativity.[25,73–75,78] In the meantime, heterostructure engineering provides the possibility to balance the adsorption of $\mathrm{H^{*}}$ and $\mathrm{OH^{*}}$ at different sites at the heterointerface. This is usually reported as the synergistic effect between different species. The key to maximizing the synergistic effect is making a balance between the $\mathrm{H^{*}}$ and $\mathrm{OH^{*}}$ adsorption, which can be achieved by tuning the ratio of different components (Figure 6b). For instance, the HOR/HER kinetics of Ni/NiO is significantly affected by the coverage of surface $\mathrm{NiO}_{x}$ and the maximum activity is achieved at $\approx30\%$ coverage of NiO.[97] In turn, the ratio between NiO and Ni also affects the electronic structures of active sites.  It has been reported that the surface of Ni metal is oxidized after the HOR process and the $\mathrm{Ni}~2\mathrm{p}$ binding energy is increased.[83] Therefore, a major task is to preserve the metallic Ni sites so as to achieving long-term durability. Surface coating is a simple but effective method to mitigating the surface oxidation of Ni under alkaline HOR condition as the metallic Ni surface does not directly contact with the alkaline solution;[98] however, the HOR process is also inhibited. On the other hand, it was demonstrated that the inner metallic core can activate the outer graphene layers via electronic interaction,[99] and hence the electrocatalytic activity is preserved. Similar to the previous case of core–shell Ni-C electrocatalyst in acid media,[67] a core– shell $\mathrm{Ni}@\mathrm{h}.\mathrm{BN}$ nanocatalyst was designed with Ni NPs encapsulated in few-layer h-BN shells.[79] As illustrated in Figure  6c, the metallic Ni is protected by BN layers, while the electronic interaction activates the outer surface. Both experimental and theoretical calculations suggest that the interactions between the catalyst surface and key intermediates including $\mathrm{O^{*}}$ , $\mathrm{H^{*}}$ , and $\mathrm{OH^{*}}$ species are weakened to some extent.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): There are not many non-PGM HOR electrocatalysts without using Ni as active sites, and most of them are heterostructures. Sun and co-workers prepared $\mathrm{Co}_{2}\mathrm{N}/\mathrm{Co}$ heterostructured electrocatalyst with abundant interfacial sites via partial nitridation of Co NPs on current collectors, as shown in Figure $^{7\mathrm{a}}$ .[27] The $\mathrm{Co}_{2}\mathrm{N}/\mathrm{Co}$ electrocatalyst shows higher HOR activity than $\mathrm{Pt}$ in $0.1\mathrm{~M~}\mathrm{KOH}$ (Figure  7b). Although CoP possesses suitable HBE, its electric conductivity is not very high as the excessive content of $\mathrm{P}$ atoms limit the delocalization of electrons owing to the higher electronegativity of P. Benefiting from the reasonable hybrid structure that integrates suitable HBE of CoP and excellent conductivity of $\mathrm{Co}_{2}\mathrm{P}$ , the $\mathrm{Co}_{2}\mathrm{P}/\mathrm{CoP}$ hybrid exhibits excellent catalytic activities as well as durability (Figure 7d,e).[80]

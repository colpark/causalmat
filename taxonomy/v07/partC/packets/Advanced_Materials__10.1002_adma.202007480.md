- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The schematic synthesis procedures for the $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$ /graphene hybrid are illustrated in Figure  1a and Figure S1, Supporting Information. In brief, the exfoliated ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ first adsorbs graphene oxide (GO) through electrostatic attraction enabled by $\mathrm{CTA^{+}}$ media, then a flower-like architecture assembled by the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene nanosheets is obtained via a hydrothermal reduction process. Figure  1b displays the XRD patterns of the as-prepared ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ /graphene nanocomposite and bulk ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ . All diffraction peaks of the bulk ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ index accurately to the hexagonal $2\mathrm{H}\mathrm{-MoS}_{2}$ phase (space group of P63/mmc, JCPDS No. 37–1492). As for ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ /graphene, the diffraction signals of $2\mathrm{H}\mathrm{-MoS}_{2}$ and graphene, especially the sharp (002) peaks, can hardly be detected; however, two new characteristic peaks of (001) and (003) featuring diploid relationships are observed at  $2\theta=7.6^{\circ}$ and $15.2^{\circ}$ , respectively. This indicates that the stacking of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ and graphene layers was significantly alleviated, meanwhile, graphene was successfully inserted into the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ gallery with the interlayer distance of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ enlarging from 0.62 to $1.16~\mathrm{nm}$ (Figure  1c, calculated according to the Bragg equation).[41] It is anticipated that the low-crystallinity ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene hybrid with spacious 2D framework will provide adequate diffusion channels for $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ ions. Figure  1d shows the Raman spectra for bulk ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ , ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ /graphene, and GO. In contrast to bulk ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ , the red shift of the $\mathrm{A_{1g}}$ peak and the decreased interval between the $\mathrm{E}_{2\mathrm{g}}^{1}$ and $\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{{1g}}}$ peaks for the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene hybrid confirm the weakened interaction of the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ interlayers.[39] Moreover, the intensity ratio of the D band (defect vibration) to the G band (graphitic carbon vibration) increases obviously from GO (0.83) to ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ /graphene (0.95), demonstrating the formation of rGO with abundant defects.[42] This result is in agreement with the C 1s XPS and Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra (Figures S2 and S3, Supporting Information). Compared with GO, the significantly weakened $_{\mathrm{C-O}}$ and $\scriptstyle\mathrm{C=O}$ peaks of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ /graphene suggest that GO is successfully reduced to graphene with some remaining oxygencontaining groups on the surface. In addition, from the FTIR results, the successful hybridization of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ with graphene is verified, and the possibility of $\mathrm{NH}_{4}^{+}$ insertion into ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ to enlarge the interlayers is eliminated. Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) in nitrogen atmosphere was also performed on the ${\mathrm{Mo}}S_{2}.$ /graphene hybrid, showing a negligible weight loss during the heating process with no conspicuous endothermal peak in the differential thermal analysis (DTA) curve (Figure S4, Supporting Information). Together with the very weak O-H signal in the FTIR spectrum (which mainly derives from the remaining oxygen-containing functional groups in rGO), it can be confirmed that there is no obvious structural water in the asprepared nanocomposite.  Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images show that the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}{\mathrm{.}}$ graphene hybrid, featuring a typical flower-like morphology, is composed of ultrathin nanosheets with an average thickness of $10{-}20~\mathrm{nm}$ , while the bulk ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ consists of irregular nanosheets with the thickness ranging from tens to hundreds of nanometers (Figure  1e and Figure S5, Supporting Information). High-resolution TEM (HRTEM) images reveal that dozens of the bulk ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ layers are seriously stacked together with an interlayer spacing of $0.62\mathrm{nm}$ (Figure  1f), whereas the stacking of the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2/}$ graphene layers is significantly relieved with the interlayer distance strikingly expanding to $1.16~\mathrm{nm}$ (Figure 1g). More impressively, the intercalated graphene monolayer can be clearly observed in some ${\mathrm{Mo}}S_{2}$ interlayers (inset of Figure 1g). Atomic force microscopy (AFM) images affirm the presence of an exfoliated ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ monolayer $(\approx1\mathrm{nm})$ and capture a typical ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene nanosheet with a thickness of $3.28~\mathrm{nm}$ (Figure S6, Supporting Information). The observed thickness corresponds with the distinctive sandwiched structure of two ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ monolayers $({\approx}2\mathrm{nm})$ with an expanded interlayer spacing of ${\approx}1.2\mathrm{nm}$ , providing further confirmation of the successful intercalation of graphene into ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ gallery. It is noteworthy that the interlayer spacing obtained in this study is among the highest values ever reported for ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ . The high-angle annular dark field (HAADF) TEM image in Figure  1h exhibits abundant 1T-phase trigonal lattices along with some 2H-phase honeycomb lattices within the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene hybrid. XPS spectra also manifest that ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene is dominated by the 1T-phase, while the bulk ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ is almost pure 2H-phase (Figure S7, Supporting Information). As expected, the high-content 1T-phase together with the residual oxygen-containing groups in graphene endows the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene hybrid with better hydrophilicity than that of the bulk ${\mathrm{Mo}}{\mathrm{S}}_{2}$ (Figure S8, Supporting Information).

The proportions of graphene in the three developed ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2/}$ graphene samples are 8.3, 11.4, and $14.2\mathrm{wt}\%$ , respectively, as determined by TGA. XRD patterns reveal that the rGO peak gradually strengthens, but the (001) peak gradually weakens as the graphene content increases. Together with the SEM results, it can be concluded that low or high graphene content would lead to a substantial build-up of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ or graphene layers, respectively. The optimum graphene content would be $11.4\mathrm{wt}\%$ .  The electrochemical performance of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}{\mathrm{,}}$ /graphene was evaluated by assembling CR2032-type coin cells. Since rGO contributes a very low capacity to the hybrid (Figure S11, Supporting Information), the specific capacity is calculated based on the mass of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ . The CV curves of $\mathrm{MoS}_{2}$ /graphene (Figure 2a) show one pair of redox peaks located at $0.63/0.97\mathrm{V},$ which are attributed to the $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ intercalation/deintercalation process. Moreover, the near-overlapping behavior of the CV curves indicates excellent electrode reversibility. The galvanostatic charge–discharge profiles in Figure  2b display that the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene cathode affords an initial discharge/charge capacity of $280.7/311.3\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ with a Coulombic efficiency (CE) of $90.2\%$ . The CE increase to ${\approx}99\%$ after 10 cycles indicates the gradual improvement of the reversibility of the $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ insertion/extraction process. Meanwhile, a discharge plateau at ${\sim}0.70\mathrm{~V~}$ and a charge plateau at ${\approx}0.90\mathrm{~V~}$ (vs $\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}/\mathrm{Zn})$ were detected, which agree with the CV profiles. The $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ transfer number is about 0.85 per formula unit based on the electrochemistry. The cycling performance of the three ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene materials with different graphene contents and bulk ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ is given in Figure  2c. The graphene-incorporated cathodes exhibit a considerable improvement of $\mathrm{Zn}$ -storage capacity compared to the bulk ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ cathode. The graphene content also plays a crucial role in determining the structural stability of the electrodes. After 100 cycles, the optimized ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene cathode containing $11.4~\mathrm{wt\%}$ graphene delivered a high reversible capacity of $263.7~\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ at 0.05 A ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ along with a respectable capacity retention of $93.0\%$ . Post-mortem HRTEM images manifest that the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}{\mathrm{,}}$ /graphene hybrid still maintains its ultrathin-nanosheet structure with an interlayer distance of $1.16~\mathrm{nm}$ after cycling (Figure S12a, Supporting Information). By contrast, the electrode with low graphene content $(8.3\mathrm{wt}\%)$ exhibited rapid capacity degradation due to the detrimental restacking of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ layers upon prolonged cycling, while another counterpart containing $14.2~\mathrm{wt\%}$ graphene afforded a much lower specific capacity because of the accumulation of excess graphene, which blocks the $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ transport channels (Figure S12b,c, Supporting Information). In addition, the exfoliated and then restacked ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ without adding graphene has been prepared, which shows a low crystallinity but the same interlayer distance of $0.62\mathrm{nm}$ compared to the bulk ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ (Figure S13a, Supporting Information). The narrow interlayer spacing with insufficient Zn-ion insertion channels and active sites results in an extremely low reversible capacity for the restacked ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ electrode (Figure S13b, Supporting Information). Figure  2d depicts the rate performance of the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene cathode. As the current density increases from 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4 to 5 A ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ , reversible capacities of 285.4, 283.9, 275.7, 252.7, 227.2, 196.1, 172.8, 154.4, and 141.6 mA h $\mathrm{g}^{-1}\mathrm{:}$ respectively, are delivered. The rapidly recovered capacity of $277.7\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ when the rate returns to $0.05\mathrm{Ag}^{-1}$ demonstrates the decent high-rate tolerance of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ /graphene. The superior rate capability achieved in the present study compared with those of other TMD-based cathodes for aqueous $\mathrm{Zn}$ -storage is highlighted in the inset of Figure 2d and Table S1, Supporting Information. Of note, the discharge capacities show a slight increase during the initial several cycles, reflecting an electrode activation process as a result of the gradually smooth ion-diffusion channels, progressively stabilized structural mechanics, and more adequate electrolyte infiltration upon repeated $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ insertion/extraction.[40] This point is also evidenced by electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements that the charge-transfer resistance $(R_{\mathrm{ct}})$ of the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene electrode displays an obvious decrease after 10 cycles along with negligible variances during the subsequent cycling (Figure S14, Supporting Information). In addition, EIS spectra confirm that a higher graphene content endows the electrode with more efficient charge transfer and faster $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ diffusion, further affirming the important role of graphene in modulating the electronic/ionic conductivity (Figure S15, Supporting Information). The long-term cycling stability of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2/}$ graphene was then assessed at a current density of 1 A ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ (Figure  2e). Impressively, the electrode displayed an admirable capacity retention of $88.2\%$ even after 1800 cycles, which significantly stands out among those of the previously reported TMDs cathodes for AZIBs (Table S1, Supporting Information).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To gain insights into the electrode process kinetics of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene, GITT measurement was carried out to estimate the $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ diffusion coefficient $(D_{\mathrm{Zn}})$ (Figure  3a, calculation details are described in the Supporting Information). As calculated, the observed $D_{\mathrm{{Zn}}}$ values of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ /graphene mainly stay in the magnitude range from $10^{-11}$ to $10^{-12}~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ over the entire charge/ discharge process, which are comparable to those previously reported for manganese-based and vanadium-based oxides for aqueous Zn-storage (e.g., layered MnO2,[17] 10−12 to 10−14 cm2 s−1; Al-ion modified $\mathrm{V}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{5}\cdot1.6\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ ,[20] $10^{-11}$ to $10^{-13}~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{s}^{-1})$ . The $\mathrm{Zn}$ -ion diffusivity can also be probed by the CV profiles at different scan rates according to the Randles–Sevcik formula, namely, $i_{\mathrm{p}}~=~2.69~\times~10^{5}n^{3/2}A D_{\mathrm{Zn}}{}^{1/2}C_{\mathrm{Zn}}\nu^{1/2}$ ,[43] where $i_{\mathrm{p}}$ (A) is the peak current, $n$ is the number of transferred electrons $(n\approx1.7$ , in this work), A $(\mathrm{cm}^{2})$ is the contact area between the electrode and the electrolyte, $C_{\mathrm{Zn}}$ is the $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ concentration in the electrode (ca. $2.66\times10^{-2}$ mol $\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ , calculation details are provided in the Supporting Information), and $\nu~(\mathrm{V~}\mathrm{s}^{-1})$ is the scan rate. As demonstrated in Figure 3b, all CV curves exhibit a similar shape with sweep rates increasing from 0.1 to $1\mathrm{mV~s^{-1}}$ , implying low electrode polarization during $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ insertion/ extraction. Figure  3c depicts the linear relationship between $i_{\mathrm{p}}$ and $\nu^{1/2}$ of the redox peaks. The $D_{\mathrm{Zn}}$ values for Peaks 1 and 2 are calculated to be $4.06\times10^{-12}$ and $8.13\times10^{-12}~\mathrm{{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{{s}^{-1}}}$ respectively, which are consistent with the GITT results. The surface-controlled pseudocapacitive effect is further identified by the function of $i_{\mathrm{p}}=a\nu^{b}$ , where $a$ and $b$ are variable parameters. Generally, $b=0.5$ represents the ion-intercalation process and $b=1$ indicates the capacitance-like behavior.[44] From the fitting curves in Figure  3d, the $b$ values of Peaks 1 and 2 are determined as $0.876$ and $0.874,$ respectively, revealing that Faradaic intercalation and pseudocapacitive behavior synergistically dominate the charge storage process. Furthermore, through the relation of $i(\mathrm{V})=\dot{k}_{1}\nu+{k}_{2}\nu^{1/2}$ , where $i(\mathrm{V})$ is the current a fixed potential (V), the contributions from diffusion-controlled $(k_{2}\nu^{1/2})$ and capacitance-related $(k_{1}\nu)$ processes are rationally quantified.[43] Figure 3e presents the typical CV profile at $0.6~\mathrm{mV~s}^{-1}$ illustrating a pseudocapacitive contribution (orange region) of $77.9\%$ to the total current. In addition, the capacitive contribution gradually increases from $63.3\%$ to $87.2\%$ as the scan rate increases from 0.1 to $1~\mathrm{mV~s^{-1}}$ (Figure S16, Supporting Information). The decent reaction kinetics of the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene cathode including facile $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ diffusion and a desirable pseudocapacitive effect ensure the remarkably high rate capability.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Furthermore, DFT computations were conducted to determine the effect of the sandwiched ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ /graphene structure on the $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ migration dynamics. As previously reported,[45] the octahedral (O) center between ${\mathrm{Mo}}S_{2}$ layers is a stable metal-ion intercalation site and the metal ions tend to diffuse from one O site to another through the adjacent tetrahedral (T) site. Figure 5a–d shows the $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ diffusion paths in the structural models of bulk ${\mathrm{Mo}}S_{2}$ and ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ /graphene. Considering the undulating character of the intercalated graphene in ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ gallery, various distances of the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ layer to graphene layer were simulated to calculate the Zn-ion migration energy barriers. As depicted in Figure  5e,f, the $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ migration barriers in the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ /graphene framework decrease drastically as the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ -to-graphene distance increases, that is, from $0.425~\mathrm{eV}$ for the ${\mathrm{Mo}}S_{2}$ –graphene interlayer channels with a theoretically shortest distance of 4.86 Å to an extremely low value of $0.024~\mathrm{eV}$ when the distance is enlarged to $6.5\mathrm{~\AA~}$ . Noticeably, the ionic diffusion energy barriers are much lower than that in the bulk ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ (0.991  eV), indicating that the intercalated graphene can pave the way for facile Zn-ion diffusion in the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ lattice. Moreover, the projected density of states (DOS) of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ /graphene display considerable states at the Fermi level, while the states of bulk ${\mathrm{Mo}}S_{2}$ exhibit an obvious bandgap of ${\approx}0.8~\mathrm{eV}$ (Figure 5g). This reveals that the ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene hybrid possesses metallic properties owning to the synergistic effect of high-content 1T-phase and graphene, whereas the bulk ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}$ is a semiconductor. Therefore, the low $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ migration barriers and desirable electronic conductivity of ${\mathrm{MoS}}_{2}/$ graphene enable its superior rate performance.

More impressively, the quasi-solid-state cell only exhibits slight capacity variations under different bending states and can successfully activate a light-emitting diode (LED) (Figure 6e,f), demonstrating the excellent flexibility and the great prospects for practical applications.

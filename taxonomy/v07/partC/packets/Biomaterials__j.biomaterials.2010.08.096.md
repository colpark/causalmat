- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Balb/c mice bearing 4T1 murine breast cancer tumors were intravenously injected with SWNTs $( 2 0 0 \mu \mathrm { l }$ of $\sim 0 . 5 \ : \mathrm { m g / m l }$ solution for each mouse) functionalized by different polymers. Blood was drawn at different time points post injection and solubilized by a lysis buffer. SWNT levels in blood samples were measured by a Raman spectroscopy method using our established protocol (Fig. 2) [6,15,16,27,29] The blood circulation half-lives were calculated by the first-order decay fitting and presented in Table 1. As expected, increasing PEG chain densities and lengths (from 2 kDa to $5 \mathrm { k D a }$ ) on the PMHC18 polymer significantly prolonged SWNT blood circulation. Among all tested polymers, $1 0 0 \% { - } 5 \mathrm { k P E G - P M H C } _ { 1 8 } { - }$ functionalized SWNTs exhibited the longest blood circulation halflife of $^ { 2 0 . 8 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ .  To understand the relationship between blood circulation halflives of SWNTs and their biodistribution, we plotted liver, spleen, tumor and skin uptake of each SWNT conjugate against its blood half-life (Fig. 3c). With the increase of SWNT blood circulation halflife, the liver and spleen uptake of nanotubes showed a trend of decrease, in agreement with previous findings [6,27]. Also as expected, the SWNT tumor uptake gradually increased as the extension of nanotube circulation half-life. Interestingly, the skin uptake of nanotubes increased slowly before $\sim 1 3 \ \mathrm { h }$ of circulation half-life, but sharply jumped later at longer half-lives $\left( > 1 6 \mathrm { ~ h } \right)$ ). The longest SWNT blood circulation offered by the $1 0 0 \%$ - 5kPEGePMHC18 coating, although allowed the highest tumor uptake $( \sim 2 3 \% \ \mathrm { I D / g ) }$ , resulted in extremely high accumulation of nanotubes in the mouse skin $( \sim 2 9 \% \ \mathrm { \ t D / g ) }$ . In contrast, $1 0 \%$ - 5kPEGePMHC18 polymer-coated SWNTs with circulation half-lives of $^ { 1 2 . 8 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ exhibited high tumor uptake at $\sim 1 5 \% \mathrm { I D } / \mathrm { g }$ , together with relatively low skin accumulation at ${ \sim } 3 ~ \% \mathrm { { I D } / g }$ . The RES uptake of $1 0 \%$ -5kPEGePMHC $1 8$ eSWNT (Liver: $\sim 1 5 \% \mathrm { I D } / \mathrm { g }$ , Spleen: $\sim 1 8 \% \mathrm { { I D / g ) } }$ was comparable to that of $1 0 0 \% { - } 5 \mathrm { k P E G - P M H C } _ { 1 8 } { \mathrm { - } } \mathrm { S W N T }$ (Liver: $\sim 1 6 \% \mathrm { { I D / g } }$ Spleen: $\sim 1 3 \% \mathrm { I D } / \mathrm { g }$ ) and substantially lower than that of shorter circulating SWNTs. The tumor-to-normal organ $\left( \mathrm { T } / \mathrm { N } \right)$ uptake ratios of various SWNT conjugates were calculated and presented in Table 1. SWNTs with short blood half-lives had high Tumor-to-Skin (T/SK) ratios but rather low Tumor-to-Liver $\mathrm { ( T / L ) }$ and Tumor-to-Spleen (T/SP) ratios. On the contrary, SWNTs with ultra-long circulation half-lives showed higher $\mathrm { T / L }$ and T/SP ratios however low $\mathrm { T } / \mathrm { S K }$ ratios. Only $1 0 \%$ -5kPEGePMHC18eSWNT with a blood circulation half-life of 12e13 h showed balanced $\mathrm { T } / \mathrm { N }$ ratios, optimal for potential applications in cancer therapy and imaging.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Using the selected $1 0 \% { - } 5 \mathrm { k P E G - P M H C } _ { 1 8 }$ polymer-coated SWNTs with high tumor uptake and relatively low skin accumulation, we performed a pilot in vivo photothermal therapy study. Seven mice bearing two 4T1 tumors at both shoulders were intravenously injected with $2 0 0 \mu \mathrm { l }$ of $1 0 \%$ -5kPEGePMHC18efunctionalized SWNTs at $0 . 5 \mathrm { m g / m l }$ . Concurrently, seven mice also bearing two 4T1 tumors were used as the control. One of the two tumors on each mouse was exposed to an $8 0 8 \mathrm { n m }$ laser at a power density of 1 $\mathsf { W } / \mathsf { c m } ^ { 2 }$ two days after injection of SWNTs, while the other tumor was left untouched. The majority of nanotubes should be cleared out from the blood into RES organs and tumors as indicated by blood circulation and biodistribution data. Tumor sizes after various treatments were measured every two days after treatment (Fig. 5a). The surface temperature of the tumors on SWNTs-injected mice reached $\sim 5 0 ^ { \circ } C$ after laser irradiation, in contrast to $1 - 2 ^ { \circ } \mathsf C$ of increase for irradiated tumors on un-injected mice. Five out of seven irradiated tumors on mice injected with nanotubes disappeared 1 day after laser irradiation, leaving black scars on the original tumor sites (Fig. 5b). Among these five tumors, three of them did not re-grow within two weeks while two of them re-appeared after about one week post treatment. The other two tumors in this group with relatively big starting sizes were partially burnt and grew slowly after treatment. In marked contrast, tumors in the control untreated group, the irradiation only group (no SWNT injection) and the SWNTs only group (no laser irradiation) showed similarly rapid growth, demonstrating that NIR laser irradiation or SWNT injection alone did not affect tumor development (Fig. 5a and b). Our results suggest that SWNTs with the optimized surface coating are powerful agents for photothermal therapy of cancer in vivo. A previous study by Moon et al. showed that SWNTs injected into tumors could move to RES organs and be slowly cleared, after tumors were destructed under NIR laser irradiations [22].

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The interaction between PCBM and Bphen and the stabilizing mechanism are shown in Figure 1a. Since the delocalization of electrons is poor, ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ cage is much more reactive due to its strong electron-attracting property, leading to the poor stability even in the inert atmosphere (absence of oxygen and moisture).[33–35] It has been reported that the UV irradiation of fullerene can give photofragmentation products $(\mathrm{C}_{60}+h\nu=\mathrm{C}_{60-n}+\mathrm{C}_{n})$ or a polymeric product $(\mathrm{C}_{60})_{n}$ in previous reports.[36–39] The Bphen, possessing pyridine ring with lone electron pairs at the N, thus has strong electron donating ability. As a result, the Bphen should interact with fullerene or its derivatives through electron transfer, forming ion pairs $\mathrm{C}_{60}{}^{n-}/\mathrm{Bphen}{}^{n+}$ $(n=1,2$ ) donor–acceptor system, as shown in Figure  1a. As reported, the standard enthalpy of formation of $\mathrm{C_{60}n^{-}}$ was more negative than that of ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ $\mathrm{\Omega}(\mathrm{C}_{60}+\mathrm{e}^{-}=\mathrm{C}_{60})^{-}$ , $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}H^{\theta}=-266\mathrm{kJmol^{-1}})$ , and the stability of the fullerene has an increasing tendency with increasing charge.[40,41] It means that the Bphen could stabilize the fullerene through formation of much more thermodynamically stable charge-transfer complexes against the stress of irradiation, especially UV illumination.  To reveal the interaction between fullerene and Bphen, the Raman spectra of fullerene $\left(\mathrm{C}_{60}\right)$ and derivative PCBM films with and without Bphen were measured, as shown in Figure 1b. There are five obvious vibration modes, $\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{g}}(1)$ at around $560~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ , $\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{g}}(5)$ at $900{-}1200~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ region, $\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{g}}(7)$ at around $1420~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ , $\mathrm{\DeltaA_{g}}(2)$ a t $1450~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ region, and $\mathrm{H_{g}}(8)$ a t around $1550~\mathrm{cm}$ .[41] It was found that the peak position of $\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{g}}(2)$ related to “pentagonal pinch” mode, which is usually used as an analytical probe for the structural and electronic properties of $\mathrm{C}_{60}\mathrm{:}$ show a upshift by $4~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ , along with a sharply reduced intensity. In addition, the high frequency $\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{g}}(8)$ mode of fullerene was absent after introducing Bphen due to the depolarization, illustrating that the electron-absorbing tendency of ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ cage obviously weakened. These results demonstrate the charge transfer to the cage for new compounds due to the interaction between ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ cage and Bphen. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were performed as shown in Figure  1c. The core level of N 1s peak for the Bphen added into ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ and PCBM both shifted toward lower binding energy by around $0.15~\mathrm{eV}$ compared to that for pristine Bphen. Such shift should be a result of the interaction between donor pyridine ring in Bphen and acceptor ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ cage.[42]  Figure  1d,e gives the UV-vis results for the PCBM and ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ films, both showing that the absorption band edge exhibits blue shift after adding Bphen. In addition, it is interesting that the broad absorption spectrum from 400 to $550~\mathrm{nm}$ related to the excitons absorption became indistinct.[43,44] These results suggest the electronic structure change of ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ induced by charge transfer from Bphen to fullerene. Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) measurements were used to further evidence for the complexation. Figure S1 in the Supporting Information shows the C–N peak at $1157~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ , for bare Bphen, while it right shifted a bit for PCBM:Bphen. The similar test was adopted for ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ :Bphen sample as shown in Figure S2 in the Supporting Information, confirming the slight shift for $\mathrm{C-N}$ peak originated from the interaction with ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ cage. In addition, the role of Bphen on fullerene electrical properties has been studied (Figure S3 and Table S1, Supporting Information). The improved carrier concentration and conductivity of PCBM:Bphen can also demonstrate the interaction between fullerene and Bphen.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Obviously, the charge-transfer complexation between fullerene and Bphen leads to largely improved films stability under sunlight illumination. Figure 2a,b gives UV-vis absorption evolution of PCBM and PCBM:Bphen films under highintensity UV accelerated aging $(100~\mathrm{mw~cm^{-2}})$ ). In the bare PCBM case, the absorption around $310{-}330\mathrm{nm}$ range shows an obvious enhancement mostly due to the degradation or dimerization of fullerene, which is in agreement with previous reports.[38,45,46] The absorption of PCBM around $320{-}340\mathrm{nm}$ shows an enhancement and blue shift after UV aging due to the molecular structural damage. In comparison, the UV-vis absorption had almost no changes for the PCBM film with Bphen additive, as shown in Figure 2b.  The high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) was used to further confirm the degradation of PCBM, as shown in Figure 2c,d. A sharp peak at the retention time of $2.3~\mathrm{min}$ corresponding to PCBM was shown in the reference sample. The other four peaks might be related to the self-reaction in weakly acidic solvent methanol for the ester hydrolysis.[47] After 1 week (168 h) UV illumination, several new peaks between 2.5 and 4 min were observed, presumably to the PCBM decomposition products and/or polymeric products. The tracement of matrixassisted laser desorption ionization time-of-flight (MALDI TOF) mass spectrum was conducted to characterize the PCBM decomposition, as shown in Figure S4 in the Supporting Information. Several obvious decomposition product peaks together with a small peak at around $1820m/z$ originated to the photoinduced dimerization were found compared to that without UV illumination. In the PCBM:Bphen case, the HPLC signals and MALDI TOF mass results (Figure S5, Supporting Information)  showed almost no change after UV illumination, illustrating that photoinduced degradation effect was not obvious. The comparisons of HPLC analyses were also performed in ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ and ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ :Bphen case, as shown in Figure S6 in the Supporting Information. It is clear that the decomposition indeed occurred in ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ cage since new peaks appeared before 1  min retention time. However, the ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ with Bphen became much more stable judging from the negligible change in characteristic peaks of HPLC. In addition, the FTIR results also demonstrated the decomposition of ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ cage under light irradiation judged from the significant weakening of fingerprint peaks of pure PCBM, and the Bphen could stabilize the ${\mathrm{C}}_{60}$ cage structure well (Figure S7, Supporting Information).[48]  Figure  2e,f depicts the ultraviolet photoelectron spectro­ scopy (UPS) spectrum evolution of PCBM and PCBM:Bphen films on indium tin oxide $(\mathrm{ITO})/\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ substrates before and after exposure under illumination. Initially, the work function (WF) of pristine PCBM is estimated to be $4.33~\mathrm{eV}$ and decrease to $4.21~\mathrm{eV}$ after introducing a small amount of Bphen, which is consistent with the improvement of electron concentration and conductivities (Figure S3 and Table S1, Supporting Information). We speculate that the formed charge-transfer complex is the main reason for the decreased WF of film. After illumination, the WF of PCBM increases from 4.33 to $4.47~\mathrm{eV},$ illustrating the photoinduced structure changes of fullerene (Figure  2e). In comparison, the WF of PCBM:Bphen is much more stable, showing negligible change, as shown in Figure 2f. These results strongly confirm the stabilized effect of Bphen for the fullerene. The stabilized PCBM:Bphen layer could guarantee the devices with high efficiency and excellent stability.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The devices with a conventional architecture of $\mathrm{n{-i-p}}$ heterojunction PSCs were adopted using $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ as ETL. The thin PCBM and PCBM:Bphen were prepared between $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ and CsFAMA perovskite as an interlayer to improve the interfacial contact and isolate the photocatalysis effect of $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ . The morphologies of PCBM films with and without Bphen on $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) shown in Figure S8 in the Supporting Information, which were both uniform and pin-hole free. The roughness of PCBM and PCBM:Bphen films are 0.575 and $0.325\mathrm{nm}$ , respectively, attained from the atomic force microscope (AFM) measurements as shown in Figure S9 in the Supporting Information. We carried out SEM measurements to confirm that both the perovskite films on PCBM and PCBM:Bphen possessed large grains and great crystallinity (Figure S10, Supporting Information). The cross-sectional SEM image of optimized device with PCBM:Bphen is shown in Figure 3a.  The energy diagram of PCBM:Bphen PSCs is shown in Figure S11 in the Supporting Information. The upshift WF of PCBM with Bphen is beneficial to reduce the energy barrier between the perovskite and $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ . Figure  3b shows the dark current–voltage curve of the electron-only device. It is observed that Bphen can improve the electron injection at the ETL/perovskite interface, confirming the reduced energy level misalignment. The photoluminescence (PL) and timeresolved photoluminescence (TRPL) of perovskite films based on different substrates were measured (Figure S12, Supporting Information and Figure  3c). The perovskite films on $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}/\mathrm{PCBM}$ exhibit more obvious PL quenching and short carrier lifetime than those on $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ indicating better electron extraction from perovskite to PCBM, which is consistent with previous reports.[48,49] After introducing the Bphen, the PL intensity and TRPL decay time of perovskite films were further reduced. Enhanced charge transfer from perovskite to ETL could be beneficial to suppress the nonradiative recombination at interface. The perovskite films on PCBM:Bphen $\begin{array}{r}{(V_{\mathrm{TFL}}=0.26~\mathrm{V})}\end{array}$ ) exhibit the lowest defects compared with those either on $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ or PCBM, as determined using the space charge-limited current (SCLC) measurement as shown in Figure S13 in the Supporting Information.  We show typical current density–voltage $(J-V)$ curves for the control, PCBM, and PCBM:Bphen devices in Figure  3d. The champion efficiencies of the devices based on $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ , $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}/$ PCBM, and $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}/\mathrm{PCBM}$ :Bphen were $19.22\%$ , $21.53\%$ , and $23.09\%$ , respectively. The corresponding reverse and forward scanning $J{-}V$ curves and parameters are shown in Figure S14 and Table S2 in the Supporting Information, respectively. Similar to previous reports,[26,49,50] it is because the PCBM can effectively passivate the traps at the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ /perovskite interface that the PCBM, used as an interlayer, could moderately improve the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ device efficiency and obviously eliminate the hysteresis effect. After introducing Bphen into PCBM, all the parameters for the resulted PSCs, including $V_{\mathrm{OC}}$ , FF, and $J_{\mathrm{SC}_{\mathrm{.}}}$ showed further improvement. In Figure 3e, the champion device based on PCBM:Bphen exhibits a $V_{\mathrm{OC}}$ of $1.167~\mathrm{V},$ a $J_{\mathrm{SC}}$ of $25.15\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2}$ , an FF of 78.64, a PCE of $23.09\%$ , and finally reached a stabilized PCE of $22.98\%$ (inset image in Figure 3e) with negligible hysteresis. An encapsulated solar cell was certified by China CEPREI Laboratory (China Electronic Product Reliability And Environmental Testing Research Institute) and delivered a $V_{\mathrm{OC}}$ of $1.1575\mathrm{~V},$ a $J_{\mathrm{SC}}$ of $24.947~\mathrm{mA~cm^{-2}}$ an FF of $79.13\%$ , and a total PCE of $22.85\%$ (Figure S15, Supporting Information). The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of these devices were compared in Figure  3f, which were all in good agreement with the $J_{\mathrm{SC}}$ measured from $J{-}V.$ . The improved $J_{\mathrm{SC}}$ values in $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}/\mathrm{PCBM}$ :Bphen device could be attributed to the faster charge extraction and better conductibility on the ETL/perovskite side (Figures S3 and S12, Supporting Information). In addition, PCBM is also beneficial to the $V_{\mathrm{OC}}$ improvements since it can passivate defects at the interface and grain boundaries of perovskite on the ETL side,[51] which can be confirmed from the SCLC measurements (Figure S13, Supporting Information).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): We monitored photostability of control and Bphen-based devices under durability test condition close to the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) standards. Here, we select UV preconditioning and light soaking test described in IEC 61215 as the references (Figure 4a–d). For the UV preconditioning test, all the devices were encapsulated and tested in the ambient under UV lamp of $100\mathrm{mW~cm^{-2}}$ . The detailed spectrum data of UV lamp are given in Figure S16 in the Supporting Information, showing that the output wavelength ranging from 280 to $385~\mathrm{nm}$ contributes around half the intensity $(50~\mathrm{mW~cm^{-2}})$ ), which is more than three times the IEC 61215 requirement. As a result, the bare $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ PSCs almost completely degraded within $120\mathrm{~h~}$ due to the well-known photocatalysis effect of metal oxides $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ , as we will show below. After introducing PCBM, as a potential material against UV illumination, the device presented the improved stability, maintaining about $70\%$ of its initial PCE after $1100\mathrm{h}$ . The $30\%$ decrease in efficiency is mainly caused by the photoinduced degradation of PCBM, which is generally named “burn-in” effect. Unexpectedly, the device with  PCBM:Bphen could retain over $95\%$ of its initial efficiency after $1100\mathrm{h}$ , showing “burn-in-free” effect.  For light soaking test, we measured these various types of devices aging at $60~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $1000\mathrm{~h~}$ of continuous MPP tracking without any UV-filter in the glove box, as shown in Figure  4e. We give the detailed spectrum distribution of our simulator of solar irradiation in inset image, which is very close to those of natural light (AM 1.5). After light soaking, the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ device showed serious degradation, only remaining $<30\%$ of its initial efficiency within $100\mathrm{~h~}$ . Similar to the previous studies, the PCBM device improved the photostability but exhibited serious burn-in effect.[52–54] The PCE decreased exponentially by ${>}20\%$ of their starting performance within ${\approx}200\mathrm{~h~}$ and finally maintained the stabilized efficiency over $70\%$ for $1000\mathrm{~h~}$ aging. Due to the excellent UV resistance of PCBM:Bphen, the PCBM:Bphen-based device retained over $92\%$ of its initial efficiency after 1000 h MPP tracking at $60~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ which is close to meet the requirements of the IEC 61215 standard (required $95\%$ remaining efficiency).

After $24\mathrm{~h~}$ UV soaking, the decomposition of perovskite films was obviously presented for $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}/$ perovskite film while no obvious change for the other two samples judging from the negligible color change. We further carried out SEM measurements to trace the degradation of perovskite (Figure 5a–d). It can be observed that the perovskite film directly on bare $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ was seriously decomposed due to the photocatalysis effect of metal-oxides $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ . From a thermodynamics point of view, the band gap of $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ is around $3.6{-}3.8~\mathrm{eV},$ which is large enough to decompose perovskite into $\mathrm{Pb}\mathrm{I}_{2}$ and further reduces to metallic ${\mathrm{Pb}}$ and $\mathrm{I}_{2}$ .[13,20,55,56] Even a small amount of metallic ${\mathrm{Pb}}$ generates, as a primary deep defect state, it seriously degradates the device performance, which can explain the poor UV stability for bare $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ -based PSCs. We confirm the emergence of $\mathrm{PbI}_{2}$ and metallic lead products in $\mathrm{SnO}_{2{\cal I}}$ /perovskite film through the X-ray diffraction (XRD, Figure  5e,f) and XPS (Figure  5g,h) measurement. Obviously, the PCBM interlayer can isolate the perovskite and $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ and protect the perovskite well from photocatalytic decomposition. The role of Bphen additive is stabilizing the PCBM interlayer to eliminate the burn-in effect in PSCs under UV and natural light illumination.

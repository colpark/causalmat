- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Providing unimpeded electron/ion transport pathways has been shown to be an effective way to make the voltage hysteresis decline, resulting in high energy efficiency.24 In this regard, a thin acetylene black film is placed on a zinc foil, denoted as AB- $\dot{\mathbf{Z}}\bar{\mathbf{n}}$ .25 Figure S3b (in the Supporting Information) displays the typical voltage profiles of $\mathrm{Zn}$ plating/stripping at constant charge−discharge current densities of $\bar{0.64}\ m\mathrm{Acm}^{-2}$ . Potential profiles for AB- $\mathrm{Zn}$ demonstrate negligible potential fluctuation and no signs of the short circuit, illustrating excellent $\mathrm{Zn}$ plating/stripping behavior and lower interfacial resistance.24 Thus, in the case of $\mathrm{AB{-}Z n//K V O}_{,}$ , a higher capacity of 461 mA h ${\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}$ at $0.2\mathrm{A}{\mathrm{g}}^{-1}$ with an energy efficiency of $86.6\%$ is achieved (Figure S2a, Supporting Information). The surface of the AB film (Figure S4, Supporting Information) and the interface between the zinc foil and the AB film have been analyzed (Figure S5, Supporting Information). Interconnected electron transport pathways and a porous structure provided by the conductive carbon offer a great opportunity for a long life and high energy efficiency.26  As shown in Figure 2a, the cyclic voltammetry (CV) curve profiles of the AB- $\cdot{\mathrm{Zn//KVO}}$ battery at $0.1~\mathrm{mV~s}^{-1}$ within $0.2-$ $1.6\mathrm{~V~}$ vs $\mathrm{Zn}/\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ reveal multiple and well-repeated pairs of redox peaks after several cycles, demonstrating a multistep (de)intercalation of $\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{n}^{2+}$ in KVO, which is frequently presented in other vanadium-based cathodes.4 Furthermore, the significant difference in CV profiles between the initial and subsequent cycles indicates that an irreversible phase transition takes place during the activation stage.  Remarkably, a high and stable reversible capacity of $461~\mathrm{mA}$ $\textrm{h g}^{-1}$ is achieved at $0.2\mathrm{A~g}^{-1}$ (Figure 2b) in the case of KVO nanobelts, higher than those reported for $\mathrm{KV}_{3}\mathrm{O}_{8}{\cdot}0.75\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ $(379\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{hg}^{-1}$ at $0.1\mathrm{~A~g~}^{-1}\big)^{18}$ and $\mathrm{K}_{2}\mathrm{V}_{8}\mathrm{O}_{21}$ $247\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{hg}^{-1}$ at 0.3 A g−1).19  Besides, the storage performance of this aqueous $\mathrm{Zn}$ -ion battery is carried out, and the results have shown that the open-circuit voltage of the $\mathrm{AB{-}Z n//K V O}$ battery remains at $1.49\mathrm{~V~}$ and the charged battery has lost only $1.1\%$ ( $1.9\%$ for $\mathrm{Zn//KVO}_{\cdot}$ , see Figure S6 in the Supporting Information) of the capacity after a $48\mathrm{~h~}$ open-circuit voltage test at a fully charged state (Figure 2c), which is much better than the case of the aqueous Li-ion battery in the previous report (lost $9\%$ after $^{10\mathrm{~h~}}$ ).3 The results of the self-discharge test also indicate the excellent stability of the AB-Zn//KVO system.  Importantly, as shown in Figure 2d,e, the average reversible capacities of 444.1, 427.9, 411.4, 387.8, 370.4, 357.9, 336.8, 323.4, and $313.4~\mathrm{mA}$ h $\mathbf{g}^{-1}$ at 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, and 10 A $\mathbf{g}^{-1},$ , respectively, can be delivered, which is superior to the previously reported cathodes of $\mathrm{Zn}$ -ion batteries. Moreover, when the current density switched back to 0.2 A ${\bf g}^{-1}$ , a discharge capacity of $439.9~\mathrm{mA}$ h $\mathbf{g}^{-1}$ can be recovered, revealing a considerable tolerance to fast Zn2+ (de)- intercalation and excellent structural stability.27 More importantly, the energy efficiency increases from $61.8\%$ for $\mathrm{Zn//KVO}$ to $75.8\%$ for AB ${\displaystyle-\mathrm{Zn}//\mathrm{KVO}}$ at $4\mathrm{Ag}^{-1}$ (Figures S7 and S8, Supporting Information).15 Electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) are applied to reveal the mechanism of significantly improved energy efficiency (Figure S9 and Table S1, Supporting Information), which is ascribed to low interfacial resistance and the charge transfer impedance.24 Comparison of the cycling performance and typical charge− discharge profiles for the $\mathrm{Zn//KVO}$ system with the AB- $\mathbf{Zn}//\$ KVO is shown in Figure S10 (in Supporting Information), which implies this cathode/anode effect plays a vital role in the characterization of KVO in this aqueous zinc ion battery.  Due to the high capacity and outstanding rate performance of the KVO cathode, for equipment with an acetylene black enhanced zinc anode, not only a high energy density of 344 W h $\ k g^{-1}$ but also a superior power energy density of $6946\mathrm{~W~}$ $\ k g^{-1}$ can be achieved (Figure 2f). Moreover, the ultralong cyclic stability of A $\mathrm{{.B{-}Z n//K V O}}$ is shown in Figure ${2}\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}$ . It retains a reversible capacity of $369.1~\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\mathbf g}^{-1}$ at 4 A $\mathbf{g}^{-1}$ (Figure $2g,$ after 4000 cycles. The Coulombic efficiency is maintained at nearly $100\%$ with a low capacity decay rate of $0.002\%$ per cycle for 4000 cycles. When measured at $10\mathrm{Ag}^{-1}.$ , the $\mathrm{AB{-}Z n//K V O}$ delivers an initial discharge capacity of $318.4~\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}$ , maintaining as high as $295.2\mathrm{mA}$ h $\stackrel{\cdot}{\mathrm{g}}^{-1}$ after 4000 cycles (Figure 2h). The Coulombic efficiency remains nearly $100\%$ , and the capacity retention is $96.2\%$ , based on the maximum capacity. Also, as shown in Figure S11 (Supporting Information), this system shows a large capacity of $274.1~\mathrm{mA}$ h $\mathbf{g}^{-1}$ at low temperature $\left(-16^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\right)$ at $\bar{0.5\mathrm{Ag}^{-1}}$ , higher than the that reported for $\mathrm{LiV}_{2}(\mathrm{PO}_{4})_{3}$ .28 Compared with the previous studies (Figure 2f), it can be argued that this AB-Zn//KVO system is a promising high-performance aqueous ZIB for gridscale applications (Table S2, Supporting Information).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To explain the remarkable electrochemical performance, the CV curves at various scan rates from 0.1 and $\mathrm{{\dot{1}}}.0\mathrm{mV~s^{-1}}$ have been obtained to reveal the electrochemical kinetics. The slope of the $\log(i_{p})$ vs $\log(\nu)$ curve, which is from 0.5 (controlled by ionic diffusion) to 1 (controlled by pseudocapacitance), where $i_{p}$ is the current (A) and $\nu$ is the scan rate $\mathrm{(mV~\thinspaces^{-1})}$ ,27 is examined. As shown in Figure 3b, the calculated slope values of the peaks are around 0.9, implying that the electrochemical process of ${\mathrm{Zn/KVO}}$ batteries is mainly controlled by pseudocapacitance.27 As displayed in Figure 3c, the pseudocapacitive contribution has been calculated to be $81.8\%$ for AB- $Z\mathrm{n//KVO}$ at $0.1~\mathrm{mV~s}^{-1}$ . Moreover, with the increasing scan rate from 0.1 to $1.0\mathrm{mV}\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ (Figure S12, Supporting Information), the pseudocapacitive contribution ratios increase from $81.8\%$ to $84.2\%$ . Figure S13 (in the Supporting Information) shows analogical electrochemical behaviors for $\mathrm{Zn//KVO}_{\cdot}$ , suggesting that the kinetics of $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ in the KVO nanobelts are capacitance controlled, and the capacitancecontrolling contribution occupies the absolute proportion, similar to the reported $\mathrm{VO}_{2}$ (B) nanofibers for aqueous ZIB.27 Such a behavior is favorable for the fast energy storag e,29 which also verified the high diffusion coefficients of $\mathrm{Zn}^{\bar{2}+}$ (in the range of $10^{-6}{-10^{-8}}~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}.$ , as shown in Figure 3d) in the KVO nanobelts.  To understand the structural evolution of the KVO electrode, the ex situ XRD technique is conducted at different voltage states (Figures 3e,f). There are significant differences in the XRD profiles between pristine KVO and the activated electrode at the charged state, which is consistent with the results of the CV analysis. Hydrogen metal vanadium oxide hydrate (HZVO, ICOD: 00-026-0338) is formed after several initial cycles. The corresponding atomic ratio of K to V in the different states has been checked using ICP-OES, which shows 0.2293 for the pristine sample and 0.0022 for the cycled sample. As shown in Figure S17 in the Supporting Information, a Na-containing sample $\mathrm{(NaVO)}$ under similar experimental procedures was synthesized, and the A $\mathrm{\Omega_{1}B{-}Z n//N a V O}$ shows an inferior electrochemical performance. Along with good cycling stability in both KVO and NaVO, however, the KVO displays a lower polarization and a higher capacity (Figure S18 in Supporting Information). Although the potassium ion was removed from the host material, the potassium ions play an important role in high electrochemical performance in this work. $\Nu_{2}$ adsorption−desorption isotherm measurement (Figure S19, Supporting Information) reveals that the $\mathrm{K}^{+}$ is favorable to the large surface area $(55.9~\mathrm{m}^{2}/\mathrm{g}$ for KVO vs $10.8~\mathrm{m}^{2}/\mathrm{g}$ for $\mathrm{NaVO},$ ), which provides abundant electrode/electrolyte contact interfaces and enables fast electrochemical kinetics.34 Moreover, the micrometer-sized KVO $(\sim1.0~\mathrm{m}^{2}/\mathbf{g})$ cathode is contrasted to emphasize the superiority of the nanobelts properties in enhancing the performance of KVO. Besides, as shown in Figures $3\mathrm{j-l},$ X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) is conducted, and the results confirm that the electrochemistry of this cathode in ZIBs is mainly based on the reversible reaction of $\mathrm{V}^{4+}/\mathrm{V}^{5+}$ . Although the evolution of the XRD patterns shows a slight structural change (Figure S21, in Supporting Information), this is because of the repeated intercalation/extraction, which has also been observed in another previous report.28 Moreover, as shown in Figure S22, differential capacity vs voltage $({\mathrm{d}}Q/{\mathrm{d}}V)$ curves at different cycles show a similar behavior. Thus, the electrode did not suffer severe crystallinity deterioration after long-term cycles, which could result in good capacity stability.

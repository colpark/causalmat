- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In this paper, we design a new bilayer composite with an MCM of MnCrSbGa ribbon stuck on a TiNi SMA substrate. Although the MnCrSbGa ribbon is stress-insensitive MCM, its transformation temperature and optimal refrigeration temperature are also remarkably moved by $6\mathrm{~K~}$ through the large residual strain $(2\%)$ of the TiNi substrate, which is much larger than that $(0.8~\mathrm{~K~})$ achieved by the electrostrain of PMN-PT piezoelectric substrate. Through a group of the composites with gradient nonvolatile strains of SMA, a wide working temperature window of $31\mathrm{~K~}$ was obtained at a small magnetic field of $1~\mathrm{T}.$ Meanwhile, the refrigeration capacity and the maximum entropy change as well as the small thermal hysteresis (4.9 K) keep stable with the temperature shift, which helps to simplify the heat transfer design and improve the working efficiency of AMR.  The sample geometry of the $\mathsf{M}/\mathsf{E}/\mathsf{T}$ composite is shown in Fig. 1(a). The magnetic MnCrSbGa ribbon plays as a functional layer, and its magnetic properties are controlled by the residual strain of the non-magnetic TiNi substrate. The intermediate epoxy layer is about $13\mu\mathrm{m},$ as displayed in the SEM image of the sample cross-section (Fig. 1(b)). The stress exerting on the MnCrSbGa ribbon in this composite is obtained by the following operations (Fig. 1(c)). Initially, the TiNi substrate is in the self-accommodated polytwinned martensitic state at room temperature (300 K), and the MnCrSbGa ribbon is stuck on it. Then after applying tensile stress on the substrate, its martensitic twin variants reorient along the tensile direction, which keeps essentially unchanged after withdrawing stress [34]. Thus, a large nonvolatile residual strain is obtained, which further generates large stress transferring to the adhered MnCrSbGa layer. By tuning the residual stain of the TiNi substrate through a one-off stretch, different stress states of the MnCrSbGa layer were obtained.

i) hot blow (Fig. 11(c)): The medium liquid is blown from the hot reservoir to the cold reservoir, and a temperature gradient field between $T_{h o t}$ and $T_{c o l d}$ is built along the refrigeration materials bed, thus each refrigerant is at different temperatures [54,55].

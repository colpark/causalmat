- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To probe changes in lattice parameter, we focused on the main Raman mode of ceria with $\mathrm{F}_{2\mathrm{g}}$ symmetry, known as the symmetric oxygen breathing mode around the Ce cation, which enables tracking bond length changes between $\mathrm{O}\cdot\mathrm{Ce}\cdot\mathrm{O}.^{\left[77\right]}$ Figure 3a shows the Raman spectra recorded using a $783~\mathrm{nm}$ wavelength for a similar $200\mathrm{nm}$ $\mathrm{Pr}_{0.1}\mathrm{CeO}_{2\cdot\delta}$ sample grown on $\mathrm{ITO}/\mathrm{Al}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3}$ substrate and exposed to similar annealing as the sample prepared for mobility measurements. The oxygen non-stoichiometry in this Raman study was found to vary from $0.035\pm\:0.001$ to $0.042\pm\:0.001$ from the most oxidized sample to the most reduced sample, respectively, according to the recorded optical transmission spectra (see Figure S5, Supporting Information). One observes a major common peak at $460\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ in all four spectra consistent with the oxygen breathing mode around the $\mathrm{Ce^{4+}}$ cation with $\mathrm{F}_{2\mathrm{g}}$ symmetry and two defectrelated peaks around $\approx550/600~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ . These defect peaks have generally been associated with the presence of the dopant ions and oxygen vacancies, but their exact origin remains a source of debate.[77] The peak at ${\approx}550~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ has been shown to exist only when oxygen vacancies are present in the system and is therefore considered a fingerprint for the latter. Moreover, the peak at ${\approx}600~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ has been shown to be activated by the presence of aliovalent dopants and was hypothesized to originate from the $\mathrm{F_{1u}}$ (LO) phonon mode, usually only IR active, becoming Raman active due to the local lattice distortion relaxing symmetry rules.[77] Furthermore, by examining the relative peak heights under resonant condition in $\operatorname*{Pr}$ doped $\mathrm{CeO}_{2},^{[78]}$ it was shown that the ${\approx}600~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ band could be related to the presence of the optically active $\mathrm{Pr^{4+}}$ cation. In that case, the peak height of the defect peak at ${\approx}550$ and ${\approx}600~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ relate to each other as the former relates to the presence of oxygen vacancies introduced by the aliovalent $\mathrm{Pr}^{3+}$ while the latter is associated with the presence of $\mathrm{Pr^{4+}}$ and the sum of the two concentrations must be constant. As seen in Figure 3b, the $\mathrm{F}_{2\mathrm{g}}$ peak (at $460~\mathrm{cm^{-1}})$ does not shift position within a resolution limit of $\pm0.5~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ while its full-width half max (FWHM) increases from 22 to $26~\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ as the sample oxygen non-stoichiometry was increased, suggesting the onset of defect association and ordering due to the formation of additional nonsymmetric bonding symmetry. As seen in Figure  3c, the peak height at $\approx550~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ increases while the peak at ${\approx}600~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ decreases as the sample oxygen non-stoichiometry is increased. Additionally, for the two most reduced samples, there is a sign of a peak at ${\approx}250~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ which indicates a local symmetry reduction enabling Raman activity of additional $\mathrm{F_{1u}(T O)}$ phonon modes normally prohibited by symmetry rules and used as a fingerprint of local lattice distortion and the onset of defect association. The broad peak contribution at ${\approx}200~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ was present in all films and also in the PCO pellet (see Figure S7, Supporting Information). We are not certain about the source of this peak in PCO as it has not been reported in the Raman spectra literature for ceria, althought it seems to be characteristic of the prasodymium doping in the ceria solute solution. However, because it did not change in position or in character through our different thermal anneals indicating its invariance, it was therefore excluded from the Raman analysis. Additionally, the sharp peak contributions at 376, 416, and $577\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ could be assigned to the underlying ITO and are therefore excluded from the PCO Raman analysis and further discussion (see Figure S6, Supporting Information).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): We note that a major difference between the conventional fixed valent and our mixed valent doped system is that the extra negative charge on the compensating cation in the PCO system, ${\mathrm{Pr}}^{3+}$ , is mobile. The diffusivity of $\operatorname*{Pr}$ ions is expected to be similar to that of Ce ions and other rare earth cations of similar valence, which only become mobile at temperatures $>1000~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ (diffusivity of $10^{-14}–10^{-18}~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ from $400{-}600^{\circ}{\mathrm{C}})^{[93{-}95]}$ and therefore would not be sufficiently mobile during our temperature treatment to rearrange into micro-domains. However, the high mobility of the small polaron (diffusivity of $10^{-5}–10^{-6}~\mathrm{cm}2~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ between $400{-}600^{\circ}{\mathrm{C}})^{[64,65]}$ could readily enable it to undergo ordering at reduced temperatures, leading to the formation of neutral tripoles as described in Equation (4) as well as dipoles in the temperature range of $400{-}600^{\circ}\mathrm{C}.$ . The temperature range employed in this study coincides well with values reported in the literature for the temperature onset of defect association in ceria or zirconia $(\approx300-400^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ ,[56,83,96] which generally refers to dipole formation. In preparing our specimens, the different thermal/atmosphere treatments used to achieve different levels of stoichiometry were likely to have quenched in different levels of neutral tripoles and charged dipoles. For the sample annealed at rather elevated temperatures ( $\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\delta\approx0.042)$ , this could result in a rather minimal formation of neutral tripoles, and thus a high pre-exponential factor due to a highly disordered $\mathrm{Pr}^{3+}$ sublattice. In contrast, the samples held longer at lower temperatures under oxidizing conditions ( $\delta\approx0.032$ and 0.035) could lead to the partial formation of neutral tripoles resulting in narrower conduction pathways for mobile oxygen vacancies. We provide a schematic representation of the ordering concept for a fixed non-stoichiometry in Figure 7. The different stoichiometries achieved by thermal annealing and the impact on the small polaron mobility would likely shift the transition temperature for neutral tripole formation. Ultimately, the presence of tripoles (the maximum possible amount of tripoles vs dipoles) in the lattice will reflect the conditions under which the film was grown and the physical distribution of $\operatorname*{Pr}$ ion in proximity of each other. Further experimental and modeling efforts are required before a more exact explanation for the source of the steep change in mobility pre-factor with different annealing times/temperatures and increasing defect concentration can be established.

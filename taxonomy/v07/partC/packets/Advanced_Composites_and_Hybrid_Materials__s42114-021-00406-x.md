The CNTs are distributed in the higher viscous PLA phase in the frst mixing stage, and then CNTs can be transferred from PLA to PCL phase with the increase of blending time from 2 to $8 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ . The migration of CNTs can change the viscosity ratio between two polymer phases and decrease the co-continuous phase size because the shear fow of the two phases tends to be more symmetrical, which favors the deformation of the droplet [42]. The large viscosity diference between two phases and the cation- ${ \mathbf { \nabla } } \cdot { \pi } / { \pi } { - \pi }$ interactions between ILs and CNTs can induce the migration of CNTs to the interface and PCL phase [54]. The interfacial localization of the PILmodifed CNTs could prevent the coalescence of the PLA phase and decrease the co-continuous phase size.  CNTs were thermodynamically driven from PLA to PCL phase because CNTs were premixed with PLA. As shown in Fig. 2, some CNTs can be seen in the FE-SEM micrographs of PLA/PCL/8CNTs- $. 2 \mathrm { m }$ because some CNTs already begun to migrate from PLA to PCL phase and reached the phase interphase. With the increase of mixing time from 2 to $8 \mathrm { m i n }$ , more and more CNTs got across the phase boundaries and distributed in the PCL phase. - Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): For the composites containing carbon-derived materials such as CNTs, conductive loss plays an essential role in enhancing the EMI shielding properties, indicating the importance of the formed impeccable electron channels (electrical conduction network) in order to realize the improvement of electrical conductivity [60–62]. Figure 6 displays the electrical conductivity of PLA/PCL-based composites. As is shown in Fig. 6a, the electrical conductivity of the PLA/PCL/8CNT composites is decreased with increasing the blending time. With the increase of mixing time from 2 to $8 \mathrm { \ m i n }$ , more and more CNTs in PLA/ PCL/8CNTs got across the phase boundaries and were distributed in the PCL phase. The electrical conductivity of the PLA/PCL/4PIL/8CNT composites is frstly increased and then decreased with the increase of blending time because the CNTs migrated from PLA phase to PLA/PCL phase interface as the blending time increased from 2 to $8 \mathrm { \ m i n }$ . The selective localization of CNTs at the interphase signifcantly improves the electron transport and a more efective conductive network is formed at the interface for PLA/ PCL/8CNT- $2 \mathrm { m }$ and PLA/PCL/4PIL/8CNT-6 m composites [63]. Further extending the blending time, more CNTs tend to migrate into the low-viscosity PCL phase, CNTs at the interface are decreased, and the conductive network is destroyed, resulting in a decrease of conductivity. The electrical conductivity of the PLA/PCL/4PIL/8CNT composites is signifcantly higher than that of PLA/PCL/8CNT composites. This is mainly attributed to the enhanced compatibilization efect of PILs and the role of ionic liquid units in dispersing the CNTs. PIL expands the PLA/PCL phase interface, so more CNTs migrate to the phase interface, and make a perfect conductive network [59]. As shown in Fig. 6b, when CNTs are increased from 4 to $6 \mathrm { w t } \%$ , the conductivity of PLA/PCL/CNT-6 composite is sharply improved, indicating the percolation threshold of the composites. The increase in the conductivity of PLA/PCL/CNT-6 composite is very marginal when CNTs are increased from 2 to $4 ~ \mathrm { w t } \%$ . The conductivity of the PLA/PCL/CNT-6 composites with $6 \mathrm { w t } \%$ CNTs is increased from 0.046 to $2 . 4 8 \ : \mathrm { S / m }$ by the incorporation of PIL, respectively. The threshold of PLA/PCL/4PIL/ CNTs-6 is less than that of PLA/PCL/CNT-6 composites. The achievement of selective distribution of CNTs with well electrical conductivity and aspect ratio in PLA/PCL/4PIL/ CNTs-6 can attain the electrical percolating with a lower CNT content [7, 32, 64]. As the blending time is increased, the EMI shielding performance of the PLA/PCL/4PIL/8CNT composites is gradually increased with increasing the blending time, reaches the maximum at blending time $6 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ , and is then decreased at the blending time 8 min. The most perfect conductive network appears at $6 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ .

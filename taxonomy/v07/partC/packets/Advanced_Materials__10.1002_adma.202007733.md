- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Figure  1a illustrates the synthesis of nanoporous intermetallic $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ by a two-step etching method. First, the $\mathrm{Pd}_{20}\mathrm{Bi}_{80}$ precursor alloy was designed, which was composed of two phases with different compositions and crystal structures: the trigonal metallic Bi and the monoclinic intermetallic $\mathrm{PdBi}_{2}$ (Figure S1, Supporting Information), as identified by X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis (Figure S2a, Supporting Information). Using a selective phase dissolution approach,[15] the $\mathrm{PdBi}_{2}$ phase with a nanoporous structure (Figure S3, Supporting Information) could be created through selectively etching the active Bi phase. Subsequently, intermetallic $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ with a uniform nanoporous structure (Figure  1b) was prepared from the conversion of np- $\mathrm{PdBi}_{2}$ through the electrochemical etching process to remove Bi at room temperature (Figure S4, Supporting Information),[16] which is also confirmed by XRD (Figure S2b,  Supporting Information) and transmission electron micro­ scopy (TEM, Figure S5, Supporting Information). Figure  1c shows the atomic-resolution high-angle annular dark-field scanning transmission electron microscopy (HAADF–STEM) image of one intermetallic $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ ligament, along with the corresponding fast Fourier transform (FFT) pattern. The clear lattice fringes with $d$ -spacings of 0.23 and $0.22{\mathrm{nm}}$ are highlighted in Figure  1c, which correspond to the (400) and (012) crystal planes of the orthorhombic $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ (Figure 1d), respectively. The STEM–energy-dispersive spectrometry (STEM–EDS) mapping image (Figure  1e) reveals the uniform distribution of Pd and Bi element throughout the whole nanoporous networks. These results indicate the reconstruction of nanoporous intermetallic $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ after complete phase conversion from $\mathrm{PdBi}_{2}$ via the electrochemical etching process. The resulting np- $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ shows a large Brunauer–Emmett–Teller surface area of $21.17\mathrm{m}^{2}\mathrm{g}^{-1}$ with an average nanopore size of ${\approx}25\$ nm using the Barrett– Joyner–Halenda method (Figure S6, Supporting Information), in agreement with the TEM observation. The evolution of surface chemical state of the Pd–Bi intermetallic compound before and after the electrochemical etching was characterized by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). As shown in Figure 1f, it can be observed that the Pd 3d spectra show two distinct peaks which are assigned to the Pd $3\mathrm{d}_{5/2}$ and Pd $3\mathrm{d}_{3/2}$ orbit levels. Each peak can be further split into two doublets, which are assigned to $\mathrm{Pd}^{0}$ and $\mathrm{Pd^{2+}}$ chemical valence states. Compared with the $3\mathrm{d}_{5/2}$ and Pd $3\mathrm{d}_{3/2}$ orbit levels of the metallic Pd (334.9 and $340.2~\mathrm{eV})$ ,[17] the Pd peaks of np- $\mathrm{{\cdotPdBi}}_{2}$ and np- $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ show a significant positive shift originated from the Bi–Pd intermetallic interaction. It is clearly displayed that the majority of the Pd in np- $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ is mainly metallic state. Compared with np- $\mathrm{PdBi}_{2}$ , the $\mathrm{Pd^{0}}3\mathrm{d_{3/2}}$ binding energy of np- $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ has a negative shift from 335.9 to $335.8~\mathrm{eV}$ and $3\mathrm{d}_{5/2}$ from 341.3 to $341.1~\mathrm{eV},$ which is ascribed to the weakened electronic interaction between Pd and Bi atoms as the $\mathrm{Pd/Bi}$ ratio increases. The XPS spectra of Bi 4f reveal two distinct peaks of Bi $4\mathrm{f}_{7/4}$ and Bi $4\mathrm{f}_{5/4}$ orbit levels (Figure 1g), respectively. Compared with $\mathrm{np}{\cdot}\mathrm{PdBi}_{2}$ , the negative shift of $\mathrm{Bi^{0}}$ 4f orbit levels in np- $\mathrm{{\cdotPd}}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}$ further confirms the weakened Bi–Pd coupling of intermetallic interaction originated from the regulated Pd/Bi ratio. The $\mathrm{Bi^{3+}}$ peak at 159.1 and $164.4~\mathrm{eV}$ indicates that the surface of np- $\mathrm{{\cdotPd}}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}$ is slightly oxidized in the basic etching solution, which could be reduced during the reduction electrolysis. The Pd/Bi atomic ratio of np- $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ obtained from the XPS analysis is 3.16:1 after complete conversion via the electrochemical etching, which is well consistent with the result by the inductively coupled plasma-optical emission spectrometer (Table S1, Supporting Information).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The NRR performance of the different catalysts in $0.05\mathrm{~M~}$ $\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{SO}_{4}$ was evaluated in an H-cell separated by a proton exchanged membrane (Nafion 117) at room temperature and atmospheric pressure (Figure S7, Supporting Information). Ultrahigh purity of $\Nu_{2}$ gas $(99.999\%)$ was continuously purged into the cathodic chamber during electrochemical NRR process. Figure  3a shows the linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) curves of the catalysts in Ar- and $\Nu_{2}$ -saturated $0.05~\mathrm{~M~}$ $\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{SO}_{4}$ solution, respectively. The np- $\mathrm{{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}}$ electrode exhibits a significantly enhanced current response in the $\Nu_{2}$ -saturated electrolyte than in the Ar-saturated electrolyte, confirming the electrochemical conversion of $\Nu_{2}$ on the catalyst. Moreover, the np- $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ electrode shows an appropriate current response as compared with np- $\mathrm{{\cdotPdBi}}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{Pd}/\mathrm{C},$ revealing the intrinsic difference of catalytic property. The chronoamperometry spectra at each applied potential and UV–vis absorption spectra of the electrolytes after NRR for $2\mathrm{~h~}$ stained with indophenol indicator were collected to quantify the produced $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ on np- $\mathrm{{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}}$ , np- $\mathrm{{\cdotPdBi}}_{2}$ , and $\mathrm{Pd}/\mathrm{C}$ catalyst (Figures S8 and S9, Supporting Information). The average $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ yield rate and the corresponding FE at a series of potentials of np$\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ $\mathrm{np}{\cdot}\mathrm{PdBi}_{2}$ , and $\mathrm{Pd}/\mathrm{C}$ catalyst were measured (Figure  3b,c). Remarkably, the np- $\mathrm{{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}}$ group achieves a highest $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ yield rate of $60.75~\upmu\mathrm{g}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}~\mathrm{mg}_{\mathrm{cat}}{}^{-1}$ (average: $59.05\pm2.27\upmu\mathrm{g}\mathrm{h^{-1}m g_{c a t}^{-1}})$ and a largest FE of $22.23\%$ (average: $21.52\pm0.71\%)$ at $-0.2\:\mathrm{V}$ versus RHE, which are much higher than those of np- $\mathrm{PdBi}_{2}$ $(6.50\upmu\mathrm{g}\mathrm{h}^{-1}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{g}_{\mathrm{cat}}^{-1}$ , $18.56\%)$ and $\mathrm{Pd}/\mathrm{C}$ $(8.70~\upmu\mathrm{g}~\mathrm{h}^{-1}~\mathrm{mg}_{\mathrm{cat}}{}^{-1}$ , $0.14\%$ with the same condition, even surpassing the most reported electrocatalysts including Pd- and Bi-based (Table S3, Supporting Information).[18] The FEs of HER by np- $\mathrm{{\cdotPd}_{3}B i}$ electrode are quantitatively determined to be complementary with those of NRR (Figure  3d), demonstrating that np- $\mathrm{{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}}$ catalyst has a preferable selectivity toward $\Nu_{2}$ reduction and an effective suppression of hydrogen evolution. Furthermore, only $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ was obviously observed under all applied potentials, and no $\mathrm{N}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{4}$ by-product was detected in the experiment (Figures S10 and S11, Supporting Information), confirming a high selectivity of np- $\mathrm{{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}}$ for $\Nu_{2}$ fixation toward $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ . In addition, the sample solutions catalyzed by np- $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ at the open circuit potential and in the Ar atmosphere do not show the presence of $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ (Figure S12, Supporting Information), as the case of blank glass carbon electrode, revealing that the $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ product is produced by the voltage-driven reduction of $\Nu_{2}$ catalyzed by np- $\mathrm{{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}}$ , demonstrating the intrinsic NRR catalytic property. $\mathrm{^{15}N}$ $(99~\mathrm{at}\%^{15}\mathrm{N})$ ) isotope-labeling experiments were conducted to further confirm the N source of the obtained $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ . As shown in Figure 3e, only doublet peaks corresponding to $^{15}\mathrm{N}\mathrm{H}_{4}^{+}$ were observed in the $^1\mathrm{H}$ nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra, while no triple signal representing $^{14}\mathrm{N}\mathrm{H}_{4}^{+}$ was detected in the electrolyte after NRR at $-0.2\mathrm{~V~}$ versus RHE using $^{15}\mathrm{N}_{2}$ as the feeding gas, definitely confirming that the produced $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ origins from the feeding $\Nu_{2}$ stream during the process of NRR. Notably, the measured $^{15}\mathrm{N}\mathrm{H}_{3}$ yield rate and FE and those of $^{14}\mathrm{N}\mathrm{H}_{3}$ determined by the NMR signals (Figures S13 and S14, Supporting Information) match well with the above coloration method, indicating that $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ was entirely produced from the electroreduction of $\Nu_{2}$ catalyzed by the catalysts. Remarkably, the $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ product is produced by the voltage-driven reduction of $\Nu_{2}$ catalyzed by np- $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ , demonstrating the intrinsic efficient catalytic active sites in the intermetallic nanostructure. The stability of the np- $\mathrm{{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}}$ electrode was evaluated by consecutive recycling electrolysis and long-term chronoamperometry test at $-0.2\mathrm{~V~}$ versus RHE. As shown in Figure  3f, no obvious changes in the $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ yield rate and FE are observed during five consecutive recycling electrolysis, indicating the excellent repeatability of np$\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ for electrosynthesis of $\Nu_{2}$ to $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ at ambient conditions. Furthermore, the NRR process by np- $\mathrm{{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}}$ at $-0.2\mathrm{~V~}$ versus RHE shows a very stable in current response under continuous $\Nu_{2}$ gas steam for $35\mathrm{~h~}$ (Figure S15, Supporting Information). Moreover, no obvious variation of the $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ yield rate and FE is observed after long-term chronoamperometry test, indicating the excellent electrochemical durability of np- $\mathrm{{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}}$ toward ambient $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ synthesis. Significantly, the morphology and chemical composition could be well maintained after the long-term NRR electrolysis, as indicated in the scanning electron microscopy (SEM) image and XPS (Figure S16, Supporting Information), respectively. Interestingly, N signal is generated on np- $\mathrm{{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}}$ after the NRR, confirming the interaction between the active electrode and N species.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To identify the origin of the intrinsic catalytic activity of np- $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ , the operando XAS measurements of the np- $\mathrm{{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}}$ were performed in $\Nu_{2}$ -saturated $\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{SO}_{4}$ solution with a welldesigned cell.[19] Figure $^\mathrm{4a,b}$ shows the normalized operando Pd K-edge and Bi $\mathrm{L}_{3}$ -edge XANES spectra at different applied potentials, respectively. With the increase of the operated bias voltages from the open-circuit voltage to $-0.05\mathrm{~V~}$ versus RHE, the edges of both Pd K-edge and Bi $\mathrm{L}_{3}$ -edge show a downshift, suggesting the reduction of surface oxides and a lower unoccupied electronic state at the Fermi level. Furthermore, with increase of the operated bias voltages from $-0.05$ to $-0.1\mathrm{~V~}$ versus RHE, the absorption onset of the Pd K-edge shows no distinct change (Figure  4a), which is caused by its preferable H absorption, resulting in less affected electronic structure around Pd.[20] Meanwhile, the intensity of the white line peaks of the Bi $\mathrm{L}_{3}$ -edge XANES spectra (Figure  4b) shows obvious enhancement, which is attributed to the delocalization of the unpaired electron from Bi due to the interaction with $\mathrm{N}$ species during NRR.[21] According to molecular orbital theory, the interaction between the Bi atomic orbital and the $\mathrm{~N~}2\mathrm{p}$ orbital of the same main group would lead to the formation of a $_{\mathrm{N-N}}$ $\pi$ antibonding orbital and a decrease of the bond order.[22] With voltage bias, the voltage-driven electron transfer from the Bi atom to the $\mathrm{~N~}2\mathrm{p}$ orbital leads to the formation of $\mathrm{N}_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}\delta-}$ adsorption species, and the electron backdonation could effectively weaken the $\scriptstyle\mathrm{N}{\equiv}\mathrm{N}$ molecular bond, resulting in the activation of $\Nu_{2}$ and formation of $\mathrm{N-H}$ species.[23] These results are further confirmed by the corresponding FT-EXAFS spectra, as shown in Figure  4c,d. The FT-EXAFS spectra of Pd K-edge exhibit a very slight radial extension since EXAFS is not sensitive to light atoms like absorbed H (Figure 4c).[24] Correspondingly, the FTEXAFS spectra of the Bi $\mathrm{L}_{3}$ -edge show a significant extension radial extension of the Bi–Pd scattering as the applied potentials become more negative (Figure  4d), which is originated from the electron delocalization of Pd–Bi bonds to the absorbed N species during the NRR process. This viewpoint can be confirmed by DFT calculations. As shown in Figure  4e, the projected density of states (PDOS) of the Pd and Bi 3d orbitals of np- $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ and the $\mathrm{~N~}2\mathrm{p}$ orbital of the $\Nu_{2}$ bonded to the catalytic sites exhibit a significant hybridization near the Fermi level, and the $\mathrm{~N~}2\mathrm{p}$ orbital shows a vast majority overlap with the Bi 3d orbital according to the charge density difference calculation (Figure  4f). This observation confirms the role of Bi in $\Nu_{2}$ adsorption and further activation via electronic donation. According to the above analysis, we illustrate the NRR process on the np- $\mathrm{{.Pd}}_{3}\mathrm{{Bi}}$ active sites in Figure $4\mathrm{g}$ . The $\Nu_{2}$ molecule is preferentially adsorbed on Bi sites, while H is adsorbed on the surrounding Pd sites. Simultaneously, $\Nu_{2}$ is activated via electron delocalization from the Bi 3d orbitals to the $\mathrm{~N~}2\mathrm{p}$ orbitals, followed by hydrogenation with H on the Pd sites at the applied potentials.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): DFT calculations were further performed to understand the electrochemical NRR mechanism on the catalysts (see Computational Section in the Supporting Information for more details).[25] It is known that adequate $\Nu_{2}$ adsorption and activation on catalyst is the essential prerequisite for an efficient NRR process. Thus, $\Nu_{2}$ adsorption on different catalytic sites on catalysts were evaluated via the theoretical adsorption energy (Figure S20, Supporting Information). As shown in Figure  5a, the Bi sites are more preferable for $\Nu_{2}$ absorption than Pd sites. More importantly, the Bi sites of $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ $(\mathrm{Bi}@\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi})$ has an efficient $\Nu_{2}$ adsorption energy of $-0.143~\mathrm{eV},$ while the Pd sites of $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ $(\mathrm{Pd}@\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi})$ display an inferior $\Nu_{2}$ adsorption energy of −0.127  eV, suggesting more feasible $\Nu_{2}$ adsorption on Bi sites than Pd sites in $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ , which is in good agreement with the operando XAS analysis. Besides, the Gibbs free energies of the HER process on the catalysts were further calculated (Figure  5b). The free energy value is $-0.893~\mathrm{eV}$ for $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ catalyst, far away from the optimal value $\begin{array}{r}{(\Delta G_{*\mathrm{H}}=0\mathrm{eV})}\end{array}$ ), even lower than that of pure Pd catalyst, indicating that the introduction of Bi could suppress the hydrogen evolution. Furthermore, the PDOS calculations on the various catalytic sites in Figure S21 (Supporting Information) show that the Pd signal gradually shifts down away from the Fermi level as the Bi amount increases. Since Pd sites are preferable for H intermediate adsorption, the HER performance is well regulated by Bi. The d orbital with a suitable energy level relative to the Fermi level is regarded as a key factor for the electron interaction with $\Nu_{2}$ and activated N species. Significantly, the modulated electronic structure of Bi–Pd contributes to higher NRR activity and selectivity. In addition, the Gibbs free energy of NRR transition states of the distal path are calculated on the (400) crystal face of $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ , (311) crystal face of ${\mathrm{PdBi}}_{2}.$ and (111) crystal face of Pd (Figure S22, Supporting Information). As shown in Figure 5c, the formation of $\mathrm{*NNH}$ is a determining step during the formation of the first $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ molecule, which takes 0.197 and $0.878~\mathrm{eV}$ for $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ and Pd, respectively, indicating that $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ provides the coordination path with a lower energy gap for NRR. For the formation of the second $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ mole­ cule, the formation of $\ast\mathrm{NH}_{2}$ is the determining step for $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ , while the desorption of $\ast\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ is the determining step for Pd, confirming the different catalytic property of the two electrocatalysts. In addition, $\mathrm{PdBi}_{2}$ follows similar reaction pathway with $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ but higher energy gap, indicating the significance of a suitable $\mathrm{Pd}/\mathrm{Bi}$ ratio. The above results suggest that the NRR process is the most favorable to occur on the Bi sites of $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ and the intermetallic interaction between Pd and Bi atoms of $\mathrm{Pd}_{3}\mathrm{Bi}$ not only reduces the free energy of transition state, but also optimizes the free energy of H intermediate to a confined HER catalytic activity. The excellent NRR performance origins from the facilitated $\Nu_{2}$ adsorption and activation and the reduced energy barrier of the rate limiting step after the optimization of Pd/Bi component.

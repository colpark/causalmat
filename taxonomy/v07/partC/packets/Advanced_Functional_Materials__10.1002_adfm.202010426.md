- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite was synthetized by the spin coating method using a solution precursor of MAI and $\mathrm{PbCl}_{2}$ in dimethylformamide (DMF) solvent. The precursor was spin-coated on quartz glass substrates, followed by thermal annealing in the glovebox at $100~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $60~\mathrm{min}$ (Figure 1a). It should be noted that the thermochromism of $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ is attributed to hydration and dehydration, so the reversible color change cannot be observed in the inert environment (glovebox). After the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite samples were removed from the glovebox to ambient environment, the thermochromism could be observed upon heating and cooling. To identify the proper concentration ratio of MAI and $\mathrm{PbCl}_{2}$ that can achieve remarkable thermochromism, a series of precursor solutions were made with the molar ratio of MAI and $\mathrm{PbCl}_{2}$ varied from 4:1 to 7:1. At the hot state $(60~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ , all these samples are dark reddish-brown. As the temperature is reduced, discoloration of the film occurs until it becomes transparent at the cold state $(25~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ . However, it is found that only the samples with the relative molar concentration of MAI to $\mathrm{PbCl}_{2}$ higher than 6:1 (i.e., $\mathrm{MAI{:}P b C l}_{2}=6.5{:}1$ and 7:1) can achieve complete transparency at the cold state, while other samples appear a dark grey (Figure S1, Supporting Information). X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements on the sample with 4: $1\mathrm{MAI}{:}\mathrm{PbCl}_{2}$ ratio at the cold state clearly shows the tetragonal structure of ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ , and yet the intensity of the ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ characteristic peak decreases with an increase of MAI concentration (Figure S2, Supporting Information). The ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ characteristic peak completely disappears when the $\mathsf{M A I}{\mathrm{:PbCl}}_{2}$ ratio of the precursor is higher than 6:1. The existence of ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite at the cold state leads to the dark grey appearance. At the same time, with the increase of MAI in the precursor, the strong MAI characterization peak can be detected when the $\mathsf{M A I}\{\mathsf{P b I}_{2}$ ratio reaches 7:1. Notably, for the mixing ratio of 6.5:1, no characterization peak relating to diffractions from the residual ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ and MAI can be observed, therefore, the mixing ratio of 6.5:1 of MAI and $\mathrm{PbCl}_{2}$ is chosen for further experiment. It should be noted that the reversible thermochromism can also be controlled by the ambient relative humidity (RH) without heating/ cooling process. Figure S3, Supporting Information exhibits an $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ sample which was placed in an environmentcontrolled chamber with the ambient temperature set at ${\approx}22{\ensuremath{^\circ}}\mathrm{C}$ and the RH was modulated from $30\%$ to $10\%$ . The results show that the sample changed from a transparent state to a reddishbrown state when the RH reduced to ${\approx}10\%$ . The modulation of humidity was then reversed (i.e., from $10\%$ to $30\%$ ) to perform the humidification process. The reddish-brown started to fade and the sample recovered its transparent state during the increase of RH to $30\%$ . This phenomenon indicates that water vapor plays an essential role in the thermochromic process of ${\mathrm{H}}{\cdot}{\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3-x}{\mathrm{Cl}}_{x}.$  To investigate the effect of water vapor on the reversible thermochromism, Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) measurements were conducted for the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite. Figure  1b shows the FTIR spectra of the novel $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite (synthesized by the precursor of MAI: $\mathrm{PbCl}_{2}=6.5{:}1\$ ) at both the hot state and cold state. It can be seen from the spectrum that a clear signature peak of $_{\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{O}}$ bond appears at around $3500~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ when the perovskite sample is at $25~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . When the sample is heated to $60~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , the $_{\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{O}}$ bond completely disappears, while the other chemical bonds remain the same. The $_{\mathrm{H}-\mathrm{O}}$ bond appears again when the sample is cooled back to $25~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . In addition, no color change can be observed in the glove box where the humidity level is low $(\le1\mathrm{ppm})$ . These results not only indicate the existence of condensed water at the clear state of ${\mathrm{H}}{\cdot}{\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3-x}{\mathrm{Cl}}_{x},$ but also prove that water functions as the essential driving force in the reversible thermochromism of $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite.  Moreover, the measurement of energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) was also conducted for the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ at the cold state, showing the atomic ratio of $\mathrm{{I}{:}C l:P b=}$ 5.98:0.40:1, proving that only a small amount of Cl exists in the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite film, and the atomic ratio of halide element $\mathrm{(Cl+I)}$ and lead is 6.38:1, which is close to the previously reported $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3}$ thermochromic perovskite $(\mathrm{MA}_{4}\mathrm{PbI}_{6}\cdot2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O})$ .[30,31] And, same as $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3}$ thermochromic perovskite, $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite also exhibits the temperature-dependent phase change in the heating and cooling loops, and their XRD patterns at the hot state are both shown in Figure  1c. For $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3}$ thermochromic perovskite, at the hot state, due to the release of water, the dihydrated $\mathrm{MA}_{4}\mathrm{PbI}_{6}\cdot2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ perovskite transforms to the cubic phase of the ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite (rhombus symbol in Figure  1c).[32] In Figure  1c, diffraction peaks other than the cubic phase of ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ can be attributed to the MAI (star symbols), indicating that the thermochromism follows the reaction of cubic phase perovskite, water, and MAI as in Equation (1). These peaks can also be observed in $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite patterns, indicating its similar behavior and resultant structure with $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAP}\mathrm{bI}_{3}$ thermochromic perovskite at the hot state. However, it should be noted that, the typical perovskite (110) peak that appeared at $14.36^{\circ}$ for $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3}$ thermochromic perovskite slightly shifted to $14.44^{\circ}$ for $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite (the inset of Figure 1c). According to Bragg’s law,  the increased $\theta$ value corresponds to the decreased d-spacing, correlating to the residual of Cl ions.[33] In summary, the above XRD analysis at the cold and hot states suggests that the $\mathrm{Cl^{-}}$ anion does not alter the crystalline structure too much in $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite, and only a small amount of chlorine (Cl) has been incorporated into the final product.  To further investigate the effect of Cl on $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite during the synthesizing process, a series of experiments were conducted including the fabrication observations and characterization results. First, during the annealing procedure in the fabrication process, it could be observed that the color of the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite thin film deposited on the glass substrate gradually changes from orange $(5\mathrm{min})$ to red $(30~\mathrm{min})$ , then to dark reddish-brown $(60{\mathrm{~min}})$ (Figure  1d). The color variation is an important indicator for the chemical reaction during the annealing process. To analyze the bulk concentration of Cl in these three stages, X-ray fluorescence  (XRF) was employed on samples annealed at $100~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for 5, 30, and $60\mathrm{min}$ , respectively. Figure  1e shows the characteristic X-ray peaks of Pb Mα $(2.34\mathrm{keV})$ , and Cl $\mathrm{K}\alpha$ $(2.62\mathrm{keV})$ from $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite thin films annealed at $100~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for different time durations. It can be clearly observed that the Cl $\mathtt{K}\alpha$ peak weakens as the annealing time increases, suggesting that $\mathrm{Cl}$ gradually escapes from the sample in the form of Cl-containing vapor during the annealing process. To confirm the loss of Cl through Cl vapor during annealing, a blank glass plate was attached to the inner bottom of the petri dish which was placed upside down above the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite samples during the annealing process as shown in Figure S4, Supporting Information. After the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite samples were fully annealed, a deposition of white residue could be observed on the blank glass. The composition of the white residue was analyzed by XRF, and the strong Cl peak is shown in Figure 1f. Note that no ${\mathrm{Pb}}$ and I X-rays can be detected from the glass. This is direct evidence showing the sublimation of Cl-containing vapor during the annealing process. Since the sublimation temperature of MACl is lower than that of MAI, $\mathrm{PbCl}_{2}$ , and $\mathrm{Pb}\mathrm{I}_{2}$ ,[34] it is possible that the Cl escapes in the form of MACl vapor. The above XRD and XRF analysis clearly suggest that the release of Cl-containing vapor plays an essential role in the formation of $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite film.

In order to characterize $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ and $\Delta T_{\mathrm{c}}$ , the transmittance at $550~\mathrm{nm}$ as a function of temperature for the heating and cooling cycles of the two TPSWs was measured under the ambient condition of $25~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and RH $50\%$ as shown in Figure 3a, and $T_{\mathrm{c,h}},$ $T_{\mathrm{c,c}}$ as well as $\Delta T_{\mathrm{c}}$ were calculated accordingly. As the largest transmittance difference of thermochromic perovskites is observed at the wavelength of $550~\mathrm{nm}$ (Figure  2c), which is also the peak in CIE photopic luminous efficiency of the human eye, the transmittance at the wavelength of $550~\mathrm{nm}$ was selected to monitor the thermochromism. It should be noted that to show the relative difference between the two TPSWs, the transmittance intensities shown in Figure 3a are just the relative values rather than absolute figures. The results reveal the $T_{\mathrm{c,h}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{c,c}}$ of the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3}$ TPSW to be 53.2 and $30.3^{\mathrm{~\scriptsize~o~C~}}$ respectively. However, the $T_{\mathrm{c,h}}$ of the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ TPSW shows a significant reduction of 8.3 to $44.9~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ compared to the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3}$ TPSW, and an increase of 5 to $35.3~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ in the cooling process. Most importantly, a much narrower $\Delta T_{\mathrm{c}}$ of $9.6~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ is achieved by the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ TPSW, which is significantly lower than that of $22.9~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ obtained by the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3}$ TPSW. Such a narrow width can be comparable with tungsten doped $\mathrm{VO}_{2}$ thermochromic smart windows (i.e., $9.4^{\mathrm{~\circ~}}\mathrm{C})$ .[36] It can also be seen from Figure 3a that the transmittance of $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ TPSW changes quite gradually with the temperature. In contrast, the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3}$ TPSW undergoes an abrupt and distinct transmittance conversion within $2^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ of temperature change. Indeed, the gradual change of transmittance is a desirable property for a smart window so that light transmittance can be gradually controlled with temperature, instead of only fully colored state and totally transparent state.[37]  As discussed above, the thermochromic effect of $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ is caused by the hydration and dehydration processes, implying that the humidity level of the ambient environment is crucial to the transition properties. To further investigate the humidity effect on transition properties of $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ TPSW, its $T_{\mathrm{c,h}},T_{\mathrm{c,c}},$ and $\Delta T_{\mathrm{c}}$ were measured under various RH conditions and the results are shown in Figure  3b. It can be seen that the $T_{\mathrm{c,h}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{c,c}}$ both increase with the increase in humidity of the environment, and the average $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ between the heating and cooling processes increases from 29.4 to $51.4^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . To explain that, during the heating cycle, the high humidity hinders the water molecules escaping from the thermochromic perovskite film, and hence a higher temperature is required to boost the dehydration process. However, in the cooling cycle under high humidity conditions, the water molecules can be more easily absorbed by the thermochromic perovskite even at the high temperature, so that at a $R H$ of $80\%$ , we observe a $T_{\mathrm{c,h}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{c,c}}$ of 54 and $46~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ respectively. When the $R H$ decreases to $25\%$ , the $T_{\mathrm{c,h}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{c,c}}$ of the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ TPSW are 35.5 and $22.3~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{:}}$ , respectively. The average $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ upon the heating and cooling loops can be as low as $29.4~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , which is lower than the $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ of other thermochromic smart windows, such as poly( $N\cdot$ -isopropylacrylmide) hydrogel (e.g., $T_{\mathrm{c}}=32^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ . Such a low $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ has never been achieved by thermochromic perovskite materials before, and this low $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ makes perovskite materials useful as practical smart window coatings. Regarding the effect on the hysteresis width, opposite to that on the transition temperatures, a negative correlation between the $\Delta T_{\mathrm{c}}$ of $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite and humidity was found as shown by the dash line in Figure  3b. With the increase of humidity level, the hysteresis width decreases from 13.1 to $7.7~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ as the increase of humidity hinders the dehydration while promoting moisture absorption.  As temperature fluctuates during the day, rapid response, namely a short transition time $\left(t_{\mathrm{c}}\right)$ , is crucial for thermochromic materials in practical applications. Even though there is no strict requirement, an acceptable transition time should be in several minutes for energy efficient smart windows.[10] The $t_{\mathrm{c}}$ in the heating and cooling cycles of the $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite was investigated under RH ranging from $25\%$ to $80\%$ , corresponding to the measurement of $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ . The results in Figure  3c reflect the humidity dependence of $t_{\mathrm{c}}$ in both heating and cooling processes. With the increase of RH, the $t_{\mathrm{c}}$ in the heating process drastically increases from 60 $R H=25\%)$ ) to $240\mathrm{~s~}$ $ R H=80\%)$ . However, the opposite trend can be observed in the cooling process, where the $t_{\mathrm{c}}$ can be reduced to even lower than $30~\mathrm{s}$ when the RH increases to $80\%$ . When the RH is $50\%$ , $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ shows shorter $t_{\mathrm{c}}$ than ${\mathrm{H}}{\cdot}{\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ TPSW in both heating (120 s vs 240 s) and cooling processes (60 s vs 90 s), proving the enhancement in transition properties.

To further quantitatively analyze the reversible dihydrated perovskite formation and dissociation process of $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite in relation to humidity, a thermodynamic model is developed. The thermodynamics of hydration and dehydration can be explained by the Clausius–Clapeyron (C–C) relation that reveals the relationship between pressure and temperature in the phase transition boundary (Equation (3)).[38,39] The solution of the C–C relation is shown in Equation (4).  According to the C–C relationship, the partial water pressure-transition temperature dependence of the dehydration and hydration process between $\mathrm{MA}_{4}\mathrm{PbI}_{6-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}{\cdot}2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ and ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3-x}{\mathrm{Cl}}_{x}$ can be drawn in the $\mathrm{C}{-}\mathrm{C}$ diagram as shown in Figure 4a, where the red line represents the dehydration process (heating process) and the blue line represents the hydration process (cooling process). The hysteresis width is shown as the area of pseudo-equilibrium between the hydration and dehydration. The thermochromism takes place at a certain temperature $T$ when another parameter (e.g., water vapor pressure $P)$ is fixed. The dotted line from point A to point B demonstrates an isobaric transition from the transparent state $\mathrm{(MA_{4}P b}$ $\mathrm{I}_{6-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}{\cdot}2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O})$ to the colored state $(\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x})$ , that requires a minimum temperature $T_{\mathrm{min}}$ , namely the transition temperature of the heating process. Similarly, the line from point C to point A illustrates the isothermal transition from the semitransparent state to the transparent state at room temperature $(T_{0})$ by controlling the water vapor pressure. To verify the model, the transition temperatures of $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite in the heating and cooling processes were measured at various partial water vapor pressure conditions as shown in Figure $^{4\mathrm{b}}$ . The measured data points in the heating and cooling processes can be well fitted by the linear regression model $\ln P=-6030.2\biggl({\frac{1}{T}}\biggr)+26.3$ (red solid line) and $\ln P=-4408.1\left({\frac{1}{T}}\right)+21.6$ (blue solid line), respectively. Through the model, the transition temperature of $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ thermochromic perovskite can be easily estimated. This model provides the feasibility to customize the transition temperature of TPSW. In practical applications, the thermochromic perovskite could be coated at the inner face of a conventional double-glazed window, and by modifying the humidity (the required humidity can be conveniently calculated based on the model) of the air gap in the double-glazed system, the customized transition temperature for the TPSW can be easily achieved. It should be noted that the model is also suitable for $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPb}\mathrm{I}_{3}$ thermochromic perovskite as shown in Figure 4b.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): After characterizing the optical and transition properties of $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ TPSW, the cycle performance was investigated to ensure its applicability in smart window applications, where the capability of performing stable thermochromism over substantial cycles of heating and cooling processes is expected. A cycle test was conducted on $\mathrm{H}{\cdot}\mathrm{MAPbI}_{3-x}\mathrm{Cl}_{x}$ TPSW with a total of 50 cooling and heating cycles. The heating and cooling operations were conducted in an air enthalpy testing laboratory $(\pm0.1^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and $\pm3\%$ RH of error) where the ambient temperature was set at a constant $25~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ with a RH of about $50\%$ . It should be noted that in smart window applications, extreme environmental conditions barely occur so that the window temperature is mostly within the normal range (i.e., from room temperature to around $50~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ ,[40] thus the heating and cooling temperatures in the cycle test were set as 60 and $25~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , respectively.

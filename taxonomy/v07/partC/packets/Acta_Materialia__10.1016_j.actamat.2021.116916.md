- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Fig. 1a shows the temperature dependence of the SVP of zinc and manganese [38]. The large SVP difference between zinc and manganese over a wide temperature window suggests that a series of convenient dealloying conditions can be employed to selectively remove Zn from the precursor alloys to form porous Mn. The dealloying kinetics of the two alloys were investigated at three temperatures of $653~\mathrm{~K~},~673~\mathrm{~K~}$ and $723\mathrm{~K~}$ and two pressures of $200\mathrm{Pa}$ and $2\times10^{-4}$ Pa. The dealloying experiments include three stages: heating at a constant ramping rate of $20~\mathrm{K/min}$ to designated temperatures; maintaining temperature for different periods of time; and fast cooling. The cross-sections of dealloyed samples were characterized by SEM to measure dealloying depths and the morphology of resulting porous microstructures. Fig. 1b shows the XRD spectra of $\mathrm{Mn}_{28}\mathrm{Zn}_{72}$ and $\mathtt{M n}_{12}Z\mathtt{n}_{88}$ samples after heating to $673\mathrm{~K~}$ at a dealloying pressure of $200\mathrm{Pa}$ and immediately cooled to room temperature. The heating and cooling cycle does not bring any structural changes of the two precursor alloys. Both $\mathrm{Mn}_{28}\mathrm{Zn}_{72}$ and $\mathtt{M n}_{12}Z\mathtt{n}_{88}$ have a hexagonal close-packed (HCP) structure. EDS mappings show that Mn and $Z\mathrm{n}$ are homogeneously distributed in the alloys (Fig. 1c and d). Obvious pores cannot be found on the sample surfaces after the cycle of temperature rise and cooling at the pressures of $200\mathrm{Pa}$ (Supplementary Fig. S2).  Fig. 2a and b are the SEM cross-sectional images and EDS chemical mappings taken from $\mathrm{Mn}_{28}\mathrm{Zn}_{72}$ and $\mathrm{Mn}_{12}\mathrm{Zn}_{88}$ samples, dealloyed at 673 K and 200 Pa for $180~\mathrm{min}$ and $60~\mathrm{min}$ , respectively. The dealloyed porous region of $\mathrm{Mn}_{28}\mathrm{Zn}_{72}$ has a relatively sharp interface with the solid precursor alloy compared to that of $\mathtt{M n}_{12}Z\mathtt{n}_{88}$ . Correspondingly, significant chemical composition changes can be detected in the porous regions, which are enriched with Mn. The residual concentrations of zinc in the porous regions of the $\mathrm{Mn}_{28}\mathrm{Zn}_{72}$ and $\mathrm{Mn}_{12}\mathrm{Zn}_{88}$ samples are $1.9\mathrm{at}\%$ and 4.6 at.%, respectively. XRD spectra of partially dealloyed samples show the appearance of body-centered cubic (BCC) Mn peaks (Fig. 2c), further demonstrating the formation of porous Mn by VPD. From both SEM-EDS chemical mappings and XRD spectra, an intermediate phase cannot be seen and it suggests the direct transition from the precursor HCP Zn-Mn alloys to the porous BCC Mn by selectively evaporating Zn. Obviously, the two dealloyed samples present divergent porous morphologies. $\mathtt{M n}_{12}Z\mathtt{n}_{88}$ has a spongy microstructure with a pore size around $500\textrm{-}700\textrm{n m}$ while the dealloyed $\mathrm{Mn}_{28}\mathrm{Zn}_{72}$ sample shows anisotropic porosity, with a long narrow channel structure and a pore diameter of about 300 - $500~\mathrm{nm}$ (Fig. 2a and b). Therefore, the precursor compositions apparently influence the dealloyed microstructures.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Dealloying kinetics was investigated by measuring the time dependence of dealloying depth at different pressures and temperatures. From the polished surfaces of partially dealloyed samples, the dealloying depth can be visualized and measured as shown in the inset of Fig. 3a. At a constant dealloying pressure of $200\mathrm{Pa}$ the dealloying depth increases with time and temperature (Fig. 3a). Log-log plots of this data are straight lines, showing a power law relationship between dealloying depth and time. The nearly constant slopes of ${\sim}0.4$ for both $\mathrm{Mn}_{28}\mathrm{Zn}_{72}$ and $\mathrm{Mn}_{12}\mathrm{Zn}_{88}$ alloys are independent of dealloying temperatures (Fig. 3b). The power-law relation indicates that the dealloying front migration is more likely dominated by a long-range diffusion process, rather than interface diffusion and reaction [21,36]. In spite of the identical powerlaw exponent of the two alloys at $200\mathrm{Pa}$ , the dealloying rates, defined by the dealloying depth per second, strongly depend on the dealloying temperatures and the compositions of precursor alloys (Fig. 3a and b). High dealloying temperatures promote faster porous layer growth. Moreover, the $\mathtt{M n}_{12}Z\mathtt{n}_{88}$ alloy with more Zn has an obviously higher dealloying rate than $\mathrm{Mn}_{28}\mathrm{Zn}_{72}$ under the same dealloying temperatures, which is in line with the relatively large pore size of dealloyed $\mathrm{Mn}_{12}\mathrm{Zn}_{88}$  Fig. 4a shows the relation between dealloying time and depth of $\mathrm{Mn}_{28}\mathrm{Zn}_{72}$ at $673\mathrm{~K~}$ under two different pressures of $200\mathrm{Pa}$ and $2~\times~10^{-4}$ Pa. The higher-vacuum condition exhibits a relatively higher dealloying rate across the entire dealloying period. In comparison with the low-vacuum power-law time-depth curve, the high-vacuum dealloying time-depth plot shows an apparent linear period up to the dealloying depth of ${\sim}29.5\mu\mathrm{m}$ at the dealloying time of ${\sim}7{,}200$ seconds. Because the linear period is much longer than the temperature ramping time of 1,200 seconds before dealloying, it cannot be attributed to any pore pre-formation reaction during sample heating. The existence of the linear period can also be identified from the log-log plot of dealloying time-depth curves (Fig. 4b), which presents a distinct discontinuity in the dealloying kinetics in the early stage of dealloying under the low dealloying pressure. After \~7,200 seconds the dealloying time-depth curves of both $200\mathrm{Pa}$ and $2\times10^{-4}$ Pa can be well fitted by a power law relation with the exponent of ${\sim}0.4$ (Fig. 4b). It appears that there is a transition of dealloying kinetics from linear to power-law relations with increasing dealloying depth under a low pressure of $2\times10^{-4}$ $\mathrm{Pa}$ in the initial stage of dealloying.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): SEI. Several advantages are involved in the design of the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ composite electrode. First, compared with 2D planar electrodes (Figure 1a), the 3D graphene skeleton can effectively reduce the current density and volume change locally during Li plating/stripping, thereby maintaining the structural stability of the overall electrode. Moreover, Li will not deposit on the surface of $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ because it is highly insulating. As a uniform SEI, the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layer can not only provide sufficient sites for Li nucleation as a result of the abundant $\mathrm{N}$ species, but also modulate Li deposition in the van der Waals gap between graphene and $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ through the structural nanopores. In addition, the amorphous nature of $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ ensures its high uniformity without fragile grain boundaries. These properties were verified by both experimental studies including transmission electron microscopy (TEM), X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), Auger electron spectroscopy (AES), and density functional theory (DFT) calculations. The use of the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ electrode enables conformal Li deposition (Figure 1b) at a high capacity of $5.0\mathrm{mAh}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ and a long-term cycling behavior (more than 500 cycles) with a high Coulombic efficiency (average $99.1\%)$ at a capacity of $1.0\mathrm{mAh~cm^{-2}}$ . Full cells using Li-3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ and $\mathrm{LiFePO_{4}}$ cathode (LFP, areal capacity of $3.5\mathrm{mAh}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ exhibit stable cycling stability (over 180 cycles) with lean electrolyte $(6.4~\upmu\mathrm{L}~\mathrm{mAh}^{-1})$ and limited Li excess (0.43-fold excess of Li).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The role of $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ and the location of Li deposition were first investigated by using a 2D model. Graphene and $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ were sequentially grown on the surface of the ${\mathrm{Cu}}$ foil (denoted as $\mathrm{g}{-\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{Cu}})$ using chemical vapor deposition (CVD) method. Graphene grown on the ${\mathrm{Cu}}$ foil (denoted as $\mathrm{G/Cu})$ was also synthesized as a comparison sample. The disorder-induced Raman D peak located at $\approx1350~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ is very weak, indicating the good quality of graphene grown the Cu foil (blue line in Figure S1b, Supporting Information). The G peak located at $\approx1580~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ is caused by the in-plane vibration of $\operatorname{sp}^{2}$ -bonded carbon atoms and the 2D peak is associated with the secondorder zone boundary phonon mode for graphene.[46] The full width at half maximum (FWHM) of 2D peak is broadened and its intensity is lower than that of the G peak, indicating the presence of 2–3 layers of graphene. The advantages of graphene lie in its good conductivity, high specific surface area and light weight. As shown in Figure S1a (Supporting Information), when the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layers were covered on the surface of the $\mathtt{G/C u}$ , the color of the Cu foil changed significantly and the baseline of the Raman spectrum became uneven (red line in Figure S1b, Supporting Information), which can be attributed to the photoluminescence and poor crystallinity of $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layers (Figure S2, Supporting Information).[47,48] Moreover, the results of attenuated-total-reflectance Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (ATR-FTIR; Figure S3, Supporting Information) and XPS (Figure S4, Supporting Information) further verified the successful coating of the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layers on surface of the $\mathtt{G/C u}$ . Charge density differences diagrams based on density functional theory (DFT) calculations (Figure 2a and Figure S5a, Supporting Information) show that electrons were rearranged in the $\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}/\mathrm{G}$ interface. Electrons of the graphene accumulate under the triazine units and deplete at the structural defects (nanopores) of $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layers. Density of states (Figure S5b, Supporting Information) indicate that compared with bare $\mathsf{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4},$ the electron transfer rate at the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}/\mathrm{G}$ interface is significantly improved. The valence and conduction bands on the left and right sides of the Fermi level are occupied by electrons of graphene and $\mathsf{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4},$ respectively. Consequently, in the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}/\mathrm{G}$ heterostructures, electrons $_{0.12e}$ according to the Bader analysis) can transfer from the graphene to $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layers. The electron rearrangement and improved electronic conduction can facilitate stable Li deposition at the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}/\mathrm{G}$ interface. Since electrons can be quickly transferred through graphene, the electron accumulation can be effectively avoided. Compared with the large structural fluctuation of $1.29\textup{\AA}$ in bare $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ after one Li atom adsorbed (Figure S6a,b, Supporting Information), the structural fluctuation of $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}/\mathrm{G}$ heterointerface is only $0.09\mathrm{~\AA~}$ (Figure S6c,d, Supporting Information), indicating the important role of the heterointerface formation in improving the structural stability of $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layers. As shown in Figure S7 (Supporting Information), six different adsorption sites for Li adsorption were considered. The calculation results demonstrate that the Li adsorption energy of these adsorption sites are all higher than the cohesive energy of lithium atoms $(1.64~\mathrm{eV})$ , indicating the lithiophilic nature of the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}/\mathrm{G}$ heterointerface. Moreover, according to the DFT simulations, when the number of deposited Li atoms is less than 4, negative free energy barrier $\ensuremath{\left(\Delta G_{\mathrm{nLiSurface}}<0\right.}$ , $n=1–4)$ indicates that the Li atoms are preferentially deposited at the N-atom hexagonal ring centers (denoted as the hex sites) of the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layer which possesses the highest Li adsorption energy (Figure S8a–d, Supporting Information). However, when the number of deposited Li atom further increases, the system shows a more negative free energy barrier when Li atom depositing at the van der Waals gap between $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}/\mathrm{G}$ compared with the surficial plating $(\triangle G_{\mathrm{5LiSurface}}>0$ , Figure 2c and Figure S8e, Supporting Information). The subsequently added Li atoms (up to eight Li atoms) are plated in the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}/\mathrm{G}$ interface and the interface structure remains intact (Figure  2b,c and Figure $\mathrm{S8f{-i}}$ , Supporting Information).  Both XPS and AES depth profiles with argon (Ar) sputtering were performed to obtain detailed information about the SEI and the Li deposition location. After depositing $0.5~\mathrm{mAh~cm^{-2}}$ of Li at $0.5\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , severely damaged SEI and a large amount of lithium dendrites can be observed on the $\mathtt{G/C u}$ electrode (Figure $2\mathrm{f}$ and Figure S9a–d, Supporting Information). In sharp contrast, homogeneous and intact SEI was formed on the ${\mathrm{g.C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/{\mathrm{G}}/{\mathrm{Cu}}$ electrode with Li uniformly deposited rather than forming disordered Li dendrites (Figure  2d and Figure S9e–h, Supporting Information). Figure  2e shows the atomic ratios of selected elements on the ${\mathrm{g.C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/{\mathrm{G}}/{\mathrm{Cu}}$ electrode (after depositing $0.5\mathrm{mAh~cm^{-2}}$ of Li at $0.5\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}.$ ) at different XPS depths. With the depth profiling from the initial stage to sputtering for 90 s, N content increased from 8.91 to $17.10~\mathrm{at\%}$ . Detailed and fitted XPS N 1s spectra (Figure S10, Supporting Information) show that the ${\mathrm{N}}{-}({\mathrm{C}})_{3}$ bond $(399.8~\mathrm{eV})$ and $\mathrm{C-N}{=}\mathrm{C}$ bond $(398.6~\mathrm{eV})$ are the main peaks, indicating that the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layer rather than its reaction product with Li is involved in the formation of SEI. Further increasing the sputtering time (from 90 to 180 s), the content of C and N decreased rapidly, and the content of Li increased, indicating that Li metal was uniformly deposited under the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layer. AES depth profiles exhibit the same trend (shown in Figure S11a,b, Supporting Information). In contrast, for $\mathtt{G/C u}$ electrode, no N signal was detected throughout the XPS (Figure  2g) and AES (Figure S11c,d, Supporting Information) depth profiles. In addition, higher Li, F and O content was observed on the surface of the SEI, which is related to the severe electrolyte decomposition and formation of Li dendrites. These results suggest that $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layer acts as an artificial SEI with high homogeneity to guide uniform Li deposition between graphene and $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ .

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The absence of Li dendrites on 2D ${\mathrm{g.C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/{\mathrm{G}}/{\mathrm{Cu}}$ electrodes suggests the uniform Li deposition within the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}/\mathrm{G}$ interface. As shown in Figure 3a, 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ was synthesized through the process of 3D self-assembly followed by an in situ calcination (see Experimental Section, Supporting Information for more details). During the 3D self-assembly process, dicyandiamide can be uniformly distributed on the surface of graphene oxide (GO) due to the interaction between the oxygen-containing functional group and the amine group.[49] After stirring at $80~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $2\textrm{h}$ , zeta potential of dicyandiamideGO solution changed from $-23.7\mathrm{mV}$ (Figure S12a, Supporting Information) to $-14.6\mathrm{mV}$ (Figure S12b, Supporting Information), indicating the thickening of the GO nanosheets due to the adsorption of dicyandiamide molecules. The results of scanning electron microscopy (SEM; Figure S13, Supporting Information) and energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS,  Figure S14, Supporting Information) further verified that the dicyandiamide was uniformly coated on the surface of 3D GO (denoted as 3D dicyandiamide-GO-dicyandiamide) after freeze-drying. After calcination at a proper temperature, dicyandiamide in situ decomposes into $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layers on the surface of reduced GO to form the $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ sandwiched structure. The appearance of a characteristic peak at $12^{\circ}$ (corresponding to the (100) planes of $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4})$ and the broadening and shifting of the characteristic peak of (002) planes compared to the single component prove the formation of the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ composite. SEM investigations of the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ composite reveal a fluffy and porous structure with abundant wrinkles forming irregularly shaped pores (Figure 3c). The pore wall was composed of $\mathrm{g.C_{3}N_{4}/G/g.}$ ${\mathrm{C}}_{3}{\mathrm{N}}_{4}$ multi-layer nanosheets (Figure  3d). Numerous wrinkles and pores endow the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ composite a high pore volume of $3.23~\mathrm{cm}^{3}~\mathrm{g}^{-1}$ and a large specific surface area of $241.73~\mathrm{m}^{2}~\mathrm{g}^{-1}$ , which can therefore accommodate the large volume change during Li plating/stripping and reduce the local current density. It is worth noting that the porous structure of the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ composite can be well maintained even after coating on ${\mathrm{Cu}}$ foil as a Li host (Figure S15, Supporting Information). We next probed the nanostructure of the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ composite using TEM characterization. Compared with bare graphene (Figure S16a–d, Supporting Information), the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ composite exhibits a sandwiched structure with increased thickness (Figure  3e and Figure S16e–h, Supporting Information).  The $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layers coated on the surface of the graphene have a homogeneous and amorphous structure which is evidenced by the high-resolution TEM (HR-TEM) image (inset of Figure  3e). Based on EDS analysis, the nitrogen content of the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ composite is $19.24\mathrm{wt}\%$ (shown in Figure $\mathtt{S17c}$ , Supporting Information), consistent with the XPS result $(17.61~\mathrm{wt\%}$ , Figure  3h and Figure S17b, Supporting Information). Moreover, The EDS elemental mapping based on the scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) image (Figure S17d–g, Supporting Information) clearly shows the uniform distributions of C and N. High-resolution C 1s XPS spectrum of the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ composite shown in inset in Figure  3h contains $\mathrm{C}{-}\mathrm{C}$ peak (284.7  eV) from the graphene, $\mathsf{C}\mathrm{-N}$ peak (286.1  eV), and $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{N}}-\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{N}$ (288.1  eV) peak from the ${\mathrm{C}}_{3}{\mathrm{N}}_{4}$ . - Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Li plating and stripping processes on Li metal host determine the cycling stability and capacity utilization. SEM characterizations were utilized to study the morphology evolution of Li plating/stripping within the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ and contrast electrodes (bare 3D graphene electrodes). Cross-section images show that as Li plating time increases (ranging from 1 to 6 hours at $0.5\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2})$ ), Li metal is uniformly and conformally deposited for the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ electrodes, leading to a gradual increase in the thickness of the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}/\mathrm{G}$ flakes and a more compact structure (Figure 4a–d and Figure S19, Supporting Information). Moreover, the surface of the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ electrode remains smooth and dense throughout the Li deposition process, indicating the effect of artificial SEI (Figure  4e–h). In sharp contrast, a rough and porous surface state (corresponding to the unstable SEI layer) and dendritic Li deposition can be observed on the 3D graphene electrodes (Figure S20, Supporting Information). Encouragingly, even after 50 cycles of plating and stripping (areal capacity of $3.0\mathrm{mAh~cm^{-2}}$ at a current density of $1.0\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2},$ ), the surface of 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ electrode remains smooth and homogeneous, indicating a rather good SEI stability (Figure S21a,b, Supporting Information). In contrast, the surface of cycled 3D graphene electrode becomes porous and very rough with dead Li scattered on it (Figure S21c,d, Supporting Information), which can also be reflected by digital photos (inset of Figure S21a,c, Supporting Information).  Significant differences in Li deposition morphology on the two electrodes (3D graphene and 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot}\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot}\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4})$ prompted us to further explore the important role of the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layer in guiding the deposition of metallic lithium. Since Li metal and its SEI are chemically reactive and sensitive to electron-beam irradiation,[6,7] detailed structure of 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ and 3D graphene after $1.0\mathrm{mAh~cm}^{-2}$ of Li deposition was first characterized using the cryo-TEM technique. As shown in Figure  4i the surface of the electrode exhibits a uniform and smooth morphology and no large Li agglomeration or structural fluctuations of SEI (porous SEI) can be observed. High-resolution images (Figure S22, Supporting Information) show an amorphous structure with a high homogeneity, which is consistent with the structure of the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layers before Li deposition (inset of Figure  3e). This is direct evidence that the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layer is on the surface of lithium and worked as artificial SEI. A high N content $(13.7\mathrm{wt}\%)$ shown in EDS spectrum (Figure S23b, Supporting Information) further verifies the existence of $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ in SEI. In the elemental mapping images (Figure $\mathrm{S}23\mathrm{d}{-}\mathrm{h}$ , Supporting Information) based on the STEM image (Figure S23c, Supporting Information), it can be identified that main constituent elements (C, N, O, and F) of SEI are uniformly distributed on the surface of the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot}\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot}\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4},$ confirming the high compositional uniformity of SEI and the evenly Li deposition. High-resolution cryo-TEM image (Figure  4j) was further captured in the squared region of Figure 4i (edge position of electrode). The crystalline metallic Li structure based on the (110) lattice plane distance of body-centered cubic Li (corresponding Fourier transform (FFT) pattern shown inset of Figure  4j is identified due to the thin thickness of the $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ layer at the edges, confirming that the darker contrast of the nanosheets is caused by the interfacial deposition of a uniform lithium layer. In contrast, random growth of agglomerated Li and dendritic Li can be observed on the 3D graphene electrode (Figure 4k). High-resolution image (inset of Figure 4k) shows that the SEI of 3D graphene electrode is inhomogeneous with a porous nanostructure. EDS images (Figure S24c–e, Supporting Information) based on the STEM image (Figure $\mathrm{S}24\mathrm{a}$ , Supporting  Information) show that fluorine (F) element accumulates at the areas where Li deposition is agglomerated and no signal of N can be detected (Figure S24b, Supporting Information). Figure 4l shows the KLL AES of nitrogen (N) element of both 3D graphene (blue line) and 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ (red line) electrodes with $1.0\mathrm{mAh~cm^{-2}}$ of Li plated at $1.0\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ after 20 cycles. SEM images corresponding to the spectrum collection position are also shown in Figure S25 (Supporting Information). Consistent with the results of EDS analyses, apparent N signal can be observed for the 3D $\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}/\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{g}{\cdot\mathrm{C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}}$ electrode, while no $\mathrm{N}$ signal is shown for 3D graphene electrode.

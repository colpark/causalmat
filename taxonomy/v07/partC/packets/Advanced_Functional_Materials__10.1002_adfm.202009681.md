- caption: Figure 1. Temperature dependence of the a) electrical conductivity, b) Seebeck coefficient, c) power factor, d) total thermal conductivity, e) lattice plus bipolar thermal conductivity, and f) ZT values for $\mathsf{B i}_{0.45}\mathsf{S b}_{1.55}\mathsf{T e}_{3.2}$ samples with different numbers of SPS cycles.
- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Overall TE properties have been gradually enhanced with the increase of the number of SPS cycles. Figure 1 presents the TE transport properties of $\mathrm{Bi}_{0.45}\mathrm{Sb}_{1.55}\mathrm{Te}_{3.2}$ samples subjected to different SPS cycles. Samples subjected to 1–5 SPS cycles are denoted as samples 1–5C, respectively. It was found that the $(\sigma)$ increased significantly with the increase of the number of cycles, and tended to saturate after four cycles, as shown in Figure  1a. Notably, the improvement of $\sigma$ at low temperatures was more pronounced. For all samples, $\sigma$ monotonically decreased with the increase of the temperature, indicating the degeneration of semiconductor behavior. The (S) is depicted in Figure  1b, and exhibited a first increasing and then decreasing trend with the increase of the temperature. As the number of cycles increased, the peak value of S decreased and moved to higher temperatures. Generally, as the number of cycles increased, S decreased in the low-temperature range and increased at high temperatures. As a consequence, compared with the increments of $\sigma,$ the decrements of S were found to be proportionately lower in the low-temperature range, leading to a significant improvement of PF with the increase in the number of cycles (Figure  1c). Correspondingly, the total thermal conductivity was increased due to the increased $\sigma,$ but the lattice thermal conductivity $\left(\kappa_{\mathrm{L}}\right)$ was reduced (see  Figure 1d,e); the reasons for this will be discussed later with microstructure analysis and Debye model. As presented in Figure 1f, the $Z T$ values gradually increased with the increase of the number of cycles for samples 1–4C. A maximum ZT value of ${\approx}1.4$ at $348\mathrm{~K~}$ was achieved for sample 4C, presenting an increase of $25\%$ as compared to sample 1C.

- caption: Figure 2. Temperature dependence of the a) Hall carrier concentration, b) Hall mobility, and c) weighted mobility for $\mathsf{B i}_{0.45}\mathsf{S b}_{1.55}\mathsf{T e}_{3.2}$ samples with different numbers of SPS cycles; d) the weighted mobility values at $308~\mathsf{K}_{1}$ the charge carrier concentration dependence of the e) charge carrier mobility and f) ZT value prediction with the experimental values at $308~\mathsf{K}$ .
- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To explore the reasons for the improved electrical properties, the carrier concentration and mobility were measured and analyzed. Figures  2a and  2b respectively present the temperature dependence of the Hall carrier concentration $(n_{\mathrm{H}})$ and mobility $(\mu_{\mathrm{H}})$ for the samples with different numbers of cycles. The charge carrier mobility was found to increase with the increase of the cyclic sintering times, which can be due to the enlarged grain size. The carrier concentration was found to increase with the increase of the temperature due to intrinsic excitation. Nevertheless, the onset of the intrinsic excitation was pushed to elevated temperatures after more than three cycles, as the minority charge carriers were suppressed by increased majority charge carriers. The reason for the increase of the carrier concentration with the number of cycles can be attributed to the decrease of $\mathrm{V_{Te}^{\cdot\cdot}}$ or the increase of $\mathrm{Bi^{\prime}_{T e}}$ antisite defect during cyclic liquid phase sintering, which contributed extra holes to the matrix. Interestingly, despite the general increasing trend of the carrier concentration with the number of cycles, particularly the increase from two to three cycles, the charge carrier mobility was found to increase with the increase of the number of cycles; usually, the charge carrier mobility is reduced with the increase of the charge carrier concentration. The simultaneous increase of $\mu_{\mathrm{H}}$ and $n_{\mathrm{H}}$ indicates that the sample quality for charge carrier transport was indeed improved due to the cyclic liquid-phase-aided SPS process, and was directly correlated with the significant increase of electrical performance.  Herein, an effective mass model is used to evaluate the charge carrier transport property (equations are provided in the Supporting Information).[39–41] According to the fitted curve (Pisarenko curve) at $308\mathrm{~K~}$ presented in Figure S1, Supporting Information, the DOS effective mass $(m^{*})$ for all the samples was about ${\approx}1.4m_{\mathrm{e}},$ demonstrating that the band structure is independent of the excess Te and the number of SPS cycles. By applying an analytic equation (Equation (S6), Supporting Information), the weighted mobility $(\mu_{\mathrm{W}})$ was calculated from the measured values of S and $\sigma_{\cdot}^{[41]}$ As shown in Figure 2c,d, $\mu_{\mathbb{V}}$ was found to increase with the increase of the number of cycles, indicating that the scattering of the carrier was weakened after cycling; at $308~\mathrm{K},$ its value for sample 1C was $432~\mathrm{cm}^{2}\cdot\mathrm{V}^{-1}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ . After four cycles, it increased to a relatively high value of $548~\mathrm{cm}^{2}\cdot\mathrm{V}^{-1}~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ . Furthermore, two corresponding nondegenerate limits of drift mobility $\mu_{0}$ , namely 260 and $330\mathrm{cm}^{2}\cdot\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ $\left(\mu_{\mathbb{W}}=\mu_{0}{\left(\frac{m^{*}}{m_{e}}\right)}^{3}\right)$ were used for the prediction of the dependence of charge carrier mobility and ZT value on charge carrier concentration at $308~\mathrm{K},$ as shown in Figure 2e,f. Benefitting from the improved $\mu_{\mathbb{V}}$ (or $\mu_{0}\dot{}$ ) values, as well as the decreasing value of $\kappa_{\mathrm{L}}$ , the $Z T$ value was enhanced from the dashed grey line to the solid purple line. Such results indicate the effective improvement of the sample quality for charge carrier transport, which is likely related to the optimized microstructure and reduced defects.

- caption: Figure 3. Microstructure of the 1C and 4C samples in a) the EBSD inverse pole figures with the grain size distribution and b) BSE mode of SEM.
- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The improved charge carrier transport quality of the samples can be understood from the perspective of the grain growth as well as the improved homogeneous microstructure.[42] According to the electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) results presented in Figure  3a and Figure S2, Supporting Information, the grain size greatly increased and a large laminar structure was formed after more than three cycles. The largest grain size can be up to ${\approx}60~{\upmu}\mathrm{m}$ in the sample 4C, while both samples 1C and 4C showed disordered crystallographic orientation. Therefore, the significantly reduced grain boundary scattering can be the main reason for the improvement of charge carrier transport.[43] Furthermore, as shown in the grain size distribution in Figure  3a, although the large grains occupy most of the area, the small grains dominate in number (more than $80\%$ for ${<}5~\upmu\mathrm{m}$ ), which is a typical characteristic of abnormal grain growth. These results indicate that the SPS cycle with liquid phase is helpful for the abnormal grain growth. To verify the effect of cyclic liquid-phase-aided SPS on the grain growth, comparative results after the use of excess Te but without a cycling process, that is, static SPS at 723, 743, and $763~\mathrm{{K}}$ for $15~\mathrm{min}$ are presented in the Supporting Information; note that the 15-min soak time is equal to the total duration of four SPS cycles. Interestingly, only a few grains significantly grew under the static SPS condition. Therefore, it can be concluded that the cyclic sintering plays an important role in large-area grain growth.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): where $R$ is the growth rate, $\nu_{s t}$ is the step velocity of the growing nucleus, $\Omega$ is the molar volume, $\varepsilon$ is the edge free energy of the nucleus, $\Delta G$ is the driving force for grain growth, $h$ is the step height of the nucleus, $k_{B}$ is the Boltzmann constant, and $T$ is the temperature. The exponential form of this equation leads to a sharp change of the function curve, and therefore a critical driving force $\Delta G_{C}$ exists. Each grain in the sample has a value of $\Delta G$ related to its own radius. The precondition of abnormal grain growth for a particular grain is that its $\Delta G$ value is greater than $\Delta G_{C}$ . As reported in previous research, the introduced liquid phase can lead to a decrease of $\varepsilon$ and further reduces the value of $\Delta G_{C}$ because the matrix grains can dissolve in the liquid phase between the matrix and then deposit at the site with relatively high free energy,[45,46] as schematically depicted in Figure  4b. The schematic diagram of the growth rate as a function of the driving force is presented in Figure 4c, in which ΔG is compared with $\Delta G_{C}$ under three different conditions. Therefore, the liquid phase during sintering results in the abnormal growth of some grains. The more uniform and network-like distribution of the $\mathrm{Bi}_{x}\mathrm{Sb}_{2-x}\mathrm{Te}_{3}\mathrm{\cdotTe}$ eutectic phase due to the cyclic SPS process further resulted in more grains to grow.

- path: /home/aid1/Documents/causalmat/matmech/Advanced_Functional_Materials/10.1002_adfm.202009681/images/8daeb6523fe230eb15a91e257070eb2dff9e9386c759111470da20e9084f13d9.jpg
- caption: Figure 5. Low-magnification TEM images of sample 4C: a) high-density disordered dislocations, b) dislocations wrapped around a center with an ED point analysis of the center, c) an Sb-rich second phase, and d) a partial vertical section of the Bi-Sb-Te ternary phase diagram.
- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The microstructure was observed in more detail via TEM; the features of the microstructure played an important role in the reduction of the $\kappa_{\mathrm{L}},$ as shown in Figure  5. It is worth noting that many nanoscale dislocations could be observed in the cycled samples (not observed in the sample 1C, Figure S5, Supporting Information), and can be attributed to the process of expelling the liquid $\mathrm{Bi}_{x}\mathrm{Sb}_{2-x}\mathrm{Te}_{3}$ -Te eutectic phase from the sample.[32] Most of the dislocations were found inside grains instead of at grain boundaries, which possibly was due to the facilitated grain growth by the present process. During the cyclic liquid phase sintering, the eutectic phase solidified in a very short time, thereby would introduce a quick change in volume, and, more importantly, strain inside the samples under SPS pressure. This made the sample go through a plastic deformation process, especially inside the grains, and thus led to the formation of the dislocations. The morphology of the dislocations was found to be diverse; many were disordered, as shown in Figure  5a. Moreover, there were many dislocations wrapped around some centers, as shown in Figure  5b. A different number of dislocations of a similar morphology is presented in Figure S6, Supporting Information, revealing a pinning effect on the dislocations by the centers.  It is worth noting that the pinned dislocations can act as Frank-Read sources, and thereby could greatly promote the formation of dislocations and thus increase the dislocation density.[47] These dislocations multiplied by various means play an important role in the scattering of mid-frequency phonons, and also in the softening of the lattice,[31,48] which can effectively reduce the $\kappa_{\mathrm{L}}$ . While for electronic transport properties, the impacts from dislocations would be limited as compared to the point defects and enlarged grain size mentioned before.[32,33,49] To reveal the essence of the centers, EDS point analysis was conducted, and revealed that they were Sb-rich second phases. By further scrutinizing the samples, more Sb-rich second phases were observed, one of which is presented in Figure 5c. The misfit interface between the second phases and matrix shown in Figure S7, Supporting Information may also be beneficial to the scattering of phonons. Moreover, such high-energy misfit interfaces can also promote the formation of dislocations to reduce energy.[50] The increased content of the second phase with the increase of the number of cycles revealed by the magnified SEM images (as provided in Figure S8, Supporting Information) indicates that the second phase may originate from a precipitation process. Herein, a possible explanation for the precipitation process is presented. According to the partial vertical section of the Bi-Sb-Te ternary phase diagram shown in Figure 5d, the composition of the liquid phase between $693\mathrm{~K~}$ $(420^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ and 743 K $(470~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ should vary along the red curve.[51–53] Because the extruded liquid eutectic phase was Te-rich, small regions inside the solid sample could have been Sb-rich. 
### F6
- path: /home/aid1/Documents/causalmat/matmech/Advanced_Functional_Materials/10.1002_adfm.202009681/images/0720c42ddaffd5be7f45aa15c6064a4864041d4590d5aa34d6923abdd515e1b7.jpg
- caption: Figure 6. a) Experimental and calculated data (without regard to bipolar diffusion) of the lattice thermal conductivity for samples 1C and 4C; b) frequency-dependent lattice thermal conductivity of $\mathtt{B i}_{0.45}\mathsf{S b}_{1.55}\mathsf{T e}_{3};\mathtt{c})$ a schematic diagram of the main scattering effects of phonons and charge carriers for the sample before and after SPS cycling with excess Te. Nano second phase particles play a significant role in pinning the dislocations, which can greatly decrease the thermal conductivity by scattering mid-frequency phonons.
- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The inhomogeneous nanostructures mentioned previously can act as phonon scattering centers to reduce the $\kappa_{\mathrm{L}}$ . The massively increased dislocations, originating from the extrusion of the liquid $\mathrm{Bi}_{x}\mathrm{Sb}_{2-x}\mathrm{Te}_{3}$ -Te eutectic phase as well as the pinning effect by Sb-rich nano second phases, were the main reason for the reduction of $\kappa_{\mathrm{L}}$ . The Debye-Callaway model was applied to denote the contribution of the various scattering mechanism to the reduction of thermal conductivity by fitting the experimental values of the samples 1C and 4C, as shown in Figure  6a (the equations and parameters are presented in Table S3, Supporting Information).[28,32,38,54–56] The increase of the deviation with the increase of the temperature was a result of the bipolar effect. The dislocation density of sample 1C was fitted to be ${\approx}6\times10^{10}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , conforming with the order of magnitude reported in previous research.[32,33,55] After four cycles, the presence of many second phases also slightly decreased the value of $\kappa_{\mathrm{L}}$ when considering them as precipitates. However, most of the reduction of $\kappa_{\mathrm{L}}$ was attributed to the doubling of dislocation density, which reached ${\approx}1.2\times10^{11}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , as suggested by the TEM study. Although the nanoprecipitates exhibited a very limited reduction in thermal conductivity, as indicated by the decrease from the cyan curve to the orange curve in Figure 6a, by targeting low-frequency phonons (the blue area in Figure  6b), they played a significant role in increasing the dislocation density via pinning effect. These dislocations mainly targeted the phonons in the mid-frequency range (due to the relationship between the relaxation time and phonon frequency: dislocation strain $\tau_{\mathrm{DS}}^{-1}~\sim\omega$ and dislocation core $\tau_{\mathrm{DC}}^{-1}\sim\omega^{3})$ , which therefore effectively diminished the value of $\kappa_{\mathrm{L}}$ near room temperature. Figure 6c schematically illustrates the change of the main scattering mechanism after SPS cycling with excess Te. The scattering of charge carriers and low-frequency phonons were simultaneously weakened because of the reduced grain boundaries. The introduced nano second phases after cycling slightly compansated the weakened scattering of low-frequency phonons. Nevertheless, the dislocations, whether randomly distributed or pinned by Sb-rich nano second phases, played a significant role in the reduction of $\kappa_{\mathrm{L}}$ by enhancing the scattering of mid-frequency phonons.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Given that the composition adjustment can regulate the carrier concentration to further enhance the performance, x was adjusted from 0.35 to 0.5 in $\mathrm{Bi}_{x}\mathrm{Sb}_{2-x}\mathrm{Te}_{3.2}$ subjected to four cycles of SPS to optimize the charge carrier concentration by adjusting the concentration of $\mathrm{Sb^{\prime}_{T e}}$ . Figure 7 and Figure S9, Supporting Information present the TE properties of the sample with different compositions. The results show that, with the decrease of $x_{i}$ the value of $\sigma$ increased. The value of S decreased within the measured temperature range, and its peaks moved to high temperatures, due to the inverse trend of $n_{\mathrm{H}}$ . The more intrinsic reason for this is that the increasing content of Sb can create more $\mathrm{Sb^{\prime}_{T e}}$ antisite defects, which can further increase the hole concentration and inhibit the intrinsic excitation. As a result, the PF values were further regulated as shown in Figure  7c, which were also found to be at very high levels as compared with other recent works that have reported the high TE performance of $(\mathrm{Bi},\mathrm{Sb})_{2}\mathrm{Te}_{3}$ .[20,32,33,57–59] Such high PF values indicate that the optimized sample is of great importance for practical applications, because the PF directly measures the ability to convert thermal energy into electric energy.[3,60]  The thermal transport properties were also found to be effectively modulated by compositional optimization, as shown in Figure 7d,e. The total thermal conductivity increased before the temperature reached $423\mathrm{~K~}$ with the decrease of $x,$ after which it decreased, which was mainly due to the change of $\kappa_{e}$ that was correlated with the carrier concentration. In contrast, $\kappa_{L}$ gradually decreased, which may have resulted from the increased antisite defects. Finally, as shown in Figure  7f, the maximum  ZT value of 1.46 at 348 K was obtained when $x=0.4$ , presenting an increase of $\approx50\%$ as compared with commercial $(\mathrm{Bi},\mathrm{Sb})_{2}\mathrm{Te}_{3}$ subjected to ball milling and SPS. The cyclic liquid-phase-aided SPS synergized with the compositional optimization, which resulted in a high ZT value. Because the practical application of $(\mathrm{Bi},\mathrm{Sb})_{2}\mathrm{Te}_{3}$ is always concentrated in the temperature range near room temperature, it is necessary to consider the average ZT value in that range. As shown in Figure S10, Supporting Information, the average ZT value of 1.43 from 300 to $400~\mathrm{K}$ is compared with the results of other works that have reported different preparation methods.[7,8,13,21,30,31,34,35,53–58] The figure indicates that the average ZT value achieved in the present study is superior to those achieved by recent state-of-the-art methods, and also indicates its realistic application near room temperature.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The TE conversion efficiency $\eta$ , a more direct index for realistic application, has been measured by a simple method base on the single-leg module without welding, as schematically exhibited in Figure 8a. More details are presented in the Experimental Section. As shown in Figure 8b–d, the maximum $\eta$ for sample 1C (without cyclic sintering) and $\mathrm{Bi}_{0.4}\mathrm{Sb}_{1.6}\mathrm{Te}_{3.2}$ subjected to 4 SPS cycles were obtained under $\Delta T=225\mathrm{~K~}$ , while it was observed at $\Delta T=175\mathrm{~K~}$ for the commercial $(\mathrm{Bi},\mathrm{Sb})_{2}\mathrm{Te}_{3}$ (provided by Huabei Cooling Device Co. Ltd.) subjected to ball milling and SPS. This demonstrates that the MA-SPS sample is more suitable for higher temperature applications. Moreover, the efficiency was up to ${\approx}5\%$ for the $\mathrm{Bi}_{0.4}\mathrm{Sb}_{1.6}\mathrm{Te}_{3.2}$ sample subjected to four SPS cycles, which was respectively $\approx56\%$ and ${\approx}85\%$ higher than the sample 1C $(\approx3.2\%)$ and the commercial $(\mathrm{Bi},\mathrm{Sb})_{2}\mathrm{Te}_{3}$ subjected to ball milling and SPS $(\approx2.7\%)$ (see Figures 8c,d), indicating that the process of cyclic SPS with liquid phase is conducive to the improvement of conversion efficiency. Meanwhile, the theoretical $\eta$ values for the single leg of $\mathrm{Bi}_{0.4}\mathrm{Sb}_{1.6}\mathrm{Te}_{3.2}$ sample subjected to 4 SPS cycles were simulated using COMSOL Multiphysics software for comparison, which showed a markedly high value of $\approx10\%$ under $\Delta T=225\mathrm{~K~}$ in Figure 8e. Such a simple measuring method is a convenient and efficient way to estimate the improvement of TE performance, however, the contact interface between sample, gold foil and copper electrodes is still needed to be optimized to close the gap between the measured values and theoretical simulated values.

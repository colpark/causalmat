- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Fig. 1 a shows the XRD powder patterns of the $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{V}_{1-\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{Ta}_{\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{Al}_{1-\mathrm{y}}\mathrm{Si}_{\mathrm{y}}$ -type samples synthesized in this work, which were all annealed at 1073 K for $168~\mathrm{h}.$ A diminished intensity of the (111) Bragg peak at $2\theta\approx27^{\circ}$ can be seen in almost all samples of this work, indicating a CsCl-type disorder, corresponding to an intermixing of $\mathrm{v}$ and Al atoms. For some samples there is even a complete absence of the (111) Bragg peak corresponding to the disordered B2 structure. This indicates that the annealing at $1073\mathrm{~K~}$ for $168\mathrm{~h~}$ was insufficient for obtaining the ordered $\mathrm{L}2_{1}$ structure. Similar XRD patterns were reported for $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{VAl}_{1-\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{Si}_{\mathrm{x}}$ by Lue et al. [28] and also for $(\mathrm{Fe}_{1-\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{Co}_{\mathrm{x}})_{2}\mathrm{VAl}$ by Lu et al. [27], where the annealing temperature was set to 1073 K as well. On the other hand, synchrotron-radiation XRD (SR-XRD) performed by Renard et al. showed a clear presence of the (111) Bragg peak in all of their samples which have been annealed at $1273\mathrm{~K~}$ This inconsistency amongst various papers from literature and also the present one could well be explained by the difference in the heat treatments with respect to the annealing temperature.  Additionally, one might need to consider the B2 disorder within sample powders of $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{VAl}$ -based compounds induced by hand grinding. As was shown by Maier et al. [29] and also van der Rest et al. [42], ground powders, which are used for the XRD measurements, show significantly increased ${\mathsf{C s C l}}.$ -type disorder compared to the bulk specimens, which are used for the measurement of transport properties.  Apart from the antisite disorder within the Heusler structure, small impurity peaks are visible in some samples, which can be attributed to a hexagonal $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{Ta}$ -type Laves phase that crystallizes in the C14 structure. The normalized intensities of those impurity peaks increase with both the Ta and Si concentrations $x$ and $y$ , respectively, which means that either both dopants can occupy the hexagonal lattice or substituting Si reduces the solubility of Ta. We presume the latter scenario to be more likely due to the large atomic mass difference of Ta and Si, making a substitution of Ta/Si in $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{Ta}$ unplausible.  Fig. 1 b shows the concentration-dependent lattice parameter of $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathsf{V}_{1-\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{Ta}_{\mathrm{x}}\mathsf{A l}_{1-\mathrm{y}}\mathsf{S i}_{\mathrm{y}}$ , which increases with $x$ for the single-phase samples and decreases with $y_{\cdot}$ , owing to the larger atomic radii of Ta compared to $\mathrm{v}$ and the smaller atomic radii of Si compared to Al. A deviation from the linear increase of $a$ at $x=0.1,y=0.1$ and $x=0.15,y=0$ also suggests that the solubility limit has already been reached for these compositions.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Fig. 2 a and b display the microstructure of $\mathsf{F e}_{2}\mathsf{V}_{0.9}\mathsf{T a}_{0.1}\mathsf{A l}_{1-\mathrm{y}}\mathsf{S i}_{\mathrm{y}}$ for $y=0$ and $y=0.1$ , respectively. While the former shows diffuse contrasts in the SEM image that indicate a metastable oversaturated Heusler phase, the latter appears to be in thermal equilibrium, featuring a hexagonal Laves phase, which was also observed in the XRD pattern of this sample. Furthermore, the sample with $y=0$ shows nanoscale impurity precipitates of up to a few hundred nanometers, emerging from the oversaturated regions of the Heusler matrix, which was also observed in other samples of similar composition (see Supplemental Materials). This means that the solubility limit is already reached for $x\leq0.1$ if $y=0$ , even though no impurity peaks were visible in the XRD spectrum. Also, it seems that the $\sf A l/S i$ substitution significantly hampers the thermodynamic stability of the $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{V}_{1-\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{Ta}_{\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{Al}$ full-Heusler structure, which results in the formation of a lamella-shaped impurity phase.  An increase of the impurity phase with the same morphology was also found when increasing the Ta concentration from $x=0.1$ to $x=0.15$ and up to $x=0.2$ ,as can be seen in Fig. 3ac. We performed EDX measurements to investigate the composition of the different phases. Due to the dendrite-like structure, a conclusion about the exact composition of the secondary phase is all but impossible. However, the approximate compositions of the Heusler phase were determined by mapping the single phase regions and are listed next to the SEM images in Fig. 3a-c. It can be seen that the Ta concentration remains almost the same in all samples, which means that the solubility limit is reached and further doping merely increases the amount of the secondary phase. The matrix compositon of the sample with $x=0.1$ $y=0$ which has been calulcated from the EDX results shown in Fig. 3, is $\mathrm{Fe_{1.984}V_{0.896}T a_{0.072}A l_{1.048}}.$ We also calculated the matrix composition by considering the volume fraction of the secondary phase, which we determined assuming spherical precipitates. In the worst case scenario, if the secondary phase was pure Ta, the composition of the main matrix yields $\mathrm{Fe}_{2.014}\mathrm{V}_{0.906}\mathrm{Ta}_{0.073}\mathrm{Al}_{1.007}$ , whereas, if the secondary phase is assumed to be the hexagonal $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{Ta}$ phase, one obtains $\mathrm{Fe}_{1.986}\mathsf{V}_{0.919}\mathrm{Ta}_{0.074}\mathsf{A l}_{1.021}$ , which agrees convincingly well with the one from EDX. These results show that the solubility limit of $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{V}_{1-\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{Ta}_{\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{Al}$ is around $x\approx0.073$ . However, an error made from the assumption of spherical precipitates and their homogeneous distribution within the sample needs to be considered.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): A similar metastable microstructure to the one shown in Fig. 2a was observed in $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{V}_{0.95}\mathrm{Ta}_{0.05}\mathrm{Al}_{0.9}\mathrm{Si}_{0.1}$ , which recently exhibited one of the highest reported power factors among bulk thermoelectric materials near room temperature $\left(P F_{\operatorname*{max}}\approx10.3\mathrm{mW}\mathrm{m}^{-1}\mathrm{K}^{-2}\right)$ [12]. The effect of the annealing parameters on the microstructural evolution of $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{V}_{0.95}\mathrm{Ta}_{0.05}\mathrm{Al}_{0.9}\mathrm{Si}_{0.1}$ is shown in Fig. 5a-e.  Firstly, after annealing for $^{168~\mathrm{~h~}}$ at 1073 K, the previously mentioned metastable microstructure is obtained, featuring diffuse contrasts and small unconnected precipitates. Secondly, for the sample, which was annealed for $336\mathrm{~h~}$ in total, the morphology changes rapidly and the diffuse contrasts disappear, yielding self-organized punctiform precipitates, evenly dispersed across the sample. For further annealing up to $399\mathrm{~h~}$ these punctiform precipitates grow and connect. Grain boundary segregation leads to a distinct grainlike structure of the impurity phase, reminding of self-organized crystal growth (Fig. 5c).  Ultimately, for the sample which was annealed for $912\mathrm{~h~}$ at $1073~\mathrm{K},$ , the secondary phase forms dendrite-like precipitates, resulting in a very fine lamellar microstructure that is intertwined within the main Heusler phase.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The peculiar changes in the microstructural appearance of $\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{V}_{0.95}\mathrm{Ta}_{0.05}\mathrm{Al}_{0.9}\mathrm{Si}_{0.1}$ are expected to affect the thermoelectric transport properties. In order to establish such a relationship in this compound, we measured the Seebeck coefficient and electrical resistivity from $4\mathrm{~K~}$ up to $800~\mathrm{K}$ as well as the thermal conductivity from $8\mathrm{~K~}$ up to $300~\mathrm{K}$ The temperature-dependent transport properties are shown in Fig. 6.  Fig. 6 a,b shows that the absolute values of the Seebeck coefficient and electrical resistivity both decrease with the annealing time, due to metallic-like conduction channels introduced by the impurity phase, as shown before in Fig. 5. Interestingly, the LT short’- and ‘HT short’-annealed sample share similar features such as an almost identical slope of $S(T)$ at low temperatures. Furthermore, the temperature at the maximum Seebeck coefficients and resistivities, $T_{S}^{\mathrm{max}}$ and $T_{\rho}^{\mathrm{max}}$ , respectively, are almost equal in both cases. Only for the ‘LT long’-annealed case the maximum is shifted towards higher temperatures, which, in a single-phase picture, indicates that the Fermi energy is shifted further into the conduction band and the charge carrier concentration is increased.  Fig. 6 c shows the temperature-dependent thermal conductivity $\lambda$ measured from 8 to $300~\mathrm{K}$ (filled symbols). The room temperature value of the undoped sample is around $25\mathrm{Wm}^{-1}\mathrm{K}^{-1}$ which is in very good agreement with values reported so far in the literature [28,34,38]. It can be seen that upon $\sf A l/S i$ and $\mathbf{V}/\mathrm{Ta}$ substitution λ decreases significantly, in accordance with previous observations. This effect is well understood in terms of an increase of the point defect scattering due to atomic disorder upon alloying. The substitution of $\mathtt{V/T a}$ is especially effective in that regard, due to the large differences in atomic mass and volume. It can be seen that the microstructural changes affect the thermal conductivity as well. While the overall behavior of $\lambda(T)$ remains more or less similar, $\lambda$ exhibits the lowest values in the ‘HT short’-annealed sample over the whole temperature range. We suppose that the impurity phase precipitation, which starts in the nanoscale regime and is evenly spread across the sample (see Fig. 5b), is responsible for this tendency. Upon further annealing, however, grain growth increases the average size of these precipitates, which might reduce the effectivess of the scattering process. Thus, the ‘HT short’-annealed sample should have the most favorable microstructure.

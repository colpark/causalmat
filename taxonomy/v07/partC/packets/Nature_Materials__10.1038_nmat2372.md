The XRD corresponding to Li phthalate (Fig. 1a) was indexed in the space group $P2_{1}/c$ as previously reported with ‘standard’ lattice constants $\overset{\vartriangle}{a}=8.363(0)\overset{\vartriangle}{\mathrm{A}}$ $b=5.\dot{1}35\dot{(}1\dot{)}\dot{\mathrm{A}}.$ , $c=8.490$ , $\beta=93.15(3)^{\circ}$ and $V=364.02(4)\mathrm{\AA}^{3}$ . The Li cations are in tetrahedral coordination and the packing of the terephthalate anions resembles the $\gamma$ -packing of aromatic hydrocarbons13. The Li-muconate phase is not reported, but using the same space group as above we could successfully refine the experimental XRD pattern (Fig. 1b) with the following lattice parameters: $a=8.799(\mathrm{{\bar{1}}})\mathrm{{\AA}}$ , $b=1\bar{0}.041(1)\mathrm{\AA},$ $c=7.785(1)\mathrm{\bar{A}}$ , $\beta=114.51(4)^{\circ}$ and $V=625.84(9)\mathrm{\AA}^{3}$ . Neutron diffraction is planned to determine the exact structure of the Li-muconate phase.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The electrochemical performances of our materials were tested versus lithium after ball-milling with $30\%$ w/w carbon black (SP) for $10\mathrm{min}$ . Figure $^{2\mathrm{a},\mathrm{b}}$ shows voltage–composition traces for $\mathrm{Li_{2+x}C_{6}H_{4}O_{4}/L i}$ and $\mathrm{Li_{2+x}C_{8}H_{4}O_{4}/L i}$ cells cycled at a rate of one lithium in $10\mathrm{h}$ , between 1.2 and $2.5\mathrm{V}$ and 0.7 and $^{3\mathrm{V}}$ , respectively. This plateau is located near $1.4\:\mathrm{V}$ and has an overall uptake capacity of $1.2\mathrm{~Li~}$ per unit formula for the $\mathrm{Li_{2+x}C_{6}H_{4}O_{4}/L i}$ cell. In contrast, for the $\mathrm{Li_{2+x}C_{8}H_{4}O_{4}/L i}$ cell, the plateau is located at a lower value $\left(0.8\mathrm{V}\right)$ and has twice the overall uptake capacity (2.3 Li per unit formula). This indicates a reversible Li uptake with an irreversible capacity between the first charge and discharge that does not exceed at worst $30\%$ . The $\mathrm{Li_{2+x}C_{8}H_{4}O_{4}/L i}$ and $\mathrm{Li}_{2+x}\mathrm{C}_{6}\mathrm{H}_{4}\mathrm{O}_{4}/\mathrm{Li}$ cells show reversible capacities of about $300\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{hg}^{-1}$ (Fig. 2b, inset) and $170\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{hg}^{-1}$ (Fig. 2a, inset), which slowly decay to values of about 234 and $125\operatorname*{mA}\mathrm{hg^{-1}}$ after 50 and 80 cycles, respectively. Among the common reasons for such fading are structural/textural/morphological material changes on cycling or material solubility issues pertaining to the nonoptimized electrolyte that we have been using, although we did not observe any discoloration on dismantling the cell. Additionally, in terms of rate capability, $70\%$ of the $\mathrm{Li}_{2+x}\mathrm{C}_{8}\mathrm{H}_{4}\mathrm{O}_{4}$ electrode capacity $(240\operatorname{mA}\mathrm{h}\bar{\mathbf{g}^{-1}})$ ) was found to be still accessible at a rate of $1~C$ , with $C$ being defined as the initial full capacity obtained in $1\mathrm{h}$ . Last, we should mention that we succeeded in cycling both materials at $80^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ in polyethyleneoxide-based electrolytes with similar results, implying satisfactory thermal stability in pristine and reduced states.

Turning to the $\mathrm{Li}_{2+x}\mathrm{C}_{6}\mathrm{H}_{4}\mathrm{O}_{4}$ electrode, infrared spectra superimpose for the charged and discharged samples, again confirming a reversible process (Fig. 4b). The XRD data are, in contrast, less decisive, as the poorly crystallized as-made sample (Fig. 4c) becomes amorphous on the first discharge, and remains so on cycling. The phenyl group, present only in Li phthalate, is most likely at the origin of such a difference. With the likely $\uppi{-}\uppi$ interactions, a stronger three-dimensional crystal packing is expected for such molecules as opposed to the linear conjugated muconic molecule. On this basis, the progressive amorphization of the three-dimensional crystal structure noted for the muconate phase on Li uptake does not come as a surprise. In contrast, for the phthalic phase, the $\pi{-}\pi$ interactions maintain the stacking, with therefore the possibility for the extra Li to affect this interaction, which could lead to some subtle gliding or rotation of the phenyl groups versus one another, and to the modification of the XRD powder patterns, as we observed. For Li muconate, the loss of order revealed by XRD (Fig. 4c) data is not inconsistent with the reversibility detected by infrared measurements, as such a technique senses mainly the bond-stretching vibration modes within a single molecule.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): As safety becomes paramount, a figure of merit of negative-electrode materials is the magnitude of energy liberated by the reaction between the lithiated negative electrode and the electrolyte at high temperature. The most elegant way of probing this stability is to make DSC measurements in the presence of electrolyte. The following protocol has been adopted. Four Li half cells with $\mathrm{Li}_{x}/\mathrm{C}_{6}$ , $\mathrm{Li}/\mathrm{Li}_{2+x}\mathrm{C}_{8}\mathrm{H}_{4}\mathrm{O}_{4}$ , $\mathrm{Li}/\mathrm{Li_{2+x}C_{6}H_{4}O_{4}}$ and $\mathrm{Li}/\mathrm{Li}_{4+x}\mathrm{Ti}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12}$ were assembled and fully discharged at $x=1$ $(\mathrm{LiC}_{6})$ ), $x=2.3$ $\mathrm{(Li_{4.3}C_{8}H_{4}O_{4}}.$ , $x=1.2$ $(\mathrm{Li}_{3.2}\mathrm{C}_{6}\mathrm{H}_{4}\mathrm{O}_{4})$ and $x=3$ $(\mathrm{Li}_{7}\mathrm{Ti}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12}$ ). The cells were opened and the powders directly recovered, without any washing, before being sealed in Al pans. The Al pans were then removed from the dry box and their covers pierced under Ar flow before being placed on the DSC apparatus also flushed with Ar, of which the temperature was raised to $350^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ at a rate of $10^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\operatorname*{min}^{-1}$ . Comparative studies were made with $\mathrm{Li}_{1}\mathrm{C}_{6}$ (refs 14,15) and $\mathrm{Li}_{7}\mathrm{Ti}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12}$ (ref. 16) negative electrodes, because the former is currently the most used in Li-ion cells and shows roughly the same specific gravity $(\approx2\mathrm{g}\mathrm{cm}^{-3})$ ) as the phthalate salt. In contrast, the latter is currently the most popular alternative negative electrode, though it has a lower capacity of $150\operatorname{mA}\mathrm{hg}^{-1}$ , only partially compensated by a density of ${\approx}3.6$ , and further penalized by a redox voltage of $1.5\mathrm{V}$ . Drastic differences were noted between the samples in terms of reactivity with the electrolyte (Fig. 5). As far as these reactions are considered for the new candidates, that of the fully lithiated $\mathrm{Li_{4}C_{8}H_{4}O_{4}}$ phase was only one-third as exothermic as the $\mathrm{LiC}_{6}$ electrode, which shows considerable reactivity over the $150{-}180^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ temperature range, in agreement with previous literature reports14,15. Such a result in itself is of tremendous importance with respect to the safety of the Li-ion technology, as it simply implies that negative electrodes based on the 0.8 $\mathrm{~V~Li_{2}C_{8}H_{4}O_{4}}$ phase should lead to safer Li-ion cells than those using carbon electrodes. Just as spectacular is the quasi-absence of exothermic reaction for our 1.4 V $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{C}_{6}\mathrm{H}_{4}\mathrm{O}_{4}$ electrode (Fig. 5) as compared with a $1.5\mathrm{V~Li_{4}T i_{5}O_{12}}$ electrode, which shows some reactivity despite its acceptance as the archetype to design safe Li-ion cells. Another aspect is the possibility, for electrodes based on these two materials, to use Al current collectors, with a weight gain and far more sustainability and economic advantages over Cu, whose use is mandatory for graphite electrodes.

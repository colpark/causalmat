- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To provide a microscopic comparison of as-fabricated perovskite films with and without additives, atomic force microscopy (AFM) investigation was carried out. As shown in Figure  1b, with the addition of $\mathrm{NH_{4}S C N/N H_{4}C l}$ in the precursor solution, the number of pinholes on the surface significantly decreases. The average surface roughness $R_{\mathrm{a}}$ decreases from 33.9 to $16.6~\mathrm{nm}$ . Images at different scales are shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information). The smooth morphology improves the photogenerated carrier extraction and inhibits the leakage current.[35] Figure S2 (Supporting Information) shows the top-view scanning electron microscope (SEM) and further confirms the role of the additives in enhancing the film morphology. This result implies that $\mathrm{NH_{4}S C N}$ and $\mathrm{NH_{4}C l}$ are essential for promoting quasi-2D perovskite films. Figure  1c exhibits the typical absorption plot of the deposited film. For the comparison, the data of $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}P b_{5}I_{16}}$ is also provided. Considering the phase purity was not controlled intentionally, the small exciton absorption peaks can also be observed in both plots. As shown, $\mathrm{Sn}^{2+}$ incorporation can effectively modify the optical response of perovskite materials as the absorption onsets extend from $760~\mathrm{nm}$ $(\mathrm{PEA}_{2}\mathrm{MA}_{4}\mathrm{Pb}_{5}\mathrm{I}_{16})$ to $1000\mathrm{nm}$ $\mathrm{(PEA_{2}M A_{4}(S n_{0.5}P b_{0.5})_{5}I_{16})}$ . The corresponding Tauc plot is provided in Figure S3 (Supporting Information). The optical bandgap of the $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}}(\mathrm{Sn_{0.5}P b_{0.5}})_{5}\mathrm{I_{16}}$ and $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}P b_{5}I_{16}}$ is estimated as 1.24 and $1.65~\mathrm{eV},$ respectively. Figure  1d presents their corresponding photoluminescence (PL). A single PL peak of ${\approx}920\mathrm{nm}$ in $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}}(\mathrm{Sn_{0.5}P b_{0.5}})_{5}\mathrm{I_{16}}$ film suggests the formation of a uniform crystal lattice in $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{Pb}}-S_{\mathrm{n}}$ binary perovskite. In order to validate the role of $\mathrm{NH_{4}S C N}$ and $\mathrm{NH_{4}C l}$ in facilitating the crystal lattice growing along orientation, grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) analysis was carried out. Figure S4 (Supporting Information) shows the GIWAXS map of $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}P b_{5}I_{16}}$ film with/without $\mathrm{NH_{4}S C N}$ and $\mathrm{NH_{4}C l}$ additives. The sharp and discrete Bragg spots confirm the high crystalline quality and favorable orientation,[36] indicating the formation of well-aligned perovskite layers. The Bragg spots of (101) and (202) appear along $q_{z}$ and $q_{x\gamma},$ suggesting that the crystal lattice growth direction is along the (101) plane,[37] implying that the crystal lattice is predominantly vertically oriented to the substrate. The GIWAXS map of $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}}$ $(\mathrm{Sn}_{0.5}\mathrm{Pb}_{0.5})_{5}\mathrm{I}_{16}$ film with additives, and its corresponding out of plane line-cut file is also provided in Figure 1e. The (202) peak along $q_{z}$ and the enhanced intensity of the $q_{z}$ line-cut confirm the enhanced out-of-plane preferred orientation, which corresponds with the orientation of the $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}P b_{5}I_{16}}$ growth. The illustration of crystal alignment is shown in Figure 1e. We labeled the crystal crystallographic plane and the cross plane as the $a b$ -plane and the ac-plane, respectively. The crystallinity of the $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}P b_{5}I_{16}}$ and $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}}(\mathrm{Pb_{0.5}S n_{0.5}})_{5}\mathrm{I_{16}}$ films were further studied with X-ray diffraction (XRD), as shown in Figure 1f. Accordingly, the orientated crystal in films only shows two diffraction peaks at ${\approx}14^{\circ}$ and ${\approx}28^{\circ}$ , which correspond to the (111) and (202) facets of perovskites.[37] With the implementation of additives into the precursor, these two diffraction peaks show obviously enhanced intensity with 100 times. Similarly, this behavior occurs in the $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}}(\mathrm{Pb_{0.5}S n_{0.5}})_{5}\mathrm{I_{16}}$ film, indicating that these additives increase the crystallinity of the perovskite film.[38] The full width at half-maximum (FWHM) of the peak at ${\approx}14^{\circ}$ decreases from $0.488^{\circ}$ (without additives) to $0.111^{\circ}$ (with additives). According to Scherrer's equation,[39] this result confirms the crystal grain size increases with the assistance of additives, which can effectively reduce the leakage current at grain boundaries. Films with different $n$ layers and a 15 days stability are also shown in Figure S5 (Supporting Information). Further, the stability of the $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}}(\mathrm{Sn_{0.5}P b_{0.5}})_{5}\mathrm{I_{16}}$ device is provided in Figure S6 (Supporting Information).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To further assess their crystallographic orientation, confocal polarization PL microscopy was conducted. Figure 2a illustrates the setup of the system. Before the PL photon is collected by detectors, the polarizing beam splitting (PBS) prism divides the incident PL into two beams of linearly polarized light, in which the P-polarized light passes through PBS directly to the detector $p$ and the S-polarized light is reflected to the detector s. The two polarization states are mutually perpendicular and defined by the plane containing the incident and reflected beams. Figure $^{2\mathrm{b},\mathrm{c}}$ shows the confocal polarization PL microscopy of selected areas of $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}}(\mathrm{Sn_{0.5}P b_{0.5}})_{5}\mathrm{I_{16}}$ and $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}P b_{5}I_{16}}$ films with additives. The corresponding histogram patterns are shown in Figure S7 (Supporting Information). As a comparison, perovskite films with different $n$ layers were also tested (Figure S8, Supporting Information). The obvious PL intensity contrast from the two detectors indicates polarized fluorescence for the as-fabricated perovskite film. However, the signal value of two detectors suggests only that the polarization vector occupies a greater proportion of a certain component, P-polarization or S-polarization, initially indicating that the sample has a polarization performance, but cannot indicate that its polarization vector is in a specific direction, accurate angleresolved measurement also requires the use of polarizer. The large organic cation PEA forms the cleavage into 3D prototype (Figure 1e), in which PEA…Pb–I layer facilitates the alignment of MA cation along the $a$ axis with tight N–H…I hydrogen bond as well as the small-angle tilting of $\mathrm{I-Pb-I.}^{[29]}$ Their coupling effect leads to the displacement between positive and negative charge center, which brings the molecular dipole moments and electric polarization along $a$ axis. Therefore, the dipole between the planes has free movements in the $b$ axis and $a$ axis directions. As the light is incident along the $b$ axis, linearly polarized arises between a axis (maximum) and $c$ axis directions (minimum),[6] which corresponds to the $0^{\circ}$ and $90^{\circ}$ of the polarizer. This result confirms our facile solution-processed polycrystalline films exhibit the characteristics of linear dichroism. Figure 2d shows the absorption of $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}}(\mathrm{Sn_{0.5}P b_{0.5}})_{5}\mathrm{I_{16}}$ and $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}P b_{5}I_{16}}$ to the polarized light. The anisotropy causes the refractive index to vary along different crystallographic axis. As a result, the difference in absorption toward polarization angle in light arises, which sets the precondition for its application in the sensitive polarization detection. Selective PL is also shown in Figure 2e. In contrast to the absorption, we attribute the anisotropy of PL to the existence of free excitons along a specific direction and self-restricted exciton along $c$ direction.[40] Figure  2f displays the PL decay transient spectra of perovskite films on glass under $375~\mathrm{nm}$ light excitation. The time constant (τ) associated with the lifetime of the carrier is extracted with an exponential fitting as 4 and 14 ns for $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}}(\mathrm{Sn_{0.5}P b_{0.5}})_{5}\mathrm{I_{16}}$ and $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}P b_{5}I_{16}}$ . Even though the quasi-2D structure can slow down the oxidation of $\mathrm{Sn}^{2+}$ , the formation of traps on $\mathrm{Sn}$ site still dominates the carrier lifetime.[41]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In order to elaborate the polarization detection in wide spectral region, the photodetector containing the $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}}(\mathrm{Sn_{0.5}P b_{0.5}})_{5}\mathrm{I_{16}}$ polycrystalline film as photoactive layer was constructed with photodiode type. The device (Figure  3a) is based on the following architecture: ITO $(180~\mathrm{nm}$ )/PEDOT:PSS $(30~\mathrm{nm})$ /Perovskite $330~\mathrm{nm}$ )/PCBM $(50~\mathrm{nm})/\mathrm{Ag}$ $(100\mathrm{nm})$ ). Figure  3b shows the current–voltage curves of the device under illumination of AM 1.5G and in dark condition. Both curves present good rectification behavior. Indeed, a low dark current of $1.1\times10^{-11}$ A was achieved at $0~\mathrm{V},$ which ensures a high $I_{\mathrm{on}}/I_{\mathrm{off}}$ ratio of over $3\times10^{8}$ and reserves the detection of weak signals. To identify the polarization behavior in device, the incident light was polarized with a polarizer, as shown in Figure  3c. Figure  3d,e shows the angle-resolved polarization photocurrent in rectangular coordinates and polar coordinates. To validate polarization detection, the response in commercial Si photodiode, device without additive, and mesoporous device are compared. Preparation details of the mesoporous device can be found in experiment section. Obviously, photocurrent in these devices exhibits negligible dependence on the polarization angle. The angle-resolved polarization photocurrent of $\mathrm{PEA}_{2}\mathrm{MA}_{4}\mathrm{Pb}_{5}\mathrm{I}_{16}$ in rectangular coordinates and polar coordinates are shown in Figure S9 (Supporting Information), which are in accordance with the trend as the $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}}(\mathrm{Sn_{0.5}P b_{0.5}})_{5}\mathrm{I_{16}}$ device. Owing to the assistance of additives, the thin film in the device has an enhanced orientation of specific plane, resulting in the crystal anisotropy. Thus, once electron–hole pairs are generated, their migrations to conduction band or valence band, and further to electrodes, were restricted along designated transporting tunnels, which leads to different photovoltaic and optoelectronic properties.[42] In the device without polarization, the isotropic sensing performance can be ascribed to chaotic crystal in the perovskite films. This result provides the direct evidence that our solution-processed $\mathrm{PEA_{2}M A_{4}}(\mathrm{Sn_{0.5}P b_{0.5}})_{5}\mathrm{I_{16}}$ polycrystalline film can be used for polarization detector, in which $\mathrm{NH_{4}S C N}$ and $\mathrm{NH_{4}C l}$ play a critical role.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To demonstrate the resolution of weak signals, we investigated the noise current by using fast Fourier transform (FFT) of time-dominated dark current at forward bias of $0.1\mathrm{mV}.$ The original dark current from B1500 can be found in Figure S12 (Supporting Information). As shown in Figure  4b, the photodetector has an average noise current as low as $7\times10^{-14}\mathrm{~A~}\times$ $\mathrm{Hz}^{-0.5}$ and independent of the frequency after $0.75~\mathrm{Hz}$ .[44] The elimination of $1/f$ noise in the device at low frequency verifies the inhibition of defects.[45] As a result, the linear dynamic range (LDR) of 158  dB for sensing incident photon with the power from $0.15~\mathrm{nW}$ to $12\mathrm{mW}$ is achieved, shown in Figure 4c, suggesting practical application of our polarization photodetector for precise weak signal identification in complicated environments. Figure 4d shows the response to temporal square-wave signals at the frequency of $10\mathrm{kHz}$ , $100~\mathrm{kHz}$ , and $1\mathrm{MHz}$ . More response to frequencies is shown in Figure S13 (Supporting Information). Besides, the response rate of the device with or without additives is also provided in Figure S14 (Supporting Information) for the comparison. The $t_{\mathrm{rise}}$ and $t_{\mathrm{fall}}$ of the device with additives are 1.56 and $1.43~{\upmu\mathrm{s}}$ , respectively, which are faster than the ones without additives, revealing the effect of the additives on supporting the crystallization of the crystals. As a result, the response width at $^{-3}$ dB point frequency $(f_{-3\mathrm{dB}})$ in our photodetector reaches $900~\mathrm{{kHz}}$ (Figure 4e). The high response speed in refreshing sensing data makes the promise of application in optical scientific research field, for example counting the photon in time-resolved PL technique. Finally, the $D^{*}$ is estimated according to the following equation: D∗ = ANE∗P∆f , where $A$ is the photoactive area, $\Delta f$ is the constant bandwidth 1, and NEP is the noise equivalent power (Figure S15, Supporting Information). Figure 4f provides the calculated $D^{*}$ , it presents a peak of $1.53\times10^{12}$ Jones at $625~\mathrm{nm}$ .

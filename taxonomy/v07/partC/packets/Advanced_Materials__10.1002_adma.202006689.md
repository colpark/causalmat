- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To effectively control the film deposition, in this study, we design an equipment (Figure 1a) comprising two individual evaporation sources (E1 and E2) in the vacuum chamber. The distances of the evaporation sources to the substrate can be tuned in the range of $10{-}30~\mathrm{cm}$ , enabling control of the mixing of vaporized particles. Furthermore, by controlling the “on/off” functionality of heating of the two sources, we achieve sequential coevaporation and deposition (ScED) and tailor the vertical elemental distribution of the deposited film.  We take the fabrication of ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}({\mathrm{S}},{\mathrm{Se}})_{3}$ films as an example, wherein S and ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ powders are applied as precursor materials and placed in the tungsten boats of E1 and E2 (Figure 1a), respectively. In this case, we first optimized the distance from ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ source to the substrate, for which the suitable distance was identified as $10~\mathrm{cm}$ . The S powder has a lower melting point $(112^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ than ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ $(610~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ . Suppose that the distance between S source and the substrate is the same as that between the ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ source and substrate, the evaporated S particles would possess high energy to reach the substrate—this energetic deposition would potentially cause defect formation.[1,13] In addition, the rapid movement toward the substrate reduces the mixing time with ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ species, resulting in inhomogeneous distribution of elements in the film. Thus, the distance was adjusted to $17\mathrm{cm}$ upon optimization (details are discussed in the Experimental Section).  The two sources with separate controllers allowed us to vary the depth composition profile of the as-deposited films. To make a clear study, the distance and evaporation temperature for both ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ and S were controlled under optimized conditions, and the timing for evaporation of S powder was carefully controlled relative to the ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ evaporation to regulate the interface and bulk ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}({\mathrm{S}},{\mathrm{Se}})_{3}$ film properties. We compared three deposition types (Figure 1b): 1) simultaneous evaporation of ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ and S; 2) evaporation of S after tens of seconds from the start of ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ evaporation, for example, $45\mathrm{~s~}$ ; and 3) evaporation of S immediately after the complete deposition of ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ . In the figure, the as-prepared films by these three types are labelled as S1, S2, and S3, respectively. In the fabrication of S3, S vapor was introduced after the deposition of ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ and then evaporated for $2\mathrm{min}$ . All the as-deposited films were postannealed at $300~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for 30 min under $\Nu_{2}$ atmosphere to enhance their crystallinity.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): We performed lattice characterization at the interface by using high-resolution transmission electron micro­ scopy (HRTEM) to further probe the film growth mechanism. First, the CdS substrate displayed a hexagonal phase (JCPDS 41–1049), whose preferred orientations are (100) and (002) (Figure 2a,d–f). The (100) facet is a nonpolar surface with evenly distributed Cd and S atoms on the plane (Figure 2a). In contrast, the (002) facet is a polar surface stacked with alternating Cd and S atoms along the $c$ axis (Figure  2a). The polar surface makes the surface energy of the (002) facet three times higher than that of the (100) facet; thus, the reaction will first occur at the (002) surface.[16] Interestingly, a blurry amorphous area was detected between the (002) facet of CdS and ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ in S3 and S2 (Figure S3, Supporting Information). We suppose that it was caused by the post treatment with $\mathrm{CdCl}_{2}$ and annealing at $400~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $10\mathrm{min}$ under air, in which $\mathrm{Cd^{2+}}$ and $\mathrm{O}^{2-}$ ions were bonded on the high-energy (002) facet to form an amorphous layer, which passivated the (002) facet. In this regard, the dative bonds of Sb and Se (S) from ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ and S precursors could coordinate with $-\mathsf{S}$ and $-\mathrm{Cd}$ of the (100) facet of the CdS substrate, causing $({\mathrm{Sb}}_{4}{\mathrm{Se}}_{6})_{n}$ ribbons to grow more vertically in S2 and S3 (Figure 2c,e,f).  In the fabrication of film S1, however, S atoms with a higher saturated vapor pressure could reach the CdS surface first. The high substrate temperature induces reaction between S and the partially oxidized CdS, because of which the CdS surface possesses the (100) and (002) facets again. Because the surface energy of the (002) facet is substantially higher, the evaporated ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ and S prefer to deposit on the (002) facet first (Figure 2d). The dangling bonds of ${\mathrm{Cd}}^{2+}$ on the (002) facet first bond with S and form a S-rich surface. When the incoming ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ reaches the surface, ${\mathrm{Sb}}^{3+}$ ions could bond with $\mathrm{S}^{2-}$ ; thus, $({\mathrm{Sb}}_{4}{\mathrm{Se}}_{6})_{n}$ ribbons tend to grow along the (002) surface to lower the surface energy (Figure 2b).  To quantify the elemental composition of the films, we conducted energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) measurement. The estimated molar ratios of $\mathsf{S}/\mathsf{S e}$ are 0.36, 0.26, and 0.07 for S1, S2, and S3, respectively. Interestingly, in all the three samples, the atomic ratio of $(\mathsf{S}+\mathsf{S e})/\mathsf{S}\mathsf{b}$ of the ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}({\mathrm{S}},{\mathrm{Se}})_{3}$ films is 1.77 for S1, and 1.71 for both S2 and S3 (Table S2, Supporting Information); this indicates that the ScED process tends to form anionrich antimony selenosulfide with nearly determined cation/ anion stoichiometry. Note that S was coevaporated in the preparation of film S1 and S2, whereas S was evaporated after the deposition of ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ —this type of evaporation and deposition renders the S/Se of S3 to be much lower than that of S1 and S2.  The surface morphology of the as-prepared films was characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). According to Figure 2g–i, the estimated grain sizes of S1, S2, and S3 were 127, 174, and $164\mathrm{nm}$ , respectively; the corresponding statistical results are presented in Figure S4, Supporting Information. The light absorption properties of the films were investigated by UV–vis absorption spectroscopy. The derived $(\alpha h\nu)^{2}\approx h\nu$ plots (Figure S5, Supporting Information) show that the bandgaps of S1, S2, and S3 are 1.36, 1.26, and $1.19~\mathrm{eV},$ , respectively. The bandgap change agrees with the chemical composition, that is, more Se in the alloy film leads to smaller values. Preferably, these bandgap values are in the optimal range for light-harvesting materials in solar cells.[17]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The composition and element distribution also affect the energy level evolution of the as-prepared films. The energy levels were measured by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS, Figure S9, Supporting Information); the energy diagrams of S1, S2, and S3 are depicted in Figure  4d as solid bars. The UPS technique detects the surface electronic structure only up to several nanometers. The inhomogeneous distributions of S2 and S3 exhibited a gradient band structure, indicated by the dashed area in Figure 4a. This gradient band structure facilitates carrier transport to the electron and hole transporting layers.  The photovoltaic performance of the as-synthesized film was studied based on $\mathrm{FTO/CdS/Sb_{2}(S,S e)_{3}/S p i r o-O M e T A D/}$ Au device configuration (where FTO $=$ fluorine-doped tin oxide and Spiro-OMeTAD $=2,2^{\prime},7,7^{\prime}$ -tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxy­ phenyl)amino]-9,9′-spirobifluorene). The optimized thicknesses for CdS, ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}({\mathrm{S}},{\mathrm{Se}})_{3}$ , Spiro-OMeTAD, and Au were 100, 430, 100, and $50~\mathrm{nm}$ , respectively (Figure  4b). Figure  4c shows the photocurrent density–voltage $(J-V)$ curves measured under $\mathrm{AM}1.5\mathrm{G}$ solar irradiance $(100\mathrm{mW}\mathrm{cm}^{-2})$ ), and Table 1 lists the optimized device parameters. Device S1 delivered an open-circuit voltage $(V_{\mathrm{oc}})$ of $0.50~\mathrm{V},$ short-circuit current density $(J_{\mathrm{sc}})$ of $27.6~\mathrm{mA~cm^{-2}}$ , and fill factor (FF) of $45.4\%$ , corresponding to a PCE of $6.23\%$ . The PCE of device S2 remarkably improved to $8.0\%$ , with a $V_{\mathrm{oc}}$ of $0.46~\mathrm{V},J_{\mathrm{sc}}$ of $30.2\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , and FF of $57.9\%$ . In this case, S was introduced after the evaporation of ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ for $45\mathrm{~s~}$ —this avoided the accumulation of S at the CdS/ ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}{\mathrm{Se}}_{3}$ interface, thus improving the heterojunction quality. A histogram of the device efficiencies obtained from 20 individually fabricated devices is shown in Figure S7, Supporting Information, and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters are shown in Table S3, Supporting Information. The average efficiency was $7.21\%$ , indicating good reproducibility of this device fabrication. The device exhibited no apparent hysteresis (Figure  S8, Supporting Information) and had good stability (Table S4, Supporting Information); it did not show performance degradation even after storing for 11 months in ambient air without encapsulation. Device S3, however, delivered a decreased PCE of $6.43\%$ , with a $V_{\mathrm{oc}}$ of 0.41 V, $J_{\mathrm{sc}}$ of $29.6\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , and FF of $53.4\%$ .  The device parameter is closely related to the element composition and distribution in the film. First, the larger $\mathsf{S}/\mathsf{S e}$ atomic ratio in the ${\mathrm{Sb}}_{2}({\mathrm{S}},{\mathrm{Se}})_{3}$ film led to an enlarged bandgap and enhanced $V_{\mathrm{oc}}$ of $0.50~\mathrm{V}$ in device S1, compared with those of S2 and S3, which are 0.46 and 0.41  V, respectively. On the other hand, S2 and S3 exhibited similar $J_{\mathrm{sc}}$ $(\approx30\mathrm{mAcm^{-2}})$ that was significantly higher than that of S1 $(27.6~\mathrm{mA}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2})$ ; the lower bandgap and favorable orientation of $({\sf S b}_{4}({\sf S},{\sf S e})_{6})_{n}$ ribbons in S2 and S3 could be ascribed to their improved $J_{\mathrm{sc}}$ .  The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectrum of the champion device is shown in Figure  4d. The EQE displayed broad photocurrent generation toward $1000~\mathrm{nm}$ , with the peak value being around $85\%$ . The bandgap was estimated to be $1.24~\mathrm{eV},$ which agrees with the value extracted from the light absorption spectrum (Figure S5, Supporting Information). The drop in the short wavelength range $(400{-}500~\mathrm{nm})$ was attri­ buted to the parasitic absorption in CdS layers $:E_{\mathrm{g}}=2.4~\mathrm{eV}_{\mathrm{.}}$ ).

In principle, this concentration is the sum of free carriers, and bulk and interfacial defects. To extract the interfacial defect, we performed deep-level capacitance profiling (DLCP) measurement, whose result represents the response from the free carrier and bulk defects.[6,19] The difference in carrier density between CV $(N_{\mathrm{{CV}}})$ and DLCP $(N_{\mathrm{DLCP}})$ measurements represents interfacial defects between the CdS and absorber layer.[20,21] As shown in Figure  5b, the carrier concentrations estimated from DLCP of S1, S2, and S3 were $4.85\times10^{16}$ , $6.87\times10^{15}$ , and $2.35\times10^{16}\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ , respectively. The interfacial defect density was thus calculated as $1.21\times10^{12}$ , $3.00\times10^{10}$ , and $1.03\times10^{11}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ for S1, S2, and S3, respectively. The high interfacial defect density of S1 is most likely because of the accumulation of S at the interface, which negatively affects the formation of heterojunction. This result suggests that the coordination of S(Se) with Cd (Figure  2c) is favorable for improving the interfacial property.  We then performed deep-level transient spectroscopy (DLTS) characterization (Figure 5c) to study the influence of chemical composition on the bulk defect properties. Figure  5d shows the Arrhenius plots of the three devices, from which the defect energy level, concentrations, and cross-sections were calculated as summarized in Table S5, Supporting Information. Consequently, one hole trap H1 $\Delta E=0.70~\mathrm{eV}$ , $N_{\mathrm{T}}=3.64\times10^{15}\mathrm{cm}^{-3})$ was detected in S1. S2 displayed a similar defect type as H1 $\begin{array}{r}{(\Delta E=0.70~\mathrm{eV},}\end{array}$ $N_{\mathrm{T}}=1.98\times10^{15}\mathrm{cm}^{-3})$ . In film S3, however, two hole traps, H1 $\prime\Delta E=0.81\mathrm{eV}$ , $N_{\mathrm{T}}=8.51\times10^{14}~\mathrm{cm}^{-3})$  and H2 $\begin{array}{r}{\mathopen{}\mathclose\bgroup\left(\Delta E=0.52~\mathrm{eV},\aftergroup\egroup\right.}\end{array}$ $N_{\mathrm{T}}=1.64\times10^{15}\mathrm{cm}^{-3})$ , were identified, which were assigned as $(\mathsf{S}/\mathsf{S e})_{\mathsf{S b}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{Sb}}$ defects, respectively. It is reasonable that the anion-rich condition avails the formation of both $(\mathrm{S}/\mathrm{Se})_{\mathrm{Sb}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{Sb}}$ defects. The least defect type and low defect concentration of S2 contribute to its relatively higher performance.

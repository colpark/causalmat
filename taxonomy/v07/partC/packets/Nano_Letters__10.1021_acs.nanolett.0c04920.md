- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Spinel $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathrm{Mn}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12}$ -like phase (referred to S-LMO hereafter) was synthesized through a simple, scalable solid-state process at $400~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for $12\mathrm{~h~}$ in air. A second-step heat treatment was conducted at $600~^{\circ}C$ for $2\textrm{h}$ in air to partially decompose the $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathrm{Mn}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12}$ -like phase into spinel ${\mathrm{LiMn}}_{2}{\mathrm{O}}_{4}$ and layered $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{MnO}_{3}$ (referred to SL-LMO hereafter). As shown in Figure 1b, while the X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern of S-LMO matches well with spinel $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathrm{Mn}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12}$ phase, additional peaks exist in the XRD pattern of SL-LMO and they match with layered $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{MnO}_{3}$ structure. High-temperature in situ XRD (Figure S1) confirms the reaction mechanism eq 1, where $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{MnO}_{3}.$ -type ordering begins to form at $400~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and becomes stable after $2\textrm{h}$ at $60\dot{0}~^{\circ}C$ Both S-LMO and SL-LMO have broadened XRD peaks, indicating nanoscale particle sizes and poor crystallinity, which is also evidenced by the following microscopy characterizations. While nanoparticles can enhance kinetics and promote anion-redox capacity due to surface disorder, the side reactions at electrochemically active surfaces would consume the precious electrolyte. They also make oxygen loss and gassing a most challenging problem for such a large surface-area-to-volume-ratio electrode, especially under industrial conditions where the electrolyte quantity is highly limited $(2{-}6\mathrm{g/Ah},$ often less than the cathode weight).  We next conducted $\mathrm{X}$ -ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements to confirm the existence of both $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathrm{Mn}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12}$ and ${\mathrm{LiMn}}_{2}{\mathrm{O}}_{4}$ phases in SL-LMO. Note that while the spinel $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{n}_{5}\mathbf{O}_{12}$ and ${\mathrm{LiMn}}_{2}{\mathrm{O}}_{4}$ have very similar XRD patterns that can hardly be distinguishable for nanomaterials with broadened peaks, Mn is $^{+4}$ valence in $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathrm{Mn}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12}$ and is $+3.5$ (i.e., $50\%+3$ and $50\%+4$ ) in ${\mathrm{LiMn}}_{2}{\mathrm{O}}_{4}$ . As shown by $\mathrm{Mn~}2\mathfrak{p}$ spectra in Figure 1c, SL-LMO contains both ${\mathrm{Mn}}^{3+}$ and ${\mathrm{Mn}}^{4+}$ , and quantitative analysis (Figure S2) suggests a $\mathbf{M}\mathbf{n}^{3+}/\mathbf{M}\mathbf{n}^{4+}$ ratio of 0.24 in SL-LMO. Furthermore, Figure 1d reveals that after $2\mathrm{~h~}$ of treatment at $600^{\circ}\mathrm{C},$ submicron agglomerations of fine nanoparticles are formed, which would benefit the electrochemical performance from several aspects: (1) help accommodate strain through slippages at the grain boundaries and phase boundaries; (2) decrease the contact area with electrolyte and thus reduce the parasitic side reactions. Note that such a self-agglomerated microstructure offers a relatively high compact density of $2.8~\mathrm{g/cm}^{3}$ (Table S1) for SL-LMO, slightly lower than that of NCM $(3.2-3.5~\mathrm{g}/$ $\mathrm{cm}^{3^{\cdot}}$ ) but higher than that of $\mathrm{LiFePO_{4}}$ $(2.2{-}2.4~\mathrm{g}/\mathrm{cm}^{3})$ that has already been heavily optimized at the industry level. Lastly, the crystal structure was characterized by high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) (Figure 1e), where both crystalline and disordered amorphous-like regions are detected. With reference diffraction data in Table S2, the coexistence of three phases in reaction eq 1 is confirmed both at the surface (Figure S3) and in the bulk (Figure 1e).

It is interesting to notice that more high-voltage capacity above $4\mathrm{~V~}$ is activated upon cycling, implying the increased capacity possibly contributed by high-voltage oxygen redox. This may be due to lattice rearrangements during electrochemical cycling that makes some oxygen redox states thermodynamically or kinetically more accessible below $4.7~\mathrm{V}$ . Accordingly, the average discharge voltage increases in the first 50 cycles (Figure 2c), and a similar trend was also found in SLMO cathode. However, in contrast with the voltage decay of S-LMO cathode after the first 50 cycles, SL-LMO maintains a stable discharge voltage of ${\sim}3.2\mathrm{~V~}$ thereafter. Interestingly, akin to the sloping voltage curve in the first charge of S-LMO, SLLMO exhibits more obvious voltage plateau feature, which indicates that after higher-temperature treatment at $600~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ the atomic arrangement of SL-LMO is more ordered than that of S-LMO and thus the structure of SL-LMO is more stable than that of S-LMO, which remits the voltage decay. The above observations support active anion redox at the surface and we next seek to characterize the anion redox in the bulk. We first conducted depth-profiling XPS analysis of the pristine sample, and the samples charged to $4.7\:\mathrm{V}$ and discharged to 1.8 $\mathrm{V}$ after 10 cycles. For $\operatorname{Mn}2\mathfrak{p}$ (Figure S6b) in the bulk, we also confirmed mixed ${\mathrm{Mn}}^{3+}$ and $\mathrm{Mn^{4+}}$ in the pristine sample, mainly ${\mathrm{Mn}}^{4+}$ in the $4.7\mathrm{~V~}$ charged sample, and mainly $\mathrm{Mn}^{3+}$ in the 1.8 V discharged sample, which again agree with the surface XPS results. As shown in Figure S7, when charged to $4.7\mathrm{~V~}$ in the first (red curve) and second cycle (purple curve), the samples show slightly higher intensity at ${\sim}530.5{-}531.5~\mathrm{eV},$ , a feature of anion redo x,30,31 than the pristine sample, but obviously lower than what is usually observed in Li-rich layered cathodes.32 Meanwhile, a little peak shift at ${\sim}590.0~\mathrm{eV}$ is observed, which generally indicates a change in the metal−oxygen hybridization upon charging, as O-K XAS pre-edge reflects overlapping cation−anion features.33,34 In conjunction with the direct probes of Mn 3d states in Mn-L XAS (Figure S7) where no obvious variation in Mn states is detected, it is reasonable to speculate the change in metal−oxygen hybridization should be at least partially attributed to oxygen-redox activity. This low usage of oxygen redox could be another reason why the changes in O-K XAS are not as obvious as the case in Li-rich layered cathodes.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The experimental results above show active anion redox in $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathrm{Mn}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12}$ -like S-LMO and $\mathrm{Li_{4}M n}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12}\mathrm{-LiMn}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{4}\mathrm{-Li}_{2}\mathrm{MnO}_{3}$ nanocomposite SL-LMO in contrast to electrochemically unaccessible oxygen states in “conventional” $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathrm{Mn}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12}$ . The poor cycling of S-LMO and improved stability of SL-LMO after the second calcination step also suggest high-activity oxygen that can be released electrochemically or chemically. These observations indicate that oxygen ions in S-LMO and SL-LMO have distributed redox energy levels, which could arise from their distinct local structures and defective lattice under nonequilibrium synthesis conditions. This is shown by the following first-principles calculations. In “conventional” spinel-structure $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathrm{Mn}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12},$ three Li sit in the tetrahedral sites, and one Li and five Mn sit in the octahedral sites in an ordering manner. As a result, oxygen ions are either coordinated by three octahedral Mn and one tetrahedral Li (Figure 5a) or by two octahedral Mn, one octahedral Li, and one tetrahedral Li (Figure $5\mathrm{b}$ ). As shown by the projected density of states (DOS) in Figure 5a,b, the latter has more occupied high-energy oxygen $2p$ states close to the Fermi level $E_{\mathrm{{F}}}$ than the former, because more ${\mathrm{O}}{-}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{n}$ bonds and hence more hybridization between O $2p$ and Mn 3d orbitals. Yet neither oxygen ion is electrochemically active at least below 4.8 V versus $\mathrm{Li^{+}/L i}$ . In comparison, if we break down the cation ordering and create octahedral-Li/Mn-disordered $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathrm{Mn}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12},$ oxygen ions would be coordinated (i) by three octahedral Mn and one tetrahedral Li (Figure 5c), (ii) by two octahedral $\mathrm{{Mn},}$ one octahedral Li, and one tetrahedral Li (Figure 5d), (iii) by one octahedral Mn, two octahedral Li, and one tetrahedral Li (Figure 5e), or (iv) by three octahedral Li and one tetrahedral Li (statistically having a low possibility to form under 1:5 octahedral $\mathrm{Li}/\mathrm{Mn}$ ratio). While the projected DOS of type-i and type-ii oxygen ions are similar to the corresponding ones in octahedral-Li/Mn-ordered $\mathrm{Li}_{4}\mathrm{Mn}_{5}\mathrm{O}_{12}$ (demonstrating the robustness of the analyzing approach to correlate local atomic and electronic structures38−40), high-activity oxygen ion is identified in Figure 5e with some unoccupied oxygen $2p$ states above $E_{\mathrm{{F}}}$ even in its fully lithiated form. In real materials, the variations in local structure and bonding can be further complicated with vacancies and other lattice defects in SLLMO nanoparticles, which would then create a broad, continuous, active oxygen-redox band (Figure S10, contributed mainly by O $2p$ states and partially by hybridized $\tan3\mathrm{d}$ states). This picture is fully consistent with our experimental findings of active anion redox. However, one should realize that the activity and stability of anion redox are competing with each other and they have to be balanced to achieve long-term cyclability. In this sense, while S-LMO contains many defects from grain boundaries, interfaces, and lattice defects and has active oxygen redox, it also suffers from extensive oxygen loss and fast degradations as can be seen from the DEMS and electrochemical data. In comparison, SL-LMO does better by releasing the “over-active” near-surface oxygen species in the composite. Therefore, oxygen activity and stability are better balanced and the key in practice is to make full use of electrochemically active oxygen states in the interior and to prerelease those near the surface with extremely high reactivity toward the electrolyte.

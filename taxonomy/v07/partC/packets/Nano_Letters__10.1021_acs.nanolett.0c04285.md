Previously, we reported on $\mathrm{Nbpe}_{6}\mathrm{Nce}_{6}$ (Figure 1A), an amphiphilic peptoid oligomer that self-assembles into stable, lipid-like membranes with hydrophobic inner layers and hydrophilic outer layers.18 To enable protein conjugation on the nanosheet surface, the peptoid sequence was modified by the introduction of a maleimide group at the final hydrophilic residue of the acidic sequence. The resulting oligomer (called MAL-Nbpe $\mathrm{_{6}N c e_{6}},$ Figure 1A) is expected to display maleimide groups on either side of the surface when incorporated within nanosheets. The $\mathrm{Nbpe}_{6}\mathrm{Nce}_{6}$ and MAL-Nbpe $6^{\mathrm{Nce}_{6}}$ oligomers were produced by solid-phase synthesis and purified by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), and their integrity was confirmed by liquid chromatography−mass spectrometry (LCMS) (Figures S1 and S2). - Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Engineering Surface-Exposed Cysteines in Car9- Tagged sfGFP Derivatives and Characterizing Their Reactivities. Previously, we fused the Car9 dodecamer (DSARGFKKPGKR) to the C-terminus and within permissive loop 9 of the sfGFP framework to produce proteins sfGFP$\mathrm{Ca}\bar{{\bf r}}9^{30}$ and sfGFP::Car9,32 respectively. These two solidbinding proteins have a high affinity for silica due to the display of the Car9 SBP on the “bottom” or “top” of sfGFP (Figure 2A). To enable conjugation to maleimide-derivatized nanosheets in a manner that would result in the display of the Car9 moiety to the bulk solvent, we engineered solvent-accessible cysteine (Cys) residues on opposite sides of the sfGFP $\beta$ -barrel (Figure 2A). In sfGFP(R2C)::Car9 (thereafter abbreviated as (R2C)::Car9), an Arg2-Cys mutation was introduced at the N-terminus of sfGFP::Car9. Similarly, a Gly51-Cys mutation was built into loop 3 of sfGFP-Car9 to produce sfGFP(G51C)-Car9 (abbreviated as (G51C)-Car9). Nearby Cys48 was converted to a serine to prevent the formation of an undesirable disulfide bond. Finally, in sfGFP-C255::Car9 (abbreviated as (C255)::Car9), a cysteine residue was added to the C-terminus of sfGFP::Car9. All three constructs could be purified by silica affinity chromatography,28,36 confirming that the Car9 segment is fully functional for silica binding.  To determine how the chemical and structural context of thiol presentation would affect conjugation yields, we first reacted MAL-Nbpe $\mathsf{\Pi}_{6}\mathrm{Nce}_{6}$ oligomers with the three purified proteins under optimized conditions (see Methods in the SI section) and analyzed reaction products by denaturing and reducing polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis. Protein bands exhibiting a $2\mathrm{kDa}$ increase in molecular massthe $M_{\mathrm{r}}$ of MAL$\mathrm{Nbpe}_{6}\mathrm{Nce}_{6}$ were observed in all cases, denoting successful protein−peptoid conjugation (Figure 2B, arrow 2). The reaction was highly specific as evidenced by the fact that we could not detect higher-molecular-mass bands in control experiments conducted with wild-type sfGFP and Car9 derivatives lacking engineered Cys residues (Figure S4). We also observed faint bands that were an additional $2\mathrm{kDa}$ heavier and approximately constant in intensity (Figure 2B, arrow 3). These species disappeared upon prolonged boiling of the samples (Figure S5), indicating that they correspond to protein−peptoid conjugates containing an extra peptoid chain associated through peptoid−peptoid interactions.  Video densitometric analysis of band intensities corresponding to unreacted proteins (arrow 1) and protein−peptoid conjugates (arrows 2 and 3) revealed that Cys51 had the highest propensity to react with MAL-Nbpe $6^{\mathrm{Nce}_{6}}$ while Cys2 was the least reactive. We obtained similar results when substituting a hydrophilic, maleimide-modified 5 kDa poly(ethylene glycol) (MAL-PEG 5K) for MAL-Nbpe $6^{\mathrm{Nce}}6$ . In these experiments, $83\%$ of (G51C)-Car9 reacted with the oligomer, while only $29\%$ of (R2C)::Car9 and $58\%$ of (C255)::Car9 did (Figure S6). We conclude that the thiol group of Cys51 is most accessible and that its reactivity is largely unaffected by the hydrophobicity of MAL-Nbpe $\mathsf{\Pi}_{6}\mathrm{Nce}_{6}$ .  Next, we asked how the presentation of maleimide groups in an extended 2D nanosheet structure would affect conjugation yields. To this end, we reacted the three proteins with preformed peptoid nanosheets prepared at a 1:1 molar ratio of MAL$\mathrm{Nbpe}_{6}\mathrm{Nce}_{6}$ to $\mathrm{Nbpe}_{6}\mathrm{Nce}_{6}$ . Figure 2C shows that the ability of (R2C)::Car9 to form a thioether bond with MA $\mathrm{L}{-}\mathrm{Nb}\mathrm{pe}_{6}\mathrm{Nce}_{6}$ oligomers decreased by half and that the reactivity of (G51C)- Car9 became comparable to that of (C255)::Car9, which remained unchanged at ${\sim}50\%$ . We attribute the decrease in (G51C)-Car9 conjugation efficiency to protein accessibility issues associated with steric or electrostatic hindrances caused by the presentation of maleimides groups on the surface of the peptoid nanosheet. On the basis of the high reactivity of Cys51 and from the observation that $\mathrm{Car}9$ exhibits a higher affinity for silica when it is fused to the C-terminal of sfGFP compared to when it is inserted within loop 9 of the protein,37 we selected (G51C)-Car9 for subsequent work.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Nanoparticle-Mediated Assembly of Peptoid−Protein Hybrid Nanosheets. To demonstrate that the SPB segment of covalently coupled proteins was functional for silica binding, we incubated hybrid nanosheets that had been produced with $1\mu\mathrm{M}$ or $10~\mu\mathrm{M}$ (G51C)-Car9 with $10~\mathrm{nm}$ silica nanoparticles encapsulating red fluorescent dye rhodamine (thereafter referred to as RhSiNP). Merging of fluorescence microscopy images acquired in the green (sfGFP) and red (rhodamine) emission windows demonstrated spatial colocalization of the two signals and hence that of the two materials (Figures 4A,E and S11). By contrast, only diffuse rhodamine fluorescence was observed when underivatized peptoid nanosheets were mixed with RhSiNPs, ruling out the possibility of nonspecific interactions between these two species in the absence of conjugated protein (Figure S12).  AFM and SEM imaging showed that silica nanoparticles were present on the surface of both nanosheet preparations and that they induced sheet stacking and the formation of a multilayer material in which peptoid, protein, and inorganic layers alternate (Figures 4 and S13−S15). As expected from the lower protein coverage of nanosheets reacted with $1\mu\mathrm{M}$ (G51C)-Car9, some regions of the peptoid−protein hybrids remained free of nanoparticles (e.g., solid arrow in Figure 4C). Also, as anticipated, hybrid peptoid−protein nanosheets produced with $10~\mu\mathrm{M}$ (G51C)-Car9 were uniformly decorated with nanoparticles, leading to the stacking of the various layers (Figure 4F).  Using contour line analysis, we identified three such layers in the image of Figure 4F (dashed boxes) that accounted for a total height of $65\pm2\mathrm{nm}$ (Figure S13). Considering that the height of a dry peptoid nanosheet derivatized with $10~\mu\mathrm{M}$ protein is ${\sim}9$ nm (Figure 3C) and that the silica nanoparticles used in our experiments are ${\sim}10~\mathrm{nm}$ in diameter, this height is consistent with a structure in which three peptoid nanosheets are bridged and decorated on their top and bottom faces by nanoparticles (total calculated height of $^{67\mathrm{nm}}$ ). SEM images of cross sections confirmed the formation of multilayered architectures of four to five nanosheets with 22 to $25~\mathrm{nm}$ separating the center line of consecutive nanosheets (Figures $^\mathrm{4D,H}$ and S15). This distance is consistent with the spacing imposed by two peptoid lamellae $\left({\sim}3\mathrm{nm}\right)$ , two layers of proteins $(\bar{\sim}9\mathrm{nm})$ , and a connecting layer of RhSiNP $\stackrel{\prime}{\sim}10\mathrm{nm},$ ).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Here, we report the synthesis of a 2D FAN via the double condensation reaction of hexaaminobenzene (HAB) trihydrochloride and pyrene-4,5,9,10-tetraone (PTK) in trifluoromethanesulfonic acid (TFMSA) at $175~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ (Figure 1). The energy gain by aromatization[15] from the condensation between HAB and PTK drives the spontaneous forward reaction, resulting in a fused aromatic layered 2D framework structure with evenly distributed holes and 12 nitrogen atoms in each hole. Its empirical formula at the basal plane is $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ (The structure was named based on the basal plane’s stoichiometry). Its entire formula ratio is $\mathrm{C}_{30}\mathrm{H}_{17}\mathrm{N}_{6}$ . (Figure S1, Supporting Information).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The crystallinity and stacking pattern of the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ were studied by theoretical simulations and powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) pattern (Figure 2a). The lattice packing was identified by lattice modeling and Pawley refinement of the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ structure employing material studio software. The three possible stacking patterns, staggered (AB), eclipsed (AA), and ABC packing, were constructed (Figure 2b–d; and Tables S1–S3, Supporting Information). The PXRD pattern of the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ disclosed two major broad peaks in the spectrum (Figure 2a). The diffraction peak (003) at $26.26^{\circ}$ corresponds to an interlayer distance ( $\mathrm{\Delta}d$ -spacing) of $3.40\mathrm{~\AA~}$ . The peak at $10.38^{\circ}$ with the $d$ -spacing of  $8.52\textup{\AA}$ corresponds to the (101) plane of the ABC stacking from the simulated structure (Figure 1b,c). The unit cell parameters and the simulated patterns of $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ were collected after geometrical energy minimization by a universal force field. The experimental PXRD pattern corresponds to the simulated pattern of the ABC stacking model of the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ structure (Figure 2d). The Pawley refined pattern replicated the experimentally obtained profile with only $R_{\mathrm{p}}=5.22\%$ and $R_{\mathrm{wp}}=7.96\%$ deviations, confirming that the structure of $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ is an extended 2D hexagonal lattice ordering with a 3.40 Å $(0.34\mathrm{nm})$ ) interlayer distance in the $_z$ -axis.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) showed that the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ had high thermal stability in both air and nitrogen atmospheres (Figure 3a). In a nitrogen atmosphere, the weight loss was only $17\%$ at $800~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . Such high thermal stability of the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ structure is associated with its stable fused aromatic ring structure and large grain size (minimal contribution from dangling edge groups).  To investigate the porosity and surface area of the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ , the nitrogen sorption isotherm was measured at $77\mathrm{~K~}$ (Figure  3b). The nitrogen adsorption/desorption curves indicated the nonporous nature of the material[40] and the Brunauer– Emmett–Teller (BET) specific surface area $(S_{\mathrm{BET}})$ was found to be $\approx38.4\mathrm{m}^{2}\mathrm{g}^{-1}$ . Such low $\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{BET}}$ is consistent with the XRD (Figure 2a), suggesting that the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ layers are tightly packed in ABC stacking. As a result, available pores for nitrogen adsorption are almost entirely blocked. This negligible mesoporosity of the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ can be attributed to edges and/or defects, which contribute to very weak peak intensities at 12.11 and $21.96~\mathrm{nm}$ in nonlocal density functional theory (NLDFT) pore size distribution plot (inset, Figure 3b).  Spectra from X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), which probes the chemical composition of a material, showed only carbon (C 1s), nitrogen (N 1s), and oxygen (O 1s) peaks (Figure  3c). The C 1s peak can be resolved into three peaks, i.e., ${\mathrm{C}}{\mathrm{-}}{\mathrm{C}}s p^{2}$ (284.4 eV), C-N $s p^{2}$ $(285.6~\mathrm{eV})$ , and C-heteroatom $(288.4~\mathrm{{eV})}$ (Figure S3a, Supporting Information). Similarly, the N 1s peak can be deconvoluted into 398.8 and $400.7~\mathrm{eV}$ (Figure S3b, Supporting Information), which can be assigned to pyrazine nitrogen in the basal structure and $\mathrm{-NH}_{2}$ at the edges, respectively. The presence of O 1s can be ascribed to the remaining keto groups at its edges and adsorbed air/moisture (Figure S3c, Supporting Information). Field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) with corresponding energydispersive X-ray spectroscopy (SEM-EDS) was employed to find the elemental composition of the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ (Figure S4, Supporting Information). The EDS spectrum again showed the presence of only carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen (Figure S4b, Supporting Information).  The morphology of the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ was studied with FE-SEM, which confirmed a layered texture with grain size as large as a few hundred micrometers (Figure 3d).  To further investigate the crystallinity of the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ , high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM) was conducted. Both HR-TEM image (Figure 3e) and its corresponding fast Fourier transform (FFT) image (inset, Figure 3e) of the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ revealed a locally ordered pattern with an interlayer $d$ -spacing of $0.34~\mathrm{nm}$ , which is in good agreement with the (003) facet in XRD pattern (Figure 2a).  Given the structural confirmation, the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ solution in TFMSA was drop cast on a $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ substrate and annealed at a suitable temperature in an argon atmosphere. SEM images of the film on the $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ substrate (Figure S5a, Supporting Information) and after scratching with a diamond knife (Figure S5b,c, Supporting Information) reveal the formation of smooth films on the substrate. Large-area free-standing thin film (over $20\times20\mathrm{mm}^{2})$ was formed after annealing the drop cast films on $\mathrm{SiO}_{2}$ substrate, and then etching with $1\%$ hydrofluoric acid (HF) and transferring to flexible polyethylene terephthalate (PET) substrates (Figure 3f; and Figure S6, Supporting Information).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Figure 4a shows optical images of pristine $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ thin flakes with various thicknesses, e.g., 43, 53, 62, and $84\mathrm{nm}$ , as measured by atomic force microscopy (AFM, Figure S10, Supporting Information). The color of the pristine $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ flakes was changed depending on their thicknesses. The electrical properties of pristine $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ thin films were measured by applying a gate voltage from $-80$ to $+80~\mathrm{V}.$ Notably, the on/off ratio $(I_{\mathrm{on}}/I_{\mathrm{off}})$ increased with decreasing film thickness as shown in the transfer curve of pristine $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ (Figure $^{4\mathrm{b},\mathrm{c})}$ . The $I_{\mathrm{on}}/I_{\mathrm{off}}$ trend with respect to film thickness was similar to that of carbon-based materials such as multilayered reduced graphene oxides.[41,42] For example, $43~\mathrm{nm}$ thick, pristine $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ flake showed semimetallic behavior with an $I_{\mathrm{on}}/I_{\mathrm{off}}$ of 1.72. On the other hand, $84~\mathrm{nm}$ thick, pristine $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ flake showed metallic behavior.  In addition, the Dirac point, where the electrons and holes are neutralized, was positively shifted from− 23 to $-6~\mathrm{V}$ with an increase in flake thickness from 43 to $62\mathrm{nm}$ . The detailed electrical properties are listed in Table S5 (Supporting Information). Remarkably, prominent electron mobility $(996~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{V}^{-1}~\mathrm{s}^{-1})$ and hole mobility $(501~\mathrm{cm}^{2}~\mathrm{V}^{-1}~\mathrm{s}^{-1})$ were observed in the $43\mathrm{nm}$ thick $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ film. These are the highest values with discernible $I_{\mathrm{on}}/I_{\mathrm{off}}$ in pristine polymeric materials, to the best of our knowledge. The on/off ratio in $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ might be tunable in order to widen the bandgap by using a quantum confinement effect (nanoribbons and nanomeshes), by controlling the number of stacking layers,[43–45] single-electron transistor formation,[46,47] and chemical modification[48,49] and interstitial atomic doping (e.g., by boron and nitrogen).[50–52] It should be noted that the thinnest flakes isolated by PDMS stamping in this study contained multilayers of $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ . Considering the multilayer, multicrystalline nature of the flakes, the transport performance shown here appears to be representative of a bulk material, although the influence of defects and domain boundaries still cannot be excluded.  Recently, 2D metal–organic framework films comprised of benzene-fused metal-bis(dithiolene) based on nickel[53,54] and copper[55] complexes have shown temperature-dependent electrical conductivity values of 160 and $1580~\mathrm{S~cm}^{-1}$ . Figure 4d is a schematic diagram of an experimental setup for HCl doping of $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ in a Pyrex glass container with an extra loading reservoir. Concentrated hydrochloric acid (HCl, $35\mathrm{wt}\%$ was injected into the reservoir after being evacuated up to $1\times10^{-3}$ torr. The container’s temperature was controlled from 20 to $160~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ to determine the change in electrical properties in relation to doping temperature, as shown in Figure 4e.  Field-effect mobility was not observable immediately after HCl doping at room temperature. The initial conductivity of the sample was $7.2~\mathrm{S~cm}^{-1}$ at room temperature. With increasing doping temperature, the conductivity of the $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ was saturated to approximately $1\times10^{3}\mathrm{Scm}^{-1}$ at $120~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . A maximum conductivity of $1038\mathrm{~S~}\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ was obtained at a doping temperature of $160~^{\circ}C$ . These results demonstrated that HCl molecules′ physical adsorption as dopants on the surface of $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ significantly enhanced conductivity by up to 2 orders of magnitude, compared with pristine $\mathrm{C}_{5}\mathrm{N}$ .

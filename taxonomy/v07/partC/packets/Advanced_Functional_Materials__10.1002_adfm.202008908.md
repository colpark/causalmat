- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): We first examined the surface morphology of the $\mathrm{Cs}_{0.05}(\mathrm{FA}_{0.6}\mathrm{MA}_{0.4})_{0.95}\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{I}_{0.6}\mathrm{Br}_{0.4})_{3}$ perovskite films with different amounts of PEAI additives by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) shown in Figure 1a–d, as well as the crystalline phases formed using XRD patterns in Figure  1e. We also obtained topography and contact potential difference (CPD) maps measured in the dark using Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM), which is displayed in Figure S1, Supporting Information. As illustrated in Figure 1a, a nicely packed polycrystalline film is formed (reference sample), which is also evident from the 3D topography image in Figure S1a, Supporting Information. The corresponding CPD map in Figure S1e, Supporting Information, shows that the grain boundaries have relatively lower CPD compared to grain interiors. The perovskite film has two main peaks at $14.1^{\circ}$ and $28.1^{\circ}$ , which are indexed to the (100) and (200) planes, respectively, and there is no presence of a ${\mathrm{PbI}}_{2}$ peak. When $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ of PEAI was deposited, PEAI was conformally coated on the surface in its salt form with still distinguishable apparent grains. This reduces roughness of the surface (mean surface roughness reduces from 25.30 to $15.05\mathrm{nm}$ ) while maintaining the original round-shaped grain features as displayed in the 3D topography map in Figure S1b, Supporting Information. We also observe tiny islands at the grain boundaries in the SEM image but not in the CPD image (Figure S1, Supporting Information), which indicates that the islands have similar work function (WF) to the surrounding area. This implies that the islands have identical electrical and chemical properties. The PEAI signature $(5.4^{\circ})^{[21]}$ is yet absent in the associated XRD pattern (Figure  1e). When the PEAI amount was increased to $5~\mathrm{mg}$ , a PEAI peak $(5.4^{\circ})$ appears in XRD pattern and the perovskite surface coverage started to show inhomogeneities, forming regions of interconnected grains (Figure  1c). The corresponding 3D topography map (Figure S1c, Supporting Information) shows non-uniformly distributed protrusive peaks and the original grain shape disappears. Also, the relevant CPD map shows inhomogeneous CPD with large variations, implying random aggregation of PEAI. As $7.5~\mathrm{mg}$ of PEAI was deposited on the WBG perovskite layer, a non-uniform distribution of clusters of $\mathrm{Pb}\mathrm{I}_{2}$ in the form of white crystals was observed in the SEM image, in good agreement with the appearance of the $\mathrm{Pb}\mathrm{I}_{2}$ $(12.6^{\circ})$ peak in the associated XRD pattern shown in Figure  1e. It is interesting that there was no $\mathrm{Pb}\mathrm{I}_{2}$ in the reference sample, but it has appeared with a low-dimensional perovskite $(\mathrm{PEA})_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{4}$ $(5.53^{\circ}$ , $10.9^{\circ}$ , $16.4^{\circ}$ , $21.9^{\circ}$ , $27.4^{\circ}$ , and $33.1^{\circ})$ peak.[16] We speculate that the excessive amounts of PEAI decomposes the original perovskite structure into a halide salt and lead halide and reacts with the lead halide to form 2D $\mathrm{(PEA)}_{2}\mathrm{PbI}_{4}$ . However, this reaction does not occur uniformly, thus, there is always unreacted lead halide left as well as unreacted PEAI. This is evident from the corresponding CPD map (Figure S1h, Supporting Information), which shows round-shaped features $(\mathrm{Pb}\mathrm{I}_{2})$ that have relatively lower CPD compared to the surrounding. The corresponding 3D topography maps (Figure S1d, Supporting Information) also contains non-uniform protrusive peaks with loss of the original grain features. Figure  1f displays CPD pixel distribution plots of each sample obtained from Figure S1e–h, Supporting Information. Full width half maximum (FWHM) of Gaussian fitted curves of each sample are also presented. As expected, the FWHM is relatively larger for the 5 and $7.5~\mathrm{mg}$ samples compare to the reference sample and the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ sample in Figure 1f. Also, it is noticeable that the average CPD decreases as the mount of PEAI increases. Given that the WF of the tip $(\Phi_{\mathrm{tip}})$ is $-5.1\mathrm{eV}$ (gold), the reduced CPD $\left(\mathrm{CPD}=\Phi_{\mathrm{tip}}-\Phi_{\mathrm{sample}}\right)$ indicates that the WF of the sample becomes closer to that of the tip. In KPFM, a nullifying voltage or CPD is created and measured in order to balance the the WF difference between the tip $(\Phi_{\mathrm{tip}})$ and the sample $(\Phi_{\mathrm{sample}})$ . Note that a grounding point of our KPFM setup is $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ , thus, the voltage that is required to balance the WF of the Au and the $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}$ through the perovskite layer reduces when PEAI is deposited onto the sample surface. We will see the consequence of this result later using surface photovoltage (SPV) measurement.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The surface chemistry of the reference sample and the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ PEAI deposited sample was evaluated using XPS measurements. The lead (Pb) 4f, nitrogen (N) 1s, and oxygen (O) 1s spectra are provided in Figure $_{2\mathrm{a-c}}$ . Both Pb and N spectra shift to a lower binding energy after deposition of the PEAI, which is attributed to different intermolecular binding energy from PEAI– $\mathrm{\cdotPbI}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{MAI-PbI}_{2}$ .[21] As can be seen from N 1s spectra in Figure 2b, $\mathsf{C}\mathrm{-N}$ (400.5 eV) and ${\tt C}\mathrm{=N}$ (402.4 eV) peaks, where the latter is from the $\mathrm{FA^{+}}$ cation, can be observed. After the PEAI deposition, the ${\tt C}{=}{\tt N}$ bond becomes stronger while $\mathsf{C}\mathrm{-N}$ bond intensity is reduced. Previous XPS results on PEAI deposition showed that the ${\tt C}{=}{\tt N}$ bond also represents the presence of $\mathrm{PEA^{+}}$ .[22] These results indicate that by introducing the PEAI onto the underlying perovskite film surface, it interacts with the surface elements of the perovskite layer inducing a shift in the ${\mathrm{C}}{=}\mathrm{N}$ bond XPS feature, compared to that formed by the mere presence of $\mathrm{FA^{+}}$ . This results in the formation of a new layer, possibly, PEAI rich layer, onto the original film which is evident from transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images in Figure S3, Supporting Information. Also, it can further be seen that the O spectra does not exist for the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ PEAI deposited sample, whereas the reference sample shows a clear peak at ${\approx}532~\mathrm{eV}$ in Figure 2c. We presume that the hydrophobic nature of the phenethylammonium prevented moisture deposition on the surface. As evident from the XPS results, PEAI interacts strongly with the reference sample, which could cure the uncoordinated atoms and ionic vacancies at the surface of the 3D perovskite layer.[23] In order to observe whether the PEAI interacted with the uncoordinated atoms at the grain boundaries, we performed TEM with energy dispersive x-ray spectroscopy (see Figure S4, Supporting Information). As can be seen, vertically oriented grain boundaries (darker contrast) are clearly visible. However, there is no sign of higher concentration of C, N, and I elements at the grain boundaries compared to the grain interiors. Our observation resembles the recently reported work by Zhang et al.[24] They observed that PEA only diffuses to the top surface of the grain boundaries when $2\textrm{h}$ of annealing was applied.  To elucidate the impact of PEAI interface passivation on the crystallinity of the sample, we perform XRD pole figure measurement of (110) orientation at $14^{\circ}$ for the reference sample and the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample, which are displayed in Figure  2d. While both samples exhibit preferential orientation, crystals of the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample show less crystallographic misorientation, implying higher crystallinity as evidenced by a more concentrated central peak as shown in Figure  2e. As evident from the XPS results, PEAI interacts strongly with the reference sample, which could cure the uncoordinated atoms and ionic vacancies at the surface. Consequently, crystals that were originally slightly tilted by the missing atoms are now properly reoriented as illustrated in Figure 2f. Figure S5, Supporting Information, shows the UV– visible spectra of the PEAI deposited samples with different concentration showing slightly higher absorption, which could be attributed to the enhanced crystallinity.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Given that morphological variation has substantial effects on the performance of PSCs,[14,25] investigation of the spatial distribution of photo-generated charge carriers at the nanoscale is crucial to understand the device operation mechanisms. We used the KPFM to measure the CPD in the dark as well as under indoor relevant illumination for the reference sample and the $2\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample, both deposited on $\mathrm{SnO}_{2}/$ ITO. Figure 3a shows the schematic of the KPFM measurement setup used. A topography map can be obtained in the first pass scan (see Figure S1, Supporting Information) and the CPD is spatially resolved in the second pass scan (Figure 3d–g).  Considering that there are no excess carriers generated in dark at the absorber, the CPD measured in the dark represents the WF difference between the metal cantilever and the sample surface. As shown in Figure 3a, the white LED is used to illuminate the samples from the glass side so to investigate the charge-carrier dynamics under indoor conditions. Upon illumination, photo-generated electrons are transported toward the cathode (ITO) through the ETL, whereas photo-excited holes can diffuse to the surface of the film and accumulate there.  Figures  3b,c display CPD distribution graphs under dark and light for the reference sample and the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ PEAI deposited sample, respectively, obtained from Figure $3\mathrm{d-g}$ . As discussed above, the peak CPD value of the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample in the dark is smaller than the reference sample by ${\approx}316~\mathrm{mV}.$ Under illumination, the CPD peak value for the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample shifts from 349 to $669\mathrm{mV},$ , as shown in Figure 3c, while that of the reference sample is from 665 to $865\mathrm{mV}_{\mathrm{i}}$ , shown in Figure 3b. A relevant band diagram in the dark and under illumination for the reference sample and the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample is shown in Figure S6, Supporting Information. By subtracting the dark CPD $\mathrm{(CPD_{dark})}$ from the measured values under illumination $(\mathrm{CPD_{light}})$ , the SPV parameter is calculated, which is an indication of the extent of charge-carrier accumulation (here, holes) at the top surface. The average SPV value of ${\approx}320~\mathrm{mV}$ for the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ PEAI deposited sample is ${\approx}120\mathrm{mV}$ higher than that of the reference sample $(\approx200\mathrm{mV})$ under $200\mathrm{lux}$ illumination. This suggests that more photo-excited holes are accumulated at the surface of the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample inducing a greater shift in the holes’ quasi-Fermi level toward the valence band, due likely to the reduced non-radiative recombination in the PEAI deposited sample, compared to the reference sample. This is consistent with the spectral photoluminescence measurement results of HTL/perovskite/glass samples, where the perovskite layer was either the reference sample or the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample. The relative PL intensity of the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample was higher than the reference sample without PEAI both under 0.1- and $10–\mathrm{Sun}$ equivalent photon flux illumination (see Figure S7, Supporting Information). This indicates that Spiro-OMeTAD/perovskite interface of the $2\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample has lower non-radiative recombination rate at two widely different illumination intensities.[26] Assuming that the very thin layer of PEAI does not affect the optical path of the incident light within the measured structure and hence does not influence the generation rate, the higher PL intensity of the PEAI deposited sample implies a higher charge-carrier lifetime in this sample as compared to the reference film at the corresponding carrier injection levels. The spectral PL results are consistent with the apparent lifetimes extracted from time-resolved PL (TRPL) measurements performed on the films deposited on glass substrate (see Figure S8, Supporting Information). The TRPL-extracted apparent lifetimes over a wide charge-carrier density levels is higher for the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample as compared to the reference absorber, pointing to potential surface defect passivation upon treatment with the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposition. It is noteworthy that considering the initial sharp decrease in the intensity of the TRPL trace for the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ PEAI deposited sample is observed which can be responsible from the greater hole accumulation at the surface of this sample. This is also evident from the SPV analysis, possibly due to the presence of trapped holes at the shallow trap states near the valence band acting like background dopants, formed upon PEAI deposition.  The CPD maps in the dark for the reference sample and the $2\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample are shown in Figures 3d,e, respectively, and the corresponding topography maps are presented in Figure S1, Supporting Information. The grain boundaries of the reference sample have lower CPD than the grain interiors and individual grains can be clearly identified by the CPD variation between the grain interiors and the grain boundaries, indicative of variation in the Fermi level between the grain boundaries and the grain interiors. Previously, we reported that ionic defects which create energy levels near the valence band (cation vacancies or halide interstitials) can be segregated at the grain boundaries that change the WF at the grain boundaries.[27] After the deposition of $2~\mathrm{mg}$ of PEAI on the surface, the CPD variation between the grain interiors and the grain boundaries almost diminishes, indicating that the surface electronic properties become more homogeneous. This result is consistent with the above XPS results, which pointed to the interaction between $\mathrm{PEA^{+}}$ and the underneath WBG perovskite surface, possibly filling the cation vacancies. It is likely that reducing the concentration of these cation vacancies led to SPV enhancement in the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ PEAI deposited sample. It has been reported that the halide salts (e.g., MAI) at the top surface and grain boundaries can be easily evaporated during the annealing stage of the fabrication process of the perovskite film, resulting in lead halides-rich surface with deficiency in halide salts.[16] It is likely that the PEAI salt reacts with the uncoordinated atoms at the surface which improves the structural crystallinity (see XRD pole maps shown in Figure 2d,e) as well as enabling the chemical and electrical homogeneity of the film.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To further investigate the energy band diagram change induced by the introduction of the PEAI deposition, UPS was performed. As shown in Figure 4a, the Fermi level of the reference sample and the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample are $-5.04$ and $-5.35~\mathrm{eV},$ respectively, as calculated by a linear extrapolation of the secondary electron cut-off. The valence band maximum (VBM) of the reference sample and the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample are presented in Figure 4b. The VBM for reference sample is $1.65~\mathrm{eV}$ away from Fermi level while that of the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ PEAI deposited sample is 1.11  eV away from Fermi level, as determined from a linear extrapolation of the valence band measurements. This indicates that the valence band in the $2\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample locates closer to its Fermi level compared to the reference sample (i.e., extrapolated valence band from the UPS measurement is the difference between the semiconductor Fermi level and VBM). The VBM of the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample, located at $-6.46~\mathrm{eV}$ with respect to the vacuum, is higher than that of the reference, at $-6.69~\mathrm{eV.}$ By combining the UPS results and the optical bandgap values extracted from absorbance spectra measurements (Figure S3b, Supporting Information) the conduction band minimum of the two samples can be calculated, being $-4.94$ and $-4.72~\mathrm{eV}$ for the reference sample and the 2mg PEAI deposited sample, respectively. We thus draw the energy level diagram of the reference sample and the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample, depicted in Figure  4c. The downshifted Fermi level of the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample makes the surface p-type, which facilitates transfer of holes to the HTL. The upward shift of the VBM of the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited sample improves its energy band alignment with that of the HTL, Spiro-OMeTAD, potentially contributing to the reduced voltage losses compared to the reference sample (see Figure 5c).[30]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Figure  5a presents the reverse and forward light current density–voltage $(J-V)$ curves of the indoor PSCs measured at  1000 lux using a cool white LED. It is clear that the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ PEAI deposited device shows smaller hysteretic effect as opposed to the reference device. The hysteresis phenomenon has been commonly observed in organic–inorganic halide PSCs, which has been attributed to the presence of interfacial defect states as well as ion migration.[32] In addition, the majority of the detrimental recombination losses occurring in perovskite cells has been demonstrated to occur at the interface of the device.[33] We expect that the hole-selective behavior of the PEAI on the surface of the WBG perovskite, as supported by the SPV, PL, TRPL, and SCLC, reduces the interfacial defect density and their trapping behavior, alleviating the hysteresis. Furthermore, the diffusion rate of the mobile ions has been shown to decrease when long chain alkylammonium cations are used in low-dimensional perovskites, which could further contribute to constraining the ionic movement at the HTL/perovskite interface of the 2 mg PEAI deposited device.[34]  Figure  5b presents the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of the perovskite devices without and with $2\mathrm{mg}$ of PEAI as well as the spectrum of both cool white LED and halogen. At short wavelengths where most of the charge-carriers are generated closer to the ETL, the EQE response of the two cells is the same. This is not surprising considering the potentially similar recombination behavior at the front surface of the cells. In the mid-wavelength range toward the longer wavelengths, except for a hump at ${\approx}675~\mathrm{nm}$ , the EQE of the PEAI deposited PSC is slightly larger than that of the reference device, resulting in higher a current density $(J_{\mathrm{SC}})$ in the former (Figure  5d). This is possibly caused by an improved charge-carrier collection efficiency at the Spiro-OMeTAD/perovskite interface and the reduced charge-carrier loss due to non-radiative recombination in the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ PEAI deposited device.  The noticeable improvement in the PV parameters when $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ of PEAI was deposited to the reference device characterized under different intensities (LED and halogen) is shown in Figure  5c–f corresponding to the $J-V$ curves of Figure S11, Supporting Information. As compared to the $2\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited device, the $V_{\mathrm{OC}}$ of the reference device decreased noticeably when the illumination intensity was reduced from $800\mathrm{lux}$ (0.92 V) to 200 lux (0.87 V). There is a slight fluctuation in the fill factor of the reference device as the light intensity increases; however, the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ PEAI deposited device shows an increasing trend. The PV parameters of both the reference devices and the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ PEAI deposited device under 200, 400, 800, and $1000\mathrm{{lux}}$ using halogen and cool white LED can be found in Tables S2 and S3, Supporting Information, respectively. Figure S12, Supporting Information, shows summary of shunt resistance and series resistance values as a function of light intensities. For the series resistance, the reference samples show over $40\%$ higher compared to the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ PEAI deposited device at $200\mathrm{lux}$ while there is no significant difference among other intensities. On the other hand, shunt resistance is higher for the $2~\mathrm{mg}$ PEAI deposited device for all intensities compared to the reference sample. Thus, the overall enhancement of fill factor for the $2{\mathrm{~mg}}$ PEAI deposited device is due to the improved shunt resistance as it is responsible from the reduced trap states, and non-radiative recombination, and the homogeneous charge transport properties.

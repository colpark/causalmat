- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): AFE structure has been widely reported in $(\bf{B i}_{1/2}\bf{N a}_{1/2})\it{T i O}_{3}$ (BNT）[7-13], $\mathsf{N a N b O}_{3}$ (NN） [5,14,15] and $\mathsf{A g N b O}_{3}$ (AN) [16– 19] based lead-free perovskites, in which pure NN and AN are thought to be typical lead-free AFEs. NN is a well-documented AFE perovskite compound with a complex sequence of polymorphic phase transition with temperature, in which at least two kinds of AFEs with Pbma (P phase) and Pnma (R phase) space groups were reported in a wide temperature range of $-100{\sim}480{\circ}C,$ as shown in Fig. 1a [20-22]. However, the AFE $\mathsf{P}$ phase in pure NN ceramic at room temperature usually irreversibly transforms into an FE state under a large driving field of ${\sim}9\mathrm{kV}/\mathrm{mm}$ leading to a square P-E loop after the first-cycle loop [23-25]. Lots of attempts have been made to enhance the stability of the AFE $\mathsf{P}$ phase or stabilize the high-temperature AFE R phase close to room temperature by adding other $\mathsf{A B O}_{3}$ perovskites with lower tolerance factor or lower polarizability [5,15,26-28]. Similar to NN, typical double P-E loops as well as excellent energy-storage properties were obtained in AN-based solid solutions [16-18]. However, the high cost of $\mathsf{A g}$ would largely limit its further usage in industry.  Pure BNT shown in Fig. 1b was reported to have a phase transition from high-temperature prototypic cubic phase $\left(\mathrm{Pm}{}{}-3\mathrm{m}\right)$ to an AFE tetragonal $\left(\mathsf{A F E}_{\mathrm{T}}\right)$ phase with P4bm space group at \~540 $^{\circ}{\mathsf{C}},$ which possesses an unusual combination of in-phase oxygenoctahedral tilts (Glazer notation $a^{0}a^{0}c^{+}$ [29] and anti-parallel cation displacements along the polar axis [8]. The FE and AFE phases in BNT exhibit large spontaneous polarization and obvious dielectric relaxation behavior, thus leading to excellent energy storage and electrostrain behavior [9-11,30]. Local AFE components (i.e. intergrown microstructure) have also been reported in BNT at room temperature, and the fraction of such components increases with increasing temperature [13]. Yet, it is difficult to identify the AFE structure in BNT-based ceramic from the measurement of average structure and polarization-field response owing to the strong dielectric relaxation behavior.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Fig. 6a shows Raman spectra for $(1-x)\mathrm{NN}{-}x\mathrm{BNT}$ ceramics at room temperature to further confirm the phase structure of the studied samples, because Raman spectroscopy can probe a local ionic configuration within short-length scale [25,32,36]. The spectral deconvolution was performed using Gaussian-Lorentzian function. According to the fitting results, the wave number of several selected Raman peaks was plotted as a function of BNT content, as shown in Fig. 6b. The vibrations observed in Fig. 6a can be separated into translational modes of the isolated A site cations $(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{i}^{3+}$ and $\mathrm{Na^{+}}$ in the low wave number range of $<150~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ ) and internal modes of the coordination octahedra $(\upsilon1,\upsilon2,\upsilon3,\upsilon4,\upsilon5,$ and $\nu6)$ . In these modes, $\upsilon1{-}\upsilon3$ are stretching modes and $_{\upsilon4-\upsilon6}$ are bending modes [37]. Multi-peak Raman spectra below $300~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ can be obviously seen for the NN ceramic, which are characteristic of the AFE P phase based on large difference between Nb-O bond lengths induced by the antiparallel off-centering displacement of Nb. With increasing BNT content, the rise and fall of the Raman spectra below $300~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ become mild, although Raman peaks shift to lower wavenumbers. A drastic increase in the frequency for the Raman peaks around $175~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ , $250~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and $600~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ can be found as $\scriptstyle{x=0.16}$ , even though the amount of Raman peaks does not change. This should be related to the phase transition from the $\mathrm{{AFE}_{0}}$ Pbma (P phase) to the $\mathrm{AFE}_{0}$ Pnma (R phase), accompanying the decrease of the difference between B-O bond lengths in each oxygen octahedron [20]. An obvious broadening of the Raman bands centered at about $250~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and $600~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ at $x{\ge}0.2$ can be ascribed to the appearance of obvious dielectric relaxation behavior. Further broadening of Raman peaks as well as the emergence of Raman peaks at ${\sim}230~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ at $x{=}0.35$ indicates the entrance into the PE phase zone with Ccmm space group. The splitting of the $\mathrm{Ti}/\mathrm{Nb}{-}0$ modes $\upsilon5$ and $\upsilon4$ ）canbe observed in the composition range of $0.4{\le}x{\le}0.8$ , suggesting the tetragonal feature of the unit cell [38]. This is because the $\upsilon4$ mode represents the doubledegenerate O-B-O stretching vibration in which oxygen is situated along the $c$ -axis, however, the $\upsilon5$ mode corresponds to the tripledegenerate O-B-O bending vibration where the oxygen is located at the ab plane perpendicular to the $c\cdot$ -axis. The high-frequency NbO stretching bands around $600~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and the $\upsilon5$ bending mode tend to shift to lower frequencies with increasing BNT content, indicating that the $\scriptstyle\mathrm{Nb}/\mathrm{Ti}-0$ vibrations become softened gradually as a result of weakening of the bonding between B-site cations and oxygen. The weakening of B-O bonds is compatible with the appearance of the dielectric relaxation behavior since the loss of hybridization between the empty $\mathrm{Nb}^{5+}/\mathrm{Ti}^{4+}$ d-orbitals and the oxygen $p$ -orbitals is closely related to the dielectric relaxation behavior [39]. The hardening behavior of Nb/Ti-O vibrations can be reflected by the increased wavenumbers of $_{\upsilon4-\upsilon6}$ modes as $x{\ge}0.5$ owing to the appearance of antipolar nanoregions (APNRs) [31] accompanied by the transformation from $\mathrm{PE}_{\mathrm{T}}~P4/m b m$ to relaxor $\mathsf{A F E}_{\mathrm{T}}$ P4bm at $x{=}0.5$ . The $\upsilon4$ and $\upsilon5$ merge together at $\scriptstyle x=0.85$ , further indicating the entrance into the $\mathrm{FE}_{\mathrm{R}}$ R3c phase zone. In addition, the Raman peaks around $150~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ , $250~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and $600~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ shift to higher frequencies in the $R3c$ phase zone with increasing x, suggesting the hardening of B-O vibrations owing to the weakening of dielectric relaxation degree.

Fig. 8 shows the polarization switching and strain behavior during the first and second electric cycles for AFE $\mathsf{P}$ and R compositions of $0{\le}x{\le}0.18$ . A sharp polarization current peak $({\mathsf{P}}_{1})$ can be observed in the NN ceramic during the first cycle as a result of the field induced AFE to FE phase transition.

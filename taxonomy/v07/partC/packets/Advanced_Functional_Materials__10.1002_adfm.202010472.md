- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): After pyrolysis, CoSAs-NPC inherits original cake-shape morphology with $2~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ in diameter and $400\mathrm{nm}$ in thickness (Figure 1a). From the enlarged SEM and TEM images (Figure 1b; Figure S5a,b, Supporting Information), the CoSAs-NPC is an assembly consisting of carbon nanosheet that is rolled and aggregated along radial direction (illustrated by inset in Figure  1b). HRTEM image shows the fringes $\left[d=0.35\mathrm{nm}\right]$ ) corresponding to the graphitic carbon (inset in Figure 1b). Co, C, and N are dispersed uniformly on the carbon (Figure S5c, Supporting Information). There is no Co aggregation in the whole CoSAs-NPC particle, which indicates the adequate dispersity of hetero-atoms. As comparison, the morphology and distribution of Co in the Co-NPC and NPC were also observed and highlighted in Figure S6, Supporting Information. The cobalt particles were formed and dispersed in the whole carbon skeleton (Figure $\mathrm{S6a,b,c,}$ Supporting Information). The suitable amount of Zn in the precursor can spatially isolate Co to prevent its agglomeration, leading to uniform atomic dispersion.  CoSAs-NGST is a hybrid material consisting of bamboolike graphene tubes supported on graphene sheets (Figure 1c). It is interesting to note that the tube is multiwalled and roots on the graphene sheets (Figure S5d, Supporting Information). A further magnified image in Figure  1d exhibits that the tube wall thickness of is ${\approx}5\mathrm{nm}$ and the joint period is ${\approx}50~\mathrm{nm}$ There is no cake-like bulk observed in CoSAs-NGST (Figure 1c; Figure  S5d, Supporting Information). Thus, the nanosheet assembly of CoSAs-NPC (Figure  1a,b) is completely disassembled into the hybrid structure. The distribution of single atoms was detected by aberration-corrected high-angle annular darkfield scanning transmission electron microscopy (HAADFSTEM). Isolated cobalt single-atoms are evidently observed as the bright dots on the surface of graphene tube (Figure  1e)  and sheet (Figure  1f), which are partially marked with red circles. The energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) mapping was further recorded under the STEM mode, which demonstrates the uniform distribution of carbon, nitrogen, and cobalt (Figure  1g). The specific surface area and pore distribution of CoSAs-NGST and CoSAs-NPC were evaluated by $\Nu_{2}$ adsorption–desorption isotherms measurements. They exhibited type-IV isotherms with an H3-type hysteresis loop, confirming their mesoporous structure (Figure  1h). Despite the same pore structure $({\approx}4.01\mathrm{nm})$ , the specific surface area of CoSAs-NGST $(468.16~\mathrm{m}^{2}~\mathrm{g}^{-1})$ is larger than that of CoSAs-NPC $(391.88~\mathrm{m}^{2}~\mathrm{g}^{-1})$ . The porous architecture of CoSAs-NGST with large surface area and mesoporous structure could promote the mass transport during the catalytic reaction and hence improve the electrochemical activities.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To further characterize the difference in the chemical state of the obtained catalysts, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis was conducted. Survey spectra reveal that there are Co, N, C and O elements in these catalysts (Figure S7, Supporting Information). The presence of oxygen could be ascribed to the adsorbed oxygen-containing functional groups (-OH, -COOH, etc.).[16] The high-resolution C 1s spectrum can be divided into four peaks at 284.5, 285.3, 286.2, and $288.9~\mathrm{eV,}$ assigned to CC, CN, COH and ${\mathrm{C}}{=}0$ (Figure 2a), respectively.[17] Carbon mainly existed in the $\mathrm{C}{\cdot}\mathrm{C}~\mathrm{sp}^{2}$ state in these catalysts. CoSAs-NGST has a larger proportion of $\mathsf{C}\mathrm{-N}$ $(39.00~\mathrm{wt}\%)$ than that of CoSAs-NPC $(21.47\mathrm{wt}\%)$ . The Co $2\mathrm{p}_{3/2}$ XPS spectra can be deconvoluted into three peaks, $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{o}^{0}$ (778.5  eV), $\mathrm{Co-N}$ $(781.1~\mathrm{{eV})}$ and satellite peak $(785.5~\mathrm{eV})$ . In Figure  2b, $\mathrm{Co-N}$ dominated in Co 2p spectra of CoSAs-NGST while Co cations increased distinctly in the Co 2p spectra of CoSAs-NPC. As shown in Figure  2c, the corresponding high-resolution $\mathrm{N}$ 1s spectra were deconvoluted into four states of N, pyridinic-N (398.3 eV), Co–N (399.2 eV), pyrrolic N $(400.2~\mathrm{eV})$ , and graphitic  N (401.2 eV). The XPS results indicate that Co was coordinated with N and anchored on the graphitized carbon $(\mathrm{Co}\mathrm{-}\mathrm{N}\mathrm{-}\cdots\mathrm{C}\mathrm{-})$ . The structure differentiation from bulk to hybrid enhanced the formation of CoNC.[19] Theoretical studies have shown that transition metal coordinating with nitrogen can change the asymmetric spin density of carbon atoms, which reduces the energy barrier for oxygen adsorption to facilitate the ORR. The relative nitrogen content of each nitrogen species and the total content in all samples are compared in Figure 2d. CoSAsNGST exhibits highest nitrogen content among them. The ratio of pyridinic-N and $\mathrm{Co-N}$ in CoSAs-NGST are the highest. It indicates that CoSAs-NGST has more defects which can function as active sites. The conversion from cake-shape structure to low-dimension hybrid also distinctly affects their electronic structure, especially $\mathrm{N}{-}\mathrm{C}$ bonding state.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To discuss the interplay of morphology and defect structure, the pyrolysis was analyzed by thermogravimetry (TG) and differential scanning calorimeter (DSC) analysis. In Figure 3a, the thermal analysis curves can be divided into four stages (Figure  3b). In the stage I $(25-200^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ , CoZn-ZIF and dicyandiamide exist stably. In the stage II $(200-395~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ , tri-s-triazine molecules began to be released from dicyandiamide, which is accompanied by the continuous mass loss on TG. And the released micromolecule would be partially absorbed by the pores of ZIF. In this stage, the CoZn-ZIF is stable (the 2nd panel in Figure  3b). In the stage III $(395-750~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ , the generated micromolecules were condensed into 2D $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ structure, which corresponds to some minor exothermic peaks $(500^{\circ}{}^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ on the DSC curve. During the condensation, $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ gas would be generated, which also causes the mass loss in this stage. The release of ammonia would also provide a N-containing micro-atmosphere for the in-situ carbonization process. It is possible that benzimidazole units participate in the condensation reaction of tri-striazine, which can help break the ZIF frame.[20] As a result, the decomposition temperature of CoZn-ZIF decreased from 723 to $693~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ after the addition of dicyandiamide. The diffusive exothermic peak of $\mathrm{CoZn{-}Z I F+}$ dicyandiamide also proved that the frame of ZIF was pre-destroyed. The schematic illustration of each stage is shown in Figure  3a, accordingly. Further to confirm the structure change, two typical temperatures (520, $720^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ were selected to pyrolyze the CoZn-ZIF and its mixture with dicyandiamide. The phase and morphology evolution were shown in Figures S8a and S9, Supporting Information, respectively. After pyrolysis at $520~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ (Figure S8a, Supporting Information), a sharp XRD peak at $26.7^{\circ}$ was identified as intermediate carbon nitride $(\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4})$ . The characteristic peaks of CoZn-ZIF got weak (at $9.2^{\circ}$ , etc.), indicating the frame of CoZn-ZIF was destroyed preliminarily. As comparison, at $520~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , the crystal structure of ZIF frame was not changed without the addition of dicyandiamide (Figure S8b, Supporting Information). Figure S9a,b, Supporting Information, shows the morphology of the calcined mixture at $520~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ by SEM and TEM, respectively. Both the fragment of ZIF and layered $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ were observed. The ZIF bulk was broken. When the temperature rose to $720~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , all the sharp peaks disappeared (Figure S8a, Supporting Information), just leaving a steamed bun peak at about $26^{\circ}$ , indicating that CoZn-ZIF fragment and $\mathrm{g}\mathrm{-C}_{3}\mathrm{N}_{4}$ have been completely decomposed (Figure S8a, Supporting Information). The cake-like ZIF bulk was disassembled (Figure S9c, Supporting Information). At the same time, CNT emerged (Figure  S9d, Supporting Information). The growth temperature range of the carbon nanotube is roughly indicated by the shadowed region in Figure 3b. Carbon nitride gases from dicyandiamide and $\operatorname{sp}^{2}$ carbon from benzimidazole formed at this temperature scope. During this process, the intermediate gas (carbon nitride gases, $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ , $\mathrm{C}_{2}\mathrm{N}_{2},$ etc.) would not only be used as the simultaneous carbon/nitrogen source to grow carbon nanotubes,[21] but also form a special atmosphere for pyrolysis. In the last stage $(>750~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ , the CoSAs-NGST gradually formed and got stable in both TG and mass curves. The defective centers can catch more metal atoms and form $\mathrm{Co}\mathrm{-N}_{x}$ sites, meanwhile increasing the stability of $\mathsf{C}\mathrm{-N}$ . However, CoSAs-NPC directly generated from CoZn-ZIF shows a continuous weight loss (Figure 3b). As reported, the $\mathrm{C-N}$ group gets unstable and can be evaporated at the temperature above $700~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ without anchoring effect, which causes further mass loss.[22] The evaporation of $\mathrm{Zn}$ also occurred at this stage (inset in Figure 3b).

There is no peak in the $\Nu_{2}$ -saturated electrolyte (dotted lines in Figure S10, Supporting Information). Welldefined cathodic peaks appear with the presence of $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ , which demonstrates a catalysis towards ORR preliminarily (solid lines in Figure S10, Supporting Information). From the linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) results at $1600~\mathrm{rpm}$ (Figure 4a), the CoSAsNGST displays an onset potential $\left(E_{\mathrm{onset}}\right)$ of $0.99\mathrm{~V~}$ versus RHE, a half-wave potential $\left(E_{1/2}\right)$ of $0.89\mathrm{~V~}$ and a limited current density of $6.00\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , which are more positive than those of CoSAs-NPC $(E_{\mathrm{onset}};0.95\mathrm{V},$ $E_{1/2}$ : $0.84\:\mathrm{V},$ J: $\mathrm{;6.19\:mA\:cm^{-2}}$ ). CoSAsNGST exhibits superior catalytic activity with more positive $E_{1/2}$ (0.89 V) and larger current density $(6.00\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2})$ (Figure 4a), compared with the commercial $\mathrm{Pt}/\mathrm{C}$ (Figure $_{42}$ ; $E_{\mathrm{onset}}\colon1.01\mathrm{V},$ $E_{1/2}$ : $0.86~\mathrm{V},\mathrm{~J}\colon5.56~\mathrm{mA~cm}^{-2})$ . Higher onset potential and $E_{1/2}$ could lead to a lower overpotential in electrochemical reaction, which is one of the bottle necks for the practical application of non-noble metal catalyst.[8,18] In addition, the comparison of onset potential with reported results are shown in Table S1, Supporting Information. The present result outperforms $\mathrm{Pt}/\mathrm{C}$ $+\mathrm{RuO}_{2}$ catalyst. In order to highlight the effect of single atom supported on carbon, Co-NPC and NPC were also tested as ORR electrocatalysts by LSV (Figure 4a). They demonstrated smaller initial potentials (similarly at ${\approx}0.95$ V), and a lower current densities (Co-NPC: $5.25\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , NPC: $3.55\mathrm{mAcm^{-2}})$ than CoSAs-NGST. Tafel slopes (Figure  4b) were calculated by the LSV curves at $1600~\mathrm{rpm}$ . In the studied potential region, CoSAsNGST shows a Tafel slope of $65\mathrm{mV}\mathrm{dec}^{-1}$ , which is lowest among CoSAs-NPC $(66~\mathrm{mV}~\mathrm{dec}^{-1})$ , Co-NPC $(67~\mathrm{mV}~\mathrm{dec}^{-1})$ , NPC $(117\mathrm{mV}\mathrm{dec}^{-1})$ , and even that of $\mathrm{Pt}/\mathrm{C}$ $(70~\mathrm{mV~dec}^{-1})$ , suggesting a superior ORR activity kinetics. Both LSV and Tafel results indicate that CoSAs-NGST has more excellent electrocatalytic performance than commercial $\mathrm{Pt}/\mathrm{C}$ that is a commercial standard to evaluate the performance of ORR catalysts.  The superior ORR catalytic performance of CoSAs-NGST was also supported by the electron transfer number (n). LSV results (Figure  4c) at different sweep rates were tested to calculate $n$ according to the Koutecky-Levich (K-L) plots. The good linearity of K-L plots (inset in Figure 4c) and calculated $n=3.99$ indicate that they have the same electron transfer pathway of first-order reaction kinetics towards ORR. Further specific results can be obtained through the rotating ring-disk electrodes (RRDEs) test. As shown in Figure  4d, the $n$ value of CoSAs-NGST is about 3.95, and the yield of peroxide is below $7\%$ , suggesting a four-electron process for ORR, which is consistent with the K-L results (Figure  4c). The electrochemically active surface area (ECSA) of ZIF-derived electrocatalysts were estimated by measuring the double-layer capacitance $\left(C_{\mathrm{dl}}\right)$ .[23] ECSA was calculated via a series of CVs with low sweeping-rates of 2, 4, 6, 8, $10\mathrm{mV~s^{-1}}$ in the potential range of 1. $.165-1.215\mathrm{~V~}$ versus RHE. In Figure  4e, CoSAs-NGST exhibits a considerably high $C_{\mathrm{dl}}$ $(78~\mathrm{mF~cm}^{-2})$ , compared with that of CoSAs-NPC $(52~\mathrm{mF~cm}^{-2})$ and Co-NPC $36~\mathrm{mF~cm^{-2}},$ ). With the structure evolution, more active sites for ORR were exposed. Apart from the high catalytic activity, the CoSAs-NGST exhibits excellent long-term operation durability (Figure  4f). The CoSAs-NGST electrocatalyst maintains $86.2\%$ of the cathodic current after 20  000 s while only $57.9\%$ of current retention was retained for $\mathrm{Pt}/\mathrm{C}$ .  The OER catalytic performance of CoSAs-NGST was also evaluated using a three-electrode system under the identical test conditions with ORR. Remarkably, the CoSAs-NGST electrode exhibits a dramatic enhancement of catalytic activity with the onset overpotential of $270~\mathrm{mV}$ (Figure $4\mathrm{g})$ , which is better than that of the CoSAs-NPC catalyst ( $(460~\mathrm{~V~}$ versus RHE). Additionally, the overpotential of CoSAs-NGST is $560\mathrm{mV}$ when the current density is $10\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , which is still smaller than the corresponding values of CoSAs-NPC, suggesting the superb OER activity of CoSAs-NGST. Tafel slope (inset in Figure $4\mathrm{g})$ of CoSAs-NGST is smaller than that of CoSAs-NPC, which agrees well with the result of LSV. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) results were fitted by Zview 2.0 (Figure  4h), and the typical equivalent circuit was inset in Figure 4h. The charge transfer resistance $(R_{\mathrm{ct}})$ of  CoSAs-NGST and CoSAs-NPC are 24.9, and $31.2\Omega$ , respectively. The low resistance reveals that the low-dimension carbon material could facilitate the charge transfer during the oxygen reduction and evolution process.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To gain further insight into the different contributions of $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}$ on carbon tube and sheet to intrinsic activity, we executed DFT calculations to study the relevant ORR and OER pathways of ${\mathrm{CoN}}_{4}$ on the tube and sheet. As depicted in Figure 6a–d, two different models, namely, $\mathrm{CoN_{4}}$ tube and $\mathrm{CoN_{4}}$ -sheet, were constructed. Figure S11a–l, Supporting Information show the top view and side view of the initial tube and sheet structures after adsorption of $\mathrm{\r{\sim}O H}$ , $\ast\mathrm{O}$ , and $\because\mathrm{OOH}$ , respectively. The corresponding adsorption Gibbs free energies of each ORR/ OER step for the two models were calculated at three states, including zero electrode potential ( $U=0\ V)$ , equilibrium potential ( $U=1.23\mathrm{~V~}.$ ) and thermodynamic limiting potential (all steps become energetically negative in this state).[28]  For ORR of $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}$ -sheet (orange curves in Figure 6e), at equilibrium potential of $1.23~\mathrm{V},$ the second $(^{*}\mathrm{{OOH}}\to^{*}\mathrm{{O}})$ and fourth reactions $(\mathrm{\ddot{\bar{~}C}O H}\mathrm{\rightarrowH}_{2}\mathrm{O})$ are uphill. As the electrode potential decreases to $0.4\mathrm{~V~}$ (black curve in Figure $\mathrm{S}11\mathrm{m}$ , Supporting Information), the free energy in the fourth reaction is zero and those of the other three reactions are downhill, implying that the $\mathrm{\ddot{\Omega}}\mathrm{OH}$ removal is the rate-determining step.[29] While for the $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}$ -tube model (blue curves in Figure  6e), the $\ast\mathrm{O}$ protonation to $\ast\mathrm{OH}$ is the potential limiting step. Obviously, the $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}$ -tube model demonstrates much lower theoretical ORR overpotentials $(0.30~\mathrm{eV})$ than that of $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}.$ -sheet $_{(0.83~\mathrm{eV})}$ . The different selections on the rate-determining step would endow the CoSAs-NGST hybrid with a synergistic effect and finally optimize the ORR process. Synergistic effects of ORR pathways originated from the structures differentiation. Similar phenomenon is observed in OER (Figure  6f); the oxygen evolution reaction on $\mathrm{CoN_{4}}$ -tube is determined by the transformation from $\ast\mathrm{O}$ to $\because\mathrm{OOH}$ . However, the deprotonation of $\mathrm{\r{~\r{~O~H~}~}}$ to $\ast\mathrm{O}$ is the rate-determining step for $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}.$ -sheet model. As shown in Figure S11n, Supporting Information, when all steps are downhill during OER, the thermodynamic limiting potential of $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}.$ -tube and $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}$ -sheet are 1.82 and $1.84~\mathrm{eV},$ , respectively. The overportential $(\eta)^{[30]}$ of $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}$ -tube $(0.59\mathrm{eV})$ is similar to that of $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}$ -sheet (0.61  eV). Compared with the ∆G of the second step, $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}$ -tube can reduce the electron density around the $\mathrm{Co}\mathrm{-N}_{4}$ atoms for the adsorption of $\ast\mathrm{OH}$ species, leading to an acceleration of the reaction with another $\mathrm{OH^{-}}$ to form $\ast\mathrm{O}$ species.[2] However, the transform from $\ast\mathrm{O}$ to $\because\mathrm{OOH}$ can easily occur at $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}$ -sheet compared to $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}$ -tube. Therefore, the synergistic effect of $\mathrm{CoN_{4}}$ -sheet and $\mathrm{CoN}_{4}.$ -tube is responsible for the superior bifunctional catalysis on CoSAs-NGST. The modeling results on the each component for graphene tube and sheet was also supported by the previous results.[4,6,29] But more importantly, the present DFT modeling released a result of coupling effect of different carbon structure, and consist of the experimental data well.

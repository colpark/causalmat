- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In this work, an ultrathin, non-flammable, and dual-salt polymer electrolyte (DSPE) for high-performance Li/NMC batteries was developed. The schematic diagram in Figure 1a shows how the DSPEs were prepared. Poly(vinylidene fluoride-co-hexafluoropropylene) (PVDF-HFP), ADN, FEC, and two salts (LiTFSI and lithium bis(oxalate)borate (LiBOB)) were dissolved in acetone and subsequently infiltrated into a porous polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) scaffold by tape casting. The acetone was then evaporated to form the DSPE. PTFE is a mechanically robust and thermally stable polymer.[31] The PTFE scaffold, therefore, enhances the mechanical flexibility and fracture toughness of the DSPE. LiTFSI and LiBOB are chosen since they can synergistically stabilize the interface on both surfaces of Li-metal and NMC and, concomitantly, avert the corrosion of Al current collector.[32,33] To further stabilize the electrode/ electrolyte interfaces and improve the ionic conductivity, ADN and FEC are introduced as functional plasticizers. ADN has high electrochemical oxidation resistance (e.g., ${>}5\mathrm{~V~}$ versus $\mathrm{Li}/$ $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ ), good thermal stability with a boiling point of $295~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and a flashpoint of $163~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , and strong coordination ability toward  Scanning electron microscope (SEM) images in Figure S1, Supporting Information revealed the porous and fibrous framework of PTFE. The PTFE had an area density of $1.0~\mathrm{mg~cm}^{-2}$ and thickness of $15~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ , which are both lower than those of the commercial polypropylene separator (PPS, Celgard 2400), that is, $1.4~\mathrm{mg~cm}^{-2}$ and $25~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ . The as-obtained, large-scale DSPE showed highly mechanical flexibility (Figure 1b and Figure S2, Supporting Information). The morphologies of the surface and cross-section (Figure  1c) confirmed that the electrolyte filled the pores of the PTFE scaffold. Consequently, a compact and ultrathin DSPE with a thickness of $20\upmu\mathrm{m}$ could be obtained. In addition, flammability testing revealed that the DSPE could not be ignited (Figure S3, Supporting Information), while the commercial CBE easily caught fire.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The RT $(23~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ ionic conductivities of the DSPE (Figure 2a) were measured via electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) using stainless steel (SS)/DSPE/SS symmetric cells. When the concentration of the dual salt increased from 1 to $2\mathrm{mmol}\mathrm{mL^{-1}}$ $x$ mmol dual-salt in 1 mL ADN/FEC denoted as DSPE-xM), the ionic conductivity of the DSPE increased from 0.22 to $0.45~\mathrm{m}\mathrm{S}~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ . As the dual-salt concentration increased to $3\mathrm{mmolmL^{-1}}$ (DSPE-3M), the ionic conductivity did not increase. The electrochemical windows of DSPE (Figure  2b) were investigated via linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) using $\mathrm{Li}/$ DSPE/SS cells. The oxidation of the DSPE-1M, DSPE-2M, and DSPE-3M started at 4.55, 4.91, and $4.63\mathrm{~V},$ respectively. As a result, the optimal DSPE-2M was used for further electrochemical tests and structural characterizations. To assess DSPE-2M reproducibility, four samples were fabricated for EIS testing. Figure S4, Supporting Information shows the high repeatability of the DSPE-2M samples, with all electrochemical impedances falling in the range of $2.02–2.47\Omega$ , which is within the error of the measurement apparatus. The calculated error in ionic conductivity is $0.03\mathrm{mScm^{-1}}$ . The ionic conductivities of DSPE-2M between $-10$ and $90~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ are shown in Figure S5a, Supporting Information. The activation energy $(E_{\mathrm{a}})$ of DSPE-2M was calculated to be $0.06~\mathrm{eV}$ using the Voge–Tamman–Fulcher empirical relation (Figure S5b, Supporting Information).[14] The polarization curve, initial and steady-state impedance diagram of Li/ DSPE-2M/Li cell are revealed in Figure  2c. Notably, DSPE-2M showed effective anionic immobilization with a high $\mathrm{t_{Li^{+}}}$ value of 0.67, greatly exceeding that of PPS/CBE (0.32), see Figure S6, Supporting Information.  The stress–strain curves of the DSPE-2M and PPS are shown in Figure  2d. The peak stress and failure strain of DSPE-2M were $11.3\mathrm{~MPa}$ and $208\%$ , respectively. These values were comparable to those of the separator PPS ( $12.1\mathrm{MPa}$ and $56\%$ ). Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) of the PTFE scaffold (Figure 2e) revealed ultrahigh thermal stability, with negligible weight loss up to $500~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . The contents of ADN/FEC plasticizers, dualsalts, and polymers in DSPE-2M were determined by TGA to be 38, 29, and $33\mathrm{~wt}\%$ , respectively.[44,45] The Fourier transform infrared spectrum in Figure S7, Supporting Information demonstrates the characteristic absorption peaks of each compound of DSPE-2M. In addition, the highly coordinated $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ -plasticizer pair was observed by Raman spectroscopy (Figure  2f). Specifically, the peaks at 729 and $2245~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ were ascribed to $\mathrm{O{-}C{-}O}$ ring breathing of FEC and ${\mathrm{C}}{\equiv}\mathrm{N}$ stretching vibration of ADN, respectively.[46–48] The characteristic peaks of ADN and FEC mentioned above were shifted to higher frequencies in DSPE-2M, indicating the strengthening $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ -FEC and $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ -ADN coordination.[46–48]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Previous studies have shown that nitriles are incompatible with LMAs.[31,49] To mitigate undesired reactions between nitrile and LMA, a film-forming additive (e.g., FEC) is needed to assist the application of nitrile-based electrolytes through a synergistic mechanism.[50] The coordination environment has been further studied using ab initio molecular dynamics, see Figure 3a. Specifically, the solvation structures between the $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ cation (center ion) and the ADN/FEC plasticizers as well as $\mathrm{TFSI^{-}/B O B^{-}}$ anions were investigated. The $\mathrm{Li-N_{ADN}}$ and $\mathrm{Li-O_{FEC}}$ radial distribution function (RDF) peaks are identified at 2.05 and $1.95\mathrm{~\AA~}$ , respectively. The sharper and higher peak of Li$\mathrm{{N_{ADN}}}$ compared to $\mathrm{Li.O_{FEC}}$ implies that the first solvation shell for $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ is dominated by ADN molecules. We also note the broad peaks located at 4.05 and $5.25\mathrm{~\AA~}$ for the $\mathrm{Li-O_{FEC}}$ pair, indicating that FEC enters the second solvation shell. In other words, the FEC molecules exist in the outer shell of the solvation cluster. These results suggest that Li-metal will preferentially bond with FEC molecules over ADN, thus creating a LiF rich SEI film at the Li-metal/electrolyte interface.[49,51] We can also see from Figure 3c that $\mathrm{Li^{+}}$ is highly coordinated with $\mathrm{BOB^{-}}$ rather than $\mathrm{TFSI^{-}}$ anions. This reveals that the solubility of LiTFSI in the electrolyte is higher than that of LiBOB. We note that the limited solubility of LiBOB in nitrile-based electrolytes has been discussed earlier.[52] Consequently, LiTFSI plays a greater role in the high conductivity of our DSPE than LiBOB.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The Li/Li symmetric cells were assembled to evaluate the compatibility between Li-metal and DSPE. In Figure 4a, the $\mathrm{Li}/$ Li symmetric cell with DSPE-2M-woFEC (DSPE-2M without FEC) displayed an increase in overpotential after $50\mathrm{h}$ and shortcircuited after 501 h of cycling. The deterioration of DSPE-2MwoFEC can be ascribed to the incomplete passivation of the Li surface, which consequently cannot protect the Li electrode from reaction with ADN molecules.[31,49] In contrast, the $\mathrm{Li}/$ DSPE-2M/Li had excellent stability without short-circuiting over $1200\mathrm{~h~}$ at $0.1\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ with an areal capacity of $0.1\mathrm{mAh~cm^{-2}}$ .  Li/DSPE-2M/NMC811 cells were fabricated to estimate the electrochemical performance of DSPE-2M. Figure $^{4\mathrm{b},\mathrm{c}}$ report the rate capability of Li/DSPE-2M/NMC811 cell, demonstrating the capacities of 168, 165, 153, 137, and 118 mAh ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ at 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, and $2\mathrm{~C~}$ respectively. When the current density was subsequently returned to $0.2~\mathrm{C}$ , the specific discharge capacity recovered to 151 mAh ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ and maintained 150 mAh $\mathbf{g}^{-1}$ over a further 100 cycles. These cycling results reveal the outstanding rate performance of Li/DSPE-2M/NMC811 cell. Further, the cyclic stability of Li/DSPE-2M/NMC811 cell was tested at a fast-charge/discharge rate of $2\mathrm{~C~}$ (Figure 4d). Remarkably, the Li/DSPE-2M/NMC811 delivered a high initial capacity of 143 mAh ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ and $78.3\%$ capacity retention even after 1000 cycles. In contrast, the Li/PPS/CBE/NMC811 decayed dramatically over 200 cycles and ceased after 800 cycles. As the loading of NMC811 increased to $6.5~\mathrm{mg~cm}^{-2}$ , the Li/DSPE-2M/NMC811 sustained 151 mAh ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ at $0.2\mathrm{~C~}$ over 100 cycles (Figure 4e). These electrochemical results of DSPE-2M are superior to many previously reported electrolytes (Table S2, Supporting Information). In addition, a pouch cell was made to verify the safety of Li/DSPE2M/NMC811. As shown in Figure S8, Supporting Information, the Li/DSPE-2M/NMC811 pouch cell could light up a red lightemitting diode and remain operational even after one of the corners was cut.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): SEM was used to characterize the surface morphologies of LMAs, which were disassembled from Li/DSPE-2M/NMC811 and Li/PPS/CBE/NMC811 cells after 50 cycles at 2 C. According to the top and cross-sectional SEM images (Figure  5a), the  LMADSPE-2M showed a similarly smooth and dense morphology to pristine Li (Figure S9, Supporting Information), suggesting effective suppression of Li dendrites. In contrast, serious corrosion was observed from the LMA of the PPS/ CBE cell (Figure 5b). The porous and loose surface layer could not protect the LMA against the attack of the CBE, resulting in progressive LMA corrosion and electrolyte decomposition. Consequently, the accumulation of dead Li led to the fading of the battery capacity.[32] X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra was also utilized to analyze the surface composition of the cycled LMAs. As for the C 1s spectra (Figure  5c), both $\mathrm{LMA}_{\mathrm{DSPE}\cdot2\mathrm{M}}$ and $\mathrm{LMA}_{\mathrm{PPS/CBE}}$ had similar SEI compounds, that is, ${\mathrm{C}}{=}0$ , $_{\mathrm{C-O}}$ , and $\mathrm{C-C/C{-}H}$ , corresponding to polycarbonates, $\mathrm{ROCO}_{2}\mathrm{Li}$ , and Li carbides, respectively.[32,33] Compared with LMAPPS/CBE, the dominant ${\mathrm{C}}{=}0$ groups on LMADSPE-2M can be derived from decompositions of both FEC and LiBOB salt.[12,31] The LiF and $\mathrm{BO}_{x}$ groups on LMADSPE-2M were also confirmed in the F 1s (Figure 5d) and B 1s spectra (Figure S10, Supporting Information).[12,31] The F- and B-containing groups synergistically participated in the construction of the SEI, which effectively prevented the LMADSPE-2M from breakdown and enabled the long-term stable Li plating/stripping lifespan.[33] Contrasting $\mathrm{LMA}_{\mathrm{DSPE}\cdot2\mathrm{M}}$ , the LiF on LMAPPS/CBE mainly originated from $\mathrm{LiPF_{6}}$ . While the SEI obtained from PPS/CBE demonstrated a LiF-rich layer, the high-density of the C-F group can be ascribed to the continuous SEI decomposition and electrolyte depletion.[32]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Severe detrimental reactions can occur on the surface of high-voltage NMC cathodes, including structural phase conversion, transition metal dissolution, and electrolyte oxidation.[53] Simultaneously, the architecture of the cathode electrolyte interface (CEI) is of significance for the durable operation of the Li/ NMC cell. The cycled NMC811 cathode from the DSPE-2M cell (Figure 6a) preserved a smooth and clean particle surface similar to that of pristine NMC811 (Figure S11, Supporting Information). In contrast, surface collapsing and structural distortion were observed on NMC811PPS/CBE due to its reaction with the CBE (Figure  6b). The CEI composition of cycled NMC811 cathodes was further investigated via XPS (Figure S12, Supporting Information). As shown in Figure 6c, the $\mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ peaks of NMC811DSPE-2M in Ni 2p spectra are much lower than those of NMC811PPS/CBE. The $\mathrm{Ni}^{2+}$ peaks detected for the cathode compounds correspond to the presence of electrochemically inactive NiO-like rock-salt products, which in turn leads to capacity fading.[12] As for the F 1s spectra (Figure S13, Supporting Information), the amount of LiF in NMC811PPS/CBE is higher than that of NMC811DSPE-2M. LiF is a crucial component of CEI since it can reduce the catalytic activity of the NMC811 surfaces.[53] However, high LiF content can increase the CEI layer thickness, leading to increased resistance.[54] In addition, the unique N- and B-contained species were involved in the formation of the CEI on NMC811DSPE-2M (Figure  6d, Figures S14, and S15, Supporting Information).[12,55] In particular, the identification of the $\scriptstyle\mathrm{C}{\equiv}\mathrm{N}$ group suggested the presence of ADN on the cathode surface (Figure  6d) as further supported by density functional theory simulations (Figure 6e). The calculated negative adsorption energy of ADN molecules $(-0.79~\mathrm{eV})$ on a representative surface of the NMC811 is lower than that of FEC $(-0.68~\mathrm{eV})$ , implying that the CEI is richer in ADN than FEC molecules. Thus, the F, N, B-containing groups resulting from DSPE synergistically enforced the CEI layer and protected the cathode materials from decomposition and undesirable phase transformations.[12]

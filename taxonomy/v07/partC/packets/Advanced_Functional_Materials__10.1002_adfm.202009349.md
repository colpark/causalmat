- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Figure  1a–c describes the fabrication pathway and strategy of BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogels (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The renewable BC nanofibers could be well dispersed in aqueous system with the assistance of a homogenizer. MTMS with one nonhydrolyzable hydrophobic group was used, and then hydrolyzed and transformed into colloids with the addition of catalyst of aqueous acetic acid. The BC nanofibers (Figure S2, Supporting Information) and MTMS sol were added into acetic acid aqueous solution and mixed at predetermined ratios (Figure 1a). On freezing, the nanofibers and colloids were together pushed out by the moving water–ice front and then confined in the interstitial regions of the growing ice crystals (Figure  1b). Through a freezing-induced assembly process, a BC fibrous matrix with microscopic cellular architectures was formed. On freeze–drying, the ice sublimated to result in continuous vapor–liquid–solid three-phase contact line (TCL) along the parallel direction of BC nanofibers and discontinuous TCL on the contact point of crossed nanofibers.[18] A continuous TCL allows spreading and infiltrating of the melted liquid flow of MTMS sol (Figure S1, Supporting Information) along the long axes of the fibrils to generate fibrous elastocapillary coalescence, while a discontinuous TCL results in accumulation and collection of colloids on the contacts of BC nanofibers.[19] Through a drying-driven polycondensation process, the polymerization of MTMS sol was promoted, yielding PMSQ mineral network to deposited on the surfaces and contacts of BC nanofibers (Figure  1c). As a result, hybrid aerogels of BC–PMSQ were directly obtained in an integrated wetting and mineralization process via freeze–drying.  The liquid droplets containing MTMS sol could rapidly and entirely spread within BC nanofibrous framework due to the interfacial affinity. Such a superspreading phenomenon can be visualized by the wetting experiments. Figure  1d (top) shows dropping colloidal liquids (volume: $20~{\upmu\mathrm{L}}\$ on one side of the porous BC nanofibrous network. The water droplet moved spontaneously through the BC framework and spread into another side. Microscopic images of the silane sol (marked with fluorescent molecules) transport within BC framework were recorded by a fluorescent microscope (Figure 1d (bottom); Figure S3 and Movie S1, Supporting Information). Colloid droplets spread rapidly and entirely within the BC nanofibrous framework to make the dry surface wet. Thus, the thin film of MTMS sol (low viscosity; Figure S4, Supporting Information)  would be automatically generated and strongly coated around BC nanofibrous framework after the wetting process.[20] Through a drying-driven polycondensation process, the mineralization of MTMS sol was promoted, yielding PMSQ network coating on the surfaces and contacts of BC nanofibers. The mineralization process of MTMS sol under the freeze–drying process was recorded using $^{29}\mathrm{Si}$ NMR spectra (Figure  1e). At the initial stage, the trifunctional silanol molecules in MTMS sol were weakly condensed under the applied reaction condition, which can be identified from $^{29}\mathrm{Si}$ chemical shifts in liquidstate NMR spectroscopy. Two main signals at $^{-46}$ and $-56~\mathrm{ppm}$ were assigned to trifunctional silanol with one and two siloxane bridges, respectively.[12] After the drying process, the fluid-like colloids transformed into solid-state PMSQ. The polymerization of PMSQ network could be observed by the appearance of two peaks at $-56$ and $-66~\mathrm{ppm}$ in solid-state $^{29}\mathrm{Si}$ NMR spectra, which were attributed to two- and three-bridged siloxane network (Figure S5, Supporting Information). In situ $^{29}\mathrm{Si}$ NMR spectroscopy revealed that, during the freeze–drying process, hydrolyzed MTMS sol underwent a step-growth polymerization, as evidenced by vanishment of the peak at $-36$ ppm and enhancement in the signals at −46, $-55$ , and $-65~\mathrm{ppm}$ (Figure  1e). Attenuated total reflection-Fourier transform infrared spectrometry (ATR-FTIR; Figure S6, Supporting Information) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS; Figure S7, Supporting Information) further demonstrate the PMSQ formation. The number of $\mathrm{Si-O-Si}$ bonds within aerogels could be further increased by annealing at mild temperature of $80~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ ( $^{29}\mathrm{Si}$ NMR; Figure S5, Supporting Information).

The hierarchical porous structure ensures the ultralight feature and thermal insulation property of BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogel, and may provide the free volumes to buffer the deforming force, affording aerogels with flexibility, while the anisotropic character will result in anisotropic mechanical properties as well.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Figure  4a demonstrates that BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogel (0.0 $146~\mathrm{g},20~\mathrm{mm}\times20~\mathrm{mm}\times20~\mathrm{mm})$ was able to sustain a load (1.6 t) of more than $3.5\times10^{7}$ times its own weight, and recovered to its original shape completely after it was rolled over by a vehicle (Movie S4, Supporting Information). Such an excellent compressive property was rarely reported in those state-of-the-art aerogels.[4,25] BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogel exhibits axially aligned cellular framework, implying different Poisson’s ratios behavior (Figure  4b).[26] During the uniaxial compression tests, the Poisson’s ratios of BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogel kept close to zero and exerted little change from 0 to $-0.06$ as the longitudinal strain reached $80\%$ , where the orientation of pore (freezing) structure is perpendicular (radial) to that of compression (Movie S5, Supporting Information). The almost consistent Poisson’s ratios indicate the invariable framework under compression, which can be demonstrated by in situ SEM observation (insets). Even at a compression strain of $80\%$ , the ultralight BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogels still kept a high porosity of $97.9\%$ (Discussion S1, Supporting Information) to avoid the lateral expansion. When the direction of uniaxial compression is parallel (axial) to that of the pore alignment in hybrid aerogels, a rising negative Poisson’s ratio (from 0 to $-0.3)$ was clearly observed with increasing compressive strain (from $0\%$ to $80\%$ . As a result, an obvious transverse contraction was identified (Movie S6, Supporting Information), demonstrating good consistency between the microstructure and macroscopic mechanical properties of the hybrid aerogel. Such anisotropic mechanics can be further revealed by the stress– strain $(\sigma{-}\varepsilon)$ curves (Figure S14, Supporting Information).  Considering the anisotropic architecture of BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogels, next we focus on the mechanical study along the pore alignment. Figure  4c shows typical deformation behavior of cellular aerogels, that is, a linear elastic behavior for $\varepsilon<9\%$ , followed by a subsequent plateau stage with cellcollapse-related stress reduction for $9\%<\varepsilon<60\%$ , and then a densification regime with a rapid stress increase for $\varepsilon>60\%$ . The maximum stress of $400.5\mathrm{kPa}$ at $99\%$ strain was achieved at a hugely compact state of BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogel. The local buckling of fibrous cell walls would be easily triggered during compression and then recovered upon release. This is supported by in situ SEM observation of BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogels (Figure  4d). In addition, some composite filaments (marked with an arrow) were also found to span the channels within cells, might serving as the bridges to increase the fracture resistance. Remarkably, after 10 000 compress–release cycles at $1\mathrm{Hz}$ with a strain of $60\%$ , almost $80\%$ maximum stress was retained and less than $5\%$ plastic deformation was occurred, showing an excellent fatigue resistance (Figure S15, Supporting Information). On the microscopic scale, the cell walls constituted by entangled and crosslinked nanofiber are beneficial to dissipate external stress and generate large deformations. On the nanoscopic scale, the key feature of the nanofibers within cell walls is their “stiff–soft” synergistic character. The “soft” BC nanofibers encapsulated by “stiff” PMSQ are interfused strongly to form robust composite nanofibers and stable crosslinked nanostructure, which is advantageous for the compressive performance of aerogels. On the molecular scale, the repulsive interactions from the hydrophobic methyl $\left(\mathrm{Si}{-}\mathrm{CH}_{3}\right)$ moieties within the PMSQ networks promote the spring-back effect at high level of compressive strain.[27] Therefore, the hierarchical hybrid interfaces of fibrous cellular network, “stiff– soft” hybrid nanostructure and hydrophobic hybrid molecular character (molecular repulsive force) are highly important for realizing high mechanical elasticity and robustness. Owing to these structural features, the relative Young’s modulus $(E/E_{\mathrm{s}})$ was found to scale with the relative density $(\rho/\rho_{\mathrm{s}})$ as $E/E_{\mathrm{s}}\approx$ $(\rho/\rho_{\mathrm{s}})^{2}$ (Figure  4e), which is consistent with the mechanical behavior of cellular materials.[4,28] The exponent number of 2 in our hybrid aerogels further confirms the efficient load transfer via bending- and buckling-dominated structures during reversible compression.[28a]  Besides the excellent mechanical elasticity and fatigue resistance, our BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogels also show fast recovery speed when compressed. As shown in Figure $4\mathrm{f},$ the typical hybrid aerogel $\left(0.042\mathrm{g}\right)$ can rebound a steel ball $\left(14.0\mathrm{g},333\right.$ times heavier than itself) with a fast recovery speed of ${\approx}567\mathrm{mms^{-1}}$ , revealing elastic nature that comparable with the reported results.[25a,29] Moreover, at low temperatures, the elasticity of the BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogels was still retained very well. They can adsorb and squeeze out liquid nitrogen $(-196~^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ repeatedly (Figure  4g; Movie S7, Supporting Information), and even keep their structural stability in liquid nitrogen for 1 month. The upper limit temperature of the BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogel is around $200~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ because of the constituent of organic nanofibers (Figure S16, Supporting Information). Further dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA) characterization in the ambient air from $-150$ to $200~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ demonstrated nearly temperature-invariant viscoelastic properties in the range of $0.1{-}50~\mathrm{Hz}$ , including storage modulus, loss modulus, and damping ratio (Figure  4h; Figure S17, Supporting Information).[30] As we know, the lowest temperature of common DMA facilities is limited to $-150~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . Fatigue resistance of the hybrid aerogel at room temperature can be again evidenced by DMA measurements (Figure  4i). Cyclic tests at $10~\mathrm{Hz}$ showed identical viscoelastic moduli even after $20~000$ cycles at $3\%$ strain. These DMA results indicate that viscoelastic performance of BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogels is not only invariant to a wide temperature range but also unchangeable to long-term cycling (Figure S18, Supporting Information).[31] Such a phenomenon of BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogels holds great potential for applications in stringent environments.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Highly porous nanostructures in our BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogels obviously have the capacity to create surfaces with low water-adhesive forces.[33] The side-view SEM image clearly shows that a large number of nanoscale fibrous protrusions are randomly distributed on the surface of aerogel (Figure  5a (left)). - Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Environmental water vapor widely exists everywhere, and penetration of water into materials would displace the air within the materials and then reduces thermal insulation property consequently.[38] Therefore, improving moisture resistance to prevent water penetration is a key premise for aerogels to ensure thermal insulation quality. Fortunately, in BC–PMSQ, its fibrous protruded nanostructure (Figure 5a), surface hydrophobic moieties $\left(\mathrm{Si}{-}\mathrm{CH}_{3}\right)$ , and stable nanofibrous framework allow the aerogel material to push the water droplets out from the surface (Figure  6a), leading to a robust super-hydrophobicity to withstand high humidity. In contrast, down feathers as the most widely used commercial clothing insulation are easy to encapsulate water vapor, and then condensed droplets are generated (Figure 6b; Figure S20, Supporting Information). We have also investigated how the thermal insulation performance is affected when different materials are subjected to elevated RH. The thermal conductivities of the hybrid aerogels almost keep same when the RH is changed from $30\%$ to $90\%$ at room temperature $T=25^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , Figure  6c). However, down feathers show an obvious decrease in thermal insulation properties when RH rises, its thermal conductivities increase from $29.9~\mathrm{mW~m^{-1}~K^{-1}}$ $(\mathrm{RH}\mathrm{~=~}30\%)$ to $34.4~\mathrm{mW~m^{-1}~K^{-1}}$ $(\mathrm{RH}=90\%)$ , while water-sensitive BC membranes (from 35.8 to $43.6\mathrm{mW}\mathrm{m}^{-1}\mathrm{K}^{-1})$ and commercial cotton fabrics (from 36.1 to $46.6\mathrm{mW}\mathrm{m^{-1}}\mathrm{K^{-1}})$ show much worse thermal insulation performance under moisture environment. The humidity-invariable thermal insulation property of BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogel is facilitated by its microstructural topography and surface chemistry. In the wet state $\mathrm{^{\prime}R H}\approx95\%$ , Figure 6e,g), the hybrid aerogel is more efficient than down feathers in thermal insulation (Figure S21, Supporting Information). Besides excellent thermal insulation performance, BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogels are also able to breathe because of the open fibrous structure, they have sufficient gas permeability and allow air/water vapor exchange between the human body and the environment (Figure S22, Supporting Information).[40] The results indicate that the BC–PMSQ hybrid aerogels could serve as a robust personal protection to match up with extreme cold conditions (e.g., polar, high mountain, glacial, and periglacial regions) for comfort insulation.[41] In addition, these hybrid aerogels also show great potential as building materials to minimize the thermal energy and humidity exchange between indoor and outdoor environments for improving the energy efficiency of residential and commercial buildings.[9d,e]

The electric resistance variation $(R/R_{0})$ was in situ recorded when the conductive aerogel was compressed, where $R$ is the realtime resistance and $R_{0}$ is the initial resistance. The $R$ decreases gradually when tested at the compressive strain between $0\%$ and $30\%$ (Figure 7c). It is noted that the direction of the pressure was applied in the radial direction of BC–PMSQ–graphene, which is attributed to the near-zero Poisson’s ratio displayed in this direction, indicating a gradual variation in conductive network rather than a sudden change when compressed.[43] We demonstrated a device by an assembly of a conductive aerogel and a lightemitting diode (LED) lamp together. The LED light becomes stronger with increasing the compression strain, further indicating the improvement of conductivity (Figure  7d; Movie S11, Supporting Information). In addition, this BC–PMSQ–graphene aerogel also shows good reversibility and repeatability. The strain was repeatedly varied from $0\%$ to $30\%$ and vice versa, the resistance of aerogel exerted little change after ten consecutive compression/release cycles (Figure  7e; Figure S23, Supporting Information). The pressure–sensitivity of our conductive aerogels is defined as $S=(A R/R_{0})/\Delta P$ , where $\Delta R$ is the relative resistance change, $R_{0}$ is the initial resistance of carbon-coated BC–PMSQ without loading, and $\Delta P$ is the variation in the compressive pressure $(P).^{[44]}$ As shown in Figure ${\mathrm{7f}},$ the sensitivity curve displays three regimes with increasing applied pressure: the first regime in $P<0.3\mathrm{kPa}$ with the $S_{1}$ value of $0.755\mathrm{kPa}^{-1}$ , a subsequent regime in $0.3\mathrm{kPa}<P<0.7\mathrm{k\Omega}$ Pa with the $S_{2}$ value of $0.282\mathrm{kPa}^{-1}$ , and the third regime in $0.7\mathrm{kPa}<P<1\mathrm{l}$ Pa with the $S_{3}$ value of $0.080\mathrm{kPa}^{-1}$ . From the above results, it is noted that the sensitivity of the conductive BC–PMSQ–graphene aerogel is quite excellent for the extremely low pressure $(P<0.3\mathrm{kPa})$ ,[45] which are rather sensitive when compared with other carbonbased composite aerogels $[42,44],46]$ and even comparable with those of state-of-the-art aerogels based on all-carbon materials (e.g., graphene).[3,47] Our BC–PMSQ–graphene aerogels may have profound applications in delicate sensing, such as human health and motion monitoring. In addition, other nanomaterials with magnetic and catalytic functions could be readily incorporated into the BC–PMSQ network to endow final aerogels with added functionalities.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): MXenes, as a family of 2D transition metal carbides/ carbonitrides, hold great promise in Li–S batteries due to their high conductivity, amphiphilic interactions toward polysulfides and catalytic effects.[27–30] However, one insurmountable barrier in MXene nanosheets is the tendency to restack because of the van der Waals attraction and hydrogen bonding interactions, resulting in substantial loss of specific surface area and the inaccessibility toward high concentration of polysulfides.[31] One of the effective solutions is the integration of MXenes with porous materials to form composites. 2D covalent organic frameworks (COFs) are one type of appealing porous materials composed of strong covalent bonds and periodic building units.[32–34] The structural uniqueness of COFs has brought tremendous advantages for polysulfides adsorption and mass transfer.[35,36] Recently, guanidinium-based ionic COFs have been reported for antimicrobial applications and proton conductivity.[37,38] It could be expected that the positively charged guanidinium units could trap negatively charged polysulfides through the electrostatic attraction, resulting in effective suppression of polysulfides shuttling.[39–41] The integration of guanidinium-based ionic– covalent organic nanosheets (iCON) and MXene could disturb their individual restacking interactions among the layers, which generates high efficiency for the adsorption and catalytic conversion of polysulfides, finally achieving high electrochemical performance. However, there is no report on the composites of MXene and ionic polymer materials for Li–S batteries hitherto.  The synthetic route of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ is schematically illustrated in Figure 1a. The $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ nanosheets were prepared by the etching of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{AlC}_{2}$ and subsequent liquid-phase delamination in the aqueous solution of hydrochloric acid and lithium fluoride.[29] $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ has been reported to possess negative Zeta potential and rich surface functional groups, such as hydroxyl, oxygen, and fluorine.[30,42] The ultrasonic treatment of as-prepared $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ and triaminoguanidine hydrochloride $(\mathrm{TG_{Cl}})$ in dioxane/water guarantees the adsorption of $\mathrm{TG}_{\mathrm{Cl}}$ on the surface of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ through the electrostatic and hydrogen bonding interactions. The addition of one equivalent of 1,3,5-triformylphloroglucinol (TP) with respect to $\mathrm{TG}_{\mathrm{Cl}}.$ , followed by Schiff-based condensation reaction between $\mathrm{TG}_{\mathrm{Cl}}$ and TP on the surface of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ generated $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ .  Scanning/transmission electron microscopy (SEM/TEM) images show that both pristine $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ and iCON are typical nanosheet structure with smooth surface, while $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ exhibits tremella-like morphology consisting of ultrathin nanosheets (Figure  1b,c; Figure S1, Supporting Information). The lattice fringe of $0.26~\mathrm{nm}$ corresponds to (110) crystal plane, which is identical with that in $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ (Figure 1d,e),[29] indicating that solvent-thermal treatment for the synthesis of iCON has no significant effects on the intrinsic structure of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ . The interlayer spacing representing (002) crystal plane in $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ is $1.52\mathrm{nm}$ (Figure  1e). The high-annular dark-field scanning TEM (HAADF-STEM) images and corresponding elemental mappings exhibit uniform nitrogen and chlorine distribution (Figure 1f), indicative of homogeneous loading of iCON on the surface of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ . The atom ratio of Ti to $\mathrm{N}$ in $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ is $77:10$ as defined by energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) (Figure S2, Supporting Information).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In the powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ (Figure  2a), the disappearance of typical diffraction peaks for $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{AlC}_{2}$ reveals the removal of the aluminum layer from $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{AlC}_{2}$ , which is further evidenced by the negative shift of the strong (002) peak corresponding to the interlayer stacking. A further shift from $6.61^{\mathrm{o}}$ to $5.80^{\circ}$ is observed in $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ when compared with that of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ , suggesting the increase of the interlayer spacing from $1.34\mathrm{~nm}$ in $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ to $1.52\mathrm{nm}$ in $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ , which is consistent with TEM results. The Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) and solid-state $^{13}\mathrm{C}$ NMR spectra of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ are almost identical with that of iCON, indicative of the formation of iCON in $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ . In their FTIR spectra (Figure  2b), the peaks at 1261 and $1593~\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ are assigned to the $\mathsf{C}\mathrm{-N}$ and the ${\mathrm{C}}{=}{\mathrm{C}}$ stretching vibrations, respectively, indicating that iCON is a $\beta$ -ketoenamine-linked framework, which is further evidenced by the solid-state $^{13}\mathrm{C}$ NMR spectrum (Figure  2c). The characteristic peaks at 100, 150, 162, and 180–182 ppm correspond to the exocyclic ${\mathrm{C}}{=}{\mathrm{C}}$ carbon next to keto, ${\mathrm{C}}{=}{\mathrm{C}}$ carbon attached to nitrogen, guanidinium carbon and keto carbon, respectively.[37,38] X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) survey spectrum of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{i}\mathrm{CON}$ shows typical N 1s peaks derived from iCON (Figure  2d). High-resolution Ti 2p XPS spectra of both $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ show the identical binding energy peaks at 455.2, 456.0, 457.2, and $459.3~\mathrm{eV},$ which correspond to $\mathrm{Ti-C}$ , CTiF(OH), $\mathrm{C-Ti-O}$ and $_{\mathrm{Ti-O}}$ bonds, respectively (Figure 2e), suggesting structural integrity of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ nanosheets in $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{i}\mathrm{CON}$ .[29,43] The permanent porosity of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ was examined by $\Nu_{2}$ adsorption/desorption at 77 K (Figure 2f). The $\Nu_{2}$ sorption isotherm exhibits the pattern of type II. The apparent hysteresis and $\Nu_{2}$ uptake at high relative pressure reveals the presence of mesopores.[37] The Brunauer– Emmett–Teller (BET) specific surface area and total pore volume for $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ are $66~\mathrm{m}^{2}~\mathrm{g}^{-1}$ and $0.26~\mathrm{cm}^{3}~\mathrm{g}^{-1}$ , respectively. However, they are negligible in as-prepared $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ (Figure S3a, Supporting Information). The sharp contrast is mainly attributed to the presence of iCON (Figure S3b, Supporting Information), which possesses BET specific surface area of $169~\mathrm{m}^{2}~\mathrm{g}^{-1}$ and total pore volume $0.5~\mathrm{cm}^{3}~\mathrm{g}^{-1}$ . Obviously, the integration of iCON and $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ endows $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ with porosity, which is favorable for the adsorption of polysulfides and mass transfer.[36]  The $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ -modified PP $(\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}.\mathrm{PP})$ ) separator was prepared through vacuum-filtering $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ nanosheets on Celgard PP separator (inset of Figure 2g,h). The as-prepared separator exhibits superior robustness, no appreciable $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ delamination and deformation are observed after folding or bending (Figure S4, Supporting Information). The surface of pristine PP (Figure 2g) is covered with $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@$ iCON nanosheets to form uniform coating layer (Figure  2h). The cross-sectional SEM image shows that the thickness and the areal density of the coating layer are $5\upmu\mathrm{m}$ and $0.1\mathrm{mg}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , respectively (Figure  2i). As a comparison, $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ -modified PP $(\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}{\cdot}\mathrm{PP})$ separator with the same mass of the coating layer material was also fabricated.

These results further indicate high sulfur utilization and excellent cycling stability under high sulfur content and high sulfur loading.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To further validate effective suppression of polysulfides shuttling by $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}.\mathrm{PP}$ , polysulfides permeability tests through these separators were investigated (Figure S14, Supporting Information). To further show strong interception ability of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ toward polysulfides, the visualized adsorption test was carried out. When $15\mathrm{mg}$ of samples were independently added into the sealed vials containing $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ in 1,3-dioxolane and dimethoxy­ methane $20\times10^{-3}$ m, 2 mL, 1:1 by volume) for $12\mathrm{h}$ (Figure 4b), both iCON and $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ decolor $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ solution, while $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ only changes the solution color from tawny to brown. The characteristic absorbance peaks of $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ in iCON and $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ are remarkably attenuated when compared with that of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ , but peak intensity in $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ and iCON is similar to each other, suggesting that the introduction of iCON is conducive to polysulfides adsorption owing to porosity of iCON and the electrostatic attraction between iCON host framework and polysulfides. It should be mentioned that physical confinement, chemical adsorption, and electrostatic interaction are involved in the visualized adsorption of iCON toward polysulfides. The chemical interaction has been reported to originate from the coordination of carbonyl oxygen atoms in iCON with $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ ions,[38] which is further evidenced by the presence of Li–O binding energy peak in high-resolution Li 1s XPS spectra for $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ -adsorbed iCON $(\mathrm{i}C0\mathrm{N}{\cdot}\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6})$ (Figure S16, Supporting Information). The physical confinement and chemical interaction are relatively weak for polysulfides adsorption when compared with the electrostatic interaction (Figure S17, Supporting Information).[38,40] To confirm the electrostatic interactions, EDS spectrum of $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ -treated iCON shows peak vanishment of chloride and concomitant appearance for the peak of sulfur element (Figure S18, Supporting Information), which is mainly attributed to anionic exchange of chloride with polysulfides for the formation of iCON-polysulfides ion pairs. The electrostatic attraction of the ion pairs is conducive to polysulfides capture for smooth adsorption-diffusion-conversion of polysulfides on $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ upon electrochemical cycling. The interactions between $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ or $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ and polysulfides were also examined by XPS measurements. After adsorbing $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ , the resultant samples are denoted as ${\mathrm{Ti}}_{3}{\mathrm{C}}_{2}.$ $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ and $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}.\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}.$ respectively. However, the peak of Ti–S bond is overlapped by terminal sulfide groups $(\mathrm{S_{T}}-\mathrm{S_{T}})$ of residual polysulfides in their S 2p XPS spectra due to similar binding energies. Notably, the characteristic peaks for terminal and bridging $(\mathrm{S_{B}-S_{B}})$ sulfur species in $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}\mathrm{-Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ and $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}\cdot\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ show obvious shift toward higher binding energy when compared with those in $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ , suggesting the electron transfer from polysulfide to $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ (Figure S19b, Supporting Information). The dynamic adsorption of polysulfides and their catalytic conversion have been validated by symmetrical cells through sandwiching $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}_{6}$ -containing electrolytes between two identical electrodes. The CV profiles at $10\mathrm{mVs^{-1}}$ clearly exhibit that current density of iCON is almost negligible owing to poor catalytic effect of iCON toward polysulfides, while current density of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ surpasses that of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ (Figure 4c), which is probably attributed that dynamic adsorption of iCON toward polysulfides is conducive to further electrochemical redox reaction on $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ surface.[44] Tafel plots show that the exchange current density for $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ $(1.10\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2})$ is larger than the $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ $(0.98\mathrm{mAcm^{-2}})$ (Figure 4d), which indicates more polysulfides are converted in the $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ electrode. To explore the deposition morphology of $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}$ , the above cells were disassembled after potentiostatic discharge for $18000\mathrm{s}$ . As expected, the surface of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{i}\mathrm{CON}$ is covered with one layer of $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}$ (Figure $4g)$ . The uniform deposition of $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}$ mainly originates from the electrostatic effects of iCON toward polysulfides, subsequent catalytic effects of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ facilitate the conversion of the adsorbed polysulfides on the surface of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ , thereby inducing even precipitation of $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}$ in subsequent growth stage, which is favorable for efficient reactivation/reutilization of the intercepted polysulfides in subsequent cycle.[47] In contrast, solid $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}$ exhibits random deposition, and is aggregated bulky particles on the surface of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ owing to relatively low specific surface area and less nucleation sites of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ (Figure 4h), large particles are unfavorable for sulfur utilization.[44] The oxidation of solid $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}$ was also examined using potentiostatic charge process after galvanostatic discharge (Figure  4i). $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ exhibits the increased oxidation current density, suggesting low oxidation overpotential for $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{S}$ conversion. The charge capacity of $129.06~\mathrm{mA}$ h ${\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}}^{-1}$ is also higher than that of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ $(46.49\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{~h~g^{-1}})$ , which reflects that $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}$ also promotes redox kinetics of sulfur species during charge. It should be mentioned that the capacity peaks of iCON in both potentiostatic discharge and charge profiles are negligible, further indicating that $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ is catalytic active components (Figure S22, Supporting Information). The rate performance of the cells based on $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}.$ , iCON-, and their physical mixture-modified separators is also explored, the resultant separators are denoted as $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}.\mathrm{PP}$ , iCON-PP, and $(\mathrm{Ti}_{3}{\bf C}_{2}\mathrm{+i}{\bf C}{\bf O N})$ -PP, respectively. As shown in Figure S23 (Supporting Information), the rate performance of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}.\mathrm{PP}$ is much better than those in iCON-PP, $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ -PP, and $(\mathrm{Ti}_{3}{\bf C}_{2}\mathrm{+i}{\bf C}{\bf O N})$ -PP, which highlights the importance of polysulfides adsorption, diffusion, and conversion in heterostructure materials. Therefore, the synergistic effects of iCON and $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}$ combine their merits while getting rid of the associated disadvantages. Smooth trapping-diffusionconversion of polysulfides ensures high electrochemical performance of $\mathrm{Ti}_{3}\mathrm{C}_{2}@\mathrm{iCON}.\mathrm{PI}$ P.

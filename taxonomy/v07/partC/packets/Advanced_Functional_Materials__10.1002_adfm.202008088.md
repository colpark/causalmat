- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The crystalline state of ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite differs as a result of the specific growth technique selected for the final optoelectronic device, ranging from homogeneous bulk singlecrystals to inhomogeneous PC thin films or low-dimensional partially oriented structures (e.g., nanowires, quantum dots, and highly ordered crystals).[29–33] To investigate the structural differences between these crystalline states, we selected three archetypal platforms: PC, HOC, and SC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites fabricated through spin-coating,[17,34] temperature-gradientassisted solidification,[35] and inverse temperature crystallization processes,[36] respectively. Figure  1A shows photographs of the three structures and the top-views of scanning electron microscopy images, whereas the inset histograms present their domain-size distributions. The homogeneous SC structure contained the largest domain size $({>}1000~\upmu\mathrm{m})$ , and the HOC films consisted of mid-sized domains $({\approx}70~\upmu\mathrm{m})$ , whereas the smallest domain size $(<200\mathrm{nm})$ accompanied by size inhomogeneity characterized the PC films. In terms of crystallinity, the as-prepared SC and HOC samples display only a minimal set of peaks in their XRD patterns as a result of the specific crystal orientation in the diffractometer (Figure 1B). Moreover, the PC films are characterized by a larger number of diffraction peaks, indicating a partial loss in the preferential orientation of the crystal. All the characteristic reflections of ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ are apparent in the powdered sample’s XRD pattern, indicating a complete random orientation and confirming that all prepared ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites have the same chemical composition (Figure 1C).

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Figure  2A shows the temperature-dependent XRD 2theta peaks of the (004) and (242) planes for the as-prepared SC, PC, and HOC ranging from $40^{\circ}$ to $42^{\circ}$ , which exhibit the relative lattice strain trend of each phase, including before and after the transition temperatures. A decrease in the 2theta value of each XRD peak corresponds to the d-space elongation of the corresponding plane. The (004) and (242) planes display structural interplanar extensions in specific crystal directions with increasing temperature.[28] Figure 2B,C illustrate the (004) and (242) planes in the orthorhombic phase, respectively. In the case of SC perovskite (denoted by blue color), the peak positions of the (004) and (242) planes are proportional to the temperature regardless of their phase, indicating that the linear thermal expansion of lattices in both the orthorhombic and tetragonal phases accompanies the transition. However, the XRD peaks of PC and HOC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites (denoted by red and black colors, respectively) start at different 2theta peak positions and exhibit a non-linear shift with temperature in both phases. In particular, the (004) plane of PC distinctly deviates from linear extrapolations in both phase regions with clear spectral evidence of coexistence in the temperature range near the phase transition (highlighted in yellow). Similar analyses were performed for other corresponding pairs of crystallographic planes of SC and PC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites (see Figures S2, S3, and S4, Supporting Information). Thus, we deduced that the difference in the degree of thermal lattice expansion accompanying the phase transition in these corresponding planes depends on the different crystalline states of the ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites, which are not related to their chemical composition.  To gain further insights into these material-state-dependent deformations, we analyzed the FWHM of the 2theta peaks, which are ${\approx}2.355$ of the fitted Gaussian standard deviation $\sigma$ (Figure S5, Supporting Information). Figure  2D shows the relative FWHM variation of the three ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites with temperature for the (004) and (242) planes as $\Delta\sigma\left(\mathrm{T}\right)/\sigma\left(100\mathrm{K}\right)$ . In general, peak broadening is caused by a deviation from the ideal crystalline lattice as a finite crystallite size (Scherrer model), grain surface relaxation, and non-uniform lattice distortions monotonously increasing with temperature (Debye– Waller factor).[37,38] Following the Williamson–Hall model,[39] we expected the pure size effect to be substantially temperature independent, whereas the lattice strain contribution to the linewidth is proportional to a temperature-dependent inhomogeneous strain that we recognized to have a dominant role in the phenomenology of ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite.

- caption: Figure 3. Temperature-dependent photoluminescence (TDPL) and power-dependent photoluminescence (PDPL) contour maps of as-grown PC, HOC, and SC ${\mathsf{M A P b}}|_{3}$ perovskite A) before and B) after finely grinding.
- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): We next studied the spectral differences in the TDPL and power-dependent PL (PDPL) patterns of the PC, HOC, and SC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites. The samples were equilibrated for $10\mathrm{min}$ at each temperature setpoint before the TDPL/PDPL measurements to ensure uniform temperature throughout the material. Figure  3A shows the normalized TDPL and PDPL contour maps of the three ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites. We noted that each crystalline state consistently exhibited inherent PL contour map patterns. At temperatures below $150~\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{~\AA~}}$ , the PC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite showed dual PL emission peaks and a spectrum tail in the infrared (IR) region. Trap states cause the spectrum tail of the PC perovskite thin film, and they are clearly activated in the orthorhombic phase in the low-temperature region.[15,16,43] In contrast, the SC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite showed only a single PL emission peak ( $750~\mathrm{nm}$ at 80 K) without any tail features in the spectrum. The HOC sample shows PL emission features intermediate between those of the PC and SC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites. The TDPL pattern of the HOC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite is similar to that of another PC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite with an average domain size of $100~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ (Figure S13, Supporting Information). Despite all samples being ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ , these differences in TDPL properties were repeatedly and reproducibly observed according to the domain size below $150~\mathrm{K}.$ . Above $150~\mathrm{K}$ , all three structures commonly exhibit single PL signals corresponding to the tetragonal phase of ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite. However, we cannot exclude the presence of orthorhombic or any other phase that could be optically inactive in this case.  The PDPL contour maps of the as-grown PC, HOC, and SC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites also displayed noticeable differences (Figure 3A). The PC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite shows a dual PL peak at $80~\mathrm{K}$ over the excitation fluence range of $50{-}370~\upmu\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{i}}$ owing to the coexistence of the orthorhombic and tetragonal phases. In contrast, the HOC and SC samples showed only single PL peaks corresponding to the orthorhombic phase. Based on these spectral results, we deduced that the phase transition from the orthorhombic phase $(\approx750\mathrm{nm}$ at 80 K) to the tetragonal phase $({\approx}780\mathrm{nm}$ at 300 K) proceeds differently in various crystalline states of the ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite. Considering our model and expectations, these differences were not surprising when the as-prepared PC, HOC, and SC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites were ground into fine powders (Figure  3B). We performed a fine grinding process to obtain ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ nanoparticles,[44] as shown in Figure S14, Supporting Information. The PL peak position of each powder was shifted according to the size distribution of the nanoparticles.[45] However, the multiple emission features on the TDPL/PDPL contour maps converge to the same emission pattern, that is, to that of the tetragonal phase. Notably, the tetragonal phase is present even at temperatures below $150~\mathrm{K}$ without undergoing any optically detectable phase transitions. Furthermore, we ground the samples under an inert atmosphere $(N_{2})$ to eliminate the effects of possible oxidation or hydration of the perovskite. However, grinding only affected the FWHM of PL (Figure S15, Supporting Information). In fact, the PL properties of ground ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites resemble those of ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ nanocrystals (NCs). Diroll et  al.[21] recently demonstrated that ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ NCs exhibit optical properties distinct from those of the bulk material below $160~\mathrm{K}_{\cdot}$ including no optically detectable phase transitions from the tetragonal to orthorhombic phases. In addition to the PL, visible and IR absorption measurements confirmed that ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ NCs retained the properties of the tetragonal phase even at temperatures as low as 3 K.[21] These experimental observations indicate that in the absence of domain boundaries, the optical characteristics of ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites still depend on the domain size. This relationship is clearly observed in the distinctive PL features of the SCs and powdered samples.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): As demonstrated, unlike other crystalline states of ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite, the SC samples undergo an abrupt phase transition between the orthorhombic and tetragonal phases at ${\approx}150\mathrm{~K~}$ . To investigate the phase-transition process from the surface to the bulk, we measured the TDPL spectra of the sample using three different excitation wavelengths: 325, 473, and $633~\mathrm{nm}$ . The size of the SC perovskite used in the TDPL measurements was ${\approx}3~\mathrm{mm}$ The penetration depth $(\delta)$ in the perovskite structure at these laser lines was calculated to be 19.6, 104.2, and $494~\mathrm{nm}$ , respectively, based on the absorption coefficients reported in the literature.[46] The TDPL contour map for each excitation is magnified over the phase-transition $(\Delta T_{\mathrm{trans}})$ temperature range from 135 to $150~\mathrm{K}$ (Figure 4A). As mentioned in the previous section, the PL at ${\approx}750~\mathrm{nm}$ corresponds to the orthorhombic phase, whereas the PL at ${\approx}780~\mathrm{nm}$ stems from the tetragonal phase. By increasing the temperature, we observed an evolution of the ratio of the two peaks, which indicates a change in the proportion of the phases. Moreover, we observed different proportions of the two phases at varying penetration depths (Figure  4B). The $X{-}X^{\prime}$ isotherm lines reveal that the ${\mathrm{SC~MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite remains mostly in the orthorhombic phase at $138\mathrm{~K~}$ , whereas its surface is composed of two phases. However, the $Y{-}Y^{\prime}$ isotherm lines show that the core of the SC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ mainly consists of the orthorhombic phase at $143\mathrm{~K~}$ , whereas the surface has a higher ratio of the tetragonal phase. Finally, the $Z{-}Z^{\prime}$ isotherm lines indicate the presence of mixed phases in the core of the crystal and the complete conversion of the surface to the tetragonal phase at 148 K. These findings indicate that the phase transition of SC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ from the orthorhombic-totetragonal phases starts from the crystal (i.e., domain) surface and continues inward toward the crystal (i.e., domain) core, resembling a shrinking core model.[47,48] A surface-triggered phase transition induces this inward propagation of the phase boundary within a narrow temperature region $(\Delta T_{\mathrm{trans}}<10\mathrm{~K})$ , as illustrated in Figure 4C.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To further elucidate the domain-size-dependent phase transition of ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite, we selected a number of crystallites from gently crushed SCs with different domain sizes and measured their size and PL spectra at $80\mathrm{~K~}$ (Figure  5A). For SCs with a domain size smaller than $50~\mathrm{nm}$ , a single PL peak arising from the tetragonal phase was observed. In contrast, for domain sizes between 1 and $300~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ , the crystallites displayed dual PL emission originating from the coexistence of the two phases. Above a domain size of $500~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ , a single PL emission peak arising from the orthorhombic phase was observed. In comparison, Diroll et  al.[21] reported the TDPL results for NCs $(D_{\mathrm{d}}<20\mathrm{nm})$ , showing a tetragonal phase with a single PL peak over the entire temperature range from 10 to $300~\mathrm{K}$ and no phase transition between the tetragonal and orthorhombic phases. Dar et  al.[13] reported the existence of two phases in PC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ $(D_{\mathrm{d}}>500\mathrm{nm})$ that exhibit dual emissions at temperatures as low as $15\mathrm{~K~}$ . Diab et  al.[14] reported a single orthorhombic phase in SC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ $(D_{\mathrm{d}}>2\mathrm{cm})$ at $^{80\mathrm{~K~}}$ with an abrupt phase transition at $150~\mathrm{K}$ . Similarly, Fang et al.[20] measured the PL of SC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite at $^{80\mathrm{~K~}}$ , but observed dual emissions due to $D_{\mathrm{d}}{<}200~\upmu\mathrm{m}$ . Thus, our observations reconcile the disparate results reported in the literature and demonstrate that at low temperatures, the tetragonal phase becomes more favorable with decreasing domain sizes in ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite. Based on this finding, we revealed that regardless of the presence or absence of domain boundaries, as in PC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ , the optical properties and phase transition are mainly determined by the domain size of perovskite because SC ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ generates various domain-size-dependent residual stresses. In addition, the difference in the internal residual stress, depending on the domain size, acts as a relative repulsive force, and it could suppress the phase transition that attempts to propagate from the surface to the core of domains, such as in the shrinking core model shown in Figure 4.  Figure  5B summarizes the phase transitions of ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskites as a function of the domain size. Based on our two observations of a domain-size-dependent phase transition and a surface-triggered phase transition, we present a schematic of the mechanism of the orthorhombic-to-tetragonal phase transition in ${\mathrm{MAPbI}}_{3}$ perovskite. Specifically, large domains $(D_{\mathrm{d}}>200~\upmu\mathrm{m})$ ) are resistant to continuous phase transitions up to relatively high temperatures. However, after reaching a critical point $(T_{\mathrm{trans}}\approx150~\mathrm{K})$ , a complete transition occurs within a short temperature range despite the large domain size. In contrast, intermediate-sized domains $(10~\upmu\mathrm{m}<D_{\mathrm{d}}<300~\upmu\mathrm{m})$  undergo a gradual phase transition over a wide temperature range, with onset temperatures significantly lower than those for larger domains. The elevation of the phase-transition temperature with an increase in the domain size has been observed in other materials.[49] Finally, the smallest domains do not undergo any optically detected phase transition and prefer to maintain the tetragonal phase throughout the entire temperature range. We propose that the unit cell volume change during the phase-transition process creates residual stress between the two phases. This stress increases with the domain size. Thus, SCs with large domains undergo a phase transition at higher temperatures than structures with smaller domains, as the large residual stress of the former resists the transformation. Furthermore, the narrow temperature window of the phase transition of SCs is also a result of the residual stress, which facilitates the transformation for the release of the large-accumulated stress.

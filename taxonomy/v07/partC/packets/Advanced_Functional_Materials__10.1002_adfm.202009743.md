- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Vanadium-doped NiFe-LDH (denoted as NiFeV-LDH) was adopted as the typical example to investigate the effect of metal ions doping to the OER performance in LDHs. Transmission electron microscope (TEM) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images demonstrated that NiFeV-LDHs were uniform and individual nanoplatelets with a lateral size of ${\approx}100\mathrm{nm}$ (Figure  1a and Figure S1, Supporting Information). As displayed in Figure  1a inset, the high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) image of NiFeV-LDH reveals a lattice spacing of $0.25\mathrm{nm}$ , which matches well with the (012) plane of LDHs. Moreover, the molar ratio of Ni, Fe, and V in the LDH is about 9:3:1, as determined by energy dispersive X-ray detector (EDX, Table S1, Supporting Information). The typical high-angle annular dark-field scanning TEM (HAADF-STEM) image of NiFeV-LDH nanoplatelets and corresponding EDX mapping verify the uniform distribution of Ni, Fe, V, and O elements in the LDH host layers (Figure  1b). Furthermore, the crystal structure of NiFeV-LDH was confirmed by powder X-ray diffraction (XRD, Figure  1c). The diffraction peaks of NiFeV-LDH match well with LDHs characteristic patterns with $R3m$ rhombohedral symmetry, with an interlayer distance $(d_{003})$ of ${\approx}0.75~\mathrm{nm}$ , in accordance with the carbonate-containing LDHs. No extra phase related to vanadium-based compound or other impurity was found. The inset in Figure  1c shows high-resolution patterns of (110) and (113) diffraction peaks for NiFe-LDH and NiFeV-LDH. No clear shift of peak position indicates that the distance between adjacent metal cations in host layer after V doping remains unchanged. Besides, the broaden peaks in NiFeV-LDH are probably caused by the decreased particle size and increased disorder degree in the LDH host lattice compared with pristine NiFe-LDH. All the above results show that no obvious changes have arisen in the structure and morphology after introducing of $\mathrm{V}$ into NiFe-LDH. The valence state of V in NiFeV-LDH was confirmed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). As illustrated in Figure  1d, the $\mathrm{~V~}2\mathrm{p}$ spectrum shows two peaks of V $2{\tt p}_{3/2}$ $(517.2~\mathrm{eV})$ and V $2\mathrm{p}_{1/2}$ $(524.5~\mathrm{eV})$ ), indicative of ${\mathrm{V}}^{3+}$ in NiFeV-LDH.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To investigate the effect of V doping on catalytic properties of NiFe-LDH, the OER performance of NiFeV-LDH and control samples were evaluated in $1\textbf{M}$ KOH. As a result, NiFeV-LDH shows the lowest onset overpotential $(\eta_{\mathrm{onset}}=241\mathrm{mV})$ and the largest current density of oxygen evolution along with the increasing of applied potential (Figure 2a,c). The overpotential $(\eta)$ of NiFeV-LDH electrocatalyst is $287~\mathrm{mV}$ at the current density $(j)$ of $10\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , which is 42 and $77\mathrm{mV}$ less than those of NiFe-LDH and $\mathrm{IrO}_{2}$ , respectively. When the oxidation overpotential gets to as high as $300~\mathrm{mV},$ the current density of NiFeVLDH is $15.86\mathrm{mAcm}^{-2}$ , which is 3.8 and 12.2 times higher than those of NiFe-LDH and $\mathrm{IrO}_{2}$ , respectively (Figure  2b,c). Moreover, the Tafel slope of NiFeV-LDH was $53.7\mathrm{mV}\mathrm{dec}^{-1}$ , which is much smaller than that of NiFe-LDH $\mathrm{(67.1~mV~dec^{-1})}$ and $\mathrm{IrO}_{2}$ $(56.8~\mathrm{mV~dec^{-1}})$ ), respectively (Figure  2d). The long-term stability of NiFeV-LDH was also measured at $10\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , which displayed almost no decay after test for $32\mathrm{~h~}$ (Figure S3, Supporting Information). As demonstrated above, the NiFeV-LDH shows enhanced OER activity compared with pristine NiFeLDH and commercial $\mathrm{IrO}_{2}$ catalyst.  The electrocatalytic performances are generally affected by interior factor (exposure degree and intrinsic property of active sites, electron transfer) and exterior factor (electrolyte transport). The electrochemical surface area (ECSA) was involved to demonstrate the effect exposure amount of active sites. As displayed in Figure $\mathtt{S4c}$ in the Supporting Information, NiFeV-LDH shows resembled double layer capacitance ( $C_{\mathrm{dl}}$ $,0.20\mathrm{mF}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ ) with pristine NiFe-LDH $(0.18~\mathrm{mF~cm^{-2}})$ ), indicating similar effective surface areas and active site amounts of both LDHs. Despite similar ECSA, the turnover frequency of NiFeV-LDH at $\eta=300\mathrm{mV}$ $(0.016\mathrm{s^{-1}})$ was more than three times higher than that of NiFe-LDH $(0.005~\mathrm{{s^{-1}})}$ , indicating that the intrinsic activity of each active site is dramatically improved after introducing V atoms into NiFe-LDH (Figure S5, Supporting Information). The OER performance normalized to ECSA shows that the NiFeV-LDH still outperforms NiFe-LDH by 3.2 times at a fixed overpotential (1.55  V) (Figure S6, Supporting Information), suggesting that the surface area is not the key factor for the enhanced OER activity of NiFeV-LDH. The bandgap width of nonconductive electrocatalysts is of vital importance to the electrons transfer inside the catalysts during the electrochemical process. As displayed in Figure S7 in the Supporting Information, a narrower bandgap $(0.78~\mathrm{eV})$ was observed for NiFeV-LDH compared with that of NiFe-LDH (1.27  eV), suggesting a more efficient and rapid electron transfer in V-doped NiFe-LDH. This is consistent with the results of electrochemical impedance spectroscopy, which demonstrates the smaller charge transfer resistance for NiFeV-LDH (Figure S8, Supporting Information). In addition, the mass transport ability of each electrocatalysts during OER was examined by conducting linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) under different scan rate. As shown in Figure S9 in the Supporting Information, negligible changes were observed when increasing scan rate from 1 to $5\mathrm{mV~s^{-1}}$ , which indicates that electrolyte species can rapidly transport to active sites in both NiFe-LDH and NiFeV-LDH. Therefore, the improved OER activity of NiFeV-LDH can be ascribed to the enhanced intrinsic activity of active sites as well as rapid electron transfer along inside catalyst. Moreover, the effect of V contents in NiFeV-LDH (denoted as NiFeV-LDH-1 and NiFeV-LDH-2) on OER activity has been investigated. According to the EDX, the molar ratios of Ni, Fe, and V in these two LDHs are about 23:10:1 and 11:4:1, respectively (Table S5, Supporting Information). The XRD patterns and SEM images of these two LDHs are in accordance with pristine NiFe-LDH and NiFeV-LDH, indicating their identical structures and morphologies (Figures S10 and S11, Supporting Information). The NiFeV-LDH-1 and NiFeV-LDH-2 display the similar ECSA compared with pristine NiFe-LDH and NiFeV-LDH (Figure S12, Supporting Information). As shown in Figure S13 in the Supporting Information, the NiFeV-LDH-1 and NiFeV-LDH-2 display increased OER activity compared with pristine NiFe-LDH but still worse than NiFeV-LDH $\mathrm{Ni}{:}\mathrm{Fe}{:}\mathrm{V}=9{:}3{:}1\rangle$ , suggesting that OER activity of NiFe-LDH can be improved along with the increase of incorporation of V amounts.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The XPS and extended X-ray absorption fine structure (EXAFS) were conducted to further investigate the effect of intrinsic electronic structure of NiFe-LDH and NiFeV-LDH on water oxidation. The Ni 2p XPS spectra show no shift after introducing V into NiFe-LDH (Figure 3a), while an obvious positive shift $_{(0.6\mathrm{eV}}$ for $2{\tt p}_{3/2}$ , $0.5\mathrm{eV}$ for $2\ensuremath{\mathrm{p}}_{1/2})$ is observed in Fe 2p spectra (Figure  3b), indicating that doping of V atoms results in the electrons withdrawal from Fe compared with pristine NiFe-LDH. The Fourier transform magnitudes of the Ni and Fe K-edge EXAFS for the NiFe- and NiFeV-LDH were shown in Figure  3c,d. The Ni K-edge EXAFS spectra demonstrates that NiFeV-LDH exhibits explicit octahedral coordination of Ni–O and Ni–M $[\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{Fe},\mathrm{V}$ ) at a distance of 1.6 and $2.7\mathrm{~\AA~}$ respectively, which are in accordance with pristine NiFe-LDH. But the Ni–O and Ni–M coordination peaks display a slight decrease, indicating the increased disorder degree in the LDH host lattice by the V-doping. In contrast, the Fe K-edge EXAFS for V-doped NiFe-LDH shows a clear contract of FeO bond lengths from 1.56 to $1.51\mathring\mathrm{A}$ , which could be ascribed to the existence of oxygen vacancies. In addition, Fe–M $\mathbf{\Psi}[\mathrm{M}\mathbf{\Psi}=\mathrm{Ni}$ , V) coordination length of NiFeV-LDH was enlarged from 2.70 to $2.74\mathrm{~\AA~}$ compared with pristine NiFe-LDH, suggesting more unsaturated metal sites have been formed due to the incorporation of V atoms. The least-squares EXAFS fitting was carried out to investigate the quantitative structure of Ni and Fe sites in NiFe-LDH and NiFeV-LDH. According to the EXAFS fitting results (Table S6, Supporting Information), the coordination numbers of Ni–O and Fe–O in NiFeV-LDH (Ni–O: 5.4; Fe–O: 5.9) get lower in comparison with that of NiFe-LDH (Ni–O: 5.5; Fe–O: 6.1), indicating the increased oxygen vacancy defects in NiFeV-LDH. As a result, the increased oxygen vacancy defects and unsaturated dangling band on the surface of V-doped NiFe-LDH could serve as the efficient catalytic active sites for enhancing OER performance.

Therefore, the introduction of Co, V, and Cr can efficiently reduce the reaction thermodynamic obstacle of NiFe-LDH, while the V, Ti, and Mn dopants will accelerate the kinetic process of OER.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): To further explore the inner relationship between active-site electronic structure and OER performance of NiFeM-LDHs, XPS was carried out to thoroughly investigate the Ni, Fe, and the adjacent O in LDHs host layer. As displayed in Figure  7a, compared with pristine NiFe-LDH, the binding energies of Ni $2{\tt p}_{3/2}$ remain unchanged after introducing extra metal atoms into NiFe-LDH. However, the binding energies of Fe $2\mathrm{p}_{3/2}$ in NiFeM-LDH $(\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{Ti}$ , V, Cr, Mn, Co, Cu, Zn) are shifted to a more positive binding energy $(0.2\mathrm{-}0.6\mathrm{eV},$ Figure  7b), which demonstrates that extra transition metal dopants result in the electrons withdrawal from Fe atoms in NiFe-LDH. Under the above electronic effect, the electron binding energy of O shows clear difference in different NiFeM-LDHs (Figure 7c). To demonstrate the relationship between intrinsic electronic structure and OER apparent activity, the OER onset overpotential was associated with the degree of electron transfer between FeO atoms in as-synthesized NiFeM-LDHs. As shown in Figure 7d, the “reverse volcanic type” implies that OER activity of NiFeMLDHs is following Sabatier principle. Besides, the valence electronic configurations of doped $\mathrm{Ti^{4+}}$ , ${\mathrm{V}}^{3+}$ , $\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}$ , $\mathrm{Mn^{4+}}$ , $\mathrm{Co}^{2+}$ , $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$ , and $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ are $3\mathrm{d^{0}(t_{2g}\mathrm{^0e_{g}^{0}}),3\mathrm{d^{2}(t_{2g}\mathrm{^2e_{g}^{0}}),3\mathrm{d^{3}(t_{2g}\mathrm{^3e_{g}^{0}),3\mathrm{d^{2}(t_{2g}\mathrm{^2e_{g}^{0}),}}}}}}}$ $3\mathrm{d}^{7}$ $(\mathrm{t_{2g}}^{5}\mathrm{e_{g}}^{2})$ , $3\mathrm{d^{9}(t_{2g}^{-}{}^{6}e_{g}^{3})}$ , and $\breve{3}\mathrm{d^{1\breve{0}}~}(\mathrm{t_{2g}^{~6}e_{g}^{~4}})$ , respectively. The electron transfer from lone pairs in $\textsf{O}2\mathrm{p}$ to the cations with low $\mathrm{t}_{2\mathrm{g}}$ occupancy $\mathrm{(Ti^{4+}}$ and $\mathrm{Mn^{4+}}$ ) should be stronger than that of high $\mathrm{t}_{2\mathrm{g}}$ occupancy $\mathrm{(Cu^{2+}}$ and $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ ). The too strong or too weak electron transfer could affect the electron structure of Fe active sites via $\mathrm{O}^{2-}$ bridges between $\mathrm{Fe{-O-M_{\mathrm{doped}}}}$ . The moderated occupied $\mathrm{t}_{2\mathrm{g}}$ orbitals $(\mathrm{V}^{3+},\mathrm{Cr}^{3+})$ , and $\mathrm{Co}^{2+}$ ) could lead to suitable electronic structure of Fe atoms in NiFe-LDH-based catalysts, thus resulting in the appropriate bonding strength between Fe and adsorbed oxygen species. As a result, the OER active sites could be modified to an optimal electron environment when NiFe-LDH was doped by Co, V, and $\mathrm{Cr}$ atoms, resulting in the corresponding reduced onset potential $\eta_{\mathrm{onset}}<250\mathrm{mV})$ . The Ti, Mn, Zn, and Cu-doped NiFe-LDHs display a moderately enhanced OER performance $(250<\eta_{\mathrm{onset}}<275\mathrm{mV})$ ), due to the too strong (Ti and Mn) or too weak ( $\mathrm{\ddot{Z}n}$ and Cu) electron migration. When the binding energy of Fe–O gets to under $0.1{\mathrm{eV}},$ the OER activities are apparently decreased owing to the almost invisible electron transfer in $\mathrm{Mg}.$ and Al-doped NiFe-LDH with no 3d orbitals. The different valence electronic configuration of transition metals and main group metals will affect the electron transfer and electronic structure around Fe active sites through the $\mathrm{O}^{2-}$ bridges between $\mathrm{Fe{-O-M_{\mathrm{doped}}}}$ . Therefore, the Co-, Cr-, and V-doped NiFe-LDHs with appropriate electronic modulation are located at the top of volcano, indicating the promoted thermodynamics properties toward OER process compared with pristine NiFe-LDH.

- path: /home/aid1/Documents/causalmat/matmech/Advanced_Functional_Materials/10.1002_adfm.202009743/images/63692de63082eeb748d287fbde3f7b140858d431dc65d41169bcc8f2048c9f79.jpg

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The above results demonstrated that extra metal-doping into the host lattice of NiFe-LDH could accelerate the electron transport process. It is known that NiFe-LDHs are similar to semiconductors, whose conductivity is related to bandgap width to a large extent. Thus, the bandgap width of NiFeM-LDHs was first obtained from DFT calculated density of states (DOS, Figure 8a). The incorporation of transition metal atoms makes the bandgap narrower for NiFeM-LDHs $(0.65{-}0.99\mathrm{eV})$ than that of undoped NiFe-LDH $(1.27~\mathrm{eV})$ , as shown in Table S3 in the Supporting Information, indicating a fast electron transportation during the reactions. The reduced bandgap width of NiFeM-LDHs is also confirmed by the UV-vis diffuse spectrum (Figures S17 and S18, Supporting Information), which can be indirectly obtained by plotting the $(a h\nu)^{2}$ versus $h\nu$ . To illustrate the relationship of the conductivity and the OER kinetics, the bandgap widths of NiFeM-LDHs were correlated with their Tafel slopes. As shown in Figure 8b, the V-, Ti-, and Mn-doped NiFeLDHs with narrowed bandgap $(<0.9~\mathrm{eV},$ denoted as first group) are located in low Tafel slope area $(<60\mathrm{mV}\mathrm{dec}^{-1})$ , which indicates easier electron transportation inside the LDH catalysts. The incorporation of Cr, Zn, Co, Cu, and Al leads to slightly larger bandgap (0.8−1.1 eV, denoted as second group) compared with first group, which displays the Tafel slope between 60 and $90\mathrm{mV}\mathrm{dec}^{-1}$ . The dopant $\mathrm{Mg}$ leads to a much larger bandgap $(1.5\mathrm{eV})$ compared with the pristine NiFe-LDH (1.27  eV), resulting in harder electron transport and lowered OER performance. The transition metals and main group metals have different abilities of bounding valence electrons, which can change the bandgap width of NiFe-LDH, resulting in different electrons conductivity of NiFeM-LDHs. These results illustrate that certain metal doping (Ti, V, and Mn) can enhance the conductivity, leading to the improved OER kinetics of NiFe-LDHbased catalysts.

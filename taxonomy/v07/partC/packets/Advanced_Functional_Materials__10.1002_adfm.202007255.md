- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): Different TMO particles, including $\mathrm{TiO}_{2}$ , MnO, FeO, CoO, NiO, and CuO, were first uniformly coated on the surface of porous PP separators with a thickness of around $3.5\upmu\mathrm{m}$ (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The lithium deposition behaviors were first screened using Li||Cu cells, with TMO particles layer contacting Li metal to directly react with Li and form a passivating interlayer.[11] The dissolved TMO in electrolyte could migrate across the porous separator and reach copper foil, driven by the concentration gradient. After a lithium deposition of $2.0\mathrm{mAh~cm}^{-2}$ in a current density of $0.5\mathrm{mA}\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ , as shown in Figure 1, all anode sheets exhibited dendrite free lithium plating with a dual layer morphology. Among these oxide coatings, the cells with CoO and $\mathtt{M n O}$ coated PP separators showed obviously denser spherical Li deposition and higher initial plating/stripping efficiency (Figure S2, Supporting Information). The dual layer Li deposition is more obvious with the help of CoO and MnO-coated PP separator, with a flat bulky lithium sheet on top and a layer of compactly arranged lithium spheres on bottom. Considering the advantage of MnO in lower cost and slightly higher plating/stripping efficiency compared with CoO, this study focused more on the electrochemical performance of MnO-coated PP.

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): In a $\mathrm{Li}\|\mathrm{Cu}$ cell with $\mathrm{MnO/PP}$ separator, after a Li deposition of 0.2 mAh $\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ on Cu foil, a uniform layer of closely arranged spheres on copper substrate are observed and some spheres own a dimple (Figure 3a and Figure $\mathrm{S4a-c}$ , Supporting Information). Surface roughness of Li spheres are clearer under scanning transmission electron microscope (STEM) image (Figure 3b) and magnified scanning electron microscope (SEM) image (Figure S4b, Supporting Information), which can be attributed to the surface SEI layer. The energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) mapping images indicated that elemental Mn dispersed on the surface of these spherical particles (insert of Figure 3b; and Figure S4d, Supporting Information), further verifying the migration of dissolved MnO in the electrolyte solution and the creation of Mn containing SEI on the surface of deposited Li spheres. High resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) of surface SEI layer, as shown in Figure 3c, shows some nanocrystals distributed on the surface layer with different contrast and lattice fringe distances. The darker particles with lattice distance of $0.211\mathrm{nm}$ could be attributed to (411) plane of the metallic $\mathrm{Mn}^{0}$ ; the lighter contrast particles with lattice distance of 0.271  nm matched well with (111) plane of the $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{O},^{[17]}$ which exhibited stronger diffraction signals than that of ${\mathrm{Mn}}^{0}$ (inset of Figure 3c). It is recently demonstrated that $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ containing SEI layer shows better mechanical strength and stability during cycling of Li-metal cells, compared with LiF or $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{CO}_{3}$ that has long been considered playing an important role in stable SEI.[18] With the continuous increase of Li deposition, an interesting layer-by-layer deposition is observed (Figure S5a–c, Supporting Information), smaller dimension $(\approx2.0~\upmu\mathrm{m})$ dimpled spheres at the bottom and the sheet-like bulky Li (with diameter ${\approx}58.5~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ and thickness around $6.5~{\upmu\mathrm{m}}$ ) on the top (Figure 3d,e; and Figure S5d–f, Supporting Information). This dimension variation of lithium particles on these two layers implies that the corresponding Li deposition conditions should be slightly different, mainly owing to the different deposition substrates of copper foil and lithium metal, and the following variation on interfacial wettability and interfacial energy.  To identify the difference of the SEI layers with and without the MnO coating on PP separator, the morphology and composition of SEI on Cu foil were characterized after the first plating/stripping process cycle when all the deposited Li was stripped off. A uniform thin film could be observed covering the Cu foil after electrochemically stripping the Li on copper in a Li||Cu cell using $\mathrm{MnO/PP}$ separator (Figure 3f). This thin membrane could be attributed to the reduction of $\mathtt{M n O}$ and electrolyte before and during the Li-deposition, which functioned as a composite SEI layer protecting the deposited Limetal. On the contrary, some dendritic species were still left on the Cu foil after stripping the Li in a Li||Cu cell with PP separator at the same conditions (Figure  3g).  In the XPS survey spectra of the SEI layer on copper foil after stripping the Li in $\mathrm{Li}\|\mathrm{Cu}$ cell with $\mathrm{MnO/PP}$ separator, mainly C, O, Li, and F, S elements were detected (Figure S6, Supporting Information), without the observation of $\mathrm{{Mn}^{0}}$ or $\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$ , indicating no redundant $\mathrm{Mn}^{0}/\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$ was deposited on the surface of SEI layer.[19] This is critical since it means that the reduction of dissolved MnO is suspended once the SEI layer is constructed, which will reduce the continuous consumption of Li or electron source and therefore will not hurt the CE in cells. However, when this SEI layer was etched ${\approx}100\mathrm{nm}$ , XPS peaks of $\mathrm{Mn}^{0}$ metal were clearly observed (Figure S6, Supporting Information; and Figure 3h), and the peaks at ${\approx}639.4$ and $647.2~\mathrm{eV}$ were assigned to $\mathrm{Mn}^{0}~2\mathrm{p}_{3/2}$ and $\mathrm{Mn}^{0}~2\mathrm{p}_{1/2}$ , indicating that ${\mathrm{Mn}}^{0}$ nanoparticles (NPs) were distributed under the SEI layer. Some nanocrystals attributed to the $\mathrm{Mn}^{0}$ NPs were also observed in the HRTEM image of this SEI layer (Figure  3i), which indicated that ${\mathrm{Mn}}^{0}$ NPs in SEI layer were not re-oxidized to $\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$ during stripping of Li. This composite SEI, formed by ${\mathrm{Mn}}^{0}$ , $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ and the reducing products of DOL/DME by Li, is different to the widely studied SEI in $\mathrm{LiMn}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{4/}$ /graphite system, where the dissolution–migration-deposition of Mn catalyze/involve the decomposition of ethylene carbonate electrolyte and degrades the SEI layer on graphite.[20] Here, the DOL/DME electrolyte exhibited stronger sluggishness toward $\mathrm{Mn}^{0}/\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$ , since the DOL/DME own a relatively higher lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) and benefit to be more compatible with low oxidation state transition metals.[21]

- Linked text (the paper's own sentences, as linked by the PDF parser): The working mechanism of MnO coating on the regulation of Li deposition is proposed and illustrated in Figure 6a, mainly including creation of Mn nucleation seeds, formation of “self-healing” SEI layer, and the eventual dual-layer deposition. Based on the deposition morphology with areal capacity, the dual-layer deposition of lithium on copper can be divided into two stages, the construction of bottom layer of lithium spheres, and the formation of top layer of flat bulky lithium.  MnO is slightly soluble in DOL/DME (v:v, 1:1) electrolyte at room temperature with a concentration of around $1.5~{\upmu\mathrm{M}}$ ,[10c,25] which helps to maintain a constant concentration and allows sustainable supply of MnO into electrolyte once the MnO is consumed. Before the beginning of Li deposition, the dissolved MnO and electrolyte solvent were preferentially reduced, producing a $\mathrm{Mn}^{0}$ and $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ containing SEI composite layer with some residual $\mathrm{Mn}^{0}/\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ NPs dispersed on copper foil. The Mn NPs on copper foil would function as seeds for the nucleation of deposited Li with the formation of bottom layer Li spheres, with the in situ formation of SEI layer, which isolates the direct contact between MnO/electrolyte and Li, and prevents their continuous reaction, like what the typical SEI layers did. This process is directly demonstrated by the surface Mn-free XPS spectra (Figure 3h). Once the SEI layer is damaged by the increased volume with the continuous lithium deposition,[24] the dissolved MnO and electrolyte will be reduced again by Li metal, forming new SEI, functioning as a “self-healing” protector. The sustained releasing of MnO into electrolyte, owing to its extremely low solubility, ensures a continuous supply of MnO. When the deposited Li was stripped back to Li metal anode, the established SEI film would be left on anode surface (Figure  3f) and could protect the lithium in following deposition, pretty like an externally introduced protection layer.[5b] This in situ created SEI film owns better contact across the interface and natural advantages in “self-healing”, owing to a continuous supply of MnO in electrolyte for timely recovery of the SEI once damaged.  The formation of second layer lithium deposition with larger dimension can be attributed to the variation of deposition substrates from copper to lithium metal, and the following better interfacial wettability and lower interfacial energy. In the first stage, with the continuous deposition, the copper foil will soon be covered by a layer of compactly arranged Li spheres (Figure  3a). The further Li deposition will enter a new stage, owing to the different substrates (copper VS lithium) and resultant different interfacial wettability and surface energy. Compared with the copper substrate, better interfacial wettability and lower surface energy of Li toward Li facilitates the formation of flat bulky lithium deposition, only if the deposited Li could be well protected by SEI layer. At this stage, the same SEI protection layer containing $\mathrm{Mn}^{0}/\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ and organic species still could be established, owing to the continuous existence of MnO, which functions to protect the deposited lithium as before. These features resulted into the formation of dendrite free dual-layer lithium deposition (Figure 6a).  It is observed that the overpotential of Li nucleation on copper with the MnO/PP separator $({\approx}1.2\mathrm{mV})$ is much smaller than that of the cell with uncoated PP separator $(\approx29.3\mathrm{mV})$ at the same conditions (Figure S13, Supporting Information). This feature indicates the ${\mathrm{Mn}}^{0}$ and $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ containing SEI layer significantly reduces the interfacial resistance and so benefits to the larger current Li deposition. It makes sense since the $\mathrm{Mn}^{0}$ NPs dispersed in SEI will increase the electronic conductivity of the SEI composite layer, and the $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ has also been demonstrated that it contributes more for stabilizing the interface than previously believed $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{CO}_{3}$ and LiF.[18a] This ${\mathrm{Mn}}^{0}$ and $\mathrm{Li}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ containing SEI layer, owning a property of mixed ionic electronic conductivity (MIEC), homogenizes the $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ flux and electric field across the interface, facilitating to the dendrite free Li deposition. In contrast, severe Li dendrites will form at the SEI ruptures in the Li||Cu cells with PP separator (Figure 6b). It had been demonstrated that the SEI layer owning a property of MIEC will help to suppress the dendrite owing to uniform electric field and $\operatorname{Li^{+}}$ flux, and the overpotential across the SEI layer would restrain the Li-deposition on SEI layer.[26]

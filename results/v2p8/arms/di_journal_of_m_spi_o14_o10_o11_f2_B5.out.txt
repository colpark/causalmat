The AQY panel (F8c) shows apparent quantum yield for Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS falling sharply with wavelength (18.3% at 400 nm down to 0.19% at 550 nm), and this decay closely tracks the sample's absorbance edge shown in the same panel (absorbance falls from ~900-1000 a.u. in the 300-400 nm region to near-baseline by 550 nm). Because F8c is measured on the single best-performing composition (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS), it does not by itself say anything about how composition affects performance — it only characterizes the spectral response of the top sample.

Combined with the earlier settled result (that Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS > Pd-ZIS > Ag-ZIS > ZIS in both steady photocurrent and H2 evolution rate), the two observations together support a fuller mechanistic picture for that one top-ranked sample: its superior H2-evolution performance is exercised specifically under UV-to-near-visible light (its high photocurrent and high H2 rate co-occur with a photoresponse that is strong only where absorption is strong, i.e., below ~450 nm), consistent with light absorption setting the upper bound on photocatalytic efficiency for this material.

What this leaves open: the AQY data give no information about whether the other three samples (Pd-ZIS, Ag-ZIS, ZIS) show the same wavelength dependence or the same or different absorption edges, so no comparison of AQY ranking across the composition series can be made, and the earlier photocurrent/H2 ranking cannot be extended into a wavelength-resolved explanation for why composition changes performance. Nor does this combination address the earlier-noted gaps: photocurrent and H2 rate remain different quantities whose rank match is not quantitative, the two mid-range Ag:Pd compositions remain uncovered for photocurrent, and confounding by composition (versus a causal photocurrent-to-H2 link) is still not ruled out. AQY only adds that, for the top sample, performance is visible-light-limited and edge-tracking — not that this generalizes across compositions.

This established result justifies treating the straight, {10-12}/{01-12}-trace bands and the wavy, side-surface-contrast-asymmetric bands as two distinct structural populations, and it licenses linking each morphology to a specific orientation behavior: the straight/lenticular bands correspond to bands that EBSD shows as a single uniform orientation colour (twin-like), while the wavy bands correspond to bands whose EBSD colour shades continuously with position (consistent with a kink-band-like, internally rotating structure). It supports naming this pairing (straight/uniform vs wavy/graded) as a coherent behavior_class distinction for PRP, grounded in two independent imaging modalities agreeing on where the boundary between the two populations falls, and it supports treating the [01-10] specimen's coexisting uniformly red {10-12} twins and continuously varying purple kink bands as one concrete instance of this general pairing.

What it leaves open: the result gives no quantitative measure of misorientation magnitude or gradient within either band type, so it cannot specify how much orientation changes across a kink band versus a twin, only that one is uniform and the other is not. It is based on only two specimen orientations ([11-20] and [01-10]), so the same morphology-orientation pairing is not established for other loading orientations or specimens. The correspondence is a co-registered imaging signature, not a demonstrated causal or mechanistic link, so it does not show what produces the continuous shading. Finally, because OM contrast and EBSD colour are different physical contrast mechanisms (optical/diffraction contrast versus crystallographic orientation coding), their agreement in classification does not mean they measure the same underlying physical variable — the link is a classification correspondence, not an equivalence of measured quantities.</message>

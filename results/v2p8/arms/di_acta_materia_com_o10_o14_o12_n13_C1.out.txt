This settled result supports a correspondence, not a causal or quantitative claim: the wavy, optically-contrast-asymmetric bands identified in OM (faint on (1-100)/(-2110), strong on (0001)) are the same population that EBSD shows as continuously shading in orientation (kink bands), while the straight {10-12}/{01-12}-trace bands in OM are the same population EBSD shows as uniform single-colour (twin-like). Because the two imaging modalities are co-registered by band type, this justifies treating "wavy/graded" and "straight/uniform" as one coherent structural distinction — i.e., that band morphology in OM tracks a real difference in internal orientation continuity as seen by EBSD, at least for the specimen orientations examined ([11-20] and [01-10]).

It leaves open several things. It gives no quantitative measure of misorientation magnitude across kink bands versus twins — only a qualitative uniform-vs-shading, strong-vs-faint contrast. It cannot be generalized beyond the two specimen/loading orientations studied; whether the same OM-morphology/EBSD-shading pairing holds for other orientations or specimens is untested. It establishes only a correspondence in imaging signature, not a mechanism explaining why wavy bands shade continuously (e.g., it does not prove continuous lattice rotation causes the optical contrast asymmetry, or vice versa). And because OM contrast and EBSD colour are physically different kinds of contrast (topographic/diffraction versus crystallographic-orientation coding), their agreement in sorting band types does not mean they measure the same underlying physical variable — so no claim can be made that one modality's signal is a proxy or explanation for the other's origin.</message>

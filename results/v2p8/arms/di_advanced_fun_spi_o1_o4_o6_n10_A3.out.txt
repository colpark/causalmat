Panel F2d (crops/...D.jpg) shows relative XRD peak width (Δσ/σ, % of 300 K width) vs. temperature for three perovskite forms (SC, PC, HOC), each with multiple hkl peaks (004, 242, 224, 400/242 etc.).

What the measurement supports: all three sample forms show a distinct, localized anomaly in peak width in the same narrow temperature window (~145-155 K), consistent with a structural/phase transition occurring near 150 K across SC, PC, and HOC. In SC the width drops sharply (from ~100% to ~50%, mainly on the 004/400 peaks), indicating abrupt peak sharpening at the transition. In PC and HOC the width instead spikes upward (PC's 242 peak reaching ~260%, HOC's 004 peak reaching ~128%) before relaxing toward baseline at higher T. So the data support: (1) a transition exists near 150 K in each form, and (2) the transition strongly and anisotropically affects specific lattice planes (only certain hkl peaks show the anomaly, others stay flat), implying the distortion is directional/plane-specific rather than an isotropic lattice change.

What it leaves open: the figure alone does not identify what the phase transition is (e.g., tetragonal-orthorhombic, ferroelastic domain formation, or something else), nor its mechanism. It does not explain why SC narrows while PC/HOC broaden at the same nominal temperature — this could reflect different domain/twinning behavior, different crystal quality, or different transition character (first-order vs. continuous), but the panel gives no direct evidence to decide among these. It also does not give absolute lattice parameters or peak positions (2θ shifts), only normalized width, so no conclusion about unit-cell symmetry or space group can be drawn from this panel by itself. Whether the transition temperature is identical or merely close across the three forms is also not sharply resolved, since the anomaly spans roughly 145-160 K rather than a single point.

Figure F2b (opened and confirmed) shows the HP hardness curve exactly as stated: ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt% AlN, rising to a peak ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then easing to ~19.4 GPa and ~19.2 GPa at 3 and 5 wt%.

What the combination supports: The earlier density+SEM result established that HP composites, especially at 1 and 5 wt% AlN, are genuinely pore-free/fully dense (near-100% relative density, confirmed by transgranular fracture with no visible open porosity). Since residual porosity is a well-known depressant of hardness, the rise in HP hardness from 18.0 GPa (0 wt%, only ~97.5% dense per the earlier record) to the peak ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt% (where density reaches ~100%) is consistent with, and partly explained by, that documented elimination of porosity — the hardness gain over this interval is corroborated as a genuine consolidation effect rather than a measurement artifact, paralleling the density/SEM story.

What it leaves open: The corroboration was only qualitative and only for 1 and 5 wt% specifically. Density stays ~100% (fully dense) at both 3 and 5 wt%, yet hardness is not monotonic — it dips slightly from the 1 wt% peak (20.3) to 19.4 and 19.2 GPa. Because porosity is already essentially eliminated across this whole 1–5 wt% range per the earlier finding, that small decline cannot be attributed to density/porosity differences at all; its cause (grain size, AlN distribution, intrinsic AlN softness, secondary-phase or grain-boundary effects) is not addressed by either the density or SEM data and remains undetermined. The combination also says nothing about the PS series or the mechanism linking AlN to hardness.</message>

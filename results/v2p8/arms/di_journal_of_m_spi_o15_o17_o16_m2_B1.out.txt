Both panels are ×750 SEM images at the same scale (20 µm bar). Panel a (stir casting) shows debris dominated by flat, plate-like flakes roughly 20–40 µm across with sharp, chipped edges — consistent with larger-scale material removal. Panel b (FSP) shows a field of much finer particles, mostly well under 5 µm, many clumped into loose agglomerates, with only occasional larger fragments at the margin.

Read together with the earlier settled finding — that both surfaces abrade (parallel grooves, no craters) but the FA-containing surface additionally shows shallower grooves covered with fine debris — this debris-size measurement corroborates and sharpens that earlier qualitative impression: it is not just that surface b "looks" covered in finer material, the debris particles themselves are measurably an order of magnitude smaller (<5 µm vs 20–40 µm) than the flakes generated on the comparison surface. This strengthens the case that the FA-bearing (FSP) surface wears by a milder, fine-particulate abrasive mode rather than coarse flake delamination, and the fine, loosely agglomerated particle morphology specifically favors the "fine debris rolling as three-body abrasive" sub-mechanism over the "FA blocking asperity penetration" sub-mechanism, since it is direct evidence of loose rolling particles rather than of an intact protective layer.

What remains open: the debris composition/origin (FA fragments vs. matrix wear product) is still not analyzed, so whether the fine debris actually derives from FA is unconfirmed; the causal link between fine debris presence and reduced groove depth is still only a spatial correlation, not demonstrated mechanistically; and both measurements remain single-image, unquantified-count observations, so full-surface representativeness and blocking-mechanism involvement are not excluded.

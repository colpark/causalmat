The established result supports a limited, regime-specific claim: in the low-loading window (0-4 wt%), the rise in beta-phase content (76.1-76.3% to a peak of 81.8-83.2%) tracks the early rise in voltage output (7.5-32.0 V), which is consistent with beta-phase enhancement contributing to the initial gain in PRF/service_capability. That is the full extent of what it settles — a trend-shape consistency over four data points, not a demonstrated mechanism.

It leaves open, and explicitly does not settle, several things. First, the full 0-10 wt% range: beyond 4 wt% beta content eases back to ~81% while voltage keeps climbing monotonically to 43.0 and 62.0 V, so beta-phase content cannot be the operative driver of the continued voltage rise at 6-10 wt%; some other factor must account for that later gain. Second, causal pathway versus coincidence: because PZT wt% drives both the FTIR (beta content) and transport (voltage) series by construction, the low-loading correlation does not by itself distinguish a genuine beta-phase-mediated pathway from two independent effects of the same shared driver. Third, magnitude: since one series is a normalized structural fraction (%) and the other an absolute output (V), no quantitative relationship or size of contribution can be read from the pairing. Fourth, with only 5-6 discrete points per series, the comparison can support only a qualitative trend-shape match, not a fitted functional relationship. Overall, the result justifies "beta-phase enhancement is consistent with contributing to the initial gain," and nothing stronger.

The three panels are a composition-graded structural series (K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6 → K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6 → ZnMnFe(CN)6) showing Mn's local coordination shrinking from octahedral MnN6, through five-coordinate MnN3, to tetrahedral MnN4, while a tetrahedral ZnN4 unit appears and becomes structurally linked to the FeC6 octahedra, driving a phase transition.

Read together with the earlier result, this settles that the rising bulk Zn at% (0→~47%, ICP-AES) has a concrete structural counterpart: as the Zn fraction in the formula increases across this series, Zn occupies N-coordinated sites within the same Mn–N/Fe–C octahedral framework identified as the starting monoclinic phase, and Mn's coordination degrades accordingly (N6→N3→N4) while a new Zn–Fe (ZnN4–FeC6) linkage forms. This licenses the stronger claim that Zn uptake is not merely a bulk compositional gain sitting somewhere in the electrode but corresponds to site-level substitution/occupation within the host framework's N-coordination sites, with increasing Zn content actively reorganizing framework connectivity and Mn coordination toward a distinct phase.

What remains open: these three panels are a composition series of defined stoichiometries (0.25, 0.5, 1.0 Zn), not samples explicitly indexed to cycle number, so the link to the actual "cycle 20, ~47 at%" plateau is inferential, not a direct match — it is not shown that the cycled electrode at the plateau is structurally identical to one of these three modeled compositions. It is also not established (from these panels alone) whether these structures were measured on real cycled electrodes or are computed/idealized models used to rationalize the mechanism, so the precise site occupancy fraction at the ~47% plateau, and whether it represents saturation of available N4/N3 sites, is still undetermined.

The three panels are structural models (not a single continuous measurement) for three discrete compositions: F5d = K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6, F5e = K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6, F5f = ZnMnFe(CN)6.

What is supported: as Zn content rises across these three compositions, the modeled Mn coordination polyhedron changes stepwise — MnN6 octahedron (d, Mn–N ≈2.20/2.00/1.98/2.28 Å) → MnN5 five-coordinate polyhedron (e, Mn–N ≈2.20/2.15/2.20 Å) → MnN4 tetrahedron (f, Mn–N ≈2.08/2.20/2.08/2.26 Å). In panel f, grey tetrahedra (labeled ZnN4 in the printed caption) appear alongside purple Mn and orange Fe polyhedra and are described as becoming linked to octahedral FeC6, which the authors propose enables a subsequent phase transition, attributing MnN4 formation to Zn2+-intensified Jahn-Teller distortion.

What this leaves open: the claim as stated says the Mn polyhedron "becomes MnN3 then MnN4" — the panels do not show this. Panel e's intermediate is explicitly MnN5, not MnN3; no MnN3 species appears in any panel, so that part of the claim is unsupported by this evidence. Also unresolved: these are three static, discrete-composition models, not a kinetic or in-situ trajectory, so they don't establish a continuous single-crystal transformation pathway; the K/Zn site-occupancy fractions (0.25, 0.5) are nominal starting stoichiometries in the models, not independently verified occupancies; and the ZnN4–FeC6 "linkage" and its causal role in the phase transition are stated as the authors' interpretation/caption text rather than something independently confirmed by additional diffraction or spectroscopic data in these panels.

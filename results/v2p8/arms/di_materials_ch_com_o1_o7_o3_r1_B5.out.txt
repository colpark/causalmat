The panel confirms the reported HP hardness trend: ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt% AlN, rising to ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then settling to ~19.4 and ~19.2 GPa at 3 and 5 wt% — a modest non-monotonic pattern (peak at 1 wt%, slight decline but staying above the baseline through 5 wt%).

Combined with the established density/SEM result, this settles that the HP series' hardness gains are consistent with, and plausibly explained by, the material actually achieving a dense, pore-free, transgranular-fracture microstructure: hardness is known to be highly sensitive to residual porosity, so the fact that hardness rises above the 0 wt% baseline at every AlN loading (1, 3, 5 wt%) — the same compositions independently shown by density+SEM to be fully or near-fully dense — corroborates that densification, not some hardness-boosting artifact, underlies the improvement. The pairing supports treating the hardness increase as a genuine consequence of the pore-free consolidation already documented.

What it leaves open: the hardness data alone cannot explain why hardness peaks at 1 wt% and then modestly declines at 3 and 5 wt% even though density stays near 100% — since density is flat across that range, the small hardness drop must come from some other factor (e.g., grain size, phase distribution, or AlN incorporation effects) that neither density, SEM, nor hardness data here identifies. The measurement also gives no compositional/microstructural mechanism, no data on the PS series' comparison to its own density/porosity, and no resolution of whether the 1 wt% peak is a real optimum or within the scatter of the error bars shown.

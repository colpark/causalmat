Panel F8c (Journal of Materials Science & Technology, j.jmst.2021.12.003) shows a curve plotting absorbance (left axis) and apparent quantum yield, AQY% (right axis) versus wavelength for the Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS sample, with discrete labeled points: 400 nm@18.3%, 420 nm@15.8%, 450 nm@6.5%, 500 nm@4.7%, 550 nm@0.19%.

This directly and fully supports the stated measurement: AQY declines monotonically from 18.3% at 400 nm to 0.19% at 550 nm, and the decline visibly tracks the absorption edge — the absorbance curve drops steeply in roughly the same 380-450 nm region where AQY also falls off sharply, then both taper into the low-value tail toward 550 nm. So the panel settles that (1) this material's photocatalytic quantum efficiency is wavelength-dependent and concentrated in the UV/near-UV-to-blue region, and (2) the AQY falloff coincides with, and is plausibly caused by, the material's optical absorption cutoff — consistent with an absorption-limited (not purely kinetic) mechanism for activity at longer wavelengths.

What it leaves open: the panel is descriptive, not mechanistic — it does not by itself prove causation between absorption and AQY (no fitted action spectrum overlay, no error bars, no replicate count, no statistical test). It gives no information on which reaction the AQY was calculated for, quantum yield calculation methodology, light source bandwidth, or how AQY was measured at each wavelength. It also says nothing about the mechanism (e.g., charge separation efficiency, surface reaction kinetics) driving the drop beyond the correlation with absorbance, nor about performance under solar/broadband illumination or long-term stability. Only figure 8c was reviewed; any broader figure_of_merit claims beyond this specific AQY-vs-wavelength trend are not addressed here.

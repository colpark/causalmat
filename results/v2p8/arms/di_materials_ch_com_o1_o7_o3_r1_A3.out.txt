Panel F2b (Vickers hardness vs. AlN wt% in ZrB2–30 vol% SiC composites, HP vs. PS processing) confirms the stated HP-route numbers: hardness rises from ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt% AlN to a peak of ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then eases to ~19.4 GPa at 3 wt% and ~19.2 GPa at 5 wt%. The error bars at 1, 3, and 5 wt% overlap substantially with each other, so the plateau/slight decline from 1→3→5 wt% is not clearly resolved point-to-point, but the jump from 0 to 1 wt% (18.0 to 20.3 GPa) is a visually clear increase with non-overlapping bars.

What this settles: for the hot-pressed (HP) route, adding a small amount of AlN (1 wt%) measurably increases Vickers hardness relative to the AlN-free baseline, and hardness remains elevated (roughly 19–20 GPa) across 1–5 wt% AlN rather than continuing to climb or collapsing back to baseline.

What it leaves open: the figure alone does not establish the mechanism (e.g., grain refinement, densification, secondary phase formation) behind the AlN-induced hardness increase, nor does it distinguish whether 3 and 5 wt% differ meaningfully from 1 wt% or from each other given overlapping error bars. It also says nothing about how this HP trend generalizes to the PS (pressureless-sintered) samples, which show the opposite trend (declining hardness with AlN), or about other properties (toughness, strength, density) — those would require reading the surrounding text and other panels, which were not part of this check.

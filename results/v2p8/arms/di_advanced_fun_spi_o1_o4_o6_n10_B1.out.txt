F2d shows FWHM-derived peak width (Δσ/σ, %) vs. temperature for SC, PC, and HOC. All three show an anomaly at essentially the same temperature, ~150-155 K, but with opposite sign and graded magnitude: SC narrows (drop from ~100% to ~50%), while PC and HOC broaden sharply (spikes to ~260% and ~128% respectively), then relax. The size of the broadening spike orders as PC > HOC > SC, matching the domain-size ordering already established (PC finest ~0.1 um, HOC intermediate ~10-100 um, SC coarsest ~1e2-1e3 um) and matching which phases showed curved/offset vs. smooth peak-position shifts.

Read together with the domain-size/peak-shift correspondence, this settles that the same ~150 K event drives both the lattice-expansion curvature and a coincident, magnitude-graded change in peak width: finer/less-uniform domains (PC, then HOC) show larger anisotropic strain-broadening at the transition, while the coarse, smooth-expanding SC domains actually sharpen. That two independent XRD observables (peak position and peak width) converge on the same temperature and the same three-phase ordering strengthens the case that this is a real structural transition whose severity of local strain/disorder tracks domain scale, not just noise in one measurement.

What remains open: this is still only three phase points, so it is a correspondence, not a dose-response law; domain size remains confounded with each phase's distinct composition/crystal structure, so whether domain size itself (versus intrinsic phase chemistry) causes the differential broadening is not established; and the mechanism linking domain boundaries to strain broadening is not demonstrated, only correlated.</message>

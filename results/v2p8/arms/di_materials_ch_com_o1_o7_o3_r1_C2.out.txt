The settled result justifies concluding that, for the HP composites—particularly the 1 and 5 wt% AlN compositions reaching near-100% relative density—the high density figure is real rather than a measurement artifact: two independent lines of evidence, a bulk density measurement and a local SEM fracture-surface inspection, agree that these materials are essentially fully dense, pore-free, and well consolidated, with fracture propagating transgranularly (through grains) rather than around residual porosity. This is a genuine corroboration between a quantitative bulk metric and a qualitative microstructural observation, so the near-100% relative density can be treated as reflecting an actually dense, well-sintered microstructure.

What it leaves open: the density number is a single whole-sample average and, alone, cannot say whether any residual porosity is open or closed, or how it is spatially distributed; the SEM view is local and qualitative, covering only one fracture surface, so it cannot itself quantify density or be compared systematically across the full 0/1/3/5 wt% AlN series. Because SEM porosity was not reported in a wt%-resolved way, the pairing does not establish a quantitative trend tying observed pore appearance to the specific density values (~97.5%, ~99.3%, ~100%) at each composition—only qualitative consistency at the high end. Finally, neither observation explains the mechanism by which AlN addition increases relative density (e.g., acting as a sintering aid or affecting grain boundaries); the result confirms the dense, pore-free end state and fracture mode but says nothing about why or how AlN produces it.</message>

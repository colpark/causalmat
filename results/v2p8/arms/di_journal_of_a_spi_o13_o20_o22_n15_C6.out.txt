The earlier result settles that beta-phase enhancement is a plausible contributor to the initial voltage rise in the low-loading regime only: from 0-4 wt%, beta content rises from 76.1-76.3% to a peak of 81.8-83.2% in step with voltage climbing from 7.5 to 32.0 V, so the trend shapes co-track over that window. This supports saying a beta-phase-mediated pathway is consistent with the data at low PZT loading, but it does not establish that pathway as the mechanism, since PZT wt% is a shared driver of both series by construction and could produce the same low-loading correlation by coincidence rather than causation.

What it leaves open is substantial. Beyond 4 wt%, the two series decouple: beta content eases back toward ~81% over 6-10 wt% while voltage keeps rising monotonically to 43.0 and 62.0 V, so beta-phase content cannot be the operative driver of output gain in that higher-loading range -- some other factor must be responsible there. The result also cannot speak to magnitude: beta content is a normalized structural fraction (%) while voltage is an absolute output (V), so no quantitative dose-response or scaling relationship can be read off the comparison. With only 5-6 discrete wt% points per series, the data support comparing trend shapes but not fitting or confirming any shared functional relationship. And because no causal mechanism was directly measured, the low-loading correlation remains merely consistent with, not proof of, a beta-phase-mediated contribution to PRF/service_capability.

The two panels (F4a: [11-20] loading, side surfaces (1-100) and (0001); F4b: [01-10] loading, side surfaces (-2110) and (0001)) do broadly support the general shift the text describes: at RT both panels label {01-12}-type traces (twin traces), and at 400 C both panels label {10-10}/{01-10 or -1100}-type traces (prismatic slip traces), so the endpoints of the claim — twinning dominant at RT, prismatic slip dominant at 400 C — are directly visible and supported.

What the images complicate is the sharpness of the 200 C/300 C boundary claimed in the text ("RT and 200 C... replaced... at 300-400 C"). In F4b, 200 C still carries {01-12}-type (twin) labels, consistent with the text, and the switch to {10-10}/{-1100} labels only appears at 300 C — matching the stated threshold. But in F4a, the 200 C column (top row, (1-100) surface) is already labeled with {10-10}/{01-10} prismatic-slip indices, not twin indices, and the RT/200 C bottom row shows twin labels only at RT before shifting. So the exact temperature at which the trace type flips is not uniform across the two loading/surface geometries shown — one orientation (F4b) supports a 200→300 C transition, the other (F4a) suggests the shift may already be underway at 200 C.

On deformation bands: "deformation band" labels appear at RT, 200 C, and 300 C in both panels, but are not explicitly labeled at 400 C in F4b (either surface) or at 300 C in F4a's bottom row. So persistence "at every temperature" is only partially supported by the visible labels — bands may still be present but unlabeled at those points, which the image alone cannot confirm.

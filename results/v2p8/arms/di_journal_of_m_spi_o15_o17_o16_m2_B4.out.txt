Panel F11a (stir casting, non-FA surface) shows large, blocky, flat flakes roughly 20-40 um across with sharp facets. Panel F11b (FSP, FA-containing surface) shows a field of fine, granular particles under ~5 um forming loose clustered agglomerates, with almost no large flakes present. This matches the stated debris description exactly.

Read together with the established groove result, this settles a further point beyond either result alone: the earlier finding (parallel grooves on both surfaces, no craters, shallower grooves plus fine debris on the FA surface) established qualitatively that both wear by abrasion and that the FA surface's debris is "fine," but gave no size scale and could not rule out that the "fine debris" was simply broken-down versions of the same flake-type debris seen elsewhere. This measurement supplies that scale: the non-FA surface's debris is coarse platelet flakes (20-40 um), a size/shape consistent with larger-scale plastic cutting/plowing of the matrix, while the FA surface's debris is genuinely an order of magnitude finer (<5 um) with agglomeration - a morphology plausibly compatible with mobile, rolling third-body particles rather than large chip formation. Combined with the no-crater, groove-only surface topography, this strengthens (but does not prove) that FA presence changes the debris regime from coarse flake generation toward fine-particle generation, consistent with the proposed moderation-of-abrasive-cutting pathway.

It leaves open the same limits as before: debris composition is unanalyzed, so whether the fine <5 um particles originate from fractured FA versus finely comminuted matrix material is still undetermined. It also does not discriminate between the barrier/asperity-blocking sub-mechanism and the three-body rolling sub-mechanism - agglomerated fine particles are only suggestively, not conclusively, more consistent with rolling third-body debris. The measurement remains single-image, qualitative-to-semi-quantitative morphology, not a systematic size distribution or count.

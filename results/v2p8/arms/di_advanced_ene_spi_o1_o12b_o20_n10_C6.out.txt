The established result justifies only a co-occurrence conclusion: in the as-prepared cathode, a single, well-matched monoclinic P21/n open framework (N-coordinated Mn, C-coordinated Fe) is present, and separately, across cycling, the bulk Zn content rises from zero to about 50 at% and settles near roughly 47 at% by the 20th cycle. Together these support the general picture that a well-defined, open host framework is present at the start of cycling and that, over cycling, the cathode's metal content comes to be dominated by a large, roughly stable fraction of Zn -- consistent with Zn2+ being the principal cation taken up.

What this leaves open is substantial. Because the structural observation is a single as-prepared snapshot, there is no evidence about the framework's phase or structural integrity at cycle 20 or beyond, so it cannot be shown that the same P21/n host persists when the Zn plateau is reached. Because the compositional observation is a bulk elemental at% measurement, it reports total Zn content in the cathode but says nothing about where that Zn is located -- it could reside in framework sites, at surfaces, in secondary phases, or elsewhere -- so it cannot establish that Zn occupies the framework sites described by the XRD data. The two measurements are of different kinds (a static structural match versus a cycle-indexed bulk composition), so their combination supports co-occurrence of an open framework and rising/plateauing Zn content in the same material, not a mechanistic or site-specific link. Absent site-resolved or post-cycling structural data, the ~47 at% plateau cannot be attributed to saturation of framework sites specifically as opposed to some other compositional limit.</message>

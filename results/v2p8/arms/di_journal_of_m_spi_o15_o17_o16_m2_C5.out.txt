The settled result supports that both composite surfaces (with and without FA) wear predominantly by abrasion, evidenced by parallel sliding-direction grooves and the absence of large craters on either surface. It further supports that the FA-containing surface additionally shows shallower grooves covered with fine debris, which is consistent with a pathway in which dispersed FA moderates the severity of abrasive cutting—either by acting as a barrier against asperity penetration or by generating fine debris that rolls and reduces three-body cutting—rather than the composite shifting to adhesive wear failure.

What this leaves open: the "shallower" groove depth and "numerous" debris characterizations are qualitative visual impressions from single SEM images, not quantified measurements, so the comparison between surfaces is not metrically established. The composition of the fine debris was not analyzed, so it cannot be confirmed to originate from fractured FA layers rather than ordinary matrix wear debris. Only two specific features were imaged (surface b morphology and groove orientation on both surfaces), so the panels do not survey the whole worn surface, meaning localized adhesive wear features elsewhere cannot be fully excluded on this evidence alone. Finally, the two candidate sub-mechanisms—FA blocking asperity penetration versus fine debris acting as rolling third-body particles that reduce cutting—are both equally consistent with the observed "shallower grooves plus fine debris" pattern, and the images do not discriminate between them. In short, the result supports an abrasion-dominant, FA-moderated wear pathway in general terms but does not pin down magnitude, debris origin, surface-wide extent, or the specific moderating sub-mechanism.</message>

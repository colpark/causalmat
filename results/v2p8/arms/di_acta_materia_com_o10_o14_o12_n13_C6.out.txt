The established result supports treating band morphology in OM as a reliable proxy for orientation behavior in EBSD, i.e., for classifying deformation bands into two coherent behavior classes: (1) straight, {10-12}/{01-12}-trace bands (with the OM contrast signature) that show a single, uniform orientation colour in EBSD — a twin-like behavior; and (2) wavy, side-surface-contrast-asymmetric bands that show continuously shading, spatially varying orientation in EBSD — a kink-like behavior. Because the two imaging modalities were co-registered on the same band populations across two specimen orientations ([11-20] and [01-10]), this pairing licenses saying that "wavy morphology" and "graded/continuous orientation" go together, and "straight lenticular morphology" and "uniform single orientation" go together, rather than treating morphology and orientation behavior as independently described but unlinked facts.

It leaves open several things: it gives no quantitative measure of misorientation magnitude, so it cannot say how much orientation changes across a kink band versus how sharp a twin boundary is; it has not been shown to hold beyond the two tested specimen orientations, so generalization to other loading directions, specimens, or materials is unproven; the pairing is a correspondence of imaging signatures, not a mechanistic demonstration of what causes the continuous shading; and because OM contrast and EBSD colour are different physical contrast mechanisms (optical/diffraction contrast vs. crystallographic orientation coding), their agreement in discriminating band types does not establish they are tracking the same underlying physical variable.</message>

The two panels give purely photographic/qualitative evidence and support only a visual ranking of anti-thrombogenicity across the five surface conditions after 2 h of blood circulation, not a quantitative service-capability value.

What is supported: In F6b (cross-sectional lumen images), 316LSS and PPAmF lumens are completely filled with dark red/black material, consistent with the caption's "filled by dark thrombus." BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF show partial clearing — a patch of bright metallic foil visible alongside residual dark/red material ("partly open"). BVLD/NO-PPAmF shows the largest area of clean, reflective foil with red material confined to the rim, matching "nearly clean." F6c (excised thrombus/foil strips) shows the same ordering: 316LSS and PPAmF are almost entirely dark maroon along their length; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF have a light/white clean central band flanked by red ends; BVLD/NO-PPAmF has the largest white/clean segment. Together the two panels corroborate each other and justify the qualitative conclusion that surface modification (BVLD and/or NO loading on PPAmF) visibly reduces thrombus coverage relative to bare 316LSS or unmodified PPAmF, with the combined BVLD/NO-PPAmF treatment appearing most effective by eye.

What is left open: These are single representative photographs per group with no scale bar, no quantified clot area/mass/weight, no replicate count or statistics, and no clotting time or platelet-activation data. The panels cannot establish statistical significance, effect magnitude, mechanism, or performance beyond this single 2 h time point — only a qualitative, visually-graded ordering.

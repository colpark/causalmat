The settled result supports a consistent, qualitative concordance: across the four samples common to both observations (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS, Pd-ZIS, Ag-ZIS, ZIS), the same ranking holds for both steady photocurrent and H2 evolution rate, with Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS highest and ZIS lowest in both measures. This justifies the conclusion that, among these four compositions, higher steady photocurrent (as a proxy for charge-carrier separation) co-occurs with higher H2 evolution performance — i.e., the two figures of merit agree in rank order on the overlapping subset.

What it leaves open is substantial. First, because photocurrent and H2 evolution rate are different kinds of quantities (current density vs. molar gas-evolution rate) measured in separate setups, the matching order is not a quantitative or unit-comparable relationship — no magnitude, slope, or proportionality can be inferred. Second, two compositions (Ag0.5Pd0.5-ZIS and Ag0.75Pd0.25-ZIS) were measured for H2 evolution but not for photocurrent, so the full Ag:Pd series is not jointly covered; the concordance is established only for four of six compositions. Third, four overlapping points are sufficient to show a matching rank order but not enough to characterize a dose-response curve or estimate the strength of any underlying relationship. Finally, because Ag:Pd composition was varied simultaneously with both measurements, the result cannot distinguish whether photocurrent differences causally drive H2 evolution differences versus composition independently influencing each outcome. In short: a rank-order association is established on a partial sample; magnitude, completeness of coverage, and causal direction remain unresolved.

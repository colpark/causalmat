The established result supports the claim that BVLD/NO-PPAmF, among the five surfaces tested, uniquely combines the lowest platelet adhesion (~5x1e2 cells/mm2 by BIO count) with a morphology dominated by few, round, unspread platelets (by SEM), whereas 316L SS and PPAmF show much higher counts (32 and 41x1e2 cells/mm2) with many spread, dendritic platelets, and BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF alone show intermediate counts (15.5 and 11x1e2 cells/mm2) with reduced but still-present spreading. This justifies saying that on BVLD/NO-PPAmF, reduced platelet number and reduced platelet activation/spreading co-occur — a joint suppression of adhesion and activation that neither measurement alone could establish, since the count says nothing about the shape of the platelets present and the SEM images give no quantitative density.

What it leaves open: the ordering across the five surfaces is descriptive only — no statistics or error bars accompany either measurement, so it is not established as a tested, statistically reliable trend. The correspondence between the qualitative SEM morphology categories ("a few round platelets") and the specific numeric BIO count (5x1e2 cells/mm2) is not measured directly on the same fields; it is an inferred pairing from separately reported descriptive characterizations, not a quantified relationship. Finally, the result is silent on mechanism — it does not indicate what surface-chemistry or biochemical property of BVLD/NO-PPAmF produces this joint suppression, only that the two measures move together across the surface panel. So the conclusion is limited to a descriptive, co-occurring pattern between adhesion count and activation morphology, not a statistically validated or mechanistically explained effect.</message>

Panel F2b (Vickers hardness vs. AlN wt% in ZrB2–30vol%SiC composites, by sintering process) confirms the stated numbers for the hot-pressed (HP, red) series: ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt%, rising to ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then settling to ~19.4 GPa at 3 wt% and ~19.2 GPa at 5 wt%, matching the measurement given.

What this supports: for hot-pressed material, adding a small amount of AlN (1 wt%) raises hardness relative to the AlN-free baseline, and that gain is largely retained (not lost) as AlN content increases to 3–5 wt%, with hardness leveling off rather than continuing to climb. The figure also shows a pressureless-sintered (PS, blue) series that behaves oppositely — hardness falls monotonically from ~12 to ~7 GPa as AlN increases — so the processing route (HP vs. PS) clearly interacts with the AlN effect: what raises hardness under hot pressing does not do so under pressureless sintering.

What it leaves open: the plot gives only one sintering condition each for HP and PS (no parametric sweep of pressure/temperature), so it cannot isolate which process variable (pressure, temperature, densification) drives the divergence. Error bars at 1, 3, and 5 wt% overlap for HP, so the apparent 1 wt% peak and the near-flat 3–5 wt% region are not clearly statistically distinguishable from each other, only from the 0 wt% baseline. The panel gives no compositional/microstructural explanation (grain size, secondary phases, porosity) for the trend, and no data beyond 5 wt% AlN, so extrapolation beyond this range or mechanistic causal claims are not supported by this figure alone.

The established result supports only a co-occurrence claim: an open, well-defined monoclinic P21/n KMnHCF framework (N-coordinated Mn, C-coordinated Fe) is confirmed to exist as a single well-matched phase at the as-prepared stage, and, independently, bulk ICP-AES shows the cathode's Zn content rising from zero to roughly 50 at% by cycle 20 and stabilizing near 47 at%. Together these justify saying that a well-defined host framework is present at the start of cycling and that a large, roughly stable share of Zn accumulates in the cathode over cycling, consistent with (but not proof of) Zn2+ as the dominant inserted cation — i.e., the two facts are compatible with a framework-hosts-Zn picture without establishing it mechanistically.

What it leaves open: (1) phase persistence — because the XRD is a single as-prepared snapshot, nothing here shows the P21/n framework still exists, is unchanged, or is even the majority phase at cycle 20 or later when the Zn plateau is reached; (2) site occupancy/location — ICP-AES gives only bulk elemental atomic percent, not spatial or crystallographic information, so it cannot show Zn resides within the framework's interstitial/coordination sites rather than on the surface, in a separate phase, or elsewhere in the electrode; (3) mechanism — since one measurement is a static structural snapshot and the other is a cycle-indexed bulk composition, their combination supports co-occurrence in the same material system, not a causal or structural link between framework identity and Zn uptake; (4) the basis of the ~47 at% plateau — with no site-resolved or post-cycling structural data, the plateau cannot be attributed to saturation of specific framework sites as opposed to some other compositional or kinetic limit.</message>

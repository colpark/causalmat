The settled result justifies a modest, order-level conclusion: across the four samples measured in both observations (ZIS, Ag-ZIS, Pd-ZIS, Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS), the composition that ranks highest in steady photocurrent also ranks highest in H2 evolution rate, and the same holds down the ranking to the lowest. This supports the claim that, within this four-sample set, higher photocurrent (as a proxy for charge-carrier separation) co-occurs with better H2 evolution performance — i.e., an association in rank order between the two measures for these compositions.

What it leaves open is considerable. First, because photocurrent and H2 evolution rate are different physical quantities in different units and setups, the match is only in ordering, not in magnitude — no quantitative relationship (slope, proportionality, or effect size) between the two can be inferred. Second, two additional compositions (Ag0.5Pd0.5-ZIS and Ag0.75Pd0.25-ZIS) have H2 evolution data but no matching photocurrent measurement, so the full Ag:Pd series is not confirmed to follow this pattern — the conclusion is restricted to the four overlapping samples. Third, four points establish a consistent order but not a dose-response curve; there is no basis for saying how much a given increase in photocurrent predicts H2 evolution gains. Finally, and most importantly, since Ag:Pd composition varies together with both measured quantities, causation cannot be established — the result cannot rule out that composition independently drives photocurrent and H2 evolution separately, rather than photocurrent differences causing the H2 evolution differences. No new data or figures were introduced in this step; this is purely an interpretation of the previously settled result and its recorded qualifications.

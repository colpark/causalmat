What this supports: across the three compositions the Mn coordination polyhedron visibly changes shape — panel d shows a six-coordinate MnN6 octahedron; panel f explicitly labels a four-coordinate MnN4 tetrahedron; panel e shows an intermediate polyhedron with a reduced coordination number (the inset label is small/blurry in my read — legible as roughly "MnN3–5," not cleanly confirmable as exactly MnN3). Panel f also shows grey tetrahedra structurally embedded in the framework immediately adjacent to and sharing corners/edges with the brown FeC6 octahedra, consistent with the caption's claim that ZnN4 tetrahedra become linked to FeC6 as Zn substitution completes. So the panels do support a Zn-content-dependent distortion of the Mn site toward lower coordination, and the appearance of a Zn-tetrahedron/Fe-octahedron network at full substitution.

What is left open: these are schematic renderings (presumably from refined or modeled structures), not raw diffraction/refinement data shown here, so I cannot verify from the image alone that the coordination numbers and bond lengths are well-determined (no R-factors, uncertainties, or site-occupancy statistics are visible). The exact intermediate-panel label (MnN3 vs. MnN5) is not confidently legible. Whether this Zn–N/Fe–C linkage causally "enables" the subsequent phase transition, versus merely correlating with it, is not established by static structure drawings alone.

The established result supports a cross-technique correspondence, not a causal or dose-response relationship. It justifies saying: across the three phase categories examined (SC, PC, HOC), coarser SEM-measured domain size co-occurs with smoother/near-linear XRD-measured lattice-expansion peak shifts, while finer/less-uniform domain size co-occurs with curved, offset expansion behavior. That is the full extent of what can be claimed.

It leaves open, and should not be read to establish: (1) any general or continuous relationship between domain size and expansion linearity, since only three phase points are compared — this is a three-point correspondence, not evidence of a dose-response trend across a range of domain sizes; (2) any causal mechanism linking domain size itself to expansion smoothness, since SEM domain size (static, real-space) and XRD peak-shift behavior (dynamic, in-situ) are measured by different techniques on different physical quantities, so the pairing is observational/associative, not a measured causal effect; (3) whether domain size is even the relevant driver, since PC, HOC, and SC differ in composition and crystal structure as well as domain size — domain size is confounded with phase identity, so the curvature/offset in lattice expansion could equally be attributed to intrinsic phase-specific properties rather than to domain coarseness per se.

In short, the result supports an associative pattern worth noting and consistent with a hypothesis that domain scale relates to expansion smoothness, but it cannot support a claim that domain size causes or predicts expansion linearity, nor that this pattern would hold outside these three specific phases.

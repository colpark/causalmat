Panel F8c (Journal of Materials Science & Technology, j.jmst.2021.12.003) shows two curves plotted against wavelength (200–800 nm) for the Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS sample: a UV-vis absorbance trace (left axis) and discrete AQY data points (right axis) at 400, 420, 450, 500, and 550 nm, labeled 18.3%, 15.8%, 6.5%, 4.7%, and 0.19% respectively.

What this settles: the measurement is directly and fully supported. The plotted AQY values confirm a monotonic decline from 18.3% at 400 nm to 0.19% at 550 nm, and the drop-off in AQY coincides with the absorbance curve's edge — absorbance is high and roughly flat from ~250–380 nm, then falls sharply starting around 400–450 nm and continues declining toward baseline by 550–600 nm. So the panel does support the claim that AQY tracks the absorption edge, since both curves fall over the same wavelength range with a similar inflection region near 400–450 nm.

What it leaves open: the panel does not give a precise quantitative correlation (e.g., no fitted overlay or correlation coefficient) between the two curves — the visual alignment of decline regions is qualitative, not statistically demonstrated. It also does not specify measurement conditions (light source, incident photon flux calibration, reaction system) or error bars/repeats for the AQY points, so precision and reproducibility of the AQY values themselves cannot be assessed from this panel alone. Finally, the panel is specific to the Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS composition only; it does not establish whether this AQY-absorption tracking generalizes to other Ag/Pd ratios or ZIS-based samples.

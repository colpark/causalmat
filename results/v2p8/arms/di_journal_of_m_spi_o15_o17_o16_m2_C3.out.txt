The established result settles that both composite surfaces wear predominantly by abrasion, evidenced by parallel sliding-direction grooves with no large craters on either surface, and that the FA-containing surface additionally shows shallower grooves covered in fine debris with no large craters. Together these support the proposed pathway that dispersed FA moderates abrasive cutting severity (via some barrier/fine-debris effect) rather than the composite shifting to adhesive wear.

What it leaves open: the observations are purely qualitative SEM morphology from single images (groove depth, presence/absence of debris and craters), not quantified measurements, so terms like "shallower" and "numerous" are visual impressions rather than a rigorously compared metric between surfaces. The composition of the fine debris was not analyzed, so it cannot be confirmed to originate from fractured FA layers rather than ordinary matrix wear debris. Because only two specific features were examined (surface b morphology and groove orientation on both surfaces) rather than a full survey of the worn surfaces, localized adhesive-wear features elsewhere cannot be fully excluded on this evidence alone. Finally, the two candidate sub-mechanisms for how FA moderates cutting -- FA blocking asperity penetration versus fine FA debris acting as a rolling third body -- are both equally consistent with "shallower grooves plus fine debris," so these images do not discriminate between them. In short, the result supports abrasion as the dominant wear mode with FA mitigating its severity, but does not establish the mechanistic detail, debris origin, or completeness of surface coverage needed to go further.

Panel F2b (read) shows the HP series rising sharply from ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt% AlN to a peak ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then easing slightly to ~19.4 GPa at 3 wt% and ~19.2 GPa at 5 wt%, with the PS series far lower and declining throughout.

Combined with the settled density/SEM result: the HP hardness jump from 0 to 1 wt% AlN tracks the same interval over which density rises toward the confirmed near-100%, pore-free, transgranular-fracture state. Since porosity is a well-known hardness degrader, this combination supports that the hardness increase from 0 to 1 wt% is consistent with, and plausibly explained by, the elimination of residual porosity already corroborated by density+SEM — the two datasets reinforce each other in that low-density 0 wt% material shows the lowest hardness, and the composition confirmed fully dense shows markedly higher hardness.

What it does not settle: hardness does not track density beyond that point. At 3 and 5 wt% hardness is slightly lower than at 1 wt%, even though density/SEM place both 1 and 5 wt% at ~100% relative density — so full densification alone cannot explain the small decline at higher AlN loadings. This mismatch shows some other factor (grain size, AlN distribution, secondary-phase or grain-boundary effects) must be influencing hardness independent of porosity, but neither the hardness curve nor the density/SEM pair identifies what that factor is. Causation (that reduced porosity, rather than some AlN-correlated microstructural change, drives the 0→1 wt% rise) is likewise not established — only correlation. No wt%-resolved porosity quantification exists to test this more rigorously.

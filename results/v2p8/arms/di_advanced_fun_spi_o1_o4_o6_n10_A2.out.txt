Panel F2d (Advanced Functional Materials 10.1002/adfm.202008088) plots Δσ/σ (%), the relative Gaussian-width variation of XRD 2θ peaks normalized to 700 K, versus temperature (100–270 K) for three sample forms: SC (single crystal, blue), PC (polycrystalline, red), HOC (black), each tracking several hkl peaks ((004), (242), (400), (224)).

What the panel supports: all three sample forms show a sharp, discontinuous change in peak width in the same narrow temperature window, ~140–155 K, consistent with a structural/phase transition occurring near 150 K regardless of crystal form. The direction of the change differs by form and by reflection: in SC the width drops from ~100% to roughly 45–80% (varies by peak) just below 150 K, indicating the peaks sharpen there. In PC the (242) peak spikes up to ~260% and other peaks rise more modestly around the same temperature, and in HOC the (004)/(242) peaks jump to ~128% while (400)/(224) do not rise as much — i.e., PC and HOC broaden rather than sharpen. This supports that the transition's microstructural signature (domain/strain response) is anisotropic across reflections and differs between single-crystal and poly/textured material.

What it leaves open: the panel alone does not identify which crystallographic phases are involved (e.g., which space groups) or the physical cause of the divergent sharpening-vs-broadening behavior — whether it reflects twin-domain formation, phase coexistence, microstrain, or crystallite-size change is not distinguishable from FWHM-percentage data alone. Absolute FWHM values and precise transition onset/offset temperatures per peak are also not given here.

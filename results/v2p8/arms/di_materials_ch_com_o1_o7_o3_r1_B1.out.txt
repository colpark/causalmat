Combining the HP hardness curve with the established density/SEM result narrows what can be said about the 1 and 5 wt% AlN HP specimens specifically, since those are the two compositions independently confirmed to be near-100% relative density with pore-free, transgranular fracture microstructure.

What is supported: for the HP series, hardness is not simply tracking relative density in a monotonic way. The 0 wt% sample (~97.5% dense, per the earlier density figure) gives ~18.0 GPa, while 1 wt% (~100% dense) rises to ~20.3 GPa — consistent with the jump to full densification removing a hardness-suppressing residual-porosity effect, since a genuinely pore-free, well-consolidated microstructure was independently confirmed there. But 3 and 5 wt% then settle back down to ~19.4 and ~19.2 GPa despite 5 wt% also being independently confirmed as ~100% dense and pore-free. So the combination supports that porosity elimination (density going from ~97.5% to ~100%) is associated with, and plausibly contributes to, part of the hardness gain from 0 to 1 wt%, but it also shows that full densification alone cannot explain the hardness plateau/decline from 1 to 5 wt%, since 5 wt% is just as dense and just as pore-free as 1 wt% yet measurably softer.

What is left open: neither the density value nor the SEM fracture image speaks to grain size, second-phase (AlN or reaction-product) distribution, residual stress, or intrinsic phase hardness — any of which could account for the hardness decline beyond 1 wt% despite sustained full density. The combination therefore establishes that density/porosity is not the controlling variable for HP hardness differences among the fully-dense compositions (1, 3, 5 wt%), but it does not identify what is.

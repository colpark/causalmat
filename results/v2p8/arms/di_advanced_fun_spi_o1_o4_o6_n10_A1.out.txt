Panel F2d (three stacked plots, Δσ/σ % = normalized Gaussian-width of XRD 2θ peaks, referenced to 700 K, vs temperature) shows a sharp, common anomaly at ~150–160 K in all three sample forms, but with opposite sign and different magnitude by form and by reflection. In SC, most tracked reflections ((400), (224), and to a lesser extent (004)) drop from ~100% down toward ~45–80% just above 150 K, i.e. the peaks narrow. In PC, the (242) reflection instead spikes sharply upward to ~260–300% right at ~150–160 K before relaxing back toward ~100% by ~250–270 K, while other PC reflections ((004), (400), (224)) stay much closer to 100% with only mild changes. In HOC, the (004) reflection shows a smaller upward spike to ~120–130% around 150–155 K before declining, while (400)/(224) show more modest, gradual changes.

This supports the conclusion that a structural anomaly — consistent with a phase transition — occurs near 150–160 K and is present across all three sample morphologies (single crystal, polycrystal, hierarchically oriented crystal), since every panel shows a distinct kink or excursion in relative peak width at that temperature. It does not, by itself, establish the specific space groups involved, the direction/mechanism of the transition (twin-domain formation, strain, mosaicity, or crystallite-size effects), or why the response is narrowing in SC but broadening in PC/HOC and reflection-dependent within each sample. Those mechanistic and symmetry assignments remain open and are not determinable from this panel alone.

The established result settles that the two SEM observations together support an abrasive wear mode for both composite surfaces (parallel sliding-direction grooves, no large craters), and that on the FA-containing surface b this abrasion presents as shallower grooves with fine debris and no large crater. This pattern is consistent with the proposed pathway that dispersed FA moderates abrasive cutting severity through a barrier/fine-debris effect, and it argues against the composite failing primarily by adhesive wear. That is the extent of what the result justifies: a qualitative, image-based case that FA's presence correlates with milder abrasive grooving rather than adhesive failure.

It leaves several things open. First, because the observations are qualitative impressions from single SEM images rather than quantified groove-depth or debris measurements, the "shallower" and "numerous debris" descriptors are not established as a rigorously compared metric. Second, the composition of the fine debris was not analyzed, so it is not confirmed that the debris actually derives from fractured FA layers rather than from ordinary matrix wear. Third, since only two specific features were examined (surface b morphology and groove orientation on both surfaces), the panels do not survey the entire worn surface, so localized adhesive wear features elsewhere cannot be fully excluded on this evidence alone. Finally, the images cannot discriminate between the two candidate sub-mechanisms for how FA moderates abrasion -- FA blocking asperity penetration versus fine FA-derived debris acting as rolling three-body particles that reduce cutting -- both remain equally consistent with what was observed, so the specific mechanistic pathway is not settled, only the general abrasion-moderation conclusion.</message>

The settled result justifies a modest, descriptive conclusion: across the three phase categories examined (PC, HOC, SC), coarser SEM-measured domain size tends to co-occur with smoother/near-linear in-situ XRD lattice-expansion behavior, while finer or less-uniform domain size co-occurs with curvature and offsets in the same peak-shift traces. This is a pattern of agreement between two independently measured properties—one static/real-space (domain size), one dynamic (peak position vs. temperature)—observed consistently in the same direction for all three phases studied.

What it leaves open is substantial. First, with only three phase categories compared, this is a correspondence across three points, not evidence of a dose-response or continuous relationship between domain size and expansion linearity—finer gradations of domain size or intermediate behavior cannot be inferred. Second, because domain size and expansion smoothness are measured by different techniques (SEM vs. in-situ XRD) on different kinds of quantities, the pairing is a cross-technique correspondence, not a measured causal or mechanistic link; nothing here shows that domain size itself governs the expansion behavior. Third, PC, HOC, and SC differ in composition and crystal structure as well as domain size, so domain size is confounded with phase identity—the curvature/offset pattern could equally be driven by phase-specific structural or compositional factors rather than domain scale per se. In short, the result supports an associative pattern worth noting and pursuing, but it does not establish causation, generalizability beyond these three phases, or that domain size (rather than phase identity) is the operative driver.

The panel (F2d) plots Δσ/σ (%), the normalized Gaussian width of several XRD 2θ peaks, versus temperature for three sample forms: SC (blue), PC (red), and HOC (black), each tracked via multiple reflections (e.g., 004, 242, 224, 400).

What it supports: all three sample types show a distinct anomaly in peak width clustered in the same narrow temperature window, ~150–160 K, which is consistent with a structural phase transition occurring near that temperature regardless of sample form. The character of the anomaly differs by form: SC shows a step-like narrowing (~100% down to ~50%) that then stays flat, consistent with a discontinuous change to a more ordered/less-disordered lattice state above the transition. PC and HOC instead show sharp transient peaks in width (PC's 242 reflection rising to ~260%, HOC's 004 to ~128%) that later relax back down, consistent with a localized burst of strain/mosaicity/microstrain right at the transition rather than a simple step change.

What it leaves open: the measurement alone does not identify the actual crystal structures or space groups on either side of the transition, nor does it give lattice parameters. It does not explain why SC behaves as a step while PC and HOC behave as transient spikes — that could reflect genuine differences in transition order/mechanism, or could instead reflect sample-form-specific effects (grain size, domain/twin formation, strain relaxation kinetics) unrelated to the intrinsic phase transition. It also cannot rule out instrumental or peak-fitting artifacts as partial contributors to the width changes. Confirming phase identity and structural origin would require complementary structural data (e.g., peak positions/splitting, indexed lattice parameters, or diffraction patterns) not shown in this panel.

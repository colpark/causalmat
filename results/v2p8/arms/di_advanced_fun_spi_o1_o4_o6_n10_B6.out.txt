Panel F2d confirms the numeric claim: SC's Δσ/σ trace is nearly flat/monotonic, sitting near 100% at low T and dropping in one smooth step to ~50% by ~150 K, then staying roughly flat with modest scatter through 270 K. PC and HOC instead show a sharp, transient bump right at ~150-155 K — PC rises to a peak of ~260% (the (242) trace) before relaxing back down through ~150-200%, and HOC shows a smaller but similar peak of ~128% before declining toward ~85-100%. So the "smooth vs. curved/offset" language from Observation A/B is now sharpened into a specific shape: SC is monotonic and step-like, while PC and HOC each show a localized overshoot/peak in peak-width variation at essentially the same temperature where SC's step occurs.

Combined with the settled domain-size/lattice-expansion correspondence, this justifies saying: the three phases' domain-scale ordering (SC coarsest, HOC intermediate, PC finest) also tracks the magnitude of an anomalous FWHM peak near 150 K — SC (coarsest) shows no overshoot, HOC (intermediate) shows a moderate one (~128%), and PC (finest) shows the largest (~260%). This strengthens the case that finer/less-uniform domain structure is associated with a more pronounced structural anomaly (peak broadening, i.e., increased mosaicity/strain heterogeneity) at the transition temperature, on top of the already-established curvature/offset in peak position itself. It does not by itself establish that domain size causes the broadening, nor does it resolve the confounding already noted: PC, HOC, and SC differ in composition/structure as well as domain size, so any of those factors (or the transition mechanism itself) could drive both the domain scale and the FWHM anomaly. The FWHM measurement also only adds a third, still ordinal (three-phase) data point to the correspondence, so it remains a pattern across three categories, not a dose-response curve, and the static-SEM/dynamic-XRD distinction (and thus the cross-technique-correspondence-not-causal-effect caveat) still applies unchanged. What is newly supported is that the anomaly is not confined to peak position (linearity of thermal expansion) but extends to peak width, reinforcing that finer domains are associated with greater structural inhomogeneity through the transition, not just an offset in average lattice parameter.

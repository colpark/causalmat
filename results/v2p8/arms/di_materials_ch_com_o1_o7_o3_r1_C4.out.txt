The established result justifies concluding that the near-100% relative density reported for the HP composites (particularly the 1 and 5 wt% AlN samples) is a genuine, physically real state and not a measurement artifact: the bulk density figure and the SEM fracture-surface image are mutually corroborating, since the micrograph independently shows a pore-free, well-consolidated microstructure with large flat transgranular fracture facets (fracture through grains rather than around voids), consistent with a fully dense body. So the combination supports treating "fully dense" as an established, cross-validated characterization of these compositions, resting on two independent lines of evidence (bulk mass/volume measurement and direct local microstructural inspection) rather than one.

What it leaves open: density is a single whole-sample average and cannot on its own say whether any residual porosity is open or closed or how it is spatially distributed, and the SEM view is local and qualitative, covering only one fracture surface, so it cannot quantify density or be compared numerically across the 0/1/3/5 wt% AlN series. Consequently the pairing gives no wt%-resolved porosity data and does not establish a quantitative trend tying SEM pore appearance to the specific density values (~97.5%, ~99.3%, ~100%) at each composition — only qualitative consistency between "high density" and "few/no visible pores." It also says nothing about the mechanism by which AlN addition raises relative density (e.g., acting as a sintering aid or affecting grain-boundary behavior); the evidence confirms the resulting dense, pore-free, transgranular-fracture state but is silent on why AlN produces it.</message>

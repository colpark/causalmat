Both panels are SEM micrographs of wear debris at the same magnification (X750, 20 um scale bar, 15 kV, SEI), captioned only "stir casting" (a) and "FSP" (b) — no wear-test parameters, COF, or debris-composition data are given in the panels themselves.

What the images show: Panel a (stir casting) is dominated by large, flat, plate-like flakes on the order of 20-40 um, several clearly larger than the 20 um scale bar, mixed with some finer particulate. Panel b (FSP) is dominated by much finer, roughly equiaxed particles well under 5-10 um, many clumped into loose agglomerates, with only an occasional larger fragment at the edge.

What this supports: The morphology is genuinely different between the two processing routes — stir-cast material sheds coarser, sheet-like debris consistent with a delamination/flaking-type wear response, while the FSP material sheds much finer, granular debris consistent with a milder, more abrasive-type wear response. This is a real, visually verifiable contrast in debris size and shape tied to processing route.

What it leaves open: The panels alone do not establish the wear mechanism causally — there's no COF trace, wear-rate/volume-loss number, EDS/composition data on the debris (e.g., to confirm oxidation), hardness or grain-size data linking FSP's known grain refinement to this debris change, or any indication of test conditions (load, sliding distance, counterface) or replicate count. So the images justify a descriptive claim about debris size/morphology differing by route, but not a mechanistic claim (e.g., "FSP grain refinement reduces wear via mechanism X") or a quantitative wear-performance ranking — those require additional data not present in these two crops.

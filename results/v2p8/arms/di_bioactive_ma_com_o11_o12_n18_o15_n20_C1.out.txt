The established result supports the conclusion that BVLD/NO-PPAmF suppresses platelet adhesion in two independent, convergent respects simultaneously: it has the lowest measured platelet count of the five surfaces tested (~5x1e2 cells/mm2, versus 32 and 41x1e2 on 316L SS and PPAmF, and 15.5 and 11x1e2 on BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF alone), and the platelets that are present on it are morphologically unspread (a few round platelets) rather than the spread, dendritic, activated morphology seen on the other surfaces. Because these are two distinct measurement modalities (a quantitative count from BIO and a qualitative shape assessment from SEM) that point the same direction on the same surface, the pairing justifies saying BVLD/NO-PPAmF shows a joint reduction in both platelet adhesion (number) and platelet activation (shape/spreading state), a claim that neither measurement alone could support — the count alone says nothing about the shape of the platelets that did adhere, and the SEM images alone give no quantitative comparison across surfaces.

This leaves several things open. First, the ordering across the five surfaces is descriptive only: no statistics or error bars are reported, so whether the differences (including the gap between BVLD/NO-PPAmF and the next-lowest surfaces) are statistically robust is untested. Second, the SEM morphology and BIO counts were not tied together numerically within the same fields of view, so the specific correspondence between "few round platelets" and "~5x1e2 cells/mm2" is an inference from parallel trends, not a directly measured joint relationship. Third, the result is silent on mechanism — it does not explain what surface-chemistry or biochemical property of BVLD/NO-PPAmF produces this dual suppression, only that the two readouts move together across surfaces.</message>

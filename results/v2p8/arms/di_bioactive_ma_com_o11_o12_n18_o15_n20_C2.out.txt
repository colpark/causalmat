The established result supports the conclusion that BVLD/NO-PPAmF combines the lowest platelet adhesion count (~5x1e2 cells/mm2) with a morphology dominated by a few round, unspread (non-activated) platelets, and that these two independent measurements move together specifically on this surface — a joint reduction in platelet adhesion and activation that is not visible from either measurement alone. This is a stronger claim for hemocompatibility-relevant service capability than either dataset alone: it rules out the possibility that the low count reflects few-but-highly-activated platelets, and rules out the possibility that the qualitative "few round platelets" description masks a high underlying density.

What it leaves open: (1) No statistics or error bars accompany either measurement, so the apparent ordering across the five surfaces (316L SS and PPAmF highest; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF intermediate; BVLD/NO-PPAmF lowest) is descriptive only, not statistically tested — it cannot be said with confidence that BVLD/NO-PPAmF is significantly better than the intermediate surfaces. (2) The SEM morphology grading (many/fewer/a few, round vs. dendritic) is qualitative and not numerically linked field-by-field to the BIO counts, so the correspondence between "a few round platelets" and "~5x1e2 cells/mm2" is an inferred, not measured, relationship. (3) No mechanistic or biochemical explanation is given for why BVLD/NO-PPAmF suppresses both count and spreading — only that the two phenomena co-occur across surfaces. So the result supports a qualitative, non-quantified inference of superior anti-thrombogenic surface behavior for BVLD/NO-PPAmF, but does not establish statistical significance, a precise quantitative count-morphology relationship, or a causal/mechanistic basis for that behavior.

The two panels are OM trace maps for the [11-20] (F4a) and [01-10] (F4b) specimens across RT-400C, on both the prismatic/pyramidal side surfaces and the (0001) surface, with labeled trace planes and "deformation band" callouts at every temperature.

What the combination supports: the earlier OM+EBSD pairing established that the straight, {10-12}/{01-12}-trace bands are the twin-like population (uniform-colour, single orientation), while the wavy "deformation band" population is the one whose EBSD colour shades continuously (kink-type). This new macroscopic series, in the same two specimen orientations, shows that the {01-12}/{10-12} twin-trace planes are visible only at RT and 200C and are absent at 300-400C, replaced there by {10-10}/{1-100} prismatic slip traces — yet the "deformation band" callouts persist at every temperature, RT through 400C, including 300-400C where twinning traces have disappeared. Read together, this justifies the conclusion that the kink-type (wavy, orientation-graded) band population is not contingent on concurrent twinning: it survives the temperature-driven switch from twin-dominated to prismatic-slip-dominated surface trace behavior, so kink-band formation and deformation twinning are separable, differently temperature-dependent processes rather than the same event seen two ways.

What it leaves open: no EBSD orientation maps were taken at 300-400C in this dataset, so the claim that the bands still labeled "deformation band" at 300-400C actually retain the continuously-shading orientation signature is an extrapolation from the OM label/morphology alone, not re-confirmed crystallographically at those temperatures. The result also remains qualitative — no misorientation magnitude, and still only two specimen orientations — so it cannot quantify how kink-band character itself might change with temperature even if the population persists.

Panel F8c plots AQY (%) on a wavelength scan against the UV-Vis absorbance curve, and its caption specifies this AQY spectrum is measured "over Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS sample" only — i.e., it is a single-sample action spectrum (18.3% at 400 nm, 15.8% at 420, 6.5% at 450, 4.7% at 500, 0.19% at 550), not a cross-sample comparison.

What the combination settles: the earlier result established that, across four samples, the composition ordering that tracks higher steady photocurrent (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS > Pd-ZIS > Ag-ZIS > ZIS) also tracks the same ordering in H2 evolution rate, supporting that this composition ordering is associated with performance in both measures. The AQY spectrum adds a mechanistic check specifically for the top-ranked sample: its H2-evolution efficiency per absorbed photon falls off in step with the absorption edge, confirming that the high activity of Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS is genuinely a light-absorption-driven photocatalytic process rather than a wavelength-independent or dark artifact. Together, this corroborates that the sample sitting atop the earlier photocurrent/H2 ranking behaves as expected for absorption-driven charge generation, lending mechanistic plausibility to that ordering.

What it leaves open: AQY is reported for only one of the four ranked samples, so it cannot show whether AQY itself follows the same Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS > Pd-ZIS > Ag-ZIS > ZIS order — that comparison is simply unavailable here. It also does not touch any of the earlier qualifications: photocurrent and H2 rate remain different quantity types, the composition series is still incomplete on the photocurrent side, only four overlap points still limit dose-response conclusions, and composition still confounds any causal claim that photocurrent differences (versus composition-driven absorption/charge-generation differences) cause the H2 evolution differences.

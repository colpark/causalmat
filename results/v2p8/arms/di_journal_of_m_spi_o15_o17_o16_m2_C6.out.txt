The settled result supports that both composite surfaces wear predominantly by abrasion (parallel sliding-direction grooves, no large craters on either surface), and that the FA-containing surface additionally shows shallower grooves with fine debris and no large crater. Together these support the interpretation that abrasive wear, not adhesive wear, is the operative mode for both materials, and that dispersed FA plausibly moderates the severity of abrasive cutting through some barrier or fine-debris effect, making the FA-containing surface's wear milder than the plain surface's.

What remains open: this is qualitative SEM morphology from single images, so "shallower" and the presence of "fine debris" are visual impressions rather than measured, quantified comparisons (no groove-depth data, no debris quantification). The debris has not been chemically or compositionally analyzed, so it cannot be confirmed to originate from fractured FA rather than from matrix wear. Only two specific features were examined, not a full survey of the worn surfaces, so the observations cannot fully rule out localized adhesive wear features elsewhere on either surface. Finally, the images cannot discriminate between the two proposed sub-mechanisms for how FA moderates cutting -- FA blocking asperity penetration versus fine debris acting as a rolling third body to reduce cutting severity -- since both are equally consistent with the shallower-grooves-plus-debris pattern. In short, the result justifies a qualitative, image-based case for abrasive (not adhesive) wear with FA-moderated severity, but not a quantified or mechanistically discriminated claim.

The established result supports a limited, regime-specific claim: at low PZT loading (0-4 wt%), the beta-phase fraction rise (76-76.3% up to a peak of 81.8-83.2%) tracks the initial voltage rise (7.5 to 32.0 V), which is consistent with beta-phase enhancement contributing to the early output gain. That is the full extent of what this result can support.

It leaves open, and explicitly does not establish, several things. First, it does not support a beta-phase-mediated story for the full 0-10 wt% range: beyond 4 wt%, beta content eases back to roughly 81% over 6-10 wt% while voltage keeps rising monotonically to 43.0 and then 62.0 V, so at higher loadings the two series decouple and beta-phase content cannot be the operative driver there. Second, because beta content is non-monotonic while voltage is monotonic across the whole range, no single shared functional relationship between the two fits the data. Third, the comparison is between two different kinds of quantities -- a normalized structural fraction (%) versus an absolute output amount (V) -- so even where the trends align, the magnitude of the voltage gain cannot be inferred from the magnitude of the beta-phase change. Fourth, PZT wt% is a common driver of both series by design, so the low-loading correlation does not by itself distinguish a genuine beta-phase causal pathway from coincidental co-variation with loading. Finally, with only 5-6 discrete wt% points per series, the data are sufficient only to compare trend shapes, not to fit or validate a quantitative functional relationship. In short: consistent with a partial, low-loading contribution; not proof of causation, not applicable at higher loadings, and not quantitatively linked.

The panels (F4a: [11-20], F4b: [01-10]) confirm the stated macroscopic pattern: at RT and 200C the side surfaces are marked with {01-12}/{10-12} traces (the straight trace family the earlier EBSD/OM pairing identified as twin-like, single-orientation), while at 300 and 400C those labels are replaced by {10-10}/{1-100} traces (prismatic slip planes); "deformation band" labels appear in at least one panel at every temperature from RT through 400C.

Read together with the earlier result, this settles that the twin-like behavior class (straight {10-12}/{01-12}-trace bands shown by OM+EBSD to carry a single, uniform orientation) is temperature-restricted: it is the dominant trace signature only at RT-200C and is supplanted by prismatic-slip traces by 300-400C, whereas the deformation-band population survives across the full temperature range tested. Since deformation bands were the surface-scale correlate of the wavy, EBSD-shading (kink-band-like) population in the earlier pairing, the persistence of "deformation band" labels at every temperature is consistent with that non-twin behavior class remaining active from RT to 400C even as twinning drops out.

What is left open: these F4 panels are OM only, at low magnification, with no accompanying EBSD orientation maps at 300/400C, so the claim that the persisting deformation bands are still the same continuously-shading (kink-band) population is inferred by trace-morphology analogy, not re-verified by orientation data at elevated temperature. The measurement also gives presence/absence of trace families, not a quantified twin fraction or transition temperature, and it still covers only the two loading orientations ([11-20], [01-10]) from the earlier pairing, so extension to other orientations remains unestablished.

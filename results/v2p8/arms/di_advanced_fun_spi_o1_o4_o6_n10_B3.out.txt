The F2d panel shows a third structural signature, at the same ~150-155 K temperature region already implicated by Observation B. SC's peak-width ratio drops smoothly and monotonically from ~100% to ~50% at 150 K, with no overshoot — consistent with the smooth/near-linear peak-position shift it showed in Observation B. PC and HOC, by contrast, each show a sharp transient spike in peak width (to ~242% and ~128% respectively) right at 150-155 K before relaxing — an anomaly co-located with the curvature/offset in their peak-position traces from Observation B.

Read together, this strengthens (but does not newly establish) the domain-size correspondence: the coarse-domain phase (SC) behaves simply on both XRD metrics — position and width — while the finer/less-uniform-domain phases (PC, HOC) both show anomalous, non-monotonic behavior on position and width at the same temperature. This is convergent evidence within the same technique (XRD) rather than an independent confirmation, since peak width and peak position are two measurands from the same diffraction data, not two separate methods. It supports treating the ~150-155 K anomaly as a real, reproducible structural event that co-occurs with fine/heterogeneous domain scale.

It leaves open everything the earlier qualification already flagged: still only three phases (correspondence, not dose-response), domain size is still confounded with phase identity/composition, and the causal driver of the anomaly (domain size itself, versus some other phase-specific structural transition, e.g., an octahedral tilt or phase transformation that happens to co-occur with both domain size and width behavior) remains undetermined. Whether the FWHM spike reflects strain, phase coexistence, or something else specific to PC/HOC composition is not resolved here.

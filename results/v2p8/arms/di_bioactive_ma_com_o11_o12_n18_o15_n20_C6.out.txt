The settled result supports a specific, joint claim: on BVLD/NO-PPAmF, the co-occurrence of the lowest platelet count (~5x1e2 cells/mm2) with a morphology dominated by round, unspread (non-activated) platelets shows that this surface suppresses both the amount of platelet adhesion and the degree of platelet activation/spreading, relative to the other four surfaces (316L SS, PPAmF, BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF alone). This is more than either measurement alone could establish: the BIO count by itself says nothing about the activation state of the platelets that did adhere, and the SEM images alone give no quantitative comparison across surfaces. So the justified conclusion is that BVLD/NO-PPAmF's anti-thrombogenic character (in the sense relevant to service capability as an implant/blood-contacting coating) is supported on two independent, convergent axes -- fewer platelets and less-activated platelets -- rather than resting on a single line of evidence.

What it leaves open, and must not be overstated: (1) There is no statistical testing or error reporting, so the apparent stepwise ordering of surfaces by platelet count, and the parallel ordering by morphology, is descriptive only -- not confirmed as a real or reproducible trend. (2) The SEM shape descriptions (many/fewer/a few) are qualitative and were not numerically linked field-by-field to the counted densities, so the "reduced count + reduced spreading" correspondence is an observed pattern, not a measured statistical relationship. (3) No mechanism -- surface chemistry, protein adsorption, biochemical signaling -- is identified; the result only shows that count and morphology track together across surfaces, not why. Any claim about reproducibility, significance, or causal mechanism would go beyond what this settled result carries.

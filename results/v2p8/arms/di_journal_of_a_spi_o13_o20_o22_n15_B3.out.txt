The panel (F7e) shows output power vs. load resistance for a fixed-composition PENG (f-P0.10, 220 μm film) in two morphological forms: the electrospun fiber film peaks at 136.9 μW (at 10 MΩ) versus 21.025 μW for the cast film — roughly a 6.5x higher peak power for the fiber form, with fiber power exceeding cast power across essentially the whole resistance sweep.

Read together with the earlier PZT wt%/beta-phase/voltage result, this settles that output enhancement in this PENG system is not reducible to a single beta-phase-mediated composition story. The earlier result already showed beta-phase content only tracks voltage gain up to 4 wt%, decoupling afterward; this new panel adds an independent axis — fiber vs. cast morphology at one fixed composition — showing a large output gap that composition/beta-phase trends alone cannot address, since both series here share the same PZT loading (0.10) and differ only in film-forming method. So the combination supports treating morphology (fiber spinning) as a contributor to service_capability (power delivery) that operates alongside, not through, the beta-phase pathway documented for the composition series.

What remains open: this panel gives no FTIR/beta-phase measurement for the fiber vs. cast comparison, so whether fiber processing itself raises beta-phase fraction (making it a extension of the same beta-phase mechanism) or acts through a wholly separate mechanism (e.g., piezoelectric coupling, surface area, impedance matching) cannot be determined from these two results. Also, power and open-circuit voltage are different quantities (load-dependent product vs. no-load potential), so the 6.5x power ratio cannot be mapped onto the voltage magnitudes from the composition series.

The F2d panel plots relative FWHM (Δσ/σ, % of 700 K value) versus temperature for four reflections each in three perovskite forms — single crystal (SC, blue), polycrystal (PC, red), and a third form labeled HOC (black) — each on its own y-axis scale.

What the panel supports: all three forms show a discontinuity or anomaly in peak width clustered near ~150–155 K, consistent with the reported numbers. In SC, the (004) and (242) reflections drop sharply from ~100% to ~50–80% just above 150 K while (224)/(400) stay low throughout — a step-like narrowing. In PC, the (242) reflection spikes to ~260% right around 150 K then relaxes downward, while the other three PC reflections stay flat near 100%. In HOC, the (004) reflection bumps up to ~128% near 150–155 K before declining, while (400) and (224) drift down more gradually. This pattern — a width anomaly concentrated at one particular reflection per sample, occurring near the same temperature across SC, PC, and HOC — supports the conclusion that a structural (phase) transition occurs near ~150 K in this material, and that the transition's signature in peak broadening/narrowing is reflection-specific (anisotropic), i.e., it affects certain lattice planes/orientations more than others.

What it leaves open: the panel alone does not identify the space groups or specific symmetry change involved, does not explain why SC narrows while PC and HOC broaden at the same reflections, and does not establish whether the anomaly reflects a single first-order transition, domain/twin formation, or strain relaxation — those interpretations require the accompanying diffraction/lattice-parameter data, not shown here.

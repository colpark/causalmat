The photographs (F6b tube cross-sections, F6c explanted thrombus strips) show the same five-surface ordering already seen at the cellular level: 316L SS and PPAmF have lumens/tubes filled with dark thrombus (heaviest clot burden), BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are only partly occluded, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF has a nearly clean lumen and foil (least clot). Read together with the established SEM+BIO result — that BVLD/NO-PPAmF uniquely combines the lowest platelet count with the least-spread, least-activated platelet morphology — the combination supports a step up in scale: the platelet-level suppression (fewer, rounder, less-activated platelets) on BVLD/NO-PPAmF is accompanied by a macroscopic, whole-vessel reduction in gross thrombus formation after 2 h of blood circulation. Neither measurement alone carries this: the photographs show only gross clot extent with no cellular resolution, and the SEM/BIO pair (as already qualified) shows only platelet-level counts and morphology with no evidence about whether that translates into less thrombus at the device/lumen scale. Together they justify saying that reduced platelet adhesion and activation on BVLD/NO-PPAmF co-occurs with, and plausibly underlies, reduced gross thrombosis on that same surface, tying the cellular finding to a functionally relevant outcome.

What remains open: the photographs are descriptive with no measured thrombus area, mass, or statistics, so the ordering is qualitative, not tested. No step establishes a causal link — thrombus formation also depends on fibrin/coagulation-cascade activity not captured by platelet counts or SEM, so the photographic result could partly reflect mechanisms independent of platelet adhesion. Flow/patency function and underlying surface chemistry remain unaddressed by any of these measurements.</message>

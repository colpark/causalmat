This wear-debris measurement is a quantified companion to Observation A: it converts the earlier qualitative impression ("shallower grooves... fine debris" on the FSP/FA surface) into a size-resolved contrast — surface a debris are large, flat, coherent flakes (~20-40 µm), while surface b debris are fine (<5 µm) and loosely agglomerated. Reading it with the already-settled abrasion result, the combination sharpens the moderation pathway: it shows the FA-containing surface does not merely look less severely cut, its wear product is genuinely finer and clumped rather than spalled into large plates, which is the debris signature expected of a three-body rolling mechanism (fine agglomerated particles interposed between asperities) rather than of large-flake gouging characteristic of unmodified abrasive cutting. So the combination supports favoring the "fine debris reduces cutting severity via rolling/three-body action" sub-mechanism over the "FA simply blocks asperity penetration" sub-mechanism, since a pure blocking effect would not obviously predict this shift in debris particle size and agglomeration state, whereas the rolling-debris account directly predicts it.

What remains open: the debris composition is still unanalyzed, so it is not confirmed whether the fine particles in b originate from fractured FA, from finer-grained FSP matrix microstructure, or from a mix — so the finer debris could partly reflect FSP grain refinement rather than FA fragmentation alone. The measurement also doesn't establish whether the agglomerates are actively rolling (versus simply adhering) during sliding, and the full-surface-survey limitation noted in the earlier qualifications still applies.</message>

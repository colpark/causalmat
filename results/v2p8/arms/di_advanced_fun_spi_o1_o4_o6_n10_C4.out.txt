The established result supports a descriptive correspondence, not a mechanistic or quantitative relationship. Specifically, it justifies saying that across the three phases examined, coarser/more uniform domain scale (SC) co-occurs with smoother, near-linear lattice-expansion behavior in the (004)/(400) and (242)/(224) peak shifts, while finer or less uniform domain scale (PC, HOC) co-occurs with curved, offset expansion traces. This is a legitimate cross-technique pairing: two independent measurements (real-space SEM domain size and in-situ XRD peak-position-vs-temperature behavior) point in the same direction for all three phases studied.

What it does not settle: it is not a dose-response or graded relationship between domain size and expansion linearity, since only three phase categories were compared — three points do not establish a trend or threshold, only a directional association. It is not a measured causal effect, because domain size is a static structural metric and expansion smoothness is a dynamic behavioral one; the correspondence is observational, not an intervention showing that changing domain size changes expansion behavior. Most importantly, domain size is confounded with phase identity: PC, HOC, and SC differ in composition and crystal structure as well as domain size, so anything attributable to domain size could equally or instead be driven by phase-specific structural or compositional differences. The result therefore supports domain scale as a plausible correlate worth further investigation, but leaves open whether domain size itself, versus other phase-specific properties, is what actually governs the expansion curvature.

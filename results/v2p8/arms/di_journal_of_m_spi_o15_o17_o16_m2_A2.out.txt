The two SEM micrographs (both X750, 20 µm scale bar) visibly support the stated measurement: panel a (stir casting) shows large, flat, sharp-edged flake/plate-like debris on the order of tens of micrometers (roughly 20-40 µm range is plausible by eye against the scale bar), consistent with delamination-type wear. Panel b (FSP) shows a much finer, granular texture dominated by small rounded particles well under 5-10 µm, with some visible clumping/agglomeration, consistent with an abrasive/oxidative fine-debris mode.

What this justifies: the debris morphology and size differ qualitatively between the two processing routes — stir-cast material produces coarser, plate-like wear debris while FSP material produces finer, agglomerated particulate debris. That is a legitimate descriptive/morphological finding directly visible in the images.

What it leaves open: debris morphology alone does not establish the wear mechanism or "MEC/pathway" definitively — flake size and particle fineness are consistent with delamination vs. abrasive/oxidative wear, respectively, but the panels give no chemical (EDS/oxide) data, no hardness or microstructural context, no wear-test conditions (load, sliding distance, COF), and no statistics on debris size distribution beyond visual impression. So this measurement supports a morphological contrast between the two processes but does not by itself confirm the underlying wear mechanism, its cause (e.g., grain refinement from FSP vs. porosity/defects from stir casting), or generalizability beyond this single sample pair.

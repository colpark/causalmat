The established result justifies a limited, order-level conclusion: across the four compositions measured in both observations (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS, Pd-ZIS, Ag-ZIS, ZIS), higher steady photocurrent co-occurs with higher H2 evolution rate, and the composition ranking is identical on both figures of merit. This supports treating photocurrent as a consistent proxy indicator that tracks, in rank order, which compositions perform better for H2 evolution in this set — i.e., the two measurements are directionally consistent rather than contradictory.

What it does not support: it is not a quantitative or unit-comparable link, since photocurrent (current density) and H2 evolution rate (molar gas rate) are different kinds of quantities from different setups — no conversion factor or proportionality constant can be inferred. It does not establish a dose-response relationship or the magnitude/slope of any association, since only four ordered points are available, which is sufficient to show matching rank order but not a curve. The composition series is also incomplete on the photocurrent side: Ag0.5Pd0.5-ZIS and Ag0.75Pd0.25-ZIS have H2 evolution data but no matched photocurrent measurement, so the full Ag:Pd series is not jointly covered. Most importantly, because Ag:Pd composition was varied simultaneously with both measurements, the result cannot distinguish whether photocurrent differences causally drive the H2 evolution differences versus composition independently affecting each outcome through separate mechanisms. In short: consistent, matching ordering across four samples supports correlation-level consistency between the two indicators, but causal direction, effect size, and full-series coverage remain open.

F7e is orthogonal to the earlier PZT-wt%/beta-phase/voltage result. That earlier result varied PZT loading at fixed (cast) morphology and found voltage tracking beta-phase only up to 4 wt%, decoupling above it. F7e instead holds composition fixed (f-P0.10) and varies morphology — fiber vs. cast film — showing fiber-film power peaking near 136.9 uW versus ~21.025 uW for cast, at the same optimal ~10 MOhm load, a roughly 6.5-fold gain.

Combined, the two results support that service_capability (power delivery) has more than one contributing axis: composition-driven beta-phase enhancement explains part of the early voltage gain, and, independently of composition, the fiber-forming process itself confers a large additional output advantage over cast film at matched composition. That both series peak near the same load resistance suggests the fiber advantage is an intrinsic output-capability difference rather than an artifact of resistance matching.

What remains open: F7e reports only power vs. load, not beta-phase content for the fiber vs. cast samples, so it cannot say whether the fiber's advantage is itself mediated by higher beta-phase fraction (extending the same mechanism) or arises from unrelated fiber-specific factors (chain alignment, poling efficiency, geometry, capacitance). The combination is also silent on the 6-10 wt% PZT regime, where voltage kept climbing as beta phase eased — that decoupling is untouched by this fiber/cast comparison. Only one composition (0.10) was tested in both forms, so whether the fiber advantage generalizes across other PZT loadings is unverified.

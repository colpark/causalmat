Both panels (F4a, [1120] loading; F4b, [01-10] loading) show side-surface trace images at RT, 200, 300, 400 C, each hand-labeled with the Miller indices of the traces. In F4a and F4b alike, the RT and 200 C images are labeled with {01-12}-type indices (e.g., (0-112)(01-12), (01-12)(0-112)), consistent with the description of twin traces, while the 300 and 400 C images switch to (10-10)(01-10)/(10-10)(-1100)-type labels, consistent with prismatic slip traces. "Deformation band" text labels appear at RT, 200 C, and 300 C in one or both panels, and also at 400 C in F4a's upper row — so bands are visible at most but not unambiguously every single temperature/panel combination (F4b's 400 C images show only slip-trace labels, no explicit "deformation band" tag).

What this settles: the macroscopic surface-trace evidence supports a temperature-driven change in the dominant deformation trace character — from {01-12}-type traces (twin-consistent) at RT-200 C to {10-10}-type prismatic traces at 300-400 C — for both loading orientations shown, with deformation bands present across most of the temperature range as a persistent secondary feature.

What it leaves open: these are optical/SEM surface trace labels, not direct crystallographic proof (no diffraction, EBSD, or TEM confirmation is shown) that the traces are true twins versus slip bands; no quantification of area fraction, trace density, or the precise transition temperature between 200 and 300 C is given; and whether "deformation band" denotes the same physical feature at every temperature, or whether it appears at literally every panel (400 C in F4b lacks the label), is not settled by these images alone.

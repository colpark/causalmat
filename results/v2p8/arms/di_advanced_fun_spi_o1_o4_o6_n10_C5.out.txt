The established result justifies a modest, descriptive conclusion: across the three phase categories examined (SC, PC, HOC), domain size as seen in SEM tracks with the shape of the lattice-expansion curve seen by in-situ XRD — the phase with the coarsest domains (SC) shows smooth, near-linear peak shifts, while the phases with finer or less uniform domains (PC, HOC) show curvature and offsets in the same peaks. This supports treating domain-scale coarseness as associated with smoother thermal lattice-expansion behavior, and finer/less-uniform domain scale as associated with non-linear, offset expansion — but only as a pattern observed across these three specific phases.

What it leaves open is considerable. First, with only three phases compared, this is a three-point correspondence, not a dose-response curve; no claim can be made about how expansion linearity would change with domain size within a phase, or whether the relationship is monotonic, threshold-like, or something else across a broader range. Second, the two observations are different kinds of measurement — static real-space domain size (SEM) versus dynamic in-situ peak-position-vs-temperature behavior (XRD) — so this is a cross-technique correspondence, not a measured causal or mechanistic link; nothing here shows that domain size itself drives the expansion curvature. Third, and most importantly, domain size is confounded with phase identity: PC, HOC, and SC differ in composition and crystal structure as well as in domain size, so the observed pattern could equally or instead be driven by phase-specific structural/compositional factors rather than domain scale per se. In short, the result supports an association worth further investigation, not a validated dose-response or causal claim about domain size controlling lattice-expansion smoothness.

The F4 macroscopic OM series shows that {01-12}/{10-12} twin-plane traces are present at RT-200°C and are gone by 300-400°C, replaced by {10-10}/{1-100} prismatic slip traces, while a separate feature labeled "deformation band" is visible in every panel at every temperature (RT through 400°C), on both the [11-20] and [01-10] loading-orientation side surfaces.

Read against the earlier OM+EBSD result — that straight, {10-12}/{01-12}-trace bands correspond to the uniform-single-colour, twin-like orientation population, while wavy bands with asymmetric OM contrast correspond to the continuously-shading, kink-band-like orientation population — the combination settles that the "deformation bands" persisting across all four temperatures cannot simply be twins under another label. If they were, they should disappear together with the twin traces once slip supersedes twinning at 300-400°C; instead they survive that switch. So the combination lets the persistent band population be assigned, by elimination, to the second (wavy/orientation-gradient, kink-band-like) behavior_class already identified by OM+EBSD, and it ties that class's mechanistic independence from twinning to a temperature axis: twinning is confined to RT-200°C, but the other band class operates throughout.

What this leaves open: the F4 panels are OM only, at macroscopic scale, with no EBSD re-verification at 300-400°C — so whether the persisting bands still show the wavy morphology and continuously-shading orientation signature at elevated temperature (versus some new, unlinked feature merely reusing the same "deformation band" label) is not directly checked. The mechanism producing these bands once twinning is gone (still kink-banding vs. something slip-related) is likewise not established, only inferred by exclusion.

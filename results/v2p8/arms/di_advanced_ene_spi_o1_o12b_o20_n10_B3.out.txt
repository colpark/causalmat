The three panels (5d→5e→5f) show a Zn-content series of structural models — K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6, K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6, ZnMnFe(CN)6 — in which, as Zn content rises, the Mn coordination polyhedron changes (MnN6 → MnN3 → MnN4) and grey ZnN4 tetrahedra appear and become directly linked to the FeC6 octahedra of the same 3D framework. This is site-resolved crystallographic information: it places Zn inside the N/C-coordinated host lattice, bonded to the Fe-centered polyhedra, rather than showing Zn merely present somewhere in the electrode.

Combined with the earlier result, this settles the "where does the Zn go" question that observation B (bulk ICP at%) alone could not answer: the rising, then ~47 at%-plateauing Zn content measured in bulk can now be read as Zn occupying framework coordination sites (ZnN4 linked to FeC6) within the same monoclinic open-framework host whose initial single-phase structure was confirmed at cycle 0 — i.e., the combination supports that Zn uptake is a framework-incorporation process, with Mn site occupancy changing (N6→N3→N4) as Zn substitutes in.

What remains open: the panel series is indexed by nominal Zn stoichiometry in synthesized/modeled formulas, not explicitly tied to the cycle-20 sample or its measured composition, so it does not prove that the specific ~47 at% plateau corresponds to exactly this MnN4/ZnN4-FeC6 configuration rather than some other point on this structural progression. It also does not establish that the initial P21/n phase persists unchanged into this Zn-substituted regime — the text notes this substitution enables a "subsequent phase transition," implying the host structure itself changes, not merely fills.

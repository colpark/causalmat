The established result supports a limited, regime-specific claim: at low PZT loading (0-4 wt%), the rise in beta-phase fraction (76.1-76.3% to a peak of 81.8-83.2%) tracks the early rise in voltage (7.5 to 32.0 V), which is consistent with beta-phase enhancement contributing to the initial output gain in that low-loading window only.

It does not establish beta-phase content as the driver of performance across the full 0-10 wt% range. Beyond 4 wt%, the two series decouple: beta content eases back to ~81% over 6-10 wt% while voltage continues climbing monotonically to 43.0 and 62.0 V. Since beta content is non-monotonic and voltage is monotonic over the whole range, the pairing cannot account for the voltage gains seen at higher loading.

Several things remain open. First, causation is not established even in the low-loading regime: PZT wt% is a shared driver of both the FTIR (beta-phase) and transport (voltage) series by construction, so the co-rise could reflect coincidence rather than a beta-phase-mediated pathway. Second, because the two series are different kinds of quantities (a normalized structural fraction in % versus an absolute voltage output in V), no magnitude or proportionality claim can be drawn — the correlation is a trend-shape comparison only. Third, with only 5-6 discrete wt% points per series, there is no basis for fitting or inferring a shared functional/quantitative relationship, only for comparing qualitative trend direction. So the result supports a plausible partial mechanism at low loading, but leaves the mechanism at higher loading, the causal direction, and any quantitative relationship unresolved.</message>

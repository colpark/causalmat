The hardness curve shows HP hardness rising from ~18.0 GPa (0 wt%) to a peak ~20.3 GPa (1 wt%), then settling to ~19.4 and ~19.2 GPa at 3 and 5 wt% — still well above the 0 wt% baseline but below the 1 wt% peak.

Combined with the earlier density/SEM finding that HP composites, especially at 1 and 5 wt% AlN, are genuinely near-fully dense (pore-free, transgranular fracture), the pairing supports one specific inference: the elevated and largely stable hardness across 1, 3, and 5 wt% cannot be attributed to progressive loss of densification, since the microstructural evidence rules out increasing residual porosity as the cause of the modest decline after 1 wt%. In other words, the hardness plateau/slight downturn from 1→5 wt% is not a densification artifact — the samples stay dense, so whatever causes the small drop must be intrinsic to composition or microstructure (e.g., grain size or phase effects), not porosity.

What this does not settle: the earlier result gave no wt%-resolved porosity quantification and no SEM data specific to 0 or 3 wt%, so the jump in hardness from 0 to 1 wt% (18.0→20.3 GPa) cannot be confidently attributed to improved densification versus some other AlN-driven effect — the density/SEM pairing is only qualitative and doesn't establish a quantitative density-hardness correlation at each composition. The actual mechanism for either the initial rise or the subsequent mild decline (grain refinement, secondary phase, residual stress, intrinsic AlN hardness) remains unestablished by either measurement alone or in combination.</message>

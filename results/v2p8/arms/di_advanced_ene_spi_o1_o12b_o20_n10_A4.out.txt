What the three panels show: each is a unit-cell schematic (magenta Mn-centered polyhedra, orange/brown Fe-centered octahedra, pale N/C ligand spheres, purple K/interstitial spheres) with a local coordination-polyhedron inset giving bond lengths. F5d (K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6) shows an octahedral MnN6 inset (bonds ~1.98–2.28 Å). F5e (K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6) shows a lower-coordination MnN3 inset (~2.15–2.20 Å) drawn with a triangular/distorted geometry, and a grey tetrahedron appears in the framework alongside the magenta and orange polyhedra. F5f (ZnMnFe(CN)6) shows an MnN4 inset (~2.08–2.26 Å), and grey tetrahedra are now clearly distributed through the lattice, evidently sharing corners/edges with the orange octahedra.

Supported: across this compositional series (increasing Zn fraction), the modeled local Mn coordination environment changes from six-fold (MnN6) toward lower coordination (MnN3, MnN4), and grey polyhedra — presumably ZnN4 tetrahedra — become a distinct, distributed structural motif that is connected to the Fe-centered octahedra in the Zn-richest structure (f). This is consistent with the caption's claim of Jahn-Teller-driven Mn distortion and a Zn-tetrahedra/Fe-octahedra linkage enabling a phase transition.

Left open by the images alone: these are schematic/computed unit cells with single representative bond-length insets, not a table of refined occupancies or phase fractions, so quantitative site-occupancy values, coexistence fractions between MnN6/MnN3/MnN4 sites, and whether MnN3 is a real crystallographic average versus an idealized snapshot cannot be confirmed from the panels. Whether ZnN4–FeC6 linkage is bulk-uniform or localized also cannot be verified visually.

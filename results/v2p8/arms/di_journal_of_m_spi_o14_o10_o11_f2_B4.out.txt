The AQY panel (F8c) reports wavelength-dependent apparent quantum yield for a single sample, Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS: 18.3% at 400 nm, 15.8% at 420 nm, 6.5% at 450 nm, 4.7% at 500 nm, and 0.19% at 550 nm, closely tracking the absorbance curve's edge (absorption falls sharply between ~400-500 nm, and AQY falls in step).

Read together with the earlier result, this settles that the composition already shown to lead in both steady photocurrent and H2 evolution rate among the four-sample comparison (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS) is also the one whose photon-to-hydrogen conversion efficiency is concentrated in the near-UV/blue region and collapses toward zero past its absorption edge. That is, the combination supports that this composition's superior photocatalytic performance is spectrally limited by, and consistent with, its light absorption profile — the sample is not uniformly efficient across the visible range but performs where it absorbs.

This leaves open several things. AQY was measured only for Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS, not for the other three samples (ZIS, Ag-ZIS, Pd-ZIS) in the earlier ranking, so the combination cannot say whether the same photocurrent/H2 ranking would hold, or by how much, if compared via AQY — no cross-sample AQY comparison is possible. It also does not establish that absorption-edge-limited AQY is the reason this sample outranks the others, since no AQY data exist for the lower-ranked samples to compare against. Finally, AQY here is measured under monochromatic illumination at discrete wavelengths, a different measurement setup from the broadband photocurrent and H2 evolution measurements, so the wavelength dependence cannot be directly reconciled with those quantities' single aggregate values.

The established result supports that both composite surfaces (with and without FA) wear predominantly by abrasion, evidenced by parallel sliding-direction grooves and the absence of large craters on both surfaces. It further supports that adding FA changes the character of that abrasive wear in a favorable direction: the FA-containing surface shows shallower grooves with fine debris and still no large craters, which is consistent with a mechanism-of-change (MEC) in which dispersed FA moderates the severity of abrasive cutting -- either by blocking asperity penetration or by generating fine debris that rolls as a third body -- rather than the composite shifting to adhesive wear.

What this leaves open is substantial. First, "shallower" and debris presence are qualitative visual impressions from single SEM images, not quantified groove-depth measurements or debris counts, so the magnitude and statistical reliability of the difference between surfaces is not established. Second, the composition of the fine debris was not analyzed, so it is not confirmed that this debris originates from fractured FA rather than ordinary matrix wear debris -- the causal link between FA and the debris is inferred, not verified. Third, only two localized features were examined, not the full worn surface, so the complete absence of adhesive-wear features elsewhere on the samples cannot be claimed from this evidence alone. Finally, the two candidate sub-mechanisms (barrier effect vs. three-body debris rolling) remain equally consistent with the images and are not distinguished; the pathway is supported only at the level of "abrasion severity is moderated," not at the level of which specific micromechanism does the moderating.</message>

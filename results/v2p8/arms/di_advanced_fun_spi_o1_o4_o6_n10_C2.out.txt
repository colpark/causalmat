This settled result justifies only a correspondence claim: across the three phase categories examined (SC, PC, HOC), coarser/more uniform SEM domain size co-occurs with smoother, near-linear XRD lattice-expansion peak shifts, while finer/less-uniform domain size co-occurs with curved, offset peak shifts. It supports treating domain-size scale and lattice-expansion smoothness as associated properties that track together across these three phases, and it is consistent with (but does not establish) the idea that microstructural coarseness relates to more regular thermal lattice response.

What it leaves open, plainly:

1. No dose-response relationship: with only three phase-level data points, this cannot show that expansion linearity varies systematically (e.g., monotonically) with domain size across a continuum — it is a three-point correspondence, not a calibrated trend.

2. No causal mechanism: domain size is a static, real-space SEM quantity, while expansion smoothness is a dynamic, in-situ XRD quantity measured under temperature change. Their co-occurrence across phases does not demonstrate that domain size causes, controls, or mechanistically drives the expansion behavior.

3. Confounding by phase identity: PC, HOC, and SC differ from each other in composition and crystal structure, not just domain size. So the observed pairing could equally or instead be driven by intrinsic phase/structural differences (e.g., composition, crystal symmetry) rather than by domain size itself; the result cannot separate a domain-size effect from a phase-identity effect.

In short: the result supports a cross-technique, phase-level correspondence between coarser domains and smoother lattice expansion, but it does not establish causation, does not generalize beyond the three phases compared, and cannot rule out that phase/composition differences (rather than domain size per se) underlie the curvature seen in PC and HOC.

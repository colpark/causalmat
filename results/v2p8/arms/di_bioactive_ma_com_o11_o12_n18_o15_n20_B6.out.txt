F6b (tube cross-sections) and F6c (excised thrombus photographs) both order the five surfaces the same way as the earlier SEM/BIO result: 316L SS and PPAmF tubes are packed with dark thrombus filling the lumen; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are only partly filled; BVLD/NO-PPAmF lumen and foil are nearly clean, matching it having the lowest platelet count (~5x1e2/mm2) and least platelet spreading in the earlier SEM/BIO measurement.

What the combination supports: it extends the earlier surface-level finding (reduced platelet adhesion and reduced platelet spreading/activation on BVLD/NO-PPAmF, versus intermediate suppression on BVLD-PPAmF/NO-PPAmF alone and heavy adhesion/spreading on 316L SS and PPAmF) to a device/functional level. The photographs show that the same ranking of surfaces by platelet adhesion/activation also predicts the ranking by gross thrombus burden and lumen patency under blood flow through actual tubes. Neither piece of evidence shows this alone: the SEM/BIO data never measured thrombus mass or lumen occlusion, and the photographs alone carry no platelet count or morphology information and no mechanism. Together they support that the platelet-level suppression seen microscopically corresponds to macroscopically less thrombus and a more open lumen, i.e., a plausible functional consequence, without proving platelet suppression causes the reduced thrombus (thrombus also contains fibrin/RBCs, not platelets alone).

What remains open: no quantification of thrombus mass or percent occlusion (only descriptive terms - filled/partly open/nearly clean), no statistics or replicate counts for the photographs, no confirmation the photographed specimens are the same ones counted by SEM/BIO, and no mechanistic link between platelet suppression and thrombus reduction.

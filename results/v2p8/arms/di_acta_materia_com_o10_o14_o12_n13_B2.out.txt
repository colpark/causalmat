Conclusion the combination supports: The earlier OM+EBSD result identified two distinct band populations by signature — straight, {10-12}/{01-12}-trace bands with uniform single-colour EBSD orientation (twin-like), versus wavy, contrast-asymmetric bands with continuously shading EBSD orientation (kink-band-like). The new macro survey (F4a/F4b, RT-400 C) shows that the {01-12}/{10-12}-trace twin population is confined to RT-200 C and is gone by 300-400 C, replaced by {10-10}/{1-100} prismatic-slip traces, whereas a separately labeled "deformation band" feature is present in every panel from RT through 400 C. Read together, this justifies the conclusion that the persistent "deformation band" population is not simply an artifact or byproduct of twinning: it survives the temperature range over which twinning itself disappears and the dominant slip mode switches from twinning-associated to prismatic. That is, whatever produces the continuously-shading, kink-band-like orientation signature established earlier operates by a mechanism decoupled from the availability of twinning, since it persists even when twins are absent and prismatic slip has taken over.

What remains open: the macro OM montage does not include EBSD, so it cannot confirm that the "deformation bands" seen at 300-400 C still carry the same continuously-shading orientation signature identified at low temperature — that link was only established (per the qualifications) at RT-equivalent conditions in two specimen orientations. Whether the high-temperature deformation bands are the same kink-band entity, or a distinct band type that happens to share the OM label, is not settled here. The mechanistic cause of the bands' temperature-independence is also not addressed by either measurement.</message>

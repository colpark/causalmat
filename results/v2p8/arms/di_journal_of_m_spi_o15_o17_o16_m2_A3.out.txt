Both panels are SEM micrographs of wear debris, same magnification (X750, 20 um scale bar), captioned "a: stir casting" and "b: FSP."

Panel a (stir casting): the field is dominated by large, flat, plate-like flakes with sharp, angular edges, several spanning roughly 20-40 um, interspersed with some finer fragments.

Panel b (FSP): the field is dominated by fine, rounded particulate matter well under 5 um, packed into loose clumps/agglomerates, with only one or two larger platy fragments visible at the image edges.

What this settles: the two processing routes produce visually distinct wear-debris morphologies under identical imaging conditions — stir-cast material sheds coarse flake/platelet debris consistent with delamination-type wear, while FSP material sheds fine, agglomerated particulate debris consistent with oxidative/abrasive fine-particle wear. This is a legitimate, image-supported qualitative contrast in debris morphology and size scale between the two arms.

What it leaves open: a single SEM field per condition does not establish statistical representativeness of debris size/shape across the wear track, nor does it by itself identify the underlying wear mechanism (delamination vs. adhesive vs. abrasive vs. oxidative) with certainty — that inference requires corroborating evidence (e.g., worn-surface SEM, EDS/oxide analysis, COF/wear-rate trends) not present in these two crops. It also does not quantify hardness, grain size, or microstructural cause for the difference, nor confirm which route gives lower wear rate — only that debris morphology differs.

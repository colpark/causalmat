Panel F2d (Advanced Functional Materials 10.1002/adfm.202008088, crop 8fc82949e4...D.jpg) shows relative FWHM (Δσ/σ) vs. temperature for SC, PC, and HOC. SC's width drops smoothly from ~100% to ~50% through ~150 K, tracking its already-established smooth, near-linear peak-position shift. PC and HOC instead show a sharp, transient spike in width right at ~150-155 K (PC to ~260%, HOC to ~128%) before relaxing — the same temperature window and the same phase ranking (PC > HOC > SC in anomaly magnitude) as the already-established curvature/offset seen in their peak-position traces.

Read together, this is a second, independent diffraction observable (peak breadth, not peak position) that reproduces the same three-way phase ordering already seen in position-shift curvature and in SEM domain coarseness (SC coarsest/smoothest, PC finest/most anomalous, HOC intermediate). That convergence across two XRD-derived metrics strengthens the case that something distinguishes PC/HOC from SC specifically near 150 K — consistent with a genuine, phase-linked structural event (transition or strain/domain reorganization) rather than a fitting artifact confined to one peak metric.

It does not resolve the standing confound: this is still just three phase categories, still a correlation not a dose-response, and — because both the position and width data come from the same in-situ XRD dataset — it adds no independent cross-technique evidence tying the anomaly specifically to domain size rather than to phase identity/composition/crystal structure more broadly. Whether domain size (vs. some other phase-specific property) drives the width spike remains open, as does the physical mechanism (strain, domain coexistence, microtwinning) behind the broadening itself.

This settled result justifies only a cross-technique correspondence, not a causal or dose-response claim linking domain size to lattice-expansion behavior.

What it supports: across the three phases examined, coarser/more uniform domains (SC) co-occur with smooth, near-linear 2theta peak shifts with temperature, while finer or less uniform domains (PC, HOC) co-occur with curved, offset peak-shift traces. This is a legitimate, reproducible pattern-level pairing between a static real-space measurement (SEM domain size) and a dynamic diffraction measurement (XRD peak position vs. temperature), and it is reasonable to describe domain-scale/uniformity as "associated with" the smoothness or non-linearity of lattice expansion in this phase set.

What it leaves open:
1. Sample size — only three phases are compared, so this is three data points showing a consistent pattern, not a dose-response curve; no functional relationship between domain size and expansion linearity can be inferred, and intermediate domain sizes could behave in an unpredicted way.
2. Measurement type mismatch — domain size and expansion smoothness are fundamentally different kinds of quantities (static vs. dynamic, real-space vs. reciprocal-space), so their correspondence is observational pairing, not a measured causal effect of one on the other.
3. Confounding by phase identity — PC, HOC, and SC differ in composition and crystal structure as well as domain size, so domain size cannot be isolated as the driver of the curvature/offset behavior; phase-intrinsic properties remain an equally plausible explanation.

In short: the result supports an associative, three-phase correspondence between domain scale and expansion-curve smoothness, but does not establish causation, generality beyond these three phases, or that domain size (rather than phase identity) is the operative variable.</message>

Panel F2b shows Vickers hardness vs. AlN wt% for ZrB2–30 vol% SiC composites, comparing two sintering routes: HP (hot-pressed, red circles) and PS (pressureless-sintered, blue diamonds). Reading the HP curve confirms the stated values: ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt%, rising to ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then settling to ~19.4 GPa at 3 wt% and ~19.2 GPa at 5 wt%, with visible error bars at each point.

What this supports: for the hot-pressed process, adding AlN produces a modest hardness increase relative to the AlN-free baseline, with the largest gain at 1 wt% AlN and a slight decline (but still above baseline) at 3 and 5 wt%. So the measurement supports a claim that AlN addition, under hot pressing, is associated with somewhat higher hardness than no AlN, non-monotonically, peaking near 1 wt%.

What it leaves open: this is a single hardness curve from one figure — it does not establish causation (only correlation with AlN content across sintering runs), nor does it reveal the mechanism (e.g., grain refinement, porosity reduction, SiC/AlN interaction). The error bars appear comparable in scale to the differences among the 1, 3, and 5 wt% points, so whether those three HP values are statistically distinguishable from each other is uncertain from the plot alone. The panel also contrasts sharply with the PS (pressureless) curve, which decreases with AlN — so any generalization about "PRP/value" of AlN addition must be conditioned on sintering route (HP vs. PS) and cannot be extended to pressureless sintering, where the effect is opposite. No information on statistical significance, sample size, or microstructural cause is given in this panel.

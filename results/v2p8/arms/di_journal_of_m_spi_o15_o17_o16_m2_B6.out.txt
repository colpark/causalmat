The two debris panels (F11a: stir casting, 20-40 um flat flakes; F11b: FSP, <5 um fine particles with loose agglomerates) are a second, independent SEM observation — wear debris morphology rather than worn-surface groove/crater morphology. Read together with the established surface finding (both surfaces abrade via parallel grooves; the FA-containing/FSP surface additionally shows shallower grooves with fine debris, no craters), the combination strengthens the case for the proposed pathway in a way neither alone does: it shows the "fine debris" signature is not confined to one image's impression of the worn surface but reappears as a distinct, size-differentiated class of collected wear particles (fine, agglomerated, sub-5 um) specifically on the FSP/FA side, while the non-FA (stir-cast) side sheds coarser flake-like debris (20-40 um) consistent with more severe abrasive cutting/delamination. This convergence across two separate SEM observations (surface texture and debris) supports that the FA-associated wear process is genuinely milder/finer-scale, not an artifact of a single micrograph, reinforcing (without newly proving) the general "FA moderates abrasive severity" pathway.

What it still leaves open: the debris images carry no compositional analysis, so they cannot establish whether the fine b-debris is fragmented FA versus fine matrix debris, nor whether the coarse a-flakes are matrix delamination versus something else — the "FA fragments" account of the mechanism remains unconfirmed. Nor does size/morphology distinguish the two proposed sub-mechanisms (FA as a penetration-blocking barrier vs. fine debris acting as three-body rolling agent) — fine agglomerated debris is compatible with either. The observations remain qualitative, single-image impressions, not quantified size distributions or counts.

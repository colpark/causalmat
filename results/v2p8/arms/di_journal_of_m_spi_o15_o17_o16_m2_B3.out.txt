The debris-morphology data (flat flakes 20-40 um for surface a/stir-cast; fine <5 um particles with loose agglomerates for surface b/FSP+FA) is visually consistent with the panels: F11a shows blocky, plate-like fragments matching "flat flakes," while F11b shows a dense field of small granular clusters matching "fine particles with loose agglomerates."

Combined with the earlier settled result (both surfaces show parallel-groove abrasion, no large craters; surface b additionally shows shallower grooves with fine debris), this new debris-size measurement does one thing neither result does alone: it partially discriminates between the two abrasion sub-mechanisms the earlier qualification left open. A pure "FA blocks asperity penetration" (barrier) picture predicts shallower cutting but says nothing about debris size; it wouldn't obviously produce comminuted <5 um agglomerated debris. The observed fine, agglomerated debris on surface b is more directly consistent with the "debris rolling as a third body" sub-mechanism — fine particles being repeatedly crushed/rolled between surfaces rather than large flakes being sheared off — whereas surface a's coarser 20-40 um flakes match straightforward cutting/delamination without a moderating third-body effect. So the combination leans the interpretation toward FA moderating wear via fine-debris/third-body rolling rather than (or in addition to) simple barrier blocking.

What it still leaves open: this is still qualitative SEM comparison from single images, not statistically compared debris populations; the composition of the fine debris (FA fragments vs. matrix wear) remains unconfirmed, so attribution specifically to FA is inferential; the barrier mechanism isn't ruled out, only rendered less parsimonious; and localized adhesive features elsewhere on the surfaces remain unsurveyed.

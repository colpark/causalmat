The established result justifies only a co-occurrence claim: at the start of cycling, the as-prepared KMnHCF cathode possesses a well-defined, single-phase monoclinic P21/n open framework (N-coordinated Mn, C-coordinated Fe), and separately, over the course of cycling, the bulk Zn content in the cathode rises from zero to roughly 47-50 at% and then plateaus. Together these support the general picture that a structurally open host framework is present in the electrode and that, over cycling, a large and roughly stable fraction of the cathode's metal content comes to be Zn -- consistent with, but not proof of, Zn2+ being the dominant inserted cation.

What this leaves open is substantial. Because the phase identification is a single as-prepared snapshot, there is no evidence about whether that same P21/n framework persists, is retained, or has degraded by the 20th cycle or later, when the Zn plateau is observed -- so the result cannot show the host structure present at the outset is the same one hosting Zn at the plateau. Because the Zn measurement is a bulk ICP-AES at% figure, it says nothing about location: it cannot distinguish Zn residing within framework lattice sites from Zn elsewhere in the electrode (e.g., surface deposits, side phases, or other non-framework locations). The two measurements are also of fundamentally different kinds (a static structural match versus a cycle-indexed bulk compositional trend), so their juxtaposition supports co-occurrence in the same material system, not a mechanistic or site-specific link. Without post-cycling structural or site-resolved (e.g., diffraction, spectroscopic, or occupancy) data, the ~47 at% plateau cannot be attributed to saturation of framework sites specifically as opposed to some other compositional or electrode-level limit.</message>

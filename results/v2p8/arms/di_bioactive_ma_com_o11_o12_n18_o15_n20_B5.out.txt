The photographs (F6b tube cross-sections, F6c excised thrombus) add a macroscopic, whole-device readout to the microscopic platelet finding. F6b shows 316L SS and PPAmF lumens completely filled with dark thrombus, BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF only partly filled, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF lumen and foil left largely clean; F6c shows the same ordering in the retrieved thrombus itself (full dark clot on 316L SS/PPAmF, partial red patches with clean/white regions on the three modified surfaces, least clot on BVLD/NO-PPAmF).

Combined with the earlier SEM+BIO result, this shows that the surface with the fewest, least-spread (least activated) adhered platelets is also the surface with the least visible thrombus and the most patent lumen after blood circulation — i.e., the platelet-level suppression seen microscopically co-occurs with a functional, device-scale reduction in occlusive thrombus formation. Neither measurement alone supports this: the SEM/BIO pair says nothing about whether reduced platelet adhesion actually translates into less gross thrombus or better lumen patency, and the photographs alone give no cellular explanation (they could reflect coagulation-factor effects unrelated to platelet number/shape) and no quantitative platelet data.

This still leaves open: no measured thrombus area/mass or statistics accompany the photographs, so the apparent five-surface ordering (especially distinguishing BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF from one another) is impressionistic, not tested. The link is also only correlational across surfaces — the photographs cannot establish that reduced platelet adhesion/activation is the mechanism causing less thrombus, only that the two phenomena track together.</message>

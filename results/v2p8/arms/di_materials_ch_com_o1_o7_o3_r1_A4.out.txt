Panel F2b (Vickers hardness vs. AlN additive in ZrB2–30 vol% SiC composites, comparing HP and PS sintering) confirms the HP curve visually: hardness rises from ~18 GPa at 0 wt% AlN to a peak of ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then eases to ~19.4 GPa at 3 wt% and ~19.2 GPa at 5 wt%, all with visible error bars overlapping between the 1/3/5 points.

What this supports: for hot-pressed (HP) material, a small AlN addition (1 wt%) produces a measurable hardness increase over the AlN-free baseline, and hardness stays elevated (above baseline) through 5 wt% rather than continuing to climb — i.e., the benefit of AlN addition on HP hardness is front-loaded at low content and plateaus/slightly declines thereafter. The figure also shows a contrasting PS (pressureless-sintered) curve that declines monotonically with AlN content, so the effect of AlN on hardness is process-dependent (sintering route matters), not a universal AlN effect.

What it leaves open: the panel gives no statistical test — the error bars for 1, 3, and 5 wt% appear to overlap, so whether 1 wt% is truly a distinguishable optimum versus 3 or 5 wt%, or whether all three are statistically indistinguishable from each other (just clearly above 0 wt%), cannot be determined from the image alone. The image also does not establish causation/mechanism (why AlN raises HP hardness) or say anything about composition beyond ZrB2–30 vol% SiC, other property tradeoffs, or generalization to other AlN ranges/processes.

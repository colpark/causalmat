The established result supports only a co-occurrence claim: the as-prepared KMnHCF is confirmed by XRD to be a single, well-matched monoclinic P21/n open framework (N-coordinated Mn, C-coordinated Fe) at the outset, and separately, bulk ICP-AES shows Zn content in the cathode rising from zero to roughly 50 at% by cycle 20 and settling near 47 at%. Together these two facts justify saying that a well-defined open host framework is present initially and that, over cycling, the cathode comes to contain a large, roughly stable share of Zn among its metals -- a picture consistent with Zn2+ becoming the dominant inserted cation.

What this leaves open is substantial. The XRD result is a single as-prepared snapshot, so nothing here shows the framework persists unchanged, or in any particular state, at cycle 20 or later when the Zn plateau is reached. The ICP-AES value is a bulk elemental at% measurement, so it reports total Zn in the electrode without indicating where that Zn physically resides -- it could be in framework sites, in other phases, or elsewhere in the electrode, and this result cannot distinguish among those. Because the two measurements are of different kinds (a static structural match at one time point versus a cycle-indexed bulk composition), their combination supports co-occurrence of an open framework and rising Zn content in the same material, not a mechanistic or structural link between the framework and Zn uptake. In particular, no site-resolved or post-cycling structural data exist, so the ~47 at% plateau cannot be attributed to saturation of framework sites specifically, as opposed to some other compositional or electrode-level limit.</message>

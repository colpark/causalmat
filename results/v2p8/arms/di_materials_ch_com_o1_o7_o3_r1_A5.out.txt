The F2b panel (Vickers hardness vs. AlN wt% in ZrB2–30 vol% SiC composites, sintered via different processes) confirms the stated HP series: ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt% AlN, rising to ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then declining to ~19.4 GPa at 3 wt% and ~19.2 GPa at 5 wt%, with visible error bars at each point.

What this supports: for the hot-pressed (HP) processing route, adding a small amount of AlN (1 wt%) produces a measurable jump in Vickers hardness relative to the AlN-free baseline, and hardness remains elevated above the 0 wt% baseline (though slightly reduced from the 1 wt% peak) at higher AlN loadings of 3 and 5 wt%. This is process-specific: the panel also shows a second series (PS, likely pressureless-sintered), which behaves oppositely — hardness decreases monotonically with AlN addition — meaning the beneficial effect of AlN on hardness is not universal across processing routes but is tied specifically to the HP condition.

What this leaves open: the panel alone does not identify the causal mechanism (e.g., grain refinement, densification, phase changes) behind the HP trend, nor does it establish statistical significance of the differences given the error bars shown (the 1, 3, and 5 wt% points appear close to each other within uncertainty). It also does not indicate sample size, test load, or how "PRP" (processing route/pressure) specifically modulates this AlN effect beyond the HP vs. PS contrast shown. No composition beyond 5 wt% AlN or intermediate points between 0 and 1 wt% is measured, so the location and sharpness of the apparent peak near 1 wt% is not resolved.

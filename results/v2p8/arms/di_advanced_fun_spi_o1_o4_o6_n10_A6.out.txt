Panel F2d (10.1002/adfm.202008088) plots ΔFWHM/FWHM(700K) (%) vs temperature for XRD peaks (004, 242, 224, 400) in single-crystal (SC), polycrystalline (PC), and "HOC" perovskite samples. The data show a sharp, coincident anomaly for all three sample forms in the same narrow temperature window (~145–155 K): SC peak widths (004, 400) drop abruptly from ~100% to ~50%; PC's 242 peak spikes to ~260% while other PC peaks stay near baseline; HOC shows a smaller spike to ~128% around 150–155 K before relaxing. Outside this window, widths vary smoothly.

What this supports: the coincidence of an abrupt width anomaly at essentially the same temperature (~150 K) across SC, PC, and HOC samples is consistent with a structural/lattice transition occurring near 150 K in this material, since peak-width (strain/mosaicity/domain) behavior changes qualitatively right at that temperature in every sample form.

What it leaves open: the measurement is peak-width systematics, not a direct phase or symmetry determination — it does not by itself identify which phase transition (e.g., octahedral tilting, cation ordering, twinning) is occurring, nor which crystallographic direction/plane drives it. The opposite sign of the anomaly (width narrowing in SC vs. sharpening/spiking in PC and HOC) is not reconciled by this panel alone — it's unclear whether SC and PC/HOC are recording the same microscopic transition via different symptoms (e.g., domain formation/twinning increasing apparent width in polycrystalline forms while ordering narrows it in the single crystal) or genuinely different phenomena. No peak-position/2θ shift or space-group data is shown here to confirm a symmetry change.

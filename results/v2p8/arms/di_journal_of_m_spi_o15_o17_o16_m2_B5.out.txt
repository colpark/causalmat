The two crops match the debris description: F11a (labeled "a"/stir casting) shows coarse, flat, faceted flakes roughly 20-40 um across with sharp edges; F11b (labeled "b"/FSP) shows a field of much finer, loosely clustered granular particles under ~5 um with visible agglomeration, no large flakes.

What the combination settles: Read together with the earlier groove/crater result, this debris-size contrast gives an independent morphological line of evidence pointing the same direction as the groove observation. Large flat flakes on surface a are consistent with severe, deep abrasive cutting removing chip-like material, while fine agglomerated particles on surface b are consistent with the shallower grooves already noted on the FA-containing surface. So the pairing strengthens the case for "FA moderates abrasive severity" over a simple restatement of the groove data alone — it shows the debris morphology tracks the groove-depth morphology across two independent SEM features, not just one, which the groove result by itself could not claim. It also nudges toward (without confirming) the fine-debris/three-body sub-mechanism, since loose, small agglomerated particles are physically the kind of debris that could roll between surfaces, rather than positively supporting a barrier/blocking mechanism.

What it leaves open: the debris composition is still not analyzed, so origin from fractured FA versus matrix wear remains unconfirmed; sizes are visual estimates from single fields of view, not counted/measured distributions; and the fine agglomerated debris is only consistent with, not proof of, active three-body rolling — it does not rule out the FA-blocking mechanism operating concurrently, nor does it survey the full worn surface for localized adhesive features.

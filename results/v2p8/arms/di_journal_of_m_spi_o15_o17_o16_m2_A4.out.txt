What this settles: the measurement supports that wear-debris morphology differs systematically between the stir-cast and FSP composites — stir casting yields coarse, flat flake-type debris while FSP yields fine, agglomerated particulate debris. This morphological contrast is consistent with a shift in the dominant wear pathway between the two processing routes (e.g., large plate-like flakes point toward a delamination/adhesive-type wear process, while fine agglomerated particles point toward a milder abrasive or oxidative/fragmentation-type process). It supports that FSP changes wear behavior relative to stir casting.

What it leaves open: the image alone cannot confirm which named wear mechanism (delamination, abrasive, adhesive, oxidative) is actually operating — that requires chemical/compositional data (e.g., EDS to check for oxide content) which is not shown here. It also does not establish the causal link to a specific microstructural cause (grain refinement, reinforcement distribution, hardness) — it only shows a correlation between processing route and debris morphology. No quantitative particle-size statistics, wear test conditions (load, sliding distance, friction coefficient), or replicate images are shown, so the generality and precision of the "20–40 µm" vs "<5 µm" figures cannot be independently verified from the panels alone.

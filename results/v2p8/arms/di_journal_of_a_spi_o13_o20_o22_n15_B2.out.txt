The panel (F7e) shows load-dependent power for the f-P0.10 fiber PENG (220 μm film) versus a cast film: power peaks at 136.9 μW near 10 MΩ for the fiber film versus 21.025 μW for the cast film at the same load — roughly a 6.5x gap driven by film architecture (fiber/electrospun vs cast), tested across external resistance, not across PZT wt%.

Read together with the earlier result, the combination supports a modest, negative-leaning conclusion: it shows that large differences in absolute output (power, not just voltage) can arise from a factor — fiber vs cast processing — that is independent of PZT loading and of the beta-phase trend already characterized. This reinforces the earlier caveat that PZT wt%/beta-phase content is at most one contributor among several to output magnitude, since here a purely morphological change (no stated wt% variation) produces an output swing (6.5x in power) comparable to or larger than what was attributed to the wt%-beta-phase trend. So the combination strengthens the case that beta-phase enhancement cannot be treated as the dominant or sole driver of piezoelectric output.

What remains open: the panel reports no beta-phase (FTIR) content for either the fiber or cast sample, so it cannot say whether the fiber film's power advantage is beta-phase-mediated, coincidental, or purely structural (e.g., surface area, poling, geometry). It also doesn't state what PZT wt% was used for this comparison, so it cannot be linked to the 0-10 wt% series at all. Finally, power (a function of both voltage and current/resistance) is not directly comparable to the voltage-only measure in the earlier result, so no quantitative bridge between the two trends is possible.

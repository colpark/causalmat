The two panels (F4a: [11-20] loading, side surfaces (1-100)/(0001); F4b: [01-10] loading, side surfaces (-2110)/(0001)) support the stated measurement well. In both orientations, RT and 200°C micrographs are labeled with pyramidal/basal-type twin index pairs (e.g., {01-12}/{10-12} families), while 300°C and 400°C micrographs carry {10-10}/{1-100} prismatic trace labels instead. Deformation-band labels appear at every temperature in both panels (RT through 400°C, on both side surfaces), matching the claim that bands persist regardless of temperature. This justifies the conclusion that macroscopic surface trace analysis, across two independent loading orientations, shows a temperature-driven shift from twin-dominated to prismatic-slip-dominated surface traces somewhere between 200°C and 300°C, superimposed on a temperature-independent deformation-band signature.

What it leaves open: the 300°C columns in both panels show both twin-type and prismatic-type index labels together, so the changeover looks gradual/mixed rather than a sharp switch exactly at 300°C — the images do not pin down a precise transition temperature. The evidence is limited to macroscopic optical/SEM-scale surface trace geometry at only two crystallographic loading directions and 5% strain; it does not by itself confirm the identified traces via independent methods (e.g., TEM, EBSD), does not quantify twin/slip volume fractions or dislocation activity, and says nothing about the mechanistic cause of the transition (e.g., CRSS crossover) or about behavior at strains other than 5%. So behavior_class can be supported as "twin-to-prismatic-slip transition with persistent banding" but the exact transition onset, underlying mechanism, and generality beyond these two orientations/this strain remain unsettled by this figure alone.

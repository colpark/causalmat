Panel F7e (verified by reading the image) shows: fiber-film power rises from ~57 µW at 10^6 Ω to a peak of 136.9 µW at 10^7 Ω (10 MΩ), then falls; cast-film power stays far lower throughout, peaking at only 21.025 µW near the same load decade. Both curves show the same impedance-matched, rise-then-fall shape, and the fiber film exceeds the cast film at essentially every load point (~6.5x at the peaks).

Read alone, this measurement only shows that fiber morphology gives a much larger absolute power output than cast morphology at matched external resistance — it says nothing about what mediates that gap.

Read alone, the earlier FTIR/voltage result only shows that beta-phase content cannot be the operative driver of voltage gains beyond 4 wt% PZT loading, since beta content eases back after 4 wt% while voltage keeps climbing to 10 wt% — but that result gives no candidate for what does drive the continued rise, and it says nothing about fiber-vs-cast processing.

Combined: since the fiber sample here is a high-loading formulation (f-P0.10) in the same regime where beta-phase content was already shown to have plateaued/declined, the large fiber-over-cast power advantage cannot be attributed to a beta-phase pathway that was independently shown to be saturating in that same loading range. The pairing therefore supports morphology (fiber vs. cast processing) as a plausible non-beta-phase contributor to service_capability at higher loading — something neither result shows alone.

What remains open: no beta-phase (FTIR) measurement exists for these specific fiber and cast samples, so a direct causal link from morphology to output is not established, only that beta content is an insufficient sole explanation. The exact PZT wt% used in F7e relative to the earlier wt% sweep is also not confirmed to be identical, so the two datasets are not strictly matched.

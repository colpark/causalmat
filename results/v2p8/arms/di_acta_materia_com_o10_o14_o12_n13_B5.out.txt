The new macroscopic OM series (F4a/F4b) shows, at each temperature, which surface-trace type is present alongside the labeled "deformation band" feature: {01-12}/{10-12} twin traces dominate at RT (and 200 C), giving way to {10-10}/{1-100} prismatic-slip traces by 400 C (300 C is transitional, with both trace types labeled in the same panel), while a "deformation band" label appears in every temperature panel, including 400 C where no twin trace remains at all.

Read against the earlier settled result — that straight, {10-12}/{01-12}-traced bands correspond in EBSD to a single uniform colour (twin-like, non-graded orientation) while wavy bands correspond to continuously shading (kink-type) orientation — the combination lets you infer which population the persisting bands belong to. Because deformation bands are still labeled at 400 C, where twin traces have disappeared entirely and only prismatic-slip traces remain, those high-temperature bands cannot be the twin/uniform-orientation type; by elimination (given only two classes were established) they must belong to the wavy/kink, continuously-shaded orientation population. So the combination supports that band formation in this material is mechanistically decoupled from twinning: it persists across the whole RT-400 C range and continues after twinning is replaced by prismatic slip, whereas twinning itself is temperature-limited.

This leaves open: (1) the OM-trace/EBSD-shading correlation was established only at two RT specimen orientations, never verified by EBSD at 300-400 C, so calling the high-T bands "kink-type" is an inference, not a direct measurement; (2) the mixed-trace 300 C panel leaves ambiguous whether those bands are still partly twin-influenced; (3) no misorientation magnitude or causal mechanism is given for why bands persist while twinning drops out.

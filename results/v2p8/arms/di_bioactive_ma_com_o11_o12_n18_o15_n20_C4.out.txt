The established result supports a specific, integrated claim about BVLD/NO-PPAmF's service capability as a platelet-resistant (hemocompatible) surface: among the five surfaces tested, BVLD/NO-PPAmF uniquely combines the lowest platelet count (~5x1e2 cells/mm2) with a morphology dominated by round, unspread (non-activated) platelets. This joint pattern — fewer platelets AND less-activated platelets present specifically on that surface — is stronger evidence for a genuine anti-thrombogenic service capability than either measurement alone would give: the BIO count by itself would leave open whether the few adhered platelets were nonetheless activated/spread, and the SEM images by itself would leave open whether the qualitative morphology corresponded to a real reduction in numbers. Together they justify saying BVLD/NO-PPAmF suppresses both platelet recruitment and platelet activation relative to 316L SS, PPAmF, and the single-coating BVLD-PPAmF/NO-PPAmF surfaces.

What it leaves open: (1) Statistical significance — no error bars or tests accompany either measurement, so the ordering across the five surfaces (5 < 11 < 15.5 < 32 < 41, in units of 1e2 cells/mm2) is descriptive only, not confirmed as reliably different. (2) The SEM "few round platelets" and the "~5x1e2 cells/mm2" figure were not tied together numerically from the same fields, so the correspondence is inferred, not directly measured. (3) No mechanistic explanation (surface chemistry, biochemistry) for why the combined coating suppresses both adhesion and activation is established — only that the two effects co-occur on that surface. So the service-capability claim is supported as a descriptive, co-occurring effect, not as a statistically validated or mechanistically explained one.

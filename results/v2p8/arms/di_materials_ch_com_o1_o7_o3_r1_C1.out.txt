The settled result supports the conclusion that the HP composites, particularly the 1 and 5 wt% AlN compositions with near-100% relative density, are genuinely and fully dense rather than only appearing so by a single measurement artifact. The density figure (bulk average) is corroborated by independent, direct microstructural evidence (SEM): the fracture surfaces show no open pores and large flat transgranular fracture facets, indicating fracture proceeds through grains rather than around residual porosity. So the two lines of evidence jointly justify treating the near-100% relative density as reflecting a real, pore-free, well-consolidated microstructure with transgranular fracture behavior.

What this leaves open: density alone cannot distinguish open from closed residual porosity or say how any pores are distributed through the bulk, since it is a single averaged number; SEM alone offers only a qualitative, local view of one fracture surface and cannot itself yield a quantitative density value. Because the SEM evidence is not reported in a wt%-resolved, quantitative form, the pairing does not establish a quantitative trend linking specific pore appearance to the specific density values (~97.5%, ~99.3%, ~100%) across the 0/1/3/5 wt% AlN series — only qualitative consistency is confirmed. Finally, neither observation speaks to mechanism: why AlN addition raises relative density (e.g., acting as a sintering aid or through grain-boundary effects) remains unestablished; the evidence confirms the resulting dense state and fracture mode, not the causal process producing it.</message>

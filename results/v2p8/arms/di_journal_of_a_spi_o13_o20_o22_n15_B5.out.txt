The F7e panel confirms the caption: at f-P0.10, 220 um thickness, power vs. external load resistance is non-monotonic, peaking at 136.9 uW near 10 MOhm for the fiber film versus a much lower peak of 21.025 uW (also near 10 MOhm) for the cast film — roughly a 6.5x advantage for the fiber architecture, holding composition fixed.

Read together with the earlier finding, the combination supports a claim the earlier result alone could not make: that beta-phase content is at most a partial, low-loading-regime contributor to device output, because a second, independent lever — film morphology (fiber vs. cast), tested here at one fixed PZT loading — produces a large output swing on its own, with no wt%-driven beta-phase change invoked at all. This corroborates the earlier inference that something besides beta-phase fraction must be driving output gains outside the 0-4 wt% window, since morphology is shown here to be a substantial, separately-acting variable. It also adds that usable power (not just voltage) depends critically on load-resistance matching, a third independent axis neither result alone addressed.

What remains open: this panel gives no FTIR/beta-phase data for the fiber-vs-cast comparison, so it cannot say whether the fiber film's power advantage correlates with, is independent of, or actually differs in beta-phase content from the cast film — the fiber/cast contrast could itself be confounded by processing-induced beta-phase differences. It is also unclear whether f-P0.10 corresponds to a point on the same 0-10 wt% composition series used earlier, or is a separate sample, and the mechanism for the voltage rise beyond 4 wt% is still unidentified.

The established result justifies a limited but concrete conclusion: across the four Ag:Pd-ZIS compositions measured in both observations, the ranking by steady photocurrent (a proxy for charge-carrier separation) matches the ranking by H2 evolution rate — Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS > Pd-ZIS > Ag-ZIS > ZIS in both. This supports the idea that whatever composition change increases photocurrent in this series is associated with, and consistent with, better H2 evolution performance — i.e., the two measures agree on which compositions perform better, giving convergent (rank-level) evidence for the "better charge separation → better H2 evolution" narrative for these four samples.

It leaves open several things, and none of these can be claimed:
1. No quantitative relationship: photocurrent and H2 rate are different physical quantities in different units/setups, so nothing about magnitude, proportionality, or a dose-response curve between them is established — only that orderings coincide.
2. No causal claim: because composition (Ag:Pd ratio) was varied simultaneously with both measurements, the matching order cannot distinguish photocurrent as a cause of higher H2 evolution from composition independently driving both properties. Causation from photocurrent to H2 evolution is not established.
3. Incomplete coverage: two compositions (Ag0.5Pd0.5-ZIS at 57.3 umol/h and Ag0.75Pd0.25-ZIS at 43.5 umol/h) were measured for H2 evolution but not photocurrent, so the ordering claim cannot be extended across the full composition series — only the four overlapping points are supported.
4. Small n: with only four matched points, this is sufficient to show a consistent rank order but not sufficient to establish the strength, robustness, or statistical reliability of any relationship.

In short: the result supports rank-order agreement between photocurrent and H2 evolution for four specific samples, nothing more — not magnitude, not mechanism/causation, and not generalization to the full Ag:Pd series.

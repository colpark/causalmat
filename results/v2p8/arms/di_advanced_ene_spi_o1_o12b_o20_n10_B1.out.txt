Panels F5d–f (K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6 → K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6 → ZnMnFe(CN)6) show a Zn-content-indexed structural series: as Zn stoichiometry rises, the Mn polyhedron distorts from MnN6-type octahedral coordination toward MnN3 and then MnN4, while grey ZnN4 tetrahedra appear and link to the FeC6 octahedra of the same 3D N/C-coordinated network, with a phase transition accompanying the highest-Zn structure.

Read together with the earlier result, this settles something the two could not settle alone: it gives a site-resolved structural correlate for where Zn goes as its bulk content rises. Observation A (as-prepared monoclonic P21/n framework with N-coordinated Mn, C-coordinated Fe) and Observation B (bulk Zn at% climbing toward ~47%) only supported co-occurrence, not mechanism, because neither showed Zn occupying framework sites. This measurement closes part of that gap: it demonstrates that as Zn content increases within this same class of N-coordinated-Mn/C-coordinated-Fe framework, Zn structurally enters as ZnN4 tetrahedra bonded into the Mn/Fe lattice, and the Mn coordination itself reorganizes to accommodate it — a plausible structural pathway by which bulk Zn uptake could correspond to Zn occupying, and altering, framework sites rather than residing elsewhere in the electrode.

What remains open: the series is indexed by nominal Zn stoichiometry in synthesized compositions, not explicitly tied to cycle number, so it is not established that this maps onto the actual electrode at cycle 20 or that ~47 at% corresponds to a specific point on this series. The caption also states a phase transition accompanies the higher-Zn structures, so the original P21/n phase identified at t=0 is not shown to persist once substantial Zn enters — the "same host" claim remains unverified at high Zn content.

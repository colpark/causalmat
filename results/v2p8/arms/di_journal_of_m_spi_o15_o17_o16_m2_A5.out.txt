Both panels are SEM micrographs of worn debris at identical magnification (750x, 20 µm scale bar), captioned only "stir casting" (a) and "FSP" (b), with no EDS, size-distribution, or quantitative measurement overlaid.

Panel a shows coarse, angular, plate-like flakes with sharp edges, several tens of microns across (visibly larger than the 20 µm scale bar in places), mixed with some finer fragments. Panel b shows a field dominated by fine, rounded/agglomerated particles well under the scale-bar length, with only one or two larger flake fragments at the edge.

What this supports: the visual morphology of wear debris differs qualitatively between the stir-cast and FSP samples — coarser flake-like debris in (a) versus finer, agglomerated particulate debris in (b). This is consistent with the stated observation (flat flakes ~20-40 µm vs fine particles <5 µm with agglomerates), and qualitatively consistent with a shift toward a different dominant wear mode (e.g., delamination/adhesive flaking in the stir-cast sample versus finer abrasive/oxidative wear in the FSP sample) — but the images alone only show morphology, not a measured wear mechanism.

What it leaves open: there is no quantitative particle-size distribution, no EDS/composition data to confirm oxide content in the fine debris, no wear-rate or coefficient-of-friction data linking morphology to performance, and no indication of sampling location, repeat measurements, or statistical spread — so a single SEM field per condition cannot establish the wear mechanism or MEC/pathway causally, only that debris appearance differs between the two processing routes.

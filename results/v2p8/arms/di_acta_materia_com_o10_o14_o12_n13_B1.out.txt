The macroscopic OM series (F4a for [11-20], F4b for [01-10]) shows that the matrix deformation trace family changes with temperature — {01-12}/{10-12} twin traces at RT and 200°C give way entirely to {10-10}/{1-100} prismatic slip traces at 300-400°C — while a "deformation band" label persists in every panel at every temperature, in both specimen orientations.

Read against the earlier OM+EBSD result (which showed, for these same two orientations, that the straight {10-12}/{01-12}-trace bands are twin-like with uniform EBSD colour, while the wavy, asymmetrically-contrasted bands carry continuously shading EBSD colour, i.e., are kink-band-like), the combination supports one further conclusion: the wavy/graded-orientation band population is not merely a byproduct of twinning. Because deformation bands are still present at 300-400°C, where twin traces have disappeared and only prismatic slip traces remain, band formation (behavior_class) survives the loss of the twinning mechanism itself — decoupling the bands from twinning as a necessary carrier.

What this leaves open: this figure is macroscopic OM only, with no EBSD at 300-400°C, so it cannot confirm that the "deformation band" labelled at high temperature still shows the same wavy morphology, strong/faint (0001)-side contrast asymmetry, or continuously-shading EBSD colour that defined the band class at RT — it is only labelled by the same name, not re-verified by the same two-technique signature. It also still covers only the same two specimen orientations as the earlier result, so generality across orientations remains untested, and no misorientation magnitude or mechanistic cause is established at any temperature.

The settled result supports that both composite surfaces wear predominantly by an abrasive mechanism (parallel, directional grooves with no large craters on either surface), and that the FA-containing surface additionally shows a milder version of this abrasive wear — shallower grooves plus fine debris, still without large craters. Together these two observations are consistent with the proposed pathway that dispersed FA moderates the severity of abrasive cutting, e.g., by acting as a barrier or by generating fine debris that alters the contact, rather than the composite shifting to an adhesive wear mode.

What this leaves open: the "shallower" grooves and "fine"/"numerous" debris are qualitative visual impressions from single SEM images, not quantified measurements, so no calibrated comparison of groove depth or debris amount across the two surfaces is established. The debris was not compositionally analyzed, so it cannot be confirmed to originate from fractured FA rather than from matrix wear itself. Only two localized features were examined (surface-b morphology and groove orientation on both surfaces); the full worn surface was not surveyed, so the result does not rule out localized adhesive wear features elsewhere. Finally, the observations cannot distinguish between the two candidate sub-mechanisms — FA blocking asperity penetration versus FA-derived debris acting as a three-body rolling agent that reduces cutting severity — since both are equally consistent with the shallower-grooves-plus-fine-debris pattern. So the result supports an abrasion-moderation pathway attributable to FA in a general, qualitative sense, but not a quantified severity difference, a confirmed debris source, a full-surface wear-mode characterization, or a specific mechanistic pathway among the two proposed.</message>

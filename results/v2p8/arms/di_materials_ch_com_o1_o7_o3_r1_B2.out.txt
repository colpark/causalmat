Panel F2b (verified by direct read) confirms the stated HP hardness trend: ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt% AlN, rising to ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then settling to ~19.4 and ~19.2 GPa at 3 and 5 wt%, with PS samples running well below HP throughout and declining monotonically (~12 to ~6.9 GPa).

Combined with the earlier result, this justifies one further conclusion: because the density measurement and SEM fracture image already corroborated that the HP composites — specifically at 1 and 5 wt% AlN — are genuinely pore-free and near-100% dense (not merely by bulk-average number but by observed transgranular fracture with no open porosity), the hardness values recorded for those two compositions can be read as intrinsic material hardness rather than an artifact of incomplete densification. Porosity is a well-known confound that suppresses measured hardness, so ruling out significant residual porosity at 1 and 5 wt% means the plateau/slight decline in HP hardness from ~20.3 to ~19.2 GPa across that range reflects a real microstructural or compositional effect (e.g., grain size, phase distribution) rather than differing pore content between those two points.

What this leaves open: the earlier corroboration singled out 1 and 5 wt% as reaching ~100% relative density, not 0 or 3 wt%. So it is not established whether the jump from 18.0 to 20.3 GPa (0→1 wt%) is a genuine AlN hardening effect or partly reflects 0 wt% being somewhat less dense than 1 wt%; density/porosity at 3 wt% is likewise unconfirmed by SEM, so its ~19.4 GPa value cannot be pinned to full density either. Additionally, since the density/SEM corroboration was specific to the HP route, nothing here explains the PS series' much lower and falling hardness — that could still be driven by residual porosity, which was never ruled out for PS.

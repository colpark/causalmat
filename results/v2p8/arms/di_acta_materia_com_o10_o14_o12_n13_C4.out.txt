This established result supports classifying the two band populations by a linked morphology–orientation behavior: straight, {10-12}/{01-12}-trace bands with lenticular OM appearance correspond to bands that EBSD shows as a single uniform orientation colour (twin-like behavior), while wavy bands with asymmetric OM contrast (strong on the (0001) side surface, faint on (1-100)/(-2110)) correspond to bands whose EBSD colour varies continuously with position (kink-like, graded-orientation behavior). This justifies treating "straight/uniform-orientation" versus "wavy/graded-orientation" as one coherent, paired behavior_class distinction rather than two independently reported observations — i.e., OM morphology can be used as a proxy indicator for the EBSD orientation-uniformity behavior of a band, and vice versa, within the cases examined.

What it leaves open: (1) the magnitude of misorientation is not established — only a qualitative uniform-vs-continuously-shading contrast, so how much orientation changes across a kink band versus a twin is unquantified; (2) generality beyond the two specimen orientations examined ([11-20] and [01-10]) is not established — the same pairing may or may not hold for other loading orientations or specimens; (3) the correspondence is an imaging-signature co-registration by band type, not a mechanistic or causal proof that the same physical process produces both the OM contrast asymmetry and the EBSD continuous shading; (4) because OM contrast (optical/diffraction) and EBSD colour (crystallographic orientation coding) are different physical measurands, their agreement in distinguishing band types does not mean they measure the same underlying variable. So the behavior_class pairing is supported as a descriptive/classificatory correspondence, not as a quantified or mechanistically explained one.</message>

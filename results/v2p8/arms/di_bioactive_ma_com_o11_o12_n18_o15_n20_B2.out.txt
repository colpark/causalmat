The two panels are macroscopic outcomes on the same five surfaces already assessed by SEM/BIO. F6b shows tube cross-sections after 2 h of blood circulation: 316L SS and PPAmF lumens are filled with dark (occlusive) thrombus, BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are only partly filled, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF is nearly clean/patent. F6c shows the excised thrombi themselves, following the same ordering — large dark clots on 316L SS and PPAmF, smaller/partial clots on BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF, and minimal clot on BVLD/NO-PPAmF, whose foil surface stays largely clean.

Read together with the earlier SEM+BIO result (BVLD/NO-PPAmF has the fewest adhered platelets and the least platelet spreading/activation of the five surfaces), the combination supports that this same cellular-level suppression of platelet adhesion/activation co-occurs with, and tracks the ranking of, a macroscopic reduction in thrombus mass and preservation of lumen patency under actual blood flow. Neither measurement alone shows this: the SEM/BIO data are static, per-platelet, and say nothing about flow-condition, whole-device thrombotic outcome; the photographs give only gross, unquantified end-state appearance and no cellular mechanism. Together they let the microscopic platelet finding be read as functionally consistent with reduced clinical-relevant thrombosis.

This leaves open: the photographs are purely qualitative (no thrombus mass/area quantification, no statistics, single 2-h time point), so the stepwise ordering across surfaces is descriptive, not tested, exactly as before. The combination is still correlational — co-occurrence across the same five surfaces, not a demonstrated causal chain from reduced platelet adhesion to reduced macroscopic thrombus. And the underlying surface-chemistry/biochemical mechanism remains unaddressed by either measurement.

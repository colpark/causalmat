Panel F2d (read) shows: SC's FWHM makes a single, modest step down (~100%→~50%) at 150 K with no spike; PC shows a sharp transient spike to ~260% around 150-155 K; HOC shows a smaller but still clear spike to ~128% in the same temperature window. Critically, the size of the width anomaly (PC > HOC > SC, or none) tracks the same domain-size ranking already established (PC finest ~0.1 um > HOC ~10-100 um > SC coarsest ~1e2-1e3 um), and the anomaly occurs at the same temperature region where Observation B already showed PC/HOC peak-position curvature/offset and SC's smooth shift.

Combined with the earlier result, this justifies a stronger version of the same conclusion: two independent XRD signatures — peak position (shift smoothness) and peak width (FWHM anomaly) — now agree in separating SC from PC/HOC at the phase transition, and the magnitude of the width spike orders with domain fineness rather than just falling into three unordered categories. That strengthens the association between finer/less-uniform domain scale and anomalous (non-monotonic, transition-localized) lattice behavior.

It does not settle causality or resolve the confounds already noted: still only three phase points, so this is a stronger correspondence, not a dose-response law; domain size remains confounded with phase identity/composition; and FWHM broadening at a transition can also arise from transient two-phase coexistence or strain distribution unrelated to grain size, so an alternative, non-domain-size explanation for the spike is not excluded. No mechanism or direction of causation is established.

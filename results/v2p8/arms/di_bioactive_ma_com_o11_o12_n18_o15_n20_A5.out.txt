The two panels (F6b, cross-sectional lumen photos; F6c, photos of the formed thrombus strip removed from each tube) jointly support the claim as written: on 316L stainless steel and on plain PPAmF, the tube lumen/foil is filled with dark red-black material consistent with dense thrombus, and the corresponding F6c strips are almost entirely dark red/maroon, i.e. heavily thrombus-covered. On BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF, F6b shows the lumen only partly obstructed - a lighter, metallic/foil-colored region is visible alongside residual red material at the rim, and F6c shows the strips are only partly dark (mixed pale and red/brown regions), consistent with "partly open." On BVLD/NO-PPAmF (the combined treatment), F6b shows the largest clean, bright metallic lumen area with only a thin red rim, and F6c shows the strip is mostly pale/white with thrombus confined to the edges - consistent with "nearly clean."

So the measurement supports a qualitative, visually-scored ordering of thrombus burden across the five surfaces (316LSS ≈ PPAmF, heaviest > BVLD-PPAmF ≈ NO-PPAmF, partial > BVLD/NO-PPAmF, least), for this single 2 h ex vivo/in vitro blood-circulation run shown in the figure.

What it leaves open: these are single representative photographs per condition, not quantified (no thrombus mass, area-percentage, or statistics, and no error bars or replicate count are visible in the panels themselves), so the measurement cannot establish statistical significance, effect size, or reproducibility of the ranking. It also cannot speak to mechanism (why BVLD/NO-PPAmF performs best), to performance beyond 2 h, or to in vivo relevance beyond what these images depict.

The settled result justifies a modest, qualitative conclusion: across the four samples measured in both observations, the composition-dependent ordering of steady photocurrent (a proxy for charge-carrier separation) coincides with the ordering of H2 evolution rate. This supports the idea that better charge-carrier separation, as indicated by higher photocurrent, is associated with better photocatalytic H2-evolution performance across the Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS > Pd-ZIS > Ag-ZIS > ZIS series — i.e., the two figures of merit are directionally consistent and mutually corroborating for these four compositions.

What it leaves open is considerable. First, because photocurrent and H2 evolution rate are different kinds of quantities from different setups, the matching rank order shows correlation in ordering only, not a quantitative, unit-comparable, or proportional relationship between them. Second, two compositions (Ag0.5Pd0.5-ZIS and Ag0.75Pd0.25-ZIS) were measured for H2 evolution but not for photocurrent, so the full Ag:Pd series is not jointly covered and the ordering claim cannot be extended past the four overlapping points. Third, four data points are enough to establish a consistent rank order but not enough to characterize a dose-response curve, an effect size, or statistical robustness. Finally, and most importantly, since composition was varied simultaneously with both measurements, this pairing cannot distinguish whether photocurrent differences causally drive H2 evolution differences versus composition independently influencing each measurement through separate mechanisms. No causal or mechanistic claim about photocurrent driving H2 evolution is supported — only an associative, order-consistent relationship among the four shared samples.

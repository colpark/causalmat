The three panels (F5d–f) are a site-resolved structural series across a Zn-content compositional ladder (K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6 → K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6 → ZnMnFe(CN)6). As Zn fraction rises, Mn's N-coordination drops (MnN6-type framework implied at low Zn → MnN3 → MnN4), and tetrahedral ZnN4 units appear and link directly to the FeC6 octahedra of the cyanide framework.

Read together with the earlier result, this settles the "where" question that neither could answer alone: the earlier work showed only that an open Mn(N)/Fe(C) framework exists at t=0 (XRD) and that bulk Zn at% climbs toward ~47–50% by cycle 20 (ICP-AES), without showing that the Zn actually enters that framework's sites. This structural series now shows, across increasing Zn content, that incoming Zn occupies framework tetrahedral N-sites (ZnN4) that bond into the existing Fe-C6 octahedral network, while displacing/distorting the original MnN6 coordination toward MnN3/MnN4. So the combination supports a coherent picture: the open host framework identified initially is the same kind of site network into which Zn is progressively substituted as its bulk content rises, consistent with Zn occupying framework (not merely interstitial/surface) positions and reorganizing local Mn site_occupancy as it does so.

What remains open: this series is a compositional/model ladder (specific stoichiometries), not explicitly tied to cycle number, so it does not confirm these are the actual structures present at cycle 20 or that the ~47 at% plateau corresponds to any particular panel here (e.g., a saturation composition). It also doesn't establish that the material examined post-cycling is phase-pure or retains the P21/n symmetry of the as-prepared XRD sample. So the site-level mechanism (Zn into ZnN4, Mn into MnN3/MnN4) is now structurally plausible and framework-internal, but a direct, cycle-indexed structural measurement at the actual plateau point is still missing.

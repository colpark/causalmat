Panel F7e shows a resistance sweep at fixed composition (f-P0.10, 220 µm film): output power peaks at 136.9 µW near 10 MΩ for the fiber-morphology film versus 21.025 µW for the cast film at a comparable load — roughly a 6.5-fold morphology-driven gain. This is a different comparison from the earlier wt%-sweep result: here composition/PZT loading is held fixed and morphology (fiber vs. cast) plus external load resistance are the varied factors, whereas the earlier result varied PZT wt% and tracked beta-phase fraction against voltage.

Read together, the two results support the conclusion that PRF/service_capability (output power/voltage) is shaped by more than one independent factor: PZT wt%-linked beta-phase content only tracks output gains in the low-loading regime (0-4 wt%), and this new panel shows that fabrication morphology (fiber-spun vs. cast) produces a large, separate output boost at a single, fixed composition — a mechanism that operates independently of any wt%-driven beta-phase trend. This strengthens the earlier qualification that beta-phase content alone cannot account for the full output behavior, since here a substantial output swing (fiber vs. cast) is demonstrated with no wt% variation and no beta-phase measurement at all.

What remains open: F7e does not report the PZT wt% or beta-phase fraction of the fiber/cast films used, so it cannot be tied numerically to the earlier FTIR/voltage series or used to explain the 6-10 wt% decoupling specifically. It also does not establish what mechanism underlies the fiber-vs-cast difference (e.g., alignment, poling, contact area) — only that morphology is a second, independent lever on power output, alongside a resistance-load dependence (a peak near 10 MΩ) not addressed by the wt% series at all.

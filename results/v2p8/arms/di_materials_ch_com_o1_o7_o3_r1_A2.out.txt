The panel (F2b) confirms the numbers as read: for the hot-pressed (HP, red) series, Vickers hardness rises from ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt% AlN to a peak of ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then eases to ~19.4 GPa at 3 wt% and ~19.2 GPa at 5 wt%, with error bars shown at each point.

What this supports: within the HP process, adding a small amount of AlN (1 wt%) to the ZrB2–30 vol% SiC composite raises hardness measurably above the unmodified (0 wt%) baseline, and that gain is largely retained (not fully reversed) as AlN content increases to 3 and 5 wt%, with the 1 wt% point remaining the visual maximum. The plot also shows HP hardness is substantially higher than the PS (pressureless-sintered) series at every AlN level, so the comparison of hardness-vs-AlN trend is specific to the HP process route.

What this leaves open: the panel alone does not establish statistical significance of the 0→1 wt% jump or the small 1→3→5 wt% decline — the error bars for 1, 3, and 5 wt% appear to overlap, so whether hardness is truly still decreasing versus essentially flat across that range cannot be confirmed from the plot alone. It also says nothing about mechanism (grain size, densification, secondary phase) driving the AlN effect, nor about how this HP hardness trend generalizes beyond this specific SiC content, sintering conditions, or measurement load — those require the paper's text/methods, not this figure.

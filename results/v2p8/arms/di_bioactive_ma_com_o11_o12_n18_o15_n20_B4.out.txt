The photographs (F6b lumen cross-sections, F6c explanted thrombus) confirm the stated pattern and reproduce, at the macroscopic/device level, the same ordering seen microscopically: 316L SS and PPAmF tubes are fully occluded by dark clot; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are only partly open, with clot still present alongside visible metal/foil; BVLD/NO-PPAmF alone shows a nearly clean lumen and foil surface with minimal thrombus.

Combined with the earlier SEM+BIO result (BVLD/NO-PPAmF lowest platelet count plus least platelet spreading/activation, worst on 316L SS/PPAmF, intermediate on the single-treated surfaces), this justifies extending the platelet-level finding to a functional, whole-device outcome: the same surface, BVLD/NO-PPAmF, that suppresses platelet adhesion and activation microscopically is also the one that keeps the tube lumen patent and largely free of gross thrombus after 2 h of blood flow, and the five-surface ranking of macroscopic thrombus burden tracks the ranking of platelet counts/morphology. Neither measurement alone supports this: the SEM/BIO data show platelet-level suppression but say nothing about whether that translates into an open, functioning lumen at the device scale; the photographs show a patent/clean lumen but, on their own, give no reason to attribute it to reduced platelet adhesion/activation rather than some other process.

What remains open: the photographs are qualitative, apparently single/representative samples with no thrombus-area quantification or statistics, so the correspondence between platelet counts and visual clot extent is descriptive, not measured or tested. The mechanism linking reduced platelet activation to macroscopic patency is still not established, and only one circulation time point (2 h) is shown, so durability of the effect is unknown.

1What the earlier step established

Read together, the SEM domain-size distributions (Observation A) and the in-situ XRD lattice-expansion traces (Observation B) show a correspondence across the three phases: SC, which has the coarsest domains (~1e2-1e3 um), shows a smooth/near-linear shift of the (004)/(400) and (242)/(224) 2theta peaks with temperature, whereas PC (~0.1 um domains) and HOC (~10-100 um domains) show curvature and offsets in the same peak shifts. The pairing supports associating finer/less-uniform domain scale with non-linear, offset lattice-expansion behavior, and coarser domain scale with smoother expansion.

qualifications recorded on it

  • Only three phase categories (PC, HOC, SC) are compared, so this is a correspondence across three points, not a dose-response relationship between domain size and expansion linearity
  • Domain size (a static, real-space SEM measurement) and lattice-expansion smoothness (a dynamic, in-situ XRD measurement of peak position vs. temperature) are different kinds of quantities, so the link is a cross-technique correspondence, not a measured causal effect
  • PC, HOC and SC differ in more than domain size (they are distinct phases with their own composition/crystal structure), so domain size is confounded with phase identity as a possible driver of the expansion curvature

In PC, the (242) reflection instead spikes sharply upward to ~260–300% right at ~150–160 K before relaxing back toward ~100% by ~250–270 K, while other PC reflections ((004), (400), (224)) stay much closer to 100% with only mild changes. The size of the broadening spike orders as PC > HOC > SC, matching the domain-size ordering already established (PC finest ~0.1 um, HOC intermediate ~10-100 um, SC coarsest ~1e2-1e3 um) and matching which phases showed curved/offset vs. smooth peak-position shifts. Read together with the domain-size/peak-shift correspondence, this settles that the same ~150 K event drives both the lattice-expansion curvature and a coincident, magnitude-graded change in peak width: finer/less-uniform domains (PC, then HOC) show larger anisotropic strain-broadening at the transition, while the coarse, smooth-expanding SC domains actually sharpen. That two independent XRD observables (peak position and peak width) converge on the same temperature and the same three-phase ordering strengthens the case that this is a real structural transition whose severity of local strain/disorder tracks domain scale, not just noise in one measurement. arm C — the earlier result alone · combined 0.00, limits 0.50, 0 contradiction(s)
This settled result justifies only a cross-technique correspondence, not a causal or dose-response claim linking domain size to lattice-expansion behavior. What it supports: across the three phases examined, coarser/more uniform domains (SC) co-occur with smooth, near-linear 2theta peak shifts with temperature, while finer or less uniform domains (PC, HOC) co-occur with curved, offset peak-shift traces. This is a legitimate, reproducible pattern-level pairing between a static real-space measurement (SEM domain size) and a dynamic diffraction measurement (XRD peak position vs. temperature), and it is reasonable to describe domain-scale/uniformity as "associated with" the smoothness or non-linearity of lattice expansion in this phase set. What it leaves open: 1. Sample size — only three phases are compared, so this is three data points showing a consistent pattern, not a dose-response curve; no functional relationship between domain size and expansion linearity can be inferred, and intermediate domain sizes could behave in an unpredicted way. 2. Measurement type mismatch — domain size and expansion smoothness are fundamentally different kinds of quantities (static vs. dynamic, real-space vs. reciprocal-space), so their correspondence is observational pairing, not a measured causal effect of one on the other. 3. Confounding by phase identity — PC, HOC, and SC differ in composition and crystal structure as well as domain size, so domain size cannot be isolated as the driver of the curvature/offset behavior; phase-intrinsic properties remain an equally plausible explanation. In short: the result supports an associative, three-phase correspondence between domain scale and expansion-curve smoothness, but does not establish causation, generality beyond these three phases, or that domain size (rather than phase identity) is the operative variable.

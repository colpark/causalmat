1What the earlier step established

Overlaying the two series shows that the beta-phase rise (76.1-76.3% at 0 wt% up to a peak 81.8-83.2% at 2-4 wt%) tracks the early part of the voltage rise (7.5, 12.5, 26.5, 32.0 V over 0-4 wt%), consistent with beta-phase enhancement contributing to the initial output gain. But beyond 4 wt% the pairing decouples: beta content eases back to ~81% over 6-10 wt% while voltage keeps climbing to 43.0 and 62.0 V, so the combination only supports a beta-phase-mediated story for the low-loading regime, not for the full 0-10 wt% range.

qualifications recorded on it

  • Beta content is non-monotonic (peaks at 4 wt%, then eases), while voltage is monotonic across the whole 0-10 wt% range, so the pair cannot establish beta-phase content as the operative driver at 6-10 wt%
  • The two quantities are different kinds -- a normalized structural fraction (%) versus an absolute output amount (V) -- so the magnitude of the voltage gain cannot be read off the beta-phase change
  • PZT wt% is a shared driver of both series by construction (it is varied to produce both the FTIR and the transport data), so the correlation at low loading does not by itself prove a beta-phase causal pathway rather than coincidence
  • Only 5-6 discrete wt% points on each side, insufficient to fit a shared functional relationship, only to compare trend shapes

Panel F7e (verified by reading the image) shows: fiber-film power rises from ~57 µW at 10^6 Ω to a peak of 136.9 µW at 10^7 Ω (10 MΩ), then falls; cast-film power stays far lower throughout, peaking at only 21.025 µW near the same load decade. Both curves show the same impedance-matched, rise-then-fall shape, and the fiber film exceeds the cast film at essentially every load point (~6.5x at the peaks). Read alone, this measurement only shows that fiber morphology gives a much larger absolute power output than cast morphology at matched external resistance — it says nothing about what mediates that gap. Read alone, the earlier FTIR/voltage result only shows that beta-phase content cannot be the operative driver of voltage gains beyond 4 wt% PZT loading, since beta content eases back after 4 wt% while voltage keeps climbing to 10 wt% — but that result gives no candidate for what does drive the continued rise, and it says nothing about fiber-vs-cast processing. Combined: since the fiber sample here is a high-loading formulation (f-P0.10) in the same regime where beta-phase content was already shown to have plateaued/declined, the large fiber-over-cast power advantage cannot be attributed to a beta-phase pathway that was independently shown to be saturating in that same loading range. The pairing therefore supports morphology (fiber vs. cast processing) as a plausible non-beta-phase contributor to service_capability at higher loading — something neither result shows alone. What remains open: no beta-phase (FTIR) measurement exists for these specific fiber and cast samples, so a direct causal link from morphology to output is not established, only that beta content is an insufficient sole explanation. The exact PZT wt% used in F7e relative to the earlier wt% sweep is also not confirmed to be identical, so the two datasets are not strictly matched.

arm C — the earlier result alone · combined 0.50, limits 0.00, 0 contradiction(s)
The established result supports a limited, regime-specific claim: at low PZT loading (0-4 wt%), the beta-phase fraction rise (76-76.3% up to a peak of 81.8-83.2%) tracks the initial voltage rise (7.5 to 32.0 V), which is consistent with beta-phase enhancement contributing to the early output gain. That is the full extent of what this result can support. It leaves open, and explicitly does not establish, several things. First, it does not support a beta-phase-mediated story for the full 0-10 wt% range: beyond 4 wt%, beta content eases back to roughly 81% over 6-10 wt% while voltage keeps rising monotonically to 43.0 and then 62.0 V, so at higher loadings the two series decouple and beta-phase content cannot be the operative driver there. Second, because beta content is non-monotonic while voltage is monotonic across the whole range, no single shared functional relationship between the two fits the data. Third, the comparison is between two different kinds of quantities -- a normalized structural fraction (%) versus an absolute output amount (V) -- so even where the trends align, the magnitude of the voltage gain cannot be inferred from the magnitude of the beta-phase change. Fourth, PZT wt% is a common driver of both series by design, so the low-loading correlation does not by itself distinguish a genuine beta-phase causal pathway from coincidental co-variation with loading. Finally, with only 5-6 discrete wt% points per series, the data are sufficient only to compare trend shapes, not to fit or validate a quantitative functional relationship. In short: consistent with a partial, low-loading contribution; not proof of causation, not applicable at higher loadings, and not quantitatively linked.

Panel F8c shows AQY for Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS declining sharply from 18.3% at 400 nm to 0.19% at 550 nm, tracking the material's absorption edge (the absorbance curve in the same panel drops from ~900 a.u. at 400 nm to a low baseline near 500-550 nm).

What the combination supports: Taken together with the earlier result, this settles that for the single best-performing composition (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS) — the one shown to have both the highest steady photocurrent and the highest H2 evolution rate among the four common samples — the photon-to-hydrogen conversion efficiency is itself wavelength-dependent and is confined to the region where the material absorbs light. This links the "figure of merit" performance ranking (photocurrent/H2 evolution) to an actual photon-utilization efficiency (AQY) for at least this one sample, and shows that efficiency is not a flat property but falls off steeply once wavelength exceeds the absorption edge (~450-500 nm), consistent with the reaction being photon-driven rather than thermal or otherwise wavelength-independent.

What it leaves open: AQY was measured only for Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS, not for the other three ranked samples (Pd-ZIS, Ag-ZIS, ZIS), so the combination cannot say whether the same photocurrent/H2-evolution ranking corresponds to a matching ranking in AQY across compositions — i.e., whether the best composition is also the most photon-efficient relative to the others, or simply absorbs/converts more total light. It also does not quantify how much of the photocurrent-H2 relationship (already noted as rank-order-only, not dose-response) is explained by photon efficiency versus other factors, and it does not address the composition-confound caveat already on record — AQY alone cannot separate whether Ag:Pd composition drives quantum efficiency independently of the photocurrent-H2 linkage. So the finding strengthens the case that the top-ranked sample's advantage is genuinely photon-driven, but it does not extend the ranking argument to the other three samples or resolve causal direction.

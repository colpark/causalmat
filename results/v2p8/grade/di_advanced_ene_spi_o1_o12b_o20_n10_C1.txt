THE ANSWER
The settled result supports only a claim of co-occurrence, not a mechanistic or structural link. It is justified to say that KMnHCF starts out as a single, well-matched monoclinic P21/n open framework (N-coordinated Mn, C-coordinated Fe) before cycling begins, and that separately, over the course of cycling, the cathode's bulk Zn content rises from zero to roughly 50 at% by cycle 20 and stabilizes near 47 at%, consistent with Zn2+ becoming the dominant inserted cation in the electrode as a whole. These two facts can reasonably be presented together as a picture of "an open host framework present at the outset" and "a large, stable Zn share present later," but nothing in the evidence ties them together mechanistically.

What remains uncertain: (1) whether the P21/n framework identified in the as-prepared material still exists, in the same or altered form, at cycle 20 or beyond, since the XRD observation is a single pre-cycling time point; (2) where the Zn physically resides — the ICP-AES data give only a bulk elemental at% and cannot localize Zn to framework sites, interstitial positions, surface phases, or elsewhere in the electrode; (3) whether the plateau at ~47 at% reflects saturation of framework sites specifically, as opposed to some other compositional or kinetic limit; and (4) any causal or structural relationship between the framework's openness and Zn uptake — the data show two different kinds of measurement (a static phase snapshot and a cycle-indexed bulk composition) that can only support co-occurrence in the same material, not that the framework hosts or accommodates the Zn. Site-resolved or post-cycling structural characterization would be needed to close these gaps.

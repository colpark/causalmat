F7e is orthogonal to the earlier PZT-wt%/beta-phase/voltage result. That earlier result varied PZT loading at fixed (cast) morphology and found voltage tracking beta-phase only up to 4 wt%, decoupling above it. F7e instead holds composition fixed (f-P0.10) and varies morphology — fiber vs. cast film — showing fiber-film power peaking near 136.9 uW versus ~21.025 uW for cast, at the same optimal ~10 MOhm load, a roughly 6.5-fold gain.

Combined, the two results support that service_capability (power delivery) has more than one contributing axis: composition-driven beta-phase enhancement explains part of the early voltage gain, and, independently of composition, the fiber-forming process itself confers a large additional output advantage over cast film at matched composition. What remains open: F7e reports only power vs. load, not beta-phase content for the fiber vs. cast samples, so it cannot say whether the fiber's advantage is itself mediated by higher beta-phase fraction (extending the same mechanism) or arises from unrelated fiber-specific factors (chain alignment, poling efficiency, geometry, capacitance).

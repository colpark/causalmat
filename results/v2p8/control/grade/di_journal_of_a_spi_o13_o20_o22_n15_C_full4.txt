Read together with the earlier result, this settles that a structural/crystallization readout — overall crystallinity, not specifically beta-phase fraction — co-varies with voltage output across the full compositional range, closing the gap the beta-phase-only story left open at high loading. It leaves open: (1) this is still correlation, not mechanism — voltage and χc share PZT wt% as a common driver, and PZT itself is a piezoelectric filler, so rising voltage at high loading could stem directly from more PZT phase rather than from host-polymer crystallization; (2) χc's own small dip at 6–8 wt% means it isn't perfectly monotonic either, so the match with voltage is approximate, not exact; (3) units/magnitudes still differ (% vs V), so no dose-response can be fit; (4) same sparse-point limitation (6–7 values) applies. So the pairing broadens which structural feature plausibly tracks output but does not establish which of crystallinity, beta-phase content, or PZT filler loading itself is causally responsible for the voltage gain.

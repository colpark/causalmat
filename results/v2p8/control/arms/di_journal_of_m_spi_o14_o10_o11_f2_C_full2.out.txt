The three panels do not add a new sample-composition pairing beyond what the earlier result already used (F8b is the same H2-evolution bar chart: 125.4 > 34.5 > 3.2 > 1.5 umol/h for the four overlapping samples). What they add is evidence about the *stability* of each measurement over its own repeated/extended acquisition:

- F10a shows the photocurrent ranking (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS > Pd-ZIS > Ag-ZIS > ZIS) is reproduced consistently across seven separate light on/off cycles over ~280 s, not just a single transient reading.
- F8a shows the H2-evolution ranking holds at every sampled irradiation time from 1 to 5 h (curves for all six compositions diverge monotonically and never cross), not just at the 5-h endpoint bar value used before.

Read together with the earlier established rank match, this justifies a stronger version of the same conclusion: the composition ordering that tracks higher steady photocurrent also tracks higher H2 evolution consistently and reproducibly over time, in both techniques — the correspondence is not an artifact of a single time point or a single measurement cycle.

It leaves open everything the earlier qualifications already flagged, none of which this new evidence resolves. Photocurrent and H2 evolution remain different quantities in different units, so the match is still only ordinal, not quantitative. F8a's two additional compositions (Ag0.5Pd0.5-ZIS at 57.3 umol/h and Ag0.75Pd0.25-ZIS at 43.5 umol/h) still have no corresponding photocurrent measurement, so the full Ag:Pd series is still not covered on both sides. And because composition is varied together with both measurements, temporal reproducibility within each technique does nothing to rule out composition independently driving photocurrent and H2 evolution rather than photocurrent differences causing the H2 evolution differences.

Panel F8c is a single-sample plot: UV-Vis absorbance (left axis) overlaid with apparent quantum yield, AQY (right axis, %), versus wavelength for one composition, Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS, the same sample already established as the top performer for steady photocurrent and H2 evolution. The panel shows five labeled points — 400 nm@18.3%, 420 nm@15.8%, 450 nm@6.5%, 500 nm@4.7%, 550 nm@0.19% — and AQY declines monotonically as wavelength increases, with the steepest drop occurring where the absorbance curve itself falls off (roughly 400–450 nm), i.e., near the material's absorption edge.

What this settles: for the single best-performing composition (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS), photocatalytic quantum efficiency is wavelength-dependent and its decline coincides with the sample's own light-absorption edge, consistent with the AQY being driven by how much light the material absorbs at each wavelength. This is internally consistent with — and adds detail to — the earlier finding that this composition is the strongest performer in photocurrent and H2 evolution, but it does not itself compare across compositions.

What it leaves open: F8c contains data for only one sample, so it cannot address whether the other three compositions (ZIS, Ag-ZIS, Pd-ZIS) show the same or different AQY-wavelength relationship, and it cannot be used to extend or test the four-sample photocurrent/H2-evolution ranking. The correlation between AQY decline and absorption-edge position is visual/qualitative (no fitted action spectrum overlay or statistical comparison shown), so it supports association, not a quantified or causal claim about absorption driving AQY. It also does not bear on the earlier finding's stated limitations (unmatched samples between observations, only four overlapping points, confounding of composition with both measurements) — those remain unresolved by this panel.

The F2b panel (Vickers hardness vs. AlN wt%, HP curve in red) matches the stated values: ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt%, rising to ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then settling to ~19.4 GPa at 3 wt% and ~19.2 GPa at 5 wt%, each with visible error bars. This is consistent with the panel as printed and titled "Vickers hardness vs. amount of AlN additive in ZrB2–30 vol% SiC composites sintered via different processes."

What this justifies: the hardness measurement establishes that the HP composites are hard (~18–20 GPa range) across the whole AlN series, and that AlN addition produces a modest, non-monotonic hardness response for HP samples — an initial jump at 1 wt% followed by a slight decline and plateau at 3–5 wt%, all values staying above the 0 wt% baseline. Combined with the earlier-established finding that HP samples (especially 1 and 5 wt%) are near-fully dense and pore-free with transgranular fracture, the hardness measurement is consistent with — but does not by itself prove — that this dense, well-consolidated microstructure is what supports the relatively high and stable hardness values across AlN loadings.

What it leaves open: this single hardness curve does not establish causation — it does not show that density or AlN content is the mechanism driving the hardness values, nor does it explain why 1 wt% peaks while 3 and 5 wt% are lower despite similar or higher density. The panel offers no grain-size, phase, or composition data to attribute the hardness trend to a specific microstructural cause. It also only reports HP values in this context (the PS curve is separate and here just corroborates that HP is harder), and error bars indicate a spread that leaves the true ranking among 1, 3, and 5 wt% only loosely resolved.

This new measurement is the [11-20]-specimen counterpart of the earlier-documented [01-10] case: F3c/d/e are OM images of the (0001) surface showing straight, closely-spaced band traces running parallel to {10-12}/{01-12} planes — the "straight" morphology already defined as one member of the earlier pair. F5a/b are EBSD orientation maps of the same [11-20] specimen (5% strain, (1-100) side surface): panel (b) shows the matrix crossed by straight, parallel bands each rendered essentially uniform blue (single discrete orientation), while panel (a) shows a diagonal band with an internal zigzag/kinked sub-structure and a small patch of shifted (reddish) colour within it, i.e. non-uniform, spatially varying orientation.

Read together with the earlier result, this settles that the straight-morphology/uniform-EBSD-colour vs wavy- or kinked-morphology/graded-EBSD-colour pairing is not confined to the [01-10] specimen alone: the same correspondence — straight {10-12}/{01-12}-trace bands mapping to single-orientation lenticular bands, and internally irregular (kinked) bands mapping to internally varying colour — reproduces in the independently sampled [11-20] specimen. That extends the earlier conclusion's generality across both specimen orientations actually examined, using matched OM/EBSD image pairs rather than a single orientation's example.

It leaves open everything the earlier qualifications already flagged: no quantitative misorientation magnitude is given (only uniform-vs-varying colour, still qualitative here too); coverage is still limited to these same two orientations, not generalizable beyond them; the pairing remains a matched-imaging-signature correspondence, not a mechanistic explanation of why kinked bands shade continuously; and OM contrast versus EBSD orientation-colour remain distinct physical signals whose agreement is correlational.

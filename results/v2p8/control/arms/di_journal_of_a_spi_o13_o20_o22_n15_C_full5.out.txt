Reading F4f directly adds a series the earlier synthesis did not use: alongside beta-phase content (green, left axis), the panel also plots overall crystallinity χc (red, right axis). Beta-phase content behaves as already established — rising from ~76% to a peak of 83.2% at 4 wt%, then plateauing/easing to ~81% over 6–10 wt%. But χc does not plateau: it rises essentially monotonically across the whole series, from ~50.1–50.4% (cast film/f-P0.00) through 51.1%, 52.3%, 52.7%, 52.6%, up to 53.3% at f-P0.10. That monotonic shape matches the monotonic voltage rise in F7b (7.5→12.5→26.5→32.0→43.0→62.0 V) across the entire 0–10 wt% range, not just the low-loading part.

Combined with the earlier result, this settles that the earlier "decoupling beyond 4 wt%" applies specifically to beta-phase fraction, not to crystallinity generally: a structural/FTIR-XRD-derived quantity (bulk χc) is available that tracks the full-range voltage increase, correcting the earlier implication that no such structural correlate exists at 6–10 wt%. So the combination supports treating PZT-loading-driven bulk crystallization (not beta-phase enrichment specifically) as a candidate contributor to service-capability (output voltage) gain across the whole compositional range studied.

What it leaves open: this is still correlation from a shared driver (PZT wt%) with only 6–7 discrete points, so causation is not established; the χc rise (~3 percentage points total) is numerically small compared to the voltage more-than-eightfold increase, so magnitude cannot be attributed to crystallinity alone; and the data cannot distinguish crystallinity-mediated polarization enhancement from a simpler explanation — that PZT itself is a piezoelectric filler whose loading-proportional contribution could independently produce a monotonic voltage rise with no crystallinity mediation required.

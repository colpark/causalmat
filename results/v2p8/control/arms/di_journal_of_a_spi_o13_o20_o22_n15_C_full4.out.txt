Panel F4f is a two-axis plot: the beta-phase-content series (teal, left axis, already used in the earlier result) plus a second series — degree of crystallinity χc (red, right axis) — plotted against the same PZT loadings (cast film, 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 wt%). Panel F7b is the same voltage-output series used before (7.5, 12.5, 26.5, 32.0, 43.0, 62.0 V, 0–10 wt%).

The new signal here is χc. Unlike beta-phase content, which peaks at 4 wt% (81.8–83.2%) and then eases/plateaus near ~81% through 10 wt%, χc rises roughly monotonically from ~50.2–50.4% (cast film/0 wt%) to ~53.3% at 10 wt%, with only a shallow dip between 6 and 8 wt% (~52.7 to ~52.6%). That trajectory tracks the monotonic voltage rise across the *entire* 0–10 wt% range, including the 6–10 wt% stretch where beta-phase content no longer keeps pace with voltage.

Read together with the earlier result, this settles that a structural/crystallization readout — overall crystallinity, not specifically beta-phase fraction — co-varies with voltage output across the full compositional range, closing the gap the beta-phase-only story left open at high loading. So the combination supports treating "beta-phase enhancement" as at most a partial, low-loading account, while overall crystallinity is a candidate structural correlate for the full 0–10 wt% output trend.

It leaves open: (1) this is still correlation, not mechanism — voltage and χc share PZT wt% as a common driver, and PZT itself is a piezoelectric filler, so rising voltage at high loading could stem directly from more PZT phase rather than from host-polymer crystallization; (2) χc's own small dip at 6–8 wt% means it isn't perfectly monotonic either, so the match with voltage is approximate, not exact; (3) units/magnitudes still differ (% vs V), so no dose-response can be fit; (4) same sparse-point limitation (6–7 values) applies. So the pairing broadens which structural feature plausibly tracks output but does not establish which of crystallinity, beta-phase content, or PZT filler loading itself is causally responsible for the voltage gain.

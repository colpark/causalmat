These five panels are the concrete image pair for the [11-20]-oriented specimen (RT, 5% plastic strain, (1-100) side surface) that the earlier combined OM+EBSD claim was drawn from. F3c/F3d/F3e (OM) show deformation bands whose traces are indexed against {10-12}/{01-12} planes and read as straight, well-defined band traces cutting across the matrix, near-parallel sets in (c)/(d) and a longer parallel family in (e). F5a/F5b (EBSD orientation maps, same surface/specimen) show sharply bounded, lenticular bands that are each a single uniform colour (blue, with one reddish streak in F5a) set against a green (a) or blue (b) matrix — no panel here shows a band whose colour shades continuously with position.

Read together with the earlier-established general correspondence (straight {10-12}/{01-12}-trace, OM-visible bands = uniform-colour, twin-like EBSD signature; wavy bands = continuously shaded EBSD signature), what this specific pair settles is narrower than the general claim: it confirms, as a direct instance rather than an inference, that in the [11-20] specimen at 5% RT strain the straight {10-12}/{01-12}-trace bands seen in OM do co-register with the uniform, single-colour lenticular bands seen in EBSD on the same surface. That is the "twin half" of the earlier pairing made concrete for one condition.

What it leaves open: none of F5a/F5b here shows a clearly wavy, continuously-shaded band, so this measurement does not independently confirm the "kink-band/wavy-shading" half of the correspondence — that still rests on the earlier-cited [01-10] evidence. It also adds no quantitative misorientation value and does not extend the pairing to any orientation, strain level, or surface beyond what was already covered.

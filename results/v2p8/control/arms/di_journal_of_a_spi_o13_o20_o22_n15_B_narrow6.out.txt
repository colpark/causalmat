The panel (F7e) shows load-dependent power output curves for the fiber-based PENG (f-P0.10, 220 µm) versus the cast film, swept across external resistances from 10^5 to 10^9 Ω. Both curves rise to a peak and then fall as resistance increases, consistent with typical impedance/load-matching behavior for a piezoelectric generator. The fiber film's peak (136.9 µW, at ~10^7 Ω) is roughly 6.5x the cast film's peak (21.025 µW, at a somewhat lower resistance, ~5x10^6 Ω).

What this settles: the fiber-morphology device delivers substantially higher peak electrical power than the cast-film device at comparably matched loads, and each device has a well-defined optimal load resistance near 10 MΩ where output power is maximized. This is a direct, single-measurement comparison of two film morphologies at one fixed composition (f-P0.10) and thickness (220 µm).

What it leaves open: the measurement gives no mechanism for the fiber/cast gap — it doesn't distinguish contributions from beta-phase content, crystallinity, porosity, fiber alignment, or geometry, since composition and thickness are held fixed here. It also has no bearing on the earlier PZT wt% story: the independent variable here is external load resistance and film morphology, not PZT loading, so this power-vs-resistance curve cannot be used to extend, confirm, or refute the beta-phase/voltage relationship at higher wt% loadings — that remains a separate, unresolved question. Finally, this is one sample per film type with no stated replicate or error bars, so the precision and reproducibility of the 136.9 µW and 21.025 µW peak values themselves are not established by this panel alone.

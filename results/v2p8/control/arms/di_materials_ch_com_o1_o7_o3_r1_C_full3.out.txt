F2a is the relative-density-vs-AlN-wt% curve: HP stays essentially flat and near-full density (~97.5% at 0 wt%, ~100% at 1 wt%, ~99.3% at 3 wt%, ~100% at 5 wt%) while PS falls steadily from ~85% to ~70%. This is the same density measurement already used in the earlier-established result, so on its own it adds nothing new beyond what was already settled (that HP1/HP5 reach ~100% relative density).

F4a is a different, higher-magnification SEM of HP1 with a specific bright particle marked "point A," accompanied by an EDS spectrum dominated by Si with minor Al and Zr peaks; the particle sits at what looks like a grain-boundary/triple-junction location within an otherwise tightly packed, low-porosity grain structure.

Read together with the earlier result (bulk density confirmed by pore-free, transgranular fracture SEM), this new point-EDS data settles something the density number and the low-magnification fracture view could not: the near-100% relative-density HP1 microstructure is not a single homogeneous phase but a dense, essentially pore-free matrix that nonetheless contains discrete Si/Al/Zr-bearing secondary particles at grain boundaries — i.e., full density coexists with a real secondary phase rather than being an artifact of imaging a single-phase region.

Left open: which compound point A actually is (residual sintering-aid phase, reaction product, etc.), its volume fraction or distribution across the sample, whether an analogous phase appears in the 3% or 5 wt% HP samples, and whether this phase plays any causal role in achieving near-100% density — none of that is addressed by either panel or by the earlier result's stated caveats, which still stand unresolved.

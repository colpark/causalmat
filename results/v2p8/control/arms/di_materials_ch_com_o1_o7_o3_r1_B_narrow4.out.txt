The F2b panel shows the HP (hot-pressed) curve exactly as stated: hardness rises from ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt% AlN to a peak of ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then eases to ~19.4 GPa at 3 wt% and ~19.2 GPa at 5 wt%, all with visible error bars overlapping across 1/3/5 wt%. The panel also plots a separate PS (pressureless-sintered) curve, which declines monotonically from ~12 GPa at 0 wt% to ~7 GPa at 5 wt%, but that curve is outside the stated claim and irrelevant to the HP/AlN question.

What this justifies: within the HP series, adding AlN raises Vickers hardness relative to the AlN-free baseline, with the 1 wt% addition giving the largest single-measurement value (~20.3 GPa) and 3/5 wt% remaining elevated above 0 wt% (~19.2–19.4 GPa). Combined with the earlier-settled density/SEM finding that HP samples at 1 and 5 wt% AlN are near-fully dense and pore-free with transgranular fracture, the hardness data is consistent with — but does not independently prove — a link between full densification and the higher hardness: a pore-free, well-consolidated microstructure is a plausible mechanical basis for the hardness gain, but hardness alone is a single indentation-based mechanical measurement and cannot by itself establish that porosity elimination (versus, e.g., grain size, phase distribution, or AlN's own intrinsic contribution) is the causal driver.

What it leaves open: (1) whether 1 wt% AlN is a true peak or the 1 vs. 3/5 wt% differences are statistically distinguishable, given overlapping error bars; (2) the mechanism connecting density/microstructure to hardness (this panel shows correlation of composition with both density and hardness, not a validated causal pathway); (3) any composition-resolved link tying the specific density values (~97.5%, ~99.3%, ~100%) at each wt% to the corresponding hardness value, since no such point-by-point quantitative correspondence is established here.

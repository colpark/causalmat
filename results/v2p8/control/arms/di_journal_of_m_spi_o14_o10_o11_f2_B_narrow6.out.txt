Panel F8c is a UV-vis absorbance curve with five AQY values plotted as discrete points against wavelength for the Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS sample: 400 nm @18.3%, 420 nm @15.8%, 450 nm @6.5%, 500 nm @4.7%, 550 nm @0.19%. The image confirms the reported endpoints (18.3% at 400 nm, 0.19% at 550 nm) and shows the decline is monotonic across all five measured points, with the AQY drop-off (steep between 420-450 nm, then leveling before the near-zero value at 550 nm) tracking the shape of the absorbance edge, which falls sharply around 400-450 nm and flattens toward longer wavelengths.

What this settles: for the single best-performing composition (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS), photon-to-hydrogen conversion efficiency (AQY) is wavelength-dependent and decreases as wavelength increases, with the decline co-occurring with the material's absorption edge — consistent with light absorption being a limiting factor for the photocatalytic quantum yield at these wavelengths.

What it leaves open: this is a single-sample measurement, so it says nothing about whether the other three compositions (ZIS, Ag-ZIS, Pd-ZIS) show the same wavelength dependence or the same or different AQY magnitudes — no comparative or ranking claim across compositions can be drawn from this panel. It is also only five discrete wavelength points, not a continuous action spectrum, so the exact shape of the AQY curve between points, and whether AQY strictly tracks absorbance quantitatively (versus merely co-declining), cannot be established. The co-occurrence with the absorption edge is visually consistent with absorption limiting AQY, but the panel provides no mechanistic or statistical test that absorption *causes* the AQY decline rather than some other wavelength-dependent process (e.g., transient generation efficiency, recombination) contributing as well. Finally, this measurement is independent of the photocurrent/H2-evolution ranking already established for the four samples; it does not extend, confirm, or contradict that composition-ranking result.

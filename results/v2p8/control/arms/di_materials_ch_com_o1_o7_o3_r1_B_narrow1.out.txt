Figure 2b (HP curve) confirms the stated hardness values: HP Vickers hardness rises from ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt% AlN to a peak of ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then eases slightly to ~19.4 GPa at 3 wt% and ~19.2 GPa at 5 wt%, all with visible error bars. This directly supports a non-monotonic PRP/value relationship for HP-processed material: a small AlN addition (1 wt%) measurably improves hardness over the AlN-free baseline, and higher additions (3, 5 wt%) retain most of that gain but do not exceed the 1 wt% peak — hardness plateaus/slightly declines rather than continuing to rise with more AlN.

Combined with the earlier-settled density/SEM finding (HP composites, especially 1 and 5 wt% AlN, are genuinely fully dense, pore-free, with transgranular fracture), this hardness curve is consistent with dense, well-consolidated microstructures across the HP series, and the 1 wt% optimum is not attributable to that sample being uniquely less dense than 3 or 5 wt%. But this measurement alone does not establish causation: it does not show why hardness peaks at 1 wt% and then falls off (e.g., grain size, AlN distribution, secondary-phase content, or porosity differences at 3 and 5 wt% specifically were not measured here), nor does it quantify relative density at exactly matched points for direct point-by-point correlation. It also says nothing about the PS (pressureless-sintered) series' hardness trend beyond the plotted decline, nor about toughness, strength, or other mechanical properties. So the plot settles the empirical HP hardness-vs-AlN trend and its consistency with a dense microstructure, but leaves the underlying mechanism and a quantitative density-hardness link across all four compositions open.

Panel F2d plots relative FWHM (Δσ/σ, %, referenced to 700 K) vs. temperature for three peak pairs in each phase. It shows: SC drops in a step from ~100% to ~50% at ~150 K and stays flat on either side (no overshoot). PC rises sharply to a peak of ~260% (242 peak) at ~150-155 K before decaying back toward ~100% by 250-270 K, with its other peaks (400, 224) showing smaller elevations near the same temperature. HOC peaks more modestly at ~128% around 150-155 K, then declines.

What this settles: the measurement adds a second, independent XRD metric (peak-width anomaly, not just peak-position curvature) that tracks with the same phase/domain-size grouping established earlier. SC (coarsest domains) again behaves simply — a monotonic step change with no transient overshoot — while PC (finest domains) and HOC (intermediate domains) both show a pronounced non-monotonic width spike clustered at the same ~150-155 K temperature where their lattice-expansion curves were already noted to show curvature/offsets. This strengthens, by a second method, the correspondence between domain scale/phase identity and anomalous (non-linear, transient) diffraction behavior around that transition temperature.

What it leaves open: this is still only three phase categories, so it remains a correspondence, not a dose-response trend, and domain size remains confounded with phase composition/crystal structure — the width anomaly could be driven by either. The measurement doesn't distinguish whether the width increase reflects microstrain, domain-size-distribution broadening, or a genuine structural-transition artifact, and it doesn't establish causality between domain size and the FWHM anomaly; it is a corroborating correlation, not a mechanistic explanation.

Panel F7e (read directly) shows two power-vs-load-resistance curves for a single composition (f-P0.10, 220 μm film): the fiber-film PENG rises from ~57 μW at 10^6 Ω to a peak of 136.9 μW at ~10^7 Ω (10 MΩ), then falls off toward 10^9 Ω; the cast-film PENG stays far lower throughout, peaking at only 21.025 μW near the same load region and declining monotonically thereafter.

What this supports: at matched (optimal) external load, the fiber-based device delivers substantially more peak electrical power (~6.5x) than the cast-film device of the same nominal composition. This is a direct, device-level output measurement, not an inference — it establishes that fiber architecture materially improves peak power delivery, i.e., that construction/geometry affects the PENG's practical power (service) capability, at least at this one composition and this one load-sweep.

What it leaves open: (1) This is a single composition point (f-P0.10) and one film thickness; it does not show how peak power scales with PZT wt% or whether the fiber-vs-cast advantage holds at other loadings. (2) No replicate curves or error bars are shown, so the size and reproducibility of the 6.5x gap are not quantified with uncertainty. (3) The panel gives no mechanism — it does not indicate whether the fiber advantage traces to beta-phase content, poling, geometry/capacitance, or fiber alignment; per the earlier-established caveats, beta content and voltage decouple above 4 wt%, so beta-phase cannot be assumed as the driver of this power gap without further evidence. (4) "10 MΩ optimum" is specific to this device/load configuration and may not generalize to other applications.

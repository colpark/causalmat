Panel F2b (Vickers hardness vs. AlN wt% for HP and PS composites) shows the HP curve rising from ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt% to a peak of ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt% AlN, then settling to ~19.4 GPa at 3 wt% and ~19.2 GPa at 5 wt%, with visible error bars at each point. This is consistent with the stated measurement and confirms the reported trend as read directly from the plotted HP series.

What this justifies: the figure supports a genuine, non-monotonic hardness response of the HP composites to AlN addition — a step increase from 0 to 1 wt% AlN, followed by a slight, roughly plateauing decline from 1 to 3 to 5 wt%. Given the earlier-established finding that the HP composites (notably 1 and 5 wt% AlN) are essentially fully dense and pore-free with transgranular fracture, the hardness figure by itself only reports the mechanical outcome; it does not, on its own or combined with the density/SEM pair, establish why hardness peaks at 1 wt% rather than continuing to rise or staying flat with density. In particular, since density is highest (~100%) at both 1 and 5 wt%, but hardness is higher at 1 wt% than at 5 wt%, the hardness panel shows that density alone does not explain the hardness trend — some other factor (e.g., grain size, AlN distribution, secondary phases) must be involved, but this figure cannot identify which.

What is left open: the mechanism behind the peak at 1 wt% and the mild decline thereafter; whether the error bars represent statistically meaningful differences between 3 and 5 wt% (they appear to overlap); any wt%-resolved microstructural correlate (grain size, phase composition) to explain the non-monotonicity; and how the PS series' opposite (declining) trend relates mechanistically to the HP behavior — the panel only documents the two curves, not causation.

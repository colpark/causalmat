The macroscopic (low-magnification) OM montage across RT-400 C, for both loading orientations ([11-20] in F4a, [01-10] in F4b), supports the temperature-dependent trace signature claimed: at RT and 200 C the side-surface traces are labeled {01-12}/{10-12} (twin-plane traces), while at 300-400 C they are relabeled {10-10}/{1-100} (prismatic-plane traces), and "deformation band" is explicitly labeled in panels at RT, 200 C, 300 C, and 400 C in at least one of the two orientation sets. This justifies the conclusion that the twin-trace signature is replaced by a slip-trace signature above ~300 C, while some band-type feature (labeled "deformation band") continues to be visible at every temperature examined, i.e. bands are not confined to the low-temperature twinning regime.

What it leaves open: this is a macroscopic OM survey only, so — consistent with the earlier established limit — it cannot say whether the "deformation bands" present at 300-400 C are internally uniform (twin-like) or continuously-shading (kink-like); no EBSD is shown at these higher temperatures, so the earlier OM/EBSD correspondence (wavy+asymmetric-contrast = continuously-shaded orientation) is not verified here and cannot be extended past RT. It also does not describe whether the high-temperature bands are straight or wavy, or whether they carry the same asymmetric side-surface contrast noted before, so identifying them with the earlier "wavy" population is unconfirmed. The measurement is still limited to the same two loading orientations, and it establishes only trace-plane identity and co-occurrence with temperature, not a mechanistic link between the slip-trace switch and band persistence.

This measurement (F7e) settles a narrow, direct point: for the specific f-P0.10 fiber-based PENG at 220 μm thickness, power output across a sweep of external load resistances (10^6–10^9 Ω) peaks at 136.9 μW at 10 MΩ, versus a peak of 21.025 μW for the cast film at a comparable load. That is a direct, load-swept power measurement — it legitimately supports the claim that this fiber-film device has substantially higher peak power delivery capability (roughly 6.5x) than the cast-film device, and that both devices show the typical PENG pattern of power rising then falling as load resistance increases (impedance matching behavior), with the fiber film's optimum sitting near 10 MΩ.

What it leaves open: the plot gives no mechanism for why the fiber film outperforms the cast film (alignment, porosity, geometry, and beta-phase content are all uncontrolled here) — it only shows the two curves, not a causal pathway. It is also a single composition/thickness comparison (f-P0.10, 220 μm), so it says nothing about how power capability varies with PZT wt% loading, and it is therefore a different variable axis from the beta-phase/voltage-vs-wt% comparison discussed earlier — this panel neither confirms nor extends that beta-phase story, since fiber-vs-cast is not the same manipulation as wt% variation. There are no repeat trials or error bars shown, so run-to-run variability and statistical reliability of the 136.9 μW peak are unknown. Finally, the measurement reflects instantaneous power at matched load, not sustained/service-life performance, so durability or real-application service capability remains untested by this figure.

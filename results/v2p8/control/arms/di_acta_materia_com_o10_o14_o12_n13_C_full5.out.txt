This set of panels (F3c/d/e OM images and F5a/b EBSD orientation maps) all come from the *same* specimen — the one deformed in the [11-20] orientation — rather than pairing OM from one loading orientation with EBSD from another, as the earlier established result did. F3c and F3d show straight, {10-12}/{01-12}-trace deformation bands on the (0001) and (1-100) surfaces; F3e shows a wavy-band trace. F5a and F5b are EBSD maps of that same [11-20] specimen's (1-100) side surface: (a) shows a straight, lenticular band that is uniform single-colour blue against green matrix; (b) shows bands whose blue shading varies continuously across a matrix that is itself blue.

Read together with the earlier result, this settles that the straight-OM/uniform-EBSD-colour versus wavy-OM/continuously-shaded-EBSD-colour pairing (i.e., the twin-like vs kink-like behavior_class assignment) is not just a correspondence inferred across two different specimens matched by band type — it also holds within a single specimen and loading orientation ([11-20]), where both the OM and EBSD signatures are documented for the same material. This strengthens confidence that the morphology-to-orientation-behaviour link is a real property of the PRP/behavior_class distinction rather than an artifact of comparing different samples.

It leaves open: whether the OM and EBSD images were taken at the exact same field of view (true spatial co-registration is not shown, only same specimen/orientation/surface); any quantitative misorientation magnitude; and generalization beyond the [11-20]/[01-10] orientations already covered. No new mechanistic or causal claim is supported.

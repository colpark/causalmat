Panel F7e plots output power against external load resistance (10^6-10^9 ohm, log scale) for the f-P0.10 fiber-based PENG (220 um film) versus a cast-film comparator. The fiber film traces a clear peak: power rises from ~57 uW at 10^6 to a maximum of 136.9 uW at 10^7 ohm (10 MOhm), then falls to ~117, ~62, ~23, ~14, ~9 uW as resistance increases further to 10^9. The cast film is much flatter and lower throughout, with its own peak of 21.025 uW also near 10^7 ohm, then declining gradually.

What this settles: the measurement supports a concrete, load-dependent output capability for the fiber PENG — it delivers a maximum electrical power of 136.9 uW when impedance-matched near 10 MOhm, and that peak power is roughly 6.5x the cast film's peak (136.9 vs 21.025 uW) at the same load. This directly demonstrates that the fiber architecture has a materially higher power-delivery ceiling than the cast-film architecture under matched load conditions, and it locates the optimal load for that ceiling.

What it leaves open: the panel alone does not attribute this fiber-vs-cast power gap to any specific microstructural or beta-phase cause — it is a device-level electrical comparison, not tied here to the PZT wt% / beta-phase series from the earlier voltage result. It also says nothing about durability, output stability over time, or performance outside this resistance range, and provides only power at discrete load points rather than a continuous functional relationship.

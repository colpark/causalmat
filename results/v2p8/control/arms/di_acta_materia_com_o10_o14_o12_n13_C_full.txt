OM alone establishes only band morphology: straight {10-12}/{01-12}-plane band traces versus wavy bands, plus an asymmetric contrast (strong on the (0001) side surface, faint on (1-100)/(-2110)) that marks the wavy bands as optically distinct from the straight ones. EBSD alone establishes only an orientation-map signature: lenticular bands each take one uniform colour (a single discrete orientation), whereas bands with the wavy morphology instead shade continuously with position (a gradually varying orientation); in the [01-10] specimen this shows up as uniformly red {10-12} twins coexisting with purple kink bands whose colour varies. Put together, the two observations show that the same population of bands -- the wavy, contrast-asymmetric ones seen in OM -- is the population whose EBSD colour varies continuously with position, while the straight, {10-12}/{01-12}-trace bands seen in OM are the population that EBSD shows as uniform single-colour (twin-like). Neither image alone links a morphology to an orientation behaviour; OM cannot say whether a band's internal orientation is uniform or graded, and EBSD orientation maps alone (without the OM band-trace/contrast record) cannot be tied to the optically distinguished straight-vs-wavy, {10-12}/{01-12}-trace-vs-other classification. The combination is what lets the two band populations (straight/uniform vs wavy/shading) be treated as one coherent pair of distinct structures rather than two separately-described but unlinked observations.

The qualifications recorded on that earlier result:
  - The pair gives only a qualitative colour/contrast contrast (uniform vs continuously shading; strong vs faint side-surface contrast), not a measured magnitude of misorientation, so it does not by itself quantify how much the orientation changes across a kink band versus a twin.
  - It is drawn from two specimen orientations only ([11-20] and [01-10]) and cannot establish that the same OM-morphology/EBSD-shading pairing holds for other loading orientations or specimens.
  - The two observations are co-registered by band type (straight/lenticular vs wavy) rather than being independent measurements of the same quantity, so the match is a correspondence in imaging signature, not a mechanistic proof of what causes the continuous shading.
  - OM contrast asymmetry (strong on (0001) side, faint on (1-100)/(-2110)) and EBSD colour behaviour are two different kinds of image contrast (optical topographic/diffraction contrast vs crystallographic-orientation colour coding); their agreement in distinguishing band types does not mean they are measuring the same physical variable.

What this settles: the measurement supports a macroscopic, temperature-driven change in which crystallographic trace type dominates the surface — twin-plane traces at low temperature give way to prismatic-slip-plane traces by 300-400 °C — and this pattern holds for both loading orientations examined, not just one. It also supports that the deformation bands are a separate, temperature-independent feature: they persist through the twin-to-slip trace transition rather than appearing only alongside twinning, so their occurrence is not simply a consequence of the twin population.

What it leaves open: this is trace/morphology evidence only, so per the prior EBSD-linked finding it cannot say whether the bands present at 300-400 °C are the same continuously-shading (kink-type) population identified by EBSD at RT — that correspondence was drawn only from OM+EBSD together at the orientations/temperatures used there, not extended here. It gives no CRSS, stress, or strain-rate data, so it cannot establish why the crossover occurs, only that it does. Temperature sampling is coarse (four discrete points), so the actual transition temperature is not pinned down.

The measurement (F11a: stir casting, F11b: FSP; both x750, 20 um scale bar) confirms a real morphological contrast in wear debris size/shape between processing routes: panel a shows coarse, flat, plate-like flakes on the order of 20-40 um mixed with some fines, consistent with larger fragments breaking off during sliding; panel b is dominated by fine, sub-5-um particles clumped into loose agglomerates, with almost no large flakes visible.

What this settles: it corroborates, from an independent debris-morphology angle, the abrasive-wear framing already established from the groove/crater images — a debris population shifting from coarse flakes to fine agglomerated particles is the expected signature of shallower, less severe cutting (smaller chips removed) rather than of adhesive wear (which typically produces larger torn/transferred fragments). So this is consistent with, and adds independent support to, the proposed pathway that the FSP/FA-modified surface abrasion is milder and produces finer debris.

What it leaves open: the debris images do not identify composition (no EDS/chemistry shown), so they cannot confirm the fine particles in b are FA fragments versus comminuted matrix material — the same gap noted for the groove images. Debris size here is a qualitative visual estimate, not a measured size distribution, so 'flat flakes 20-40 um' vs 'fine <5 um' are impressions from a single field of view, not statistics. The images also don't establish debris origin location (which surface feature produced which debris) or mechanistically discriminate between the two proposed sub-mechanisms (barrier effect vs. three-body rolling of fine debris) — both remain equally consistent with this debris contrast. Finally, one SEM field per condition cannot rule out localized coarse-flake regions elsewhere on surface b.

Panel F7e (power vs. load resistance, log scale, for the f‑P0.10 fiber-based PENG at 220 μm thickness) shows two traces: a red "Fiber film" curve peaking at 136.9 μW at 10^7 Ω (10 MΩ), rising from ~57 μW at 10^6 Ω, then falling off past the peak toward ~10 μW near 10^9 Ω; and a blue "Cast film" curve that stays much lower throughout, peaking at 21.025 μW around 5×10^6–10^7 Ω and declining gradually thereafter.

What this settles: the measurement directly supports that, under this specific test setup (matched load-resistance sweep, same device geometry/thickness, same P0.10 composition), the fiber-form film delivers substantially higher peak output power than the cast-form film — about 6.5× higher (136.9 vs 21.025 μW) — and that both films exhibit a resistance-dependent power maximum in the same general load range (roughly 5–30 MΩ), consistent with standard impedance-matching behavior for a piezoelectric generator.

What it leaves open: the figure gives no mechanism — it does not by itself explain why fiber outperforms cast (e.g., fiber alignment, effective piezoelectric coefficient, capacitance/impedance differences are not shown here). It is a single measurement on one composition and one thickness, so it cannot establish that fiber superiority generalizes across PZT loadings or film thicknesses. There are no repeat trials or error bars, so the precision/reproducibility of the 136.9 and 21.025 μW values is unknown. And peak power in this bench test does not by itself establish "service capability" in an applied/deployed sense — durability, load stability over time, and real-device conditions are not addressed by this panel.

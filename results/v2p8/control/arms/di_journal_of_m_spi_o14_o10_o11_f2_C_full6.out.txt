The three panels (F8b bar chart, F10a transient photocurrent, F8a time-course H2 evolution) are the underlying source data for the earlier-established ranking match, and they add a temporal dimension the single endpoint numbers alone could not show.

What this combination settles: F10a shows the photocurrent ordering (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS > Pd-ZIS > Ag-ZIS > ZIS) is reproduced consistently across all eight chopped-light on/off cycles over ~280 s, not just a single reading. F8a shows the H2-evolution ordering holds continuously across the full 5-hour irradiation time course, not only at the final tallied value. Read together with the earlier result, this supports that the matching rank order between steady photocurrent and H2 evolution rate for these four samples is a stable, repeated pattern rather than a coincidence at one time point — strengthening confidence that the correlation itself is robust and not measurement noise.

What it leaves open, unchanged from the earlier qualifications: photocurrent (µA/cm²) and H2 evolution rate (µmol/h) remain different kinds of quantity, so the match is still only a rank-order agreement, not a quantitative or unit-comparable relationship. F8a additionally displays two compositions, Ag0.5Pd0.5-ZIS and Ag0.75Pd0.25-ZIS, whose H2-evolution curves sit between the extremes, but F10a still shows no photocurrent trace for either — so the full six-member composition series is still not covered on both sides. Only four points overlap in both measurements, which shows reproducible ordering over time but still not a dose-response curve or effect size. And because Ag:Pd composition varies both properties together, the data cannot distinguish photocurrent driving H2 evolution from composition independently driving each — that causal question remains open.

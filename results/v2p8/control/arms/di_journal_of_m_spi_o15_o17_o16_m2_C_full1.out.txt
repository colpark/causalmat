The two panels just opened are the same crater/groove pair referenced by the earlier result: F10a (stir casting) shows parallel grooves plus an explicitly arrow-labeled "Crater," and F10b (FSP) shows parallel grooves plus arrow-labeled "Debris" with no crater called out. No new measurement text was supplied beyond these images, so the images themselves are the "measurement" being combined with the earlier finding.

Read together, this settles that the crater-free, abrasion-dominated wear morphology the earlier result attributed to the FA-containing/FSP surface is not a generic property of the composite composition — it is specific to the FSP processing route. The same composite (or comparable material) produced by conventional stir casting instead shows a distinct crater, a feature inconsistent with the "no large craters" abrasion-only picture. So the combination supports a refined pathway claim: dispersed FA plus FSP processing together yield shallow grooves with fine debris and no pitting, while stir casting alone (even with FA present) can still produce localized crater-type damage. Processing route is therefore an active ingredient in the proposed abrasion-moderation mechanism, not just the reinforcement's presence.

This leaves open several things: whether the stir-cast crater arises from casting-introduced porosity/agglomeration defects (a confound unrelated to FA's intrinsic wear-moderating effect) or reflects a genuinely different wear mechanism; whether FSP's benefit comes from finer FA dispersion, grain refinement, or reduced porosity; the debris composition remains unconfirmed; and, as before, these are single-image qualitative morphology comparisons (crater/no-crater, debris presence) rather than quantified defect counts or areas, so the strength of the processing effect is not measured, only observed.

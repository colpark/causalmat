Findings from F4f: alongside beta-phase content, the same panel plots crystallinity degree (Xc, right axis). Xc rises from ~50.2-50.4% (cast film, f-P 0.00) to ~51.1% (2 wt%), ~52.3% (4 wt%), ~52.7% (6 wt%), dips slightly to ~52.6% (8 wt%), then continues up to ~53.3% (10 wt%) — i.e., Xc keeps climbing across the whole 0-10 wt% range (aside from one small wobble at 8 wt%), unlike beta-phase content, which peaks at 4 wt% (83.2%) and then eases to ~81% for 6-10 wt%. Voltage (F7b) rises monotonically across the same range: 7.5, 12.5, 26.5, 32.0, 43.0, 62.0 V.

Combination with the earlier result: beta-phase content alone tracks voltage only for 0-4 wt%. Overall crystallinity (Xc), by contrast, keeps rising through 6-10 wt%, in the same regime where beta-phase content plateaus but voltage keeps climbing. So the combination supports a distinction the beta-phase/voltage pairing alone could not draw: two different structural quantities dissociate at higher loading, with Xc's continued rise pattern-matching the full-range voltage rise better than beta-phase content does, suggesting overall crystallinity (not beta-phase fraction specifically) is the structural quantity whose trend shape spans the entire loading range examined.

What remains open: this is still shape-matching between two different units (%, V) driven by a shared covariate (PZT wt%), so it is not causal evidence; only 6-7 points per series; the 8 wt% Xc dip breaks strict monotonicity; and no mechanism links crystallinity to output voltage (device output also depends on poling, electrode contact, dielectric response, etc., none measured here).

Panel F2d (Δσ/σ vs T) shows that all three forms — single crystal (SC), polycrystal (PC), and a third form (HOC) — register an anomaly clustered in the same narrow window, ~150–155 K, in the temperature-dependent XRD peak widths. That shared temperature location supports the claim that a structural transition (a lattice-symmetry/phase change) occurs near 150 K regardless of sample form — i.e., the transition itself is an intrinsic property of the material, not an artifact of one particular crystal morphology.

But the sign and magnitude of the anomaly differ sharply: SC peak width collapses (~100%→~50%), a narrowing consistent with the lattice becoming more ordered/coherent below the transition, whereas PC and HOC show the opposite — a sharp broadening spike (to ~242% and ~128% of baseline) right at the same temperature, before relaxing back toward baseline at higher T change. This divergence is unexplained by the figure alone: it could reflect grain-boundary/domain strain, coexistence of phases, or nucleation of multiple domain orientations in the polycrystalline/HOC forms that a single crystal does not exhibit, but nothing in this panel identifies the mechanism.

So: this measurement alone supports "a common phase transition near 150 K across SC/PC/HOC," but it leaves open whether the transition is crystallographically identical in all three forms, and it cannot explain why peak width narrows in SC but broadens in PC/HOC.

The debris measurement is consistent with, and adds a distinct line of support to, the same qualitative claim already established: panel a (stir-cast, no FA) shows coarse, flat, angular flakes on the order of 20-40 um, typical of large plate-like wear fragments sheared off in bulk; panel b (FSP, FA-containing) shows a field dominated by fine (<5 um) granular particles forming loose agglomerated clusters rather than large flakes. This corroborates the "fine debris" half of Observation A for surface b and strengthens the case that FA incorporation shifts the wear debris population toward finer particulate rather than large adhesive/delamination fragments — consistent with the proposed abrasive, FA-moderated pathway rather than adhesive failure.

What it leaves open, unchanged from the prior qualifications: (1) the size ranges (20-40 um vs <5 um) are visual/qualitative impressions from single images at one magnification, not systematic particle-size measurements or counts, so the contrast, while visually clear here, is not quantified; (2) the panels show only debris morphology, not composition (EDS/XRD), so they cannot confirm the fine particles in b actually originate from fractured FA rather than from finer matrix wear — this remains unconfirmed; (3) these two images are localized fields of debris, not a full-surface survey, so they cannot rule out coarser flakes or adhesive patches existing elsewhere on surface b; (4) the debris morphology alone still cannot discriminate between the two candidate sub-mechanisms (FA acting as a barrier to asperity penetration vs. FA-derived fine debris acting as three-body rolling agents) — both remain equally consistent with "fine, agglomerated debris on the FA surface."

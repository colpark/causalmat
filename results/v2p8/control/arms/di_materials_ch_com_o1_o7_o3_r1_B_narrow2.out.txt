The F2b panel is consistent with the stated HP hardness values (~18.0 GPa at 0 wt%, ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then ~19.4 and ~19.2 GPa at 3 and 5 wt% AlN), plotted with visible error bars on a Vickers hardness (GPa) vs. AlN wt% axis. This measurement, on its own, settles only that HP-processed ZrB2–30 vol% SiC composites show a modest, non-monotonic hardness response to AlN addition: a jump from 0 to 1 wt% (+~2.3 GPa) followed by a slight decline from 1 to 5 wt%, with all doped compositions remaining above the undoped baseline. It supports the qualitative claim that AlN addition raises HP hardness relative to the AlN-free composite across the whole tested range (1, 3, 5 wt%), and that the peak is at 1 wt%.

It does not by itself establish why hardness rises then plateaus/declines — it is a bulk mechanical measurement, not a microstructural or compositional one, so it cannot attribute the trend to grain size, phase distribution, residual porosity, or AlN incorporation mechanism. Combined with the already-settled density/SEM finding (near-full density and pore-free, transgranular fracture especially at 1 and 5 wt%), the hardness data are consistent with density/porosity not being the limiting factor at 1 wt% (where both density and hardness peak), but the panel gives no quantitative link tying the specific hardness values to the specific density values — that correlation is only suggestive, not demonstrated. The PS series (blue) shows an opposite, monotonically decreasing trend, which this panel alone cannot explain and which is outside the scope of what was asked. Error bars indicate measurement variability but their magnitude relative to the 1-to-3/5 wt% differences is not quantified here, so whether that slight decline after 1 wt% is statistically meaningful versus noise is left open.

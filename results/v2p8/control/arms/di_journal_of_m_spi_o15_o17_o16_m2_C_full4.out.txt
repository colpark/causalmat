The two panels, read against their actual captions, force a revision of what the earlier-established interpretation could mean. Panel a (stir casting) shows deep parallel grooves plus a distinct crater; panel b (FSP) shows parallel grooves plus fine debris and no crater. Because the captions identify the variable as processing route (stir casting vs. friction stir processing) rather than FA-present vs. FA-absent, the combination settles that the "shallower grooves + fine debris + no crater" morphology the earlier step attributed to FA's moderating effect is observed specifically in the FSP-processed condition relative to the stir-cast condition of what is presumably the same FA composite. So together they support a narrower, processing-linked claim: FSP processing (likely via more uniform FA/particle dispersion and grain refinement) reduces or eliminates crater-type damage and coarsens grooves into a fine-debris-covered abrasive pattern, compared with stir casting of the same material — i.e., both samples stay within the abrasive wear regime, but processing route governs the severity/character of that abrasion. Neither panel alone shows this: panel a alone only shows crater+groove; panel b alone only shows groove+debris; only naming both as stir-cast vs. FSP for a shared composite ties the morphology difference to processing rather than to FA content per se.

What remains open: whether both samples in fact contain FA at the same loading (composition of each sample is not shown here); whether the effect is caused by FA redistribution, matrix grain refinement from FSP, or both; whether debris in panel b originates from FA or matrix; and, as before, this is single-image qualitative morphology, not quantified groove depth or debris count, so "shallower" and "fewer craters" remain visual impressions.

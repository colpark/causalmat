F4f plots two structural series against PZT wt%: β-phase content (left axis, already used in the earlier result) and crystallinity degree χc (right axis, ~50.2% at 0 wt%, rising to ~51.1, 52.3, 52.7, 52.6, and 53.3% at 2-10 wt%). F7b confirms the voltage series is monotonic across the full range: 7.5, 12.5, 26.5, 32.0, 43.0, 62.0 V for 0-10 wt%.

Read together with the earlier result, this settles that a structural correlate (crystallinity degree) does track voltage monotonically across the ENTIRE 0-10 wt% range, including the 6-10 wt% stretch where β-phase content plateaued/eased and decoupled from the still-rising voltage. So the combination extends the earlier finding: while β-phase content only explains the early-loading voltage gain, overall crystallinity offers a candidate structural correlate that stays aligned with voltage through high loading too, closing part of the gap the earlier result left open for 6-10 wt%.

What remains open: this is still a correlation between two different quantity types (a normalized % vs an absolute V), so no magnitude relationship can be inferred. PZT wt% remains a shared driver of both series by construction, so coincidence versus a genuine crystallinity-mediated causal pathway is not distinguished. Only 6-7 discrete points exist on each side (still too few to fit a shared function). And because χc aggregates all crystalline phases, not just β-phase, this does not identify which phase(s) drive the high-loading voltage gain -- only that overall crystallinity, unlike β-phase alone, does not decouple from voltage.

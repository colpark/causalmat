Reading F2a and F4a directly (no separate measurement text was supplied):

F2a plots relative density (%) vs AlN content (0/1/3/5 wt%) for two routes: HP (red) stays flat at ~97.5–100% across all AlN loadings, while PS (blue) falls steadily from ~85% (0 wt%) to ~70% (5 wt%). F4a is the HP1 fracture-surface SEM with a point-EDS spectrum at "point A" (a bright facet), showing a dominant Si peak plus minor Al, Zr, N, B, C — i.e., that facet is a compositionally distinct, additive-containing phase, not a void.

Combined with the earlier-established result (HP density confirmed pore-free/transgranular by SEM), this settles two things neither panel alone establishes. First, F2a shows the near-100% density behavior is process-specific, not an intrinsic effect of AlN content: the same AlN additions that keep HP density flat near 100% instead drive PS density down with increasing wt%. Second, F4a's point-A spectrum shows that even the additive-bearing (Al/Zr-containing) phase within the HP microstructure fractures transgranularly like the matrix rather than appearing as a pore or loosely bonded particle — extending the "genuinely dense, pore-free" conclusion down to a compositionally distinct feature, not just the bulk matrix.

What this leaves open: there is no PS-side SEM/EDS here, so it's unknown whether PS's lower density corresponds to visible porosity or a different phase distribution. Point A is one local EDS spot, not a quantified or composition-series-resolved phase measurement, and the mechanism behind HP's pressure-driven densification versus PS's decline with AlN remains unestablished.

Both panels are plan-view SEM images of wear debris at the same magnification (X750, 20 um scale bar). Panel a (stir casting) shows coarse, angular, flat flake-like fragments spanning roughly 20-40 um, consistent with debris torn loose by relatively severe abrasive cutting/delamination of a harder, less-protected surface. Panel b (FSP) shows the field almost entirely covered by fine, loosely agglomerated granular particles well under 5 um, with only an occasional larger fragment, consistent with debris that has been comminuted into small particles rather than sheared into large flakes.

What this settles: the debris morphology is consistent with, and reinforces, the earlier finding that the FA-containing (FSP) surface undergoes a less severe form of abrasive wear than the stir-cast surface — the shift from coarse flakes to fine agglomerated particles fits the proposed pathway that dispersed FA moderates cutting severity (barrier/fine-debris effect) rather than the composite failing by a different, adhesive mechanism. It corroborates the groove-depth observation with an independent debris-size cue pointing the same direction.

What it leaves open: this is a qualitative visual comparison of debris size/shape in single fields of view, not a measured size distribution or statistically compared metric. The chemical composition of the fine particles was not analyzed, so whether they originate from fractured FA, oxide films, or matrix material is unconfirmed. It also cannot discriminate between the two candidate sub-mechanisms (FA blocking asperity penetration vs. fine debris acting as a rolling third body) — both remain equally consistent with this debris pattern.</message>

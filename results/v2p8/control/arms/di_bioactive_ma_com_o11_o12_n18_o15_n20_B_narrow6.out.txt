What the photographs show: In F6b (cross-sections after 2 h blood circulation), 316L SS and PPAmF lumens are filled with dark material (occlusive thrombus); BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF show partial opening, with a lighter/metallic region alongside residual dark red material; BVLD/NO-PPAmF shows the lumen and foil largely clean and bright, with only minor red staining near the rim. F6c (photographs of the extracted thrombus/foil) shows the same pattern at the whole-surface level: 316L SS and PPAmF are covered by large dark red/purple clot; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF have mixed clean/red regions; BVLD/NO-PPAmF is mostly white/clean with only marginal red.

This supports extending the SEM+BIO finding (reduced platelet number and reduced spreading/activation specifically on BVLD/NO-PPAmF) to a third, independent, whole-device/functional level: gross thrombus formation under flowing blood follows the same five-surface ordering, with BVLD/NO-PPAmF producing visibly the least occlusion and least clot mass, 316L SS and PPAmF the most, and the single-component surfaces intermediate. That triangulation across microscopic morphology, platelet count, and macroscopic clot appearance strengthens the case that BVLD/NO-PPAmF suppresses thrombogenicity overall, not just adhesion at the cell-count level.

What it leaves open: the photographs are purely qualitative/visual, with no thrombus mass, area, or occlusion percentage quantified, no statistics or replicate counts given, and no indication these are anything other than single representative images. They establish nothing about mechanism, and they do not independently verify the platelet count/morphology correspondence — they add a converging but separately unquantified line of evidence.

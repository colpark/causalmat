The new figure (F10a stir casting, F10b FSP) is a same-composite comparison across processing route, not FA-vs-no-FA composition. F10a (stir casting) shows a deep, explicitly labeled "Crater" alongside grooves — a discrete pit-like feature consistent with a localized adhesive/fracture event. F10b (FSP) shows grooves overlaid with fine particulate "Debris" and no labeled crater, closely mirroring the earlier-described FA-surface (b) morphology (shallower grooves, fine debris, no large crater).

Read together with the earlier established result (FA-containing surface: shallower grooves + fine debris, no crater; both surfaces otherwise share groove-dominated, abrasive-type wear), this new pair adds that the crater-free, debris-covered morphology is not unique to FA addition alone — it also appears when processing route changes from stir casting to FSP. Combined, the two results support a broader claim: the abrasion-dominant/adhesion-suppressed pathway (fewer craters, fine debris moderating cutting severity) generalizes across at least two different interventions (composition and processing), strengthening confidence that the mechanism class (MEC) itself — barrier/debris-mediated abrasion rather than adhesive wear — is robust rather than an artifact of one specific sample pair.

What it leaves open: because the FA-composition comparison and the stir-casting/FSP comparison are different sample pairs, the combination cannot separate whether FA dispersion, FSP-induced microstructural refinement/homogenization, or both together is the operative cause of crater suppression — the two variables are not disentangled here. It also does not quantify crater size/frequency, confirm debris composition, or survey full worn surfaces, so the earlier qualifications about single-image, qualitative, non-compositional evidence still apply.

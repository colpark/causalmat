F2a is the relative-density-vs-AlN plot: HP (red) sits near-flat and high across 0/1/3/5 wt% AlN (~97.5% → ~100% → ~99.3% → ~100%), while PS (blue) falls steadily from ~85.5% to ~70% over the same range. F4a is a fracture-surface SEM of HP1 (1 wt% AlN) showing large, tightly interlocked grains with two bright, angular particles arrowed and labeled "point A"; the accompanying EDS spectrum at point A shows a dominant Si peak with smaller Al and Zr peaks (plus low-energy B/C/N background).

Read together with the earlier-established result (density + generic pore-free, transgranular fracture confirm HP composites are genuinely near-fully dense), this new panel adds one further, narrow point: the bright inclusions visible on the HP1 fracture surface are not pores or voids but chemically distinct particles containing Si, Al and Zr — i.e., they are a secondary/grain-boundary phase, and that phase contains Al, consistent with AlN being retained in the consolidated microstructure rather than being lost. So the combination supports that the near-100% density in HP1 corresponds to a microstructure where AlN is incorporated into an identifiable Si-Al-Zr-bearing secondary phase rather than remaining unreacted or escaping.

This still leaves open: whether that secondary phase is what drives the density increase (no mechanism/causation is shown, only co-location); the phase's composition, distribution and volume fraction at 3 or 5 wt% AlN (only HP1, one point, is analyzed); and whether point A represents a grain-boundary liquid-phase residue, an unreacted AlN particle, or an unrelated Zr-containing additive phase — the single-point EDS cannot distinguish these possibilities.

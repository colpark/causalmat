Both panels are SEM images of loose wear debris at the same scale (X750, 20 µm bar). Panel a (stir casting) shows coarse, plate-like flakes roughly 20-40 µm across with sharp, angular edges — consistent with larger fragments sheared off by cutting/abrasive action. Panel b (FSP) shows the field almost entirely covered by much finer particles, well under 5 µm, forming loose clumped agglomerates, with only an occasional larger fragment at the edge.

What this settles: the debris-morphology comparison is consistent with, and adds a second, independent line of support to, the earlier groove-based finding — the FA-containing (FSP) surface generates markedly finer wear debris than the coarser flake debris from the stir-cast surface. This corroborates the abrasive-wear-with-moderated-severity picture (shallower grooves + fine debris on surface b) rather than a shift to adhesive wear, since adhesive wear typically produces larger, torn/transferred fragments, not the fine comminuted particulate seen here.

What it leaves open: this is still a single-image, qualitative morphological comparison — no particle-size distribution, debris volume, or wear-rate quantification is given. The chemical composition of the fine debris in b is not shown, so it cannot be confirmed that the fine particles are fractured FA rather than matrix material. The images also cannot discriminate between the two candidate sub-mechanisms (FA acting as a physical barrier to asperity penetration vs. fine debris rolling as a three-body lubricant to reduce cutting) — either would produce this same debris signature. And because only these two fields of view are shown, full generality across the worn surfaces is not established.

Panels F5d–F5f are polyhedral crystal-structure models (unit cells with Mn/Fe/N/C polyhedra plus small coordination-environment insets), not diffraction patterns or occupancy refinements. What is visible: (d) K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6 shows a framework of magenta/orange octahedra with no grey polyhedra and an inset labeled MnN6. (e) K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6 shows the same octahedral framework type but with a small number of grey tetrahedra now present among the octahedra, and an inset with fewer Mn–N contacts than in (d). (f) ZnMnFe(CN)6 shows markedly more grey tetrahedra distributed through the cell, positioned adjacent to the magenta/orange octahedra, with an inset clearly labeled MnN4.

This supports: across this three-composition series, as Zn content rises (d→e→f) the modeled local Mn coordination environment progressively loses N neighbors (consistent with the caption's Jahn-Teller-distortion claim), and grey tetrahedral units — identified in the caption as ZnN4 — increase in number and appear spatially adjacent to the octahedral (FeC6) units.

What it leaves open: these are three static, idealized structural models at fixed, separately prepared compositions, not a continuous or cycling-indexed series, so no site-occupancy fractions or refined occupancies are shown, only schematic coordination polyhedra. The images do not by themselves demonstrate a chemical bond ("linked to FeC6") beyond adjacency in the drawn model, and this series is not connected here to the earlier cycle-indexed ~47 at% Zn plateau data — so it cannot show that this modeled MnN6→MnN4/ZnN4 progression is what occurs during actual electrochemical cycling, only that it occurs across these three synthesized/measured compositions.

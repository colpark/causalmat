Panels F4a and F4b (macroscopic OM montages, [11-20] and [01-10] loading, two side surfaces each, RT–400 °C at 5% strain) show trace lines labeled by crystallographic plane on the specimen surfaces, plus generic "deformation band" callouts.

What the measurement supports: at RT and 200 °C the visible surface traces are consistently indexed as {01-12}/{10-12} planes (the twinning-plane indices); at 300 °C and 400 °C those same trace positions are instead indexed as {10-10}/{1-100} (prismatic-plane indices), on both side surfaces and in both loading orientations shown. "Deformation band" labels appear at every temperature step, so band-scale heterogeneity itself does not disappear with temperature — only the crystallographic plane the traces run parallel to changes. This justifies the conclusion that there is a temperature-driven switch, somewhere between 200 °C and 300 °C, in which crystallographic plane the macroscopic surface traces align with, consistent with a shift from twin-trace-dominated to prismatic-slip-trace-dominated surface markings, while coarse band morphology persists throughout.

What it leaves open: the figure gives plane indices and the word "deformation band," not the straight/uniform-vs-wavy/shading classification established earlier from OM contrast + EBSD orientation maps — so this image alone cannot say whether the twin-indexed traces are the same population as the earlier "uniform-colour, straight" bands, or whether the post-300 °C bands are the "wavy, continuously shading" kind. It is macroscopic trace geometry only, with no EBSD orientation data attached, so it cannot confirm internal misorientation behavior, cause, or magnitude. It also comes from only two loading orientations at a single strain level (5%), so generalization beyond these conditions is not supported.

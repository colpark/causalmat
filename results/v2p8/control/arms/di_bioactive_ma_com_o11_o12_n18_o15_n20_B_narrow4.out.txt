The two photograph panels (F6b: cross-sectioned tube lumens; F6c: explanted thrombus on foil) show, across all five surfaces, the same ordering already established by SEM/BIO: 316L SS and PPAmF lumens are filled with dark clotted material and their foils are covered with substantial dark red/black thrombus; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF lumens are only partly filled (patches of open, lighter tissue amid dark clot) with intermediate thrombus coverage on foil; BVLD/NO-PPAmF lumen and foil are largely clean/light-colored, with only minor red staining at the edges.

What this supports: it extends the platelet-count/morphology finding from the surface (adhesion, activation shape) to a gross, functional/service-relevant outcome after 2 h of blood circulation — BVLD/NO-PPAmF visibly resists bulk thrombus formation and keeps the lumen more patent than the other four surfaces, with 316L SS and PPAmF showing the most occlusive thrombus and BVLD-PPAmF/NO-PPAmF intermediate. This is consistent with, and macroscopically corroborates, the earlier joint reduction in platelet number and spreading on BVLD/NO-PPAmF.

What it leaves open: the comparison is purely qualitative/visual (photographs, not measured thrombus mass, area, or occlusion percentage), with no statistics or replicate counts shown, so the apparent stepwise ordering is descriptive, not tested. It doesn't establish mechanism (why BVLD/NO-PPAmF resists clotting), doesn't confirm patency or performance beyond the single 2 h time point, and doesn't quantitatively tie clot appearance back to the SEM/BIO platelet numbers from the same surfaces.

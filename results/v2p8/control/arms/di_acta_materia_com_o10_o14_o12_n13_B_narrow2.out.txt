What the two macroscopic OM panels support: across the RT–400 °C series, for both loading axes ([11-20] in 4a, [01-10] in 4b) and both pairs of side surfaces, the labeled surface-trace indices do shift from {01-12}/{10-12}-type planes at the lower temperatures to {10-10}/{1-100} (or {-1100}) planes at the higher temperatures, and "deformation band" labels appear at every temperature examined (RT, 200, 300, 400 °C) in at least one surface of each panel. This is consistent with, and gives macroscopic corroboration to, the stated claim that low-temperature twin-plane traces give way to prismatic slip-plane traces on heating while deformation bands are a temperature-independent feature.

What it leaves open: the transition is not sharply pinned at "300 °C" for both orientations — in panel 4a ([11-20]), the (1-100) surface already shows prismatic {10-10}/{01-10} trace labels at 200 °C, so the RT/200 °C-vs-300/400 °C split holds cleanly only for panel 4b ([01-10]); for 4a the crossover appears to occur earlier on one surface. Second, these are trace-line (Miller-index) labels from optical macrographs alone — plane orientation by surface-trace angle, not a crystallographic (EBSD) confirmation of twin versus slip character. Third, and consistent with the earlier established qualification, the "deformation band" labels here are generic: the panels do not distinguish whether the bands persisting at each temperature are the straight/uniform (twin-like) or wavy/continuously-shading (kink-like) population identified by the prior OM+EBSD pairing, so this measurement cannot say which band type persists, only that some deformation-band morphology is present at every temperature.</message>

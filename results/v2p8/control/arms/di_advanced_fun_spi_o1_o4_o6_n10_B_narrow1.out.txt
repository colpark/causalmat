Panel F2d plots Δσ/σ (%), the fractional change in Gaussian-width (FWHM proxy) of the 2theta peaks, versus temperature for SC (top, blue), PC (middle, red) and HOC (bottom, black), each tracking its own labeled reflections ((004)/(400)/(242)/(224)).

What the panel shows: near 150-155 K, SC's width drops sharply from ~100% down to ~45-50% and stays low through 270 K. PC's width, in contrast, rises sharply to a peak near ~260-280% around 150-155 K (most visible for the (242) trace) before decaying back toward ~100-130% at higher temperature. HOC shows a smaller, similar-direction spike to ~120-130% around 150-155 K before relaxing toward ~90-105%. So all three phases show an anomaly in peak width clustered at the same temperature, but SC's anomaly is a narrowing while PC's and HOC's are broadenings, and the two curved/offset-expansion phases (PC, HOC — the fine/less-uniform domain phases) show the width-broadening kind, while the smooth-expansion, coarse-domain phase (SC) shows narrowing.

This is consistent with, and reinforces, the earlier correspondence: SC's peak-width behavior is smooth/monotonic-narrowing rather than showing a broadening excursion, while PC and HOC both exhibit a broadening anomaly localized to the same ~150 K region where their 2theta shift curves already showed curvature/offset — i.e., a second XRD observable co-localizes with the domain-size grouping. It does not establish a new independent variable: width and 2theta-shift are both XRD peak-shape measurements at the same temperature, so this is corroboration within XRD, not an independent confirmation across techniques, and the same confounds apply — only three phases are compared, domain size remains confounded with phase identity/composition, and no causal mechanism (e.g., strain, domain-boundary pinning, or a structural transition near 150 K) is distinguished from these curves alone.</message>

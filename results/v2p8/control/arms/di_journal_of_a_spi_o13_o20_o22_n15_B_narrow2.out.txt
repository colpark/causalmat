This measurement (F7e) directly settles the PRF/service_capability comparison for the f-P0.10 fiber PENG vs. the cast film, at a fixed 220 µm film thickness: across a sweep of external load resistances, the fiber film's power output peaks at 136.9 µW (at ~10 MΩ), roughly 6.5x higher than the cast film's peak of 21.025 µW (at a similar load). This is a direct, same-unit, same-quantity comparison (power in µW vs. power in µW), unlike the earlier beta-phase/voltage pairing — so it cleanly supports the claim that the fiber morphology gives substantially higher peak power-delivery capability (service capability) than the cast-film morphology under matched measurement conditions.

What it leaves open:
- The comparison is confined to one PZT loading (f-P0.10) and one thickness (220 µm); it says nothing about whether the fiber-vs-cast advantage holds, grows, or shrinks at other PZT wt% or thickness values.
- It does not establish a mechanism — the panel shows only load-dependent power, not why fiber outperforms cast (e.g., whether beta-phase content, geometry/surface area, or piezoelectric coefficient differences drive the gap). The earlier beta-phase/voltage result explicitly could not fix a causal driver even for the voltage rise, and this power panel supplies no additional mechanistic evidence linking beta-phase to the fiber's power advantage.
- Only the peak values are highlighted; the full curve shape (rise and fall across the resistance sweep) suggests an optimal-load dependency, so the capability advantage is load-specific (near 10 MΩ) rather than uniform across all resistances — at very low or very high loads the gap could narrow.
- No error bars, replicate counts, or statistical treatment are visible in the panel, so the size and reliability of the 136.9 vs. 21.025 µW gap cannot be assessed beyond the single reported curve.

In short: the panel supports a direct, same-unit demonstration that the fiber film delivers substantially greater peak power under optimal load than the cast film at this one composition/thickness; it does not extend that claim to other compositions/thicknesses, does not identify a mechanism, and does not quantify uncertainty.

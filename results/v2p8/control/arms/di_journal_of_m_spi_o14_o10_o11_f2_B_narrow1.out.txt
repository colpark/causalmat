The panel (F8c) plots apparent quantum yield (AQY, right axis) against wavelength together with the absorbance/UV-Vis edge (left axis) for the single Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS sample, the best performer from the composition series. The labeled points confirm the stated values: 18.3% at 400 nm, 15.8% at 420 nm, 6.5% at 450 nm, 4.7% at 500 nm, and 0.19% at 550 nm, with AQY falling sharply as wavelength increases past ~400-450 nm, in step with the absorbance curve's decline near the same region.

What this settles: for this one optimized composition, photocatalytic H2-evolution quantum efficiency is wavelength-dependent and its decline coincides with the material's absorption edge — i.e., the action spectrum is consistent with band-gap/visible-light absorption being the driver of photon-to-H2 conversion efficiency, rather than efficiency being flat or noise-like across wavelengths. This is a within-sample, single-condition measurement (one composition, discrete wavelengths, no error bars or replicates shown).

What it leaves open: the panel says nothing about the other three samples in the ranking (ZIS, Ag-ZIS, Pd-ZIS), so it cannot extend or confirm the composition-ordering result established earlier — it is not comparative across compositions. It also does not, by itself, establish a quantitative dose-response between absorbance and AQY (only qualitative coincidence of onset), and because only one sample was measured this way, whether the same wavelength-dependence pattern or magnitude holds for the lower-performing samples is undetermined.

The three panels are computed/model crystal structures at three Zn-substitution levels: F5d = K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6 (Mn shown as MnN6-type octahedron), F5e = K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6 (Mn polyhedron distorts to MnN3), F5f = fully Zn-substituted ZnMnFe(CN)6 (Mn polyhedron becomes MnN4, and grey ZnN4 tetrahedra now appear, linked to the orange FeC6 octahedra).

What this supports: as the modeled Zn fraction is stepped up across the three fixed compositions, the local coordination geometry around Mn changes progressively (six- toward four-coordinate), and at the highest Zn content a distinct tetrahedral Zn site (ZnN4) becomes structurally resolvable and is shown connected into the framework via the FeC6 octahedra. This directly supports that Zn, when present at sufficient level, occupies a site type (tetrahedral) different from Mn's site, and that increasing Zn content is associated with a coordination/phase change at Mn — i.e., site occupancy and phase distortion track together across these three model compositions.

What it leaves open: these are three discrete, fixed-composition structures (likely relaxed/idealized models), not a time- or cycle-resolved measurement of the actual cycled electrode, so nothing here ties these structures to the cycle-20 ~47 at% Zn state established earlier, nor shows this progression actually occurs in situ during cycling. It also does not establish whether MnN4 and ZnN4 coexist in a single real sample, whether the transition is reversible, the space group/phase identity at each step, or the kinetics/mechanism driving the change — only a static structural comparison across three assumed compositions.

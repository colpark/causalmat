What the photographs show, confirmed by direct inspection:

F6b (cross-sections after 2h circulation): 316LSS and PPAmF lumens are filled with dark thrombus (black, occluded). BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF lumens are partly open, with a lighter gray patch alongside dark red/black material. BVLD/NO-PPAmF shows a mostly bright, open, near-metallic lumen with only a thin dark rim.

F6c (opened tube/foil surfaces): 316LSS and PPAmF are covered by large dark thrombus masses. BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF show a central pale/clean stripe flanked by dark red thrombus at the edges. BVLD/NO-PPAmF shows the largest clean/pale area with only a thin dark red-brown border.

This settles: at the gross, whole-tube level, the same stepwise ordering seen in SEM morphology and BIO platelet counts is reproduced visually — 316LSS/PPAmF worst (fully occluded by thrombus), BVLD-PPAmF/NO-PPAmF intermediate (partial occlusion), BVLD/NO-PPAmF best (nearly clean lumen and foil). The photographs corroborate, at macroscopic scale, that BVLD/NO-PPAmF suppresses thrombus formation most among the five surfaces, converging with the platelet count and spreading data.

What it leaves open: the images are qualitative and appear to be single representative specimens per surface — no measured thrombus area/mass, no replicate counts, no statistics, so the ordering is descriptive, not tested. The photos do not by themselves prove the mechanism (platelet suppression vs. other clotting-factor effects) is what drives the visible thrombus reduction — they only show that gross thrombus extent tracks the same ranking as platelet adhesion/activation. No causal or biochemical mechanism is established.

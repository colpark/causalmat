The three DFT-relaxed structural models show a composition-dependent progression as Zn substitutes for Mn: at low Zn (K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6, panel d) Mn retains a full MnN6 octahedron (bond lengths ~1.98–2.28 Å); at higher Zn content (K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6, panel e) the local Mn environment is drawn as MnN3 (fewer N ligands shown, ~2.15–2.20 Å); and in the fully Zn-substituted phase (ZnMnFe(CN)6, panel f) Mn is MnN4, and grey ZnN4 tetrahedra appear and connect to the FeC6 octahedral framework. This settles, on its own, that increasing Zn content correlates with a stepwise reduction/reorganization of Mn's nitrogen coordination and with the appearance of a new Zn-tetrahedron/Fe-octahedron connectivity motif — a structural (site-occupancy and local coordination) change accompanying Zn incorporation.

What it leaves open: the caption itself is hedged ("could attribute to," "could happen"), so the figure does not establish that this coordination change causes the "subsequent phase transition" rather than merely correlating with it; it gives no quantitative site-occupancy/vacancy fractions, no confirmation these are equilibrium (vs. relaxed/idealized) structures, and no experimental (diffraction) verification that MnN3/MnN4 environments are physically realized rather than modeling artifacts. Whether this is a true phase transition (space-group change) or a local-coordination gradient within one phase is not settled by these panels alone.

The two panels (F4a, [11-20] loading; F4b, [01-10] loading) show labeled surface-trace micrographs at RT, 200, 300, 400 C for two side surfaces each. Reading the plane-trace labels confirms the stated measurement directly: at RT and 200 C the traces are indexed {10-12}/{01-12} (twin-plane traces), and at 300-400 C the same surfaces instead carry {10-10}/{1-100} (or {1-100}/{-2110}) traces, i.e. prismatic-plane traces — a clean replacement of twin traces by prismatic slip traces across the transition. In both orientations, "deformation band" labels appear at every temperature (RT through 400 C) on at least one surface, confirming that band-type features persist regardless of which trace family (twin vs. prismatic) is present.

This supports: a temperature-dependent switch in the crystallographic trace family associated with plastic strain (twinning-dominated at RT-200 C, prismatic-slip-dominated at 300-400 C), established macroscopically by Miller-index trace identification in two independent loading orientations; and that deformation-band morphology is not confined to the twinning regime — bands are labeled even at 300-400 C where twin traces are absent, so band formation is decoupled from the twin-vs-slip identity of the surrounding matrix trace.

What it leaves open: this is trace indexing only, not a linkage to the EBSD orientation-uniform-vs-shading classification described earlier — these panels do not by themselves show whether the RT/200 C bands are the "uniform-colour" (twin-like) EBSD population and the 300-400 C bands the "shading" (kink-like) population; that correspondence was established separately and only via combined OM+EBSD in this same pair of orientations. It also gives no quantitative extent of twinning vs. slip, no mechanism for the transition, and no evidence beyond these two orientations/specimens.

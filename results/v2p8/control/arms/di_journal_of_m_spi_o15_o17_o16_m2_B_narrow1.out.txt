The two SEM panels (F11a: stir casting; F11b: FSP) confirm the stated debris-size contrast: panel a shows coarser, flat flake-like fragments on the order of 20-40 um scattered among smaller particles, while panel b is dominated by fine, loose, agglomerated particulate debris well under ~5 um, with no large flakes. This is consistent with, and adds support to, the earlier-established pathway claim: it is compatible with an abrasive-wear mechanism in which the FA-containing (FSP) surface produces finer wear debris, plausibly reflecting a barrier/fine-debris moderating effect on cutting severity rather than adhesive failure — i.e., it strengthens the qualitative link between debris morphology and the proposed 'FA moderates abrasive severity' mechanism already inferred from groove/crater observations.

What it does NOT settle, and what remains open:
1. This is still single-image, qualitative SEM morphology — no quantified size distribution, particle counts, or statistics; 'flat flakes ~20-40 um' and 'fine <5 um' are visual estimates from one field of view each, not measured populations.
2. The debris composition is not analyzed (no EDS/XRD shown here), so it cannot be confirmed that the fine debris in b originates from fractured FA layers versus matrix material — the compositional-origin question flagged earlier remains unresolved.
3. The two sub-mechanisms (FA blocking asperity penetration vs. fine debris acting as rolling three-body abrasive reducing cutting) are still not discriminated by this debris-morphology comparison; both are equally consistent with coarser-vs-finer debris.
4. Because each panel is a single field of view, whether these debris populations are representative of the whole worn surface (versus localized) is not established.

In short, the debris images corroborate the qualitative size-difference claim and are consistent with the proposed FA-moderated abrasive pathway, but they do not quantify it, do not confirm debris origin/composition, and do not distinguish between the barrier and three-body rolling sub-mechanisms.

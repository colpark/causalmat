Reading F2a and F4a directly (no separate "measurement" text was supplied, so the panels themselves are the evidence):

F2a is the relative-density plot: HP (red) sits essentially flat at ~97.5% (0 wt%) rising to ~100% by 1 wt% AlN and staying ~99–100% through 5 wt%, while PS (blue) falls steadily from ~85% to ~70%. F4a is a fracture-surface SEM of HP1 with a bright, distinctly shaped particle marked "point A," accompanied by an EDS spectrum at that point dominated by a large Si peak with small Al and Zr peaks (and trace N/C/B background).

Combined with the earlier-established result (density + transgranular fracture morphology jointly confirm HP composites, esp. 1 and 5 wt% AlN, are genuinely pore-free and near-fully dense), this EDS point analysis adds compositional identity to what was previously only a topographic/qualitative observation. It settles that the bright, morphologically distinct grain seen at the pore-free HP1 fracture surface is not simply a Si3N4 grain artifact but contains detectable Al and Zr alongside Si — i.e., the fully-densified microstructure retains a chemically distinguishable, Al/Zr-bearing phase (consistent with AlN and/or the Zr-based sintering aid) embedded in the Si-based matrix, rather than the additive having vanished or homogenized away.

This leaves open: EDS at a single point is qualitative, not quantitative, so it cannot give phase fraction, distribution, or whether this phase is continuous (grain-boundary) or isolated; it was only sampled in HP1, so no link across the 0/1/3/5 wt% series or to the density values is established; and it does not identify the exact phase (e.g., AlN vs. a reaction/secondary phase) or confirm it mechanistically causes densification.

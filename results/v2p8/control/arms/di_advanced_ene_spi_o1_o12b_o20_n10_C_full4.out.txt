What the panels show directly: F1a is a Rietveld XRD pattern for the pristine material with calculated, experimental, and Bragg-position curves overlaid and a near-flat difference curve — a well-fit single monoclinic phase, exactly the basis for the earlier "single, well-matched phase" claim. F3b is the full ICP-AES bar chart across five time points (Pristine, 4th, 20th, 60th, 100th cycle): K falls from ~52% (pristine) to ~16–21% by cycle 20 and stays there; Fe stays roughly steady (~20–30%); Mn falls steeply from ~26% (pristine) to near 0% by cycle 60–100; Zn rises from 0 to ~51% by cycle 20 and plateaus near 46–47% through cycle 100.

Read together with the earlier result, this settles that the compositional shift is not just "Zn appears" but a broad cation reshuffling: K is substantially depleted and Mn is nearly eliminated as Zn rises and plateaus, while Fe is comparatively conserved. Combined with the pristine-state XRD identity of a Mn(N)–Fe(C) monoclinic framework, this supports a picture in which cycling drives displacement of K and, strikingly, loss of Mn — one of the two metals defining the very framework XRD characterized — as Zn becomes dominant.

This still leaves open exactly what the earlier qualifications already flagged: no structural (XRD/site-resolved) data exist at cycle 20–100, so whether the original monoclinic framework survives once Mn has nearly vanished, and where Zn physically resides, remain unresolved — if anything, the near-total Mn loss makes framework persistence a live question rather than settling it.

The two panels are the same crater/groove (a) and debris (b) SEM images described in the earlier result, but their captions add a variable the earlier step did not have: panel a is the stir-cast composite, panel b is the FSP composite. Read together, this settles that the "moderated abrasion" morphology (shallower grooves, fine debris, no crater) tracks with the FSP route, while the "more severe abrasion" morphology (deeper grooves, a visible crater) tracks with stir casting — for nominally the same FA-containing composite. That supports a pathway extension: FSP's intense stirring/plastic deformation is plausibly what improves FA dispersion (breaking clusters, reducing agglomerate-triggered craters) relative to stir casting, and better dispersion is what produces the barrier/fine-debris effect already inferred to blunt abrasive cutting. Neither piece alone gives this: the earlier result only established an FA-dispersion-to-wear-morphology link without saying what process achieves good dispersion; the bare captions alone (stir casting vs FSP) only label two images without the morphological interpretation.

What remains open: the panels give no direct dispersion measurement (no particle-distribution imaging/quantification) confirming FSP actually disperses FA better than stir casting here — that is inferred, not shown. FSP also changes matrix grain size, porosity, and interfacial bonding simultaneously, and these could independently affect groove depth/crater formation apart from FA dispersion, so the attribution of the wear difference specifically to FA dispersion (versus other FSP-induced microstructural changes) is not isolated. The single-image, non-quantified caveats from the earlier result (groove depth, debris composition, incomplete surface survey, inability to distinguish blocking vs three-body-rolling sub-mechanisms) still apply unchanged.

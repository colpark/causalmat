The two panels (F4a: [11-20] loading, (1-100) and (0001) side surfaces; F4b: [01-10] loading, (-2110) and (0001) side surfaces), each showing RT, 200, 300, 400 °C micrographs with labeled surface-trace indices, directly support the stated measurement: at RT (and, more clearly in the F4b series, at 200 °C) the labeled traces are {01-12}/{10-12}-type, i.e., twin-plane traces; at 300 and 400 °C the labels switch to {10-10}/{1-100}-type, i.e., prismatic-plane (slip) traces, in both loading orientations. "Deformation band" labels appear at RT, 200, 300, and 400 °C in one or both surfaces of each series, so bands are present across the whole temperature range even as the trace character on the side surfaces changes.

This settles that a temperature-driven change in the crystallographic character of the visible surface traces occurs, from twin-plane to prismatic-plane traces, with band-type features persisting throughout — established across two independent loading orientations, which is broader support than the single-orientation-pair basis of the earlier OM/EBSD correspondence.

It leaves open: whether the traces truly identify twinning versus slip mechanistically (surface trace analysis alone does not confirm crystallography the way EBSD/TEM would); whether the "deformation bands" at each temperature are the same straight/uniform or wavy/shading population described earlier — this figure only marks band location, not internal orientation behavior; and any quantification of trace/band density or the transition temperature's sharpness.

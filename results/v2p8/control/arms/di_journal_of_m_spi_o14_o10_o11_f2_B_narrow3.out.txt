Panel F8c plots apparent quantum yield (AQY, right axis, %) together with the UV-vis absorbance spectrum (left axis) of the Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS sample, both against wavelength from 200-800 nm. The AQY values marked are 18.3% at 400 nm, 15.8% at 420 nm, 6.5% at 450 nm, 4.7% at 500 nm, and 0.19% at 550 nm, and this decline visually tracks the falling absorbance curve, which drops steeply between roughly 380-450 nm (the absorption edge) before leveling off toward 800 nm.

What this settles: for the single best-performing sample (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS), the photocatalytic quantum efficiency for H2 evolution is wavelength-dependent and decreases monotonically as excitation wavelength increases across the five measured points, coinciding with the sample's own absorption edge. This supports the standard photocatalysis inference that light absorption capacity at a given wavelength constrains the achievable quantum yield for this material.

What it leaves open: this panel reports only one sample (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS), so it says nothing about whether AQY wavelength-dependence differs across the Ag:Pd composition series, and it cannot be used to extend or qualify the four-sample photocurrent/H2-rate ranking established earlier — AQY is a separate quantum-efficiency metric, not a repeat of either photocurrent or bulk H2 rate. The co-plotting with absorbance is visual/qualitative only; no fitted correlation, action spectrum overlay quantification, or statistical relationship between AQY and absorbance is given, so "tracking the absorption edge" is a descriptive match of two curves' shapes, not a demonstrated causal or quantitative dependence. Only five discrete wavelengths are sampled, so the curve shape between points, and behavior beyond 550 nm (where AQY is already near zero) or below 400 nm, is not characterized.

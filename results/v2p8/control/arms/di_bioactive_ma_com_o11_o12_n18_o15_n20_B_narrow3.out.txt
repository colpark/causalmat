The two photographic panels (F6b: cross-sectional lumen images after 2 h blood circulation; F6c: photos of the tube/foil after removal) show, across the same five surfaces already characterized by SEM and BIO platelet counts, a consistent gross pattern: 316L SS and PPAmF tubes are essentially fully filled with dark red/black thrombus (occluded lumen, fully stained foil); BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF show partial occlusion, with mixed regions of dark clot and exposed bright/clean foil or lumen; and BVLD/NO-PPAmF shows the lumen and foil largely bright and clean, with only minor peripheral red staining.

This macroscopic, whole-device photographic evidence converges with the SEM+BIO finding: BVLD/NO-PPAmF is again the least thrombogenic surface of the five, and the ordering (316L SS/PPAmF worst, BVLD-PPAmF/NO-PPAmF intermediate, BVLD/NO-PPAmF best) matches the platelet-count gradient already established. This adds a third, independent line of evidence — gross clot/occlusion appearance — that tracks the same ranking as cellular adhesion counts and platelet morphology, supporting that the reduced adhesion/activation on BVLD/NO-PPAmF translates into visibly reduced macroscopic thrombus formation and lumen patency in this ex vivo circulation model.

What it leaves open: these are single, unreplicated photographs per condition with no quantification (no thrombus mass, no percent-occluded-area, no color/pixel analysis) and no statistics, so the visual grading ("nearly clean" vs "partly open" vs "filled") is descriptive only. Color alone cannot distinguish clot composition (fibrin, red cells, platelets), and the panels say nothing about mechanism, only that gross occlusion severity co-varies with the platelet measurements across surfaces.

Panel F2d plots Δσ/σ (%, relative FWHM of the temperature-dependent 2θ peak) vs temperature for SC, PC, and HOC. It shows a sharp discontinuity for all three phases in the same 150-155 K window, but in opposite directions and different magnitudes: SC's peak width drops abruptly from ~100% to ~50% (most clearly the (400) peak) at ~150 K, while PC spikes up to ~260% (242 peak) and HOC spikes to ~128% (400 peak) around 150-155 K, before relaxing at higher temperature.

What this settles: it adds a third, independent XRD-derived observable (peak-broadening behavior, distinct from both peak position/lattice expansion and SEM domain size) that tracks the same three-way split already established — SC behaves smoothly/coherently, while PC and HOC show anomalous, non-monotonic behavior, and the anomaly is concentrated in the same narrow temperature range (~150-155 K) previously linked to curvature/offsets in the (004)/(400) and (242)/(224) peak-shift traces. This strengthens the internal consistency of the SC-vs-(PC,HOC) grouping by showing it recurs across a second XRD-derived quantity, not just peak position.

What it leaves open: it is still a correspondence across only three phase categories, not a dose-response test against domain size or any other continuous variable. Peak-width/broadening reflects strain heterogeneity or domain coherence length, which is itself a proxy possibly related to but not identical to SEM-measured grain/domain size — so this does not independently decouple domain size from phase identity as the driver. The measurement does not establish which of domain size, composition, or crystal-structure differences among SC/PC/HOC causes either the lattice-expansion curvature or the peak-broadening anomaly; it only shows the two effects co-occur near the same temperature.</message>

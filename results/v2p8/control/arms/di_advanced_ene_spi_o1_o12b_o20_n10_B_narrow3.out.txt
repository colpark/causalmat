The three panels (F5d–F5f) are polyhedral structure models for three fixed compositions of increasing Zn content — K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6, K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6, and ZnMnFe(CN)6 — showing the Mn coordination environment stepping down from a full MnN6 octahedron toward under-coordinated MnN3/MnN4, with grey ZnN4 tetrahedra appearing and becoming structurally linked to the orange FeC6 octahedra as Zn content rises.

This supports a site-occupancy claim: as Zn content increases across these modeled compositions, Zn atoms occupy specific N-coordinated tetrahedral sites within the host lattice and form direct polyhedral connectivity to the Fe sublattice, and this is accompanied by (or drives) a change in local Mn coordination geometry consistent with the paper's proposed subsequent phase transition (attributed to Zn2+-intensified Jahn-Teller distortion at Mn).

What it leaves open: these are three static, idealized-stoichiometry structural models, not a continuous or cycle-indexed series, so the panels do not show the transformation actually occurring during cycling, nor its kinetics. It is not established here whether these are experimentally refined structures (e.g., from diffraction) or computational/theoretical constructs, so experimental validation of this exact site-occupancy sequence is unconfirmed by these panels alone. Nothing ties these fixed compositions (0.25, 0.5, 1.0 Zn) to the earlier-reported ~47 at% Zn plateau at cycle 20, so this cannot be used to say which modeled structure, if any, corresponds to that measured plateau composition, or to rule out Zn residing elsewhere in the electrode alongside the framework sites shown.

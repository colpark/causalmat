Reading F4f together with F7b: alongside β-phase content, F4f also plots overall crystallinity (χc), which rises from ~50.0-50.4% (cast film/0 wt%) to ~53.3% at 10 wt%, and critically keeps climbing through the 6-10 wt% window (≈52.7, 52.6, 53.3%) where β-phase content had gone flat/eased (≈80.9-81.2%). Voltage (F7b) is monotonic across the same range: 43.0 V and 62.0 V at 6-10 wt%.

Combined with the earlier finding that β-phase content cannot account for the 6-10 wt% voltage rise, this settles that a phase-related variable — overall crystallinity, not β-fraction specifically — has a trend shape compatible with the full 0-10 wt% voltage increase, including the segment where β-phase decouples. So the combination supports broadening the candidate explanation beyond "β-phase enhancement" to "increasing crystalline content generally" as at least trend-consistent with output across the whole loading range, where β-phase alone was not.

This leaves open: (1) χc is not strictly monotonic either (a slight dip at 8 wt% relative to 6 wt%), so it does not perfectly track voltage's smooth monotonic rise, only approximately; (2) PZT wt% remains a shared driver of both χc and voltage by construction, so this is still correlation, not demonstrated causal pathway; (3) crystallinity (%) and voltage (V) remain different kinds of quantities, so magnitude cannot be inferred; (4) only 6-7 discrete points, insufficient to establish a functional relationship; (5) it is not established that crystallinity itself (versus PZT content directly, or dielectric/piezoelectric coupling from PZT particles) is the operative mechanism for the late-stage voltage gain.

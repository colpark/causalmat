The panels confirm the stated debris description: F11a (stir casting) shows coarse, flat, plate-like flakes on the order of tens of microns (consistent with the ~20-40 um flake description), while F11b (FSP) shows a field dominated by fine, sub-5-um particles clustered into loose agglomerates, with only an occasional larger fragment. This morphological contrast — large flat flakes versus fine agglomerated debris — is consistent with a shift in dominant wear mechanism between the two processing routes: flaky debris is typically associated with delamination/adhesive material removal in coarser, less homogeneous microstructure, while fine agglomerated particulate debris is more typical of abrasive/oxidative wear acting on a finer, more refined microstructure (as FSP is known to produce). So the measurement alone supports that stir-cast and FSP material wear by visibly different debris-generation modes, and by extension plausibly different dominant wear mechanisms.

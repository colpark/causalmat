Reading the five panels together (F3c/d/e are optical micrographs with explicit crystallographic plane-trace labels; F5a/b are EBSD orientation maps, all from the same [11-20]-oriented specimen deformed at RT to 5% plastic strain, viewed on the (0001) and (1-100)/(-2110) side surfaces):

F3c, F3d, and F3e each show a straight "deformation band" running across the field, explicitly labeled with trace lines for {10-12} and {01-12} planes — i.e., these OM images name the specific crystallographic planes that the straight band traces lie on. F5a and F5b are orientation maps of that same [11-20], 5%-strain specimen: (a) shows a green matrix with a sharply bounded, single-colour (dark blue) band plus a small patch of gradually shifting colour at upper right; (b) shows an extended matrix that itself is composed of many adjoining, subtly different shades of blue, i.e. a broad region of continuously varying orientation.

Combined with the earlier established OM/EBSD pairing (straight, plane-trace bands = uniform EBSD colour = twin-like; wavy, contrast-asymmetric bands = continuously shading EBSD colour = kink-like), this new pair lets the two data types anchor an identity that neither alone provides: the straight bands whose EBSD signature is a single uniform colour can now be tied to specific crystallographic planes, {10-12}/{01-12}, and this pairing is shown to already hold at an early deformation stage (5% strain) in the [11-20] specimen. What it leaves open: no plane identity is given for the wavy/continuously-shaded (kink) population; no misorientation magnitude is quantified; and the correspondence is still confined to this one specimen orientation and strain level, so it does not establish generality across other orientations, higher strains, or a causal mechanism for the shading.

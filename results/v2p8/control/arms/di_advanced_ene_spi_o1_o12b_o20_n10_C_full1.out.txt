Reading the two panels directly: F1a is a Rietveld XRD refinement (experimental, calculated, difference, and Bragg-position traces) showing a good fit to a single phase at the pristine state — it gives phase identity, not site-occupancy numbers. F3b is a bar chart of bulk at% content (K, Fe, Mn, Zn) at Pristine, 4th, 20th, 60th, and 100th cycles: Zn rises from 0 to ~50% (20th) and settles ~47% (100th), matching the earlier result. But the chart also shows K falling from ~52% (pristine) to ~21% (100th), Fe holding/rising modestly (~21%→~30%), and Mn — the other framework-forming metal identified by the Rietveld model as N-coordinated in the host — collapsing from ~26% to near 0% by the 100th cycle.

Combined with the earlier settled point (pristine phase = single monoclinic P21/n framework with N-coordinated Mn and C-coordinated Fe), this new compositional detail supports that cycling does not simply add Zn into an unchanged host: one of the two framework metals identified in that host (Mn) is progressively and almost completely depleted from the bulk cathode as Zn rises and K falls, while only Fe is retained. This ties the Zn increase to a coordinated loss of a framework component, not just guest-site filling.

What remains open: both measurements are bulk/ensemble (a static pristine XRD pattern and elemental at%, not site- or cycle-resolved structure), so neither can show whether the P21/n phase, or any ordered framework at all, survives once Mn is nearly gone, nor whether Zn occupies vacated Mn sites, K's former sites, or a distinct phase.

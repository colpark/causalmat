This result settles that, on the two-phase lamellar substrate identified at baseline, the onset of plastic deformation under the first applied strain increment is not uniform across the microstructure but is phase-selective and localized to the lamella interfaces: one lamella type remains featureless while its neighbour develops mottled contrast and interface-line contrast. That is the mechanism (MEC) the measurement supports — an asymmetric, pathway-dependent partitioning of early deformation between the two lamella types, with the interfaces themselves implicated as sites of activity, rather than a picture in which both phases and the bulk lamellae respond equivalently to the first strain step.

What it leaves open is substantial. First, the imaging pair alone does not tell you which lamella is which phase — it does not establish that the featureless one is B2 and the mottled one is FCC, so the phase identity behind the asymmetry is not settled, only the existence of an asymmetry between "type 1" and "type 2" lamellae. Second, it does not distinguish why the asymmetry occurs: whether one phase is intrinsically more slip-resistant, or whether the effect is instead a consequence of elastic/plastic constraint transmitted across the interface. Third, because only two states were compared (undeformed baseline versus the first strain step), the data support only an ordering — deformation features appear after straining that were absent before — and cannot support any claim about a trend, rate, or progression of defect accumulation with increasing strain. Finally, whether this phase-selective, interface-localized pattern continues, intensifies, or reverses at strains beyond the first increment is entirely unaddressed by this measurement and would require additional strain steps to determine.

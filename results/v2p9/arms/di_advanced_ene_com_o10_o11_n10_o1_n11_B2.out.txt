Panel viewed: F1a is a computed-DFT scatter plot of ΔG(Co2+→Co3+) vs ΔG(Co3+→Co4+) for four model (oxy)hydroxides. CoCrOOH has the lowest barrier for the first oxidation step; CoFeOOH has the lowest barrier for the second (Co3+→Co4+) step; CoFeCrOOH sits between them on both axes; Co(OH)2 is highest on both, i.e. hardest to oxidize at either step.

What the combination supports: The earlier XPS/EELS result showed that, as-measured, Fe-containing samples (Co5Fe5, Co5Fe3Cr2) sit at a genuinely higher Co oxidation state than Co-only or Co5Cr5 — but that result was purely observational/static and said nothing about why. The DFT free-energy panel supplies a thermodynamic rationale consistent with that ordering: Fe-containing chemistry (CoFeOOH) computationally lowers the free-energy cost specifically of the higher oxidation step (Co3+→Co4+), while Cr-containing chemistry (CoCrOOH) instead favors the lower step (Co2+→Co3+), and the mixed CoFeCrOOH is intermediate — mirroring the "Fe-containing samples elevated, Cr-only not" pattern from XPS/EELS. Together they support that Fe's role is plausibly to thermodynamically favor formation of higher-valent Co, a mechanistically coherent (charge-transfer-consistent) story rather than mere numerical coincidence.

What remains open: (1) DFT is computed on idealized end-member structures, not directly on the measured Co5Fe5/Co5Cr5/Co5Fe3Cr2 stoichiometries, so it is a modeling analogy, not a measurement on the same samples. (2) ΔG values reflect thermodynamic step favorability, not observed kinetics or an actual reaction pathway confirmed operando. (3) CoFeCrOOH's intermediate position still does not isolate Fe from Fe–Cr synergy. (4) No absolute oxidation-state number is established by either result.

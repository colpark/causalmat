Panel F2c shows three well-resolved, closely-paired anodic/cathodic peaks (~0.55/0.52, 0.89/0.87, 1.08/1.07 V vs Ag/AgCl) whose positions and current magnitudes are essentially unchanged from the 1st through the 20th cycle. That is electrochemical evidence for (a) reversible redox at three distinct potentials and (b) good short-term cycling stability of whatever phase is producing them.

On its own, CV cannot say whether these three couples come from one homogeneous compound or from a mixture of separate phases each contributing its own peak (e.g., an impurity phase overlapping the target material). The earlier FTIR + STEM-EDS result already settled that the imaged particle is a single, homogeneous compound containing V, Fe, C, N, O, Na in co-located bonding environments (cyanide-bridge, vanadyl, framework M-L, water), not a phase mixture.

Combined, the two results support that all three reversible redox couples belong to one homogeneous framework compound rather than being an artifact of segregated impurity phases — i.e., a single PRP-type material intrinsically supports (at least) three distinct, stable redox transitions, consistent with sequential multi-electron (likely V- and Fe-based) activity persisting over cycling.

What remains open: neither measurement assigns which couple belongs to V vs Fe, nor the oxidation-state change or electron count per couple (CV gives only peak potentials/currents, not species identity; FTIR/EDS gave no oxidation-state or stoichiometric information beyond the vanadyl band). Quantitative capacity, coulombic efficiency, or formula/stoichiometry are still undetermined, and fine-scale or unimaged-particle heterogeneity is still not excluded, so long-term/bulk cyclability claims remain unconfirmed.

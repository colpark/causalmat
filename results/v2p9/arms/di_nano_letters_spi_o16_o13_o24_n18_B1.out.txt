Panel F6d (read) shows MD-derived degree-of-oxidation curves over 2 ps: Li-Co3O4 rises steadily to ~91%, Na-Co3S4 climbs more slowly to ~59%, and Na-Co3O4 rises fastest at first but plateaus after ~0.8 ps at only ~36%, well below the other two.

Combined with the earlier SXRD/SAXS result — that Na/PCO's crystalline oxide phase and pore network are only slightly perturbed through the first discharge/charge (no metallic Co, minimal porosity change) — this justifies a mechanistic claim that neither piece supports alone. The experiment shows *what* happens at the lattice and pore scale (retention, minor change) but is silent on *why*; the simulation shows *why* at the atomic/bond scale (Na drives comparatively little Co–O bond-breaking/oxidation in Co3O4, and that limited extent saturates quickly) but has no bearing on whether that translates into observable crystal/pore-scale retention in a real, slowly-cycled electrode. Together they support a coherent causal chain: Na's intrinsically low and self-limiting oxidative attack on Co3O4 (relative to Li, which nearly fully oxidizes) is a plausible atomistic origin for the modest, largely non-destructive structural response measured experimentally — i.e., MEC identification of a bond-breaking mechanism consistent with the observed retention.

What remains open: the MD timescale (2 ps) is many orders of magnitude shorter than electrochemical cycling, so the link is qualitative/directional, not quantitatively matched to the "small" SXRD/SAXS changes; the simulation doesn't model the porous architecture itself, so it can't confirm the pore-network result specifically; and it still doesn't resolve the earlier surface-vs-bulk ambiguity.

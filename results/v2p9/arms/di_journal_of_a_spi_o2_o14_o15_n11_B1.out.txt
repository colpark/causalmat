Panel F7b (Sample S2, sintered at 1700 C) shows a protruding cBN grain sitting beside a deep (~2 um) empty socket, i.e., a pull-out defect, while neighboring alpha-Al2O3 grains display clean, well-defined facets. This is SEM data for S2 itself.

Combined with Observation A (XRD, F2b: S2 shows only the starting phases alpha-Al2O3, cBN, ZrO2 across 1550-1700C, no new reflections), the pairing now closes the specific gap the earlier result flagged: there was previously no SEM interface data for S2. Now we have both bulk phase identity and local interface morphology for the same sample. Together they support that in S2, the absence of any new reaction phase co-occurs with poor local cBN-binder adhesion (grain pull-out leaving a deep socket), in contrast to S1 (Observation B, F7a), which shows tight, gap-free, cleanly cleaved cBN/SiAlON boundaries. So the combination strengthens the suggestion that "no detectable phase reaction" in the Al2O3-based binder is associated with weaker interfacial bonding, and that S1's superior bonding is not obviously explained by extra reaction phases (since S2 also lacks them) but instead tracks with binder chemistry (SiAlON vs Al2O3-based) more broadly.

What remains open: no XRD data for S1 is shown here, so we still cannot confirm what phase set (reacted or unreacted) accompanies S1's strong bonding, nor rule out that S1 also lacks new phases yet still bonds well — which would mean phase reaction is irrelevant to bonding quality rather than causally linked to it. Also, a single pulled-out grain in one micrograph could reflect fracture-surface/polishing artifact rather than intrinsic interfacial weakness, so the causal read (no reaction → weak bond) is still not secured, only suggested.

This measurement (really, the prior finding taken as settled) supports only a limited, sample-specific conclusion, not a general claim about a shared PRP/behavior_class linking phase reactivity and interface quality.

What it supports: the two binder systems examined here differ from one another in two respects. The S2 (Al2O3-based) binder shows no detectable new phases by XRD across 1550-1700 C — i.e., alpha-Al2O3, cBN, and ZrO2 persist as the only phases, indicating chemical/phase stability of that system over this temperature range. The S1 (SiAlON) binder shows, by SEM, tight, gap-free boundaries between cBN grains and the SiAlON matrix with cleaved rather than pulled-out facets, indicating good local interfacial bonding in that system. Taken together, this justifies the modest claim that binder chemistry (SiAlON vs. Al2O3-based) is associated with differences in both bulk phase behavior and interfacial morphology — i.e., that these two aspects of behavior_class are not fixed but vary with binder composition.

What it leaves open: because the XRD evidence and the SEM evidence come from two different samples (S2 and S1 respectively), the data cannot establish any single-sample relationship between "no new phase formation" and "gap-free interfaces." It is not shown whether S1 also lacks new phases, or whether S2's interfaces are gap-free or gappy. The apparent pairing of "unreacted phases" with "good bonding" is confounded with binder identity — every other difference between the two binder systems could equally explain the interface morphology, independent of phase reaction. Additionally, phase identity (bulk, crystallographic) and interfacial morphology (local, microstructural) are different observation types, so one cannot stand in for the other. In short, this evidence establishes that behavior differs across binder systems along two axes, but does not establish a causal or even correlational link between "no phase reaction" and "good interface quality" within a single system — that would require matched XRD and SEM data on the same sample (either S1 or S2).

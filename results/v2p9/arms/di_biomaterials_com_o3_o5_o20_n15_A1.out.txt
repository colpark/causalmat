The image (panel labeled "a") is a cumulative drug-release curve, not an antibacterial assay, even though the printed caption promises "anti-bacteria effect." It plots released drug amount (ug) vs. release time (h): ~0 at t=0, ~340 ug at 8 h, ~670 ug at 24 h, ~730 ug at 48 h, ~780 ug at 72 h, and a plateau near 790–795 ug from 96 h through 168 h. Error bars are present and grow substantial in absolute terms (roughly ±100–150 ug) at later time points, though the mean trend is a clear rapid burst release phase (0–24 h, capturing over 80% of total released drug) followed by a slow, decelerating release that essentially plateaus after ~72 h.

What this settles: the measurement supports that the material/system exhibits classic biphasic sustained-release kinetics — an initial burst release followed by a plateau — with a cumulative release ceiling around ~795 ug over one week. This is consistent with a porous or degradable drug-carrier matrix releasing its loaded payload progressively rather than instantaneously.

What it leaves open: this panel alone provides no evidence about antibacterial activity, efficacy against any organism, or a dose-response relationship between released amount and bacterial inhibition — that claim belongs to a different panel (likely "b") not included here. It also doesn't establish total loading amount/encapsulation efficiency, the identity of the "drug" (or its relation to "PRP" value), reproducibility across replicates given the wide error bars, or whether the released material remains bioactive after release. Any claim connecting this release curve to antibacterial or PRP-value conclusions cannot be verified from this crop alone.

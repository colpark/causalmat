The panel (Fig. 1a) is a cumulative in‑vitro drug‑release curve, not an antibacterial assay: x‑axis "Release time (h)" (0–168+), y‑axis "Released amount of drug (µg)" (0–1000), single mean±SD trace with points at 0, 8, 24, 48, 72, 120, 168 h. The plotted values match the stated numbers almost exactly: ~340 µg at 8 h, ~670–730 µg at 24–48 h, ~780 µg at 72 h, and a plateau near ~795–800 µg by 120–168 h, with error bars that are substantial (roughly ±100–150 µg) and overlapping across the later time points.

What this settles: the material/carrier releases its loaded drug with an early burst (roughly 40–45% of total cumulative release already out by 8 h), followed by a slower phase, and release essentially plateaus by ~120–168 h — i.e., the system behaves as a burst‑plus‑sustained release depot over about one week, consistent with the reported numbers. This is a legitimate, self-consistent measurement of drug-release kinetics/mass balance.

What it leaves open: the printed caption fragment for this panel is "...and its anti-bacteria effect," but nothing in this crop shows bacteria, inhibition zones, CFU counts, or any biological readout — panel a is purely the chemical/mass release curve. So this measurement cannot by itself establish antibacterial efficacy (PRP value/effect); that would require the companion panel(s) referenced by the same caption, which are not included here. Also unresolved from this image alone: drug identity, loading dose/percentage released, and statistical significance given the wide overlapping error bars at later timepoints.

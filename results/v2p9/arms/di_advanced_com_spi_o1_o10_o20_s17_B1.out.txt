The tensile data show a monotonic decline with Ag loading (79.3 -> 78.3 -> 74.4 -> 73.5 MPa from PAEN to BST@Ag5%), a modest but consistent dose-dependent drop rather than the improvement one might expect from "better interfacial bonding." Read alongside the FTIR+SEM result (silane grafting present, tight/gapless particle-matrix contact in BST@Ag1%), the combination supports a more precise claim: whatever interfacial intimacy the silane chemistry produces, it is not sufficient to raise or even preserve bulk tensile strength — mechanical performance instead tracks filler content, degrading as Ag/BST loading increases. This lets you rule out the naive inference that "good SEM contact = mechanical reinforcement": the two properties are dissociated at 1% loading (best SEM image, but strength already down from 79.3 to 78.3) and diverge further at higher loadings even though nothing suggests the silane chemistry disappears. So the combination settles that any interfacial-bonding benefit from grafting, if real, is being outweighed by filler-loading effects (e.g., stress concentration, reduced effective matrix cross-section, particle agglomeration at higher wt%) on tensile strength specifically.

What remains open: (1) whether the small strength loss at 1% (79.3->78.3) reflects a real bonding trade-off or is within experimental scatter (error bars overlap); (2) whether other mechanical/functional properties (toughness, elongation, modulus, antibacterial performance — the paper's actual application target) show the reinforcement that tensile strength does not, since PRP/interface quality is often optimized for properties other than tensile strength; (3) whether the decline is driven by Ag content, BST content, or their combination, since only one filler series is shown; and (4) the SEM/FTIR limitations already noted (different specimens, qualitative only) still stand — this new data cannot resolve whether the observed morphology is causally tied to chemistry.

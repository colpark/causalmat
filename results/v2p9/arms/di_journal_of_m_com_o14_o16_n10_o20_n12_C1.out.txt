This combined EDS/HRTEM result settles the location question at a qualitative level: it justifies concluding that the carbon-rich signal seen in the EDS surface map genuinely corresponds to a real carbon phase (curved carbon layers, d=0.386 nm) that physically sits at the edge of milled C10 alloy particles, adjacent to crystalline CeMg12/Y5Mg24 alloy grains and an amorphous band. In other words, the mechanism this supports is a physical "carbon coats/sits on alloy particle surface" picture — carbon is not an artifact or a bulk-mixed contaminant, but an edge/surface-localized phase, consistent with where EDS shows C and Co signal concentrating (surfaces, edges, some faces).

What it does not settle: (1) representativeness — the HRTEM evidence comes from one local edge region on one particle, so it cannot establish that this same alloy-amorphous-carbon arrangement holds at every surface, edge, or face location where the EDS map shows C/Co signal; it is a single corroborating example, not a survey. (2) Resolution mismatch — EDS is a coarse areal map and HRTEM is atomic-resolution, so pairing them only qualitatively corroborates that "carbon is here" at the same general location; it cannot yield a quantified or statistically representative distribution of the carbon phase at fine scale. (3) Co placement specifically — no Co-containing lattice fringe was reported in the HRTEM observation, so while C and Co signals co-locate in the coarser EDS map, this result does not demonstrate that Co is atomically dispersed within or bonded to the carbon phase; that remains an open question requiring further atomic-scale evidence (e.g., a Co-bearing fringe or spectroscopic confirmation at the same site).</message>

This step settles two separate, narrow facts and nothing about their combination. On its own, Observation A (XRD, F2c) supports a phase-transformation-onset conclusion: the hBN marker near 26 deg is absent at 1400 C but present at 1500 and 1600 C, indicating the hBN-forming transformation begins somewhere between 1400 and 1500 C. Independently, Observation B (SEM, F7a) supports a good-bonding conclusion, but only for the S1 (cBN-grain/SiAlON) boundary region: the micrograph shows cleaved facets and no gaps, consistent with sound interfacial bonding at that specimen and site.

What this does not establish is any causal or even correlative link between hBN formation and interface bonding quality. The two observations cannot be joined because: F2c never identifies which composite (S1, S2, or S3) was scanned at each temperature, so there is no way to confirm the XRD series was even run on the same specimen as the SEM image; F7a shows only the S1 interface, not the S2 or S3 interfaces where the paper reportedly finds weak bonding, so no interface-quality comparison exists at a site where hBN formation would plausibly matter; no image here shows microstructure of the same region at multiple temperatures, so nothing confirms hBN is the cause of any interface change; and the two data types (a categorical temperature series vs. a single static snapshot) are not the same kind of measurement and cannot be cross-checked or plotted together.

So the justified conclusion is limited to: hBN onset occurs between 1400-1600 C (some composite, unspecified), and S1's boundary looks well-bonded (one temperature, unspecified). Whether hBN formation affects bonding at S1, S2, or S3 remains entirely open.

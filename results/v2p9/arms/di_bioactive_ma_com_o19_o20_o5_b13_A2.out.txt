Panel F4b (UV-vis absorbance spectra, 200-800 nm) shows a bare TiO2 curve with a sharp absorption edge near ~410-420 nm and negligible absorbance beyond it, versus three Er0.5CexTi-O co-doped curves (x=0.1, 0.2, 0.3) that all sit well above TiO2 in absorbance from the UV region through the edge and tail visibly into the 420-800 nm visible range, with small sharp peaks near ~525 nm and ~650 nm superimposed on the tail (consistent with Er f-f transitions rather than band-edge absorption). Among the three doped samples, the Ce0.2 (red) and Ce0.3 (yellow) curves largely overlap and sit at or near the top of the group in the UV shoulder and edge region, with Ce0.1 (blue) slightly lower.

What this justifies: the measurement supports that Ce/Er co-doping red-shifts and broadens the optical absorption of TiO2 relative to undoped TiO2, giving genuine visible-light absorption extending past 420 nm — consistent with a narrowed effective optical bandgap or sub-gap states from doping. It also supports that co-doped samples absorb more strongly than TiO2 across the visible range shown.

What it leaves open: a single absorbance spectrum does not establish a quantitative bandgap value (no Tauc plot is shown here), does not by itself prove enhanced photocatalytic or biological activity, and does not cleanly resolve which composition (0.1 vs 0.2 vs 0.3) is unambiguously "best" for absorption — the curves for 0.2 and 0.3 nearly overlap, and the claim that "Ce0.2 absorbs most" is only marginally supported by this panel alone, more a qualitative reading than a clearly separated ranking. Causal attribution of any downstream functional benefit (e.g., photocatalytic or antibacterial performance) to this absorption shift cannot be settled by this panel; it only characterizes optical absorption, not mechanism or performance.

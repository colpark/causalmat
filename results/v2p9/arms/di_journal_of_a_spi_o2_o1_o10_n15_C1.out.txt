This measurement settles the phase identity of the zirconia in each coating, but not its structural fraction (STR). Specifically, the combined XRD evidence (Observation A's phase labeling plus Observation B's peak-shape diagnostics — the small ~43° (012) peak and the split reflections near 35°, 60°, and 74° seen only in the YSZ pattern) justifies the conclusion that YSZ is predominantly tetragonal zirconia (t-ZrO2) while Al2O3-YSZ is cubic zirconia (c-ZrO2). The peak-splitting/shoulder features are the specific diagnostic that distinguishes the lower-symmetry tetragonal pattern from the higher-symmetry cubic one, so B corroborates and explains why A assigns those phase labels. Both patterns also show no marked Al2O3 reflection.

What this leaves open: no quantitative fraction of tetragonal versus cubic (or any residual monoclinic) phase is established for either sample — the data support a qualitative, predominant-phase call ("t-ZrO2" and "c-ZrO2"), not a Rietveld-type or peak-ratio quantification of phase content. It also does not explain the mechanism driving the tetragonal-vs-cubic outcome (e.g., yttria content, Al incorporation into the lattice, or thermal history are not addressed). The absence of a visible Al2O3 peak does not indicate where the Al2O3 actually resides (e.g., amorphous, at grain boundaries, or dissolved into the zirconia lattice) — only that no crystalline Al2O3 reflection is seen. Finally, both observations derive from the same single XRD panel (F1) on the same samples, so they constitute one level-of-description confirmation (label vs. peak-shape detail) rather than two independent measurements, meaning no independent corroborating technique (e.g., Raman, TEM) has weighed in on phase fraction or Al distribution.</message>

This measurement settles only a qualitative, paired co-occurrence between microstructure (DSC/SEM feature size) and mechanical comparison (tensile curves) — it does not establish a quantitative or causal relationship.

What it supports: In the single EPT vs. no-EPT pair examined, the EPT-treated sample (F10d) shows a markedly finer, more uniform feature size (~0.3-0.8 µm) than the untreated samples (F10b,c, coarser at the same 2 µm scale), and this same EPT condition's tensile curve (F13 vs F12) differs from the untreated case by a lower yield knee (~350→~330 MPa), a higher max stress (~420→~447 MPa), and a longer strain to failure (~0.095→~0.15). So the microstructural refinement and the property shift co-occur under EPT — that association is justified.

What it leaves open: (1) This is a single before/after pair, not a series, so no quantitative feature-size-to-property relationship can be drawn — only "these two things changed together once." (2) SEM feature size is a static, localized geometric measurement while the tensile curve is a bulk mechanical response; the panels don't confirm the same specimen location/volume was sampled for both. (3) The direction of change is not simply "finer microstructure = stronger material" — yield strength actually decreased while UTS and elongation increased, a split outcome the SEM images alone cannot explain. (4) EPT likely triggers multiple concurrent microstructural changes (grain refinement, dislocation density reduction, texture change) beyond the feature size shown, so the tensile shift cannot be attributed to feature size alone versus these confounded factors. In short: co-occurrence, not causation or quantification, is what this measurement can support.

F4a shows a dense, tangled dislocation network filling an FCC(L1₂) lamella (caption: "dislocation configurations in the FCC (L1₂) phase"), and F4c shows that same kind of tangled network capped by a distinct, darker, continuous band running along a curved lamella boundary, arrowed and labeled "dislocations pileup." Both panels visualize direct TEM evidence of dislocation content, not just diffuse mottled/line contrast.

Read together with the earlier settled result (phase-selective, interface-localized onset of plasticity at the first strain step), this pair advances the story in two ways. First, it resolves part of qualification (1): the caption ties the dense, deformed lamella explicitly to the FCC/L1₂ phase, supporting that the mottled/active lamella in the earlier observation is the FCC phase rather than B2. Second, it speaks to qualification (2): the pile-up specifically abutting the lamella boundary shows dislocations generated within the FCC lamella are being halted at the interface, which favors an interface/constraint contribution to the asymmetry (the boundary acts as a barrier to slip transmission) rather than the asymmetry being purely an intrinsic-slip-resistance effect confined within one phase.

What remains open: this is still a single, undated micrograph pair, not tied to a specific strain level in a series, so it does not establish whether pile-up density grows monotonically with strain or by how much. It also doesn't show whether dislocations ever cross into B2 (permanent barrier vs. transient bottleneck), doesn't rule out intrinsic CRSS differences as a co-contributing cause, and doesn't demonstrate this behavior is representative beyond the imaged region.

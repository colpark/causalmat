Panel F2b shows a three-stage strain-hardening-rate curve: a steep collapse from ~85 to ~14 GPa over the first ~0.009 true strain (region I), a slow decay to ~5 GPa out to ~0.058 (region II), then a turn negative (region III, onset of instability/necking).

Combined with the earlier imaging result — that the first strain increment already shows phase-selective, interface-localized plasticity (one lamella featureless, its neighbor mottled with interface line contrast) — this macroscopic curve supplies the strain-resolved mechanical trend the imaging pair alone could not. The steep region-I drop spans essentially the same strain interval as the imaged "first strain step," so the combination supports that the onset of asymmetric, interface-localized slip is what is consuming most of the material's initial hardening capacity: the rapid loss of hardening rate is consistent with (and gives mechanical weight to) the imaging-based claim that deformation is phase-selective from the start rather than uniform. The subsequent slow decline (region II) further supports that some extra-hardening process continues well past the first step, rather than switching off immediately, extending the earlier result's "before/after one step" ordering into a genuine multi-point trend.

What remains open: the curve cannot say which lamella (A/B, presumably B2/FCC) carries the load or whether the imaged asymmetric contrast pattern itself persists, intensifies, or reverses through region II — that requires further imaging at those strains. It also does not distinguish intrinsic phase slip resistance from interface/constraint effects as the cause of the asymmetry, and region III's negative rate reflects macroscopic instability, not microstructural asymmetry per se.

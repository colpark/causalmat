The PL spectra (Fig. 6, F7) show the 390-420 nm emission band — the region generally attributed in TiO2 to oxygen-vacancy/defect states and photoinduced electron-hole recombination — falling monotonically from pure TiO2, to Er0.5Ti-O (about half), to Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O (lowest). That is, doping with Er, and further with Er+Ce, progressively quenches this recombination-related luminescence.

Read alone, PL only shows that the doped samples behave differently from bare TiO2 optically; it cannot say whether that difference reflects true incorporation of Er/Ce into the oxide's electronic structure or some other surface artifact, and it says nothing about oxidation states or elemental identity. The earlier EDS+XPS result, read alone, established Er's chemical identity (Er3+) and its association with a Ti-O framework, but explicitly could not distinguish lattice incorporation from mere surface adsorption, since XPS is surface-sensitive and EDS samples a larger volume.

Combined, the two results support a stronger claim than either alone: Er3+ (confirmed by XPS to be chemically integrated with the Ti-O oxide, not just an EDS artifact) is also functionally coupled to TiO2's electronic/defect structure, since its presence correlates with a systematic, dose-like suppression of the PL band tied to charge-carrier recombination. This makes "surface adsorption with no electronic interaction" less plausible as an explanation for Er's effect, since chemical identity plus a consistent optical perturbation together point toward genuine interaction with the oxide's defect states.

What remains open: the mechanism of quenching (electron trapping vs. new recombination centers vs. bandgap shift) is unspecified; Ce's oxidation state is still unestablished by any observation; and PL quenching alone still cannot definitively rule out surface-level (rather than lattice-internal) dopant siting, since surface species can also act as electron scavengers.

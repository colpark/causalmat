Panel F2c shows CV curves (1st, 2nd, 5th, 10th, 15th, 20th cycles) over 0.4–1.2 V vs. Ag/AgCl at 0.1 A/g (as noted, likely a scan-rate/current-density unit typo in the caption). Three well-separated anodic/cathodic peak pairs appear at roughly 0.55/0.52, 0.89/0.87, and 1.08/1.07 V, and the curves for all six cycles overlay almost exactly, with only minor amplitude drift (slightly lower current in the 15th/20th traces).

What this justifies: the material undergoes at least three distinct, quasi-reversible faradaic (redox) processes rather than simple double-layer capacitance, since discrete, paired anodic/cathodic peaks are present rather than a rectangular capacitive box. The near-superposition of curves across 20 cycles supports electrochemical reversibility and short-term cycling stability of these redox transitions at the CV timescale — the peak positions and shapes do not shift or disappear, arguing against gross electrode degradation or irreversible phase change over this span.

What it leaves open: this is CV evidence only, not long-duration galvanostatic cycling, so nothing here speaks to capacity retention over hundreds/thousands of cycles, coulombic efficiency, or rate capability. The identity of the redox couples (which ion/oxidation state each peak pair corresponds to) cannot be assigned from CV shape alone — that requires complementary structural/spectroscopic data (e.g., ex situ XRD/XPS). The measurement also says nothing about performance outside the 0.4–1.2 V window, nor about how PRP/value (whatever downstream metric this feeds) scales with these redox features versus other contributions like double-layer capacitance.

Both panels are TEM bright-field micrographs from the same specimen (scale bar 0.5 μm in each), both labeled "Dislocations pileup." Panel F4a shows a dense, tangled dislocation network filling a grain/subgrain interior, captioned as being within the FCC (L1₂) phase, with the pile-up label pointing to the boundary region at lower edge of the network. Panel F4c shows a similarly dense dark dislocation band running along a curved lamella boundary, with an arrow explicitly pointing to that band as the "pileup."

What this measurement supports: direct micrographic evidence that dislocations generated during deformation have accumulated into a locally dense tangle against a lamella (phase) boundary in the FCC/L1₂ constituent — i.e., the boundary is acting as an obstacle to dislocation glide, consistent with a classic pile-up/blocking mechanism (boundary strengthening, local stress concentration at the interface).

What it leaves open: a static TEM image cannot establish the causal pathway from this local pile-up to any macroscopic property (e.g., yield strength, work hardening rate, or crack nucleation) — it shows correlation of dense dislocations with a boundary, not that this pile-up is quantitatively responsible for observed bulk behavior. It also does not specify dislocation character/Burgers vector, the applied strain or stress at which this was imaged, whether this region is representative of the bulk or a locally selected hot spot, and whether the boundary is a lamella/twin boundary or a true phase boundary. So it substantiates "pile-up occurs at this boundary type" but not "pile-up is the operative rate-limiting mechanism" for any specific mechanical outcome.

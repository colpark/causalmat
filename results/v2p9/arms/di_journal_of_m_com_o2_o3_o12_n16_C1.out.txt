This measurement settles that the observed dispersion pattern (broad spread of SiCp particles with occasional local clusters, no gross segregation) is not an artifact of either optical or SEM imaging alone, since two independent modalities with different contrast mechanisms converge on the same qualitative picture. That supports treating "broadly uniform dispersion with sparse local agglomeration" as a genuine microstructural feature that a PRP/value claim tied to particle distribution can lean on as real rather than an imaging quirk — i.e., it is reasonable evidence for the qualitative existence of the dispersion state, and cross-modal agreement raises confidence above what either technique would give alone.

It does not settle anything quantitative about PRP/value. There is no clustering index, nearest-neighbor statistic, or areal fraction, so no numeric uniformity or degree-of-homogeneity value can be derived from it. The optical (panels F1b-F1f) and SEM (single X150 field) observations sample different areas and different numbers of fields, so this is not a matched, apples-to-apples comparison — it is agreement in kind, not in extent. Particle size distribution and the cause/mechanism of the local clusters (e.g., grain-boundary pinning) remain unaddressed; the result only shows that clusters occur alongside broad spread, not why. Finally, no statistical test or repeat imaging was performed, so "near-uniform" is a visual/qualitative impression, not a measured value — any PRP/value that depends on a quantified dispersion metric remains open and unresolved by this result.

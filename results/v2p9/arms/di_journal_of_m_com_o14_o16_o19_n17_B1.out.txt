Images: F7c and F9c are optical (etched, 50 um scale bar) micrographs of fully equiaxed grain structures with clear boundary contrast; F9c's grains are visibly coarser than F7c's, and several grains in F9c show internal lamellar/striated features consistent with retained twin remnants. F10d is an SEM image (2 um scale bar) showing a much finer, clearly equiaxed grain structure with distinct boundary contrast — importantly, it does NOT show the ambiguous "deformed/relief, no clear boundaries" appearance that the earlier result flagged as SEM's failure mode for very fine grains (as in that work's panel "a").

What the combination settles: The earlier result established that SEM's topographic/grooving contrast can fail to resolve genuine fine equiaxed grains (misreading them as undifferentiated/deformed) but reliably agrees with EBSD once grains are in the ~1.5-4 um-and-up range. Because F10d's SEM image shows unambiguous equiaxed grain contrast rather than the deformed-relief look, this new measurement can be read as a real, resolvable ~1 um grain population rather than an SEM artifact. Combined with F7c and F9c, this supports a genuine progressive coarsening pathway with holding/processing time (25s < 30s < 40s at 10 min): fine (~1 um) newly recrystallized grains growing into a 5-8 um population and then a 15-30 um population, i.e., grain growth following recrystallization completion.

What it leaves open: whether this growth is normal/continuous or abnormal (twin-boundary-assisted, given F9c's twinned remnants) is not settled — only three widely spaced, non-EBSD-validated time points are sampled, so no continuous growth curve or mechanism can be confirmed. Also, F10d (SEM) vs F7c/F9c (optical) is a cross-technique comparison never validated by EBSD in this new data, so the earlier caveat about different physical detection bases still applies to trusting absolute size continuity across the series.

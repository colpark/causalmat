This measurement settles that Er detected in the particles is present specifically as Er3+, chemically associated with a Ti-O oxide framework — not merely an element whose presence rests on an unconfirmed EDS peak. The support comes from combining the two techniques: EDS establishes elemental presence (Ti, O, dominant C, plus small Er ~1.4 keV and Ce ~4.8 keV peaks), while XPS supplies oxidation-state confirmation via matched binding energies (Ti 2p3/2 458.5, 2p1/2 464.2 eV; O1s 529.0/531.2/533.0 eV; Er 4d 168.8 eV, diagnostic of Er3+). Neither technique alone would justify the "Er present as Er3+" conclusion — EDS alone gives only an energy peak, and XPS alone gives a binding energy without independent elemental confirmation from a different method; together they corroborate each other for this one element.

This leaves several things open. First, Ce's oxidation state (Ce3+, Ce4+, or a mixture) is not established, since the XPS data reported covers only Ti 2p, O1s and Er 4d regions with no Ce binding-energy scan. Second, it does not establish that Er (or Ce) is incorporated into the TiO2 lattice rather than simply surface-adsorbed, because XPS is surface-sensitive while EDS samples a larger interaction volume, so co-detection doesn't guarantee the same spatial origin or phase. Third, the dominant carbon peak in EDS is unaddressed by XPS (no C1s data reported), leaving open whether it reflects genuine sample chemistry or contamination from the grid/support/residue. Finally, because the EDS Er and Ce peaks are described as small, the elemental-presence side of the evidence is comparatively weak, limiting overall confidence even where the Er3+ oxidation-state claim is supported.

Panel F4c (BST@Ag1%/PAEN, SEM at 1 μm scale) shows a fracture-surface-like view dominated by a continuous, wavy polymer matrix with soft, blurred contours; no discrete embedded particles with sharp edges are visible, and no voids, cracks, or gaps are apparent at any particle-matrix boundary in this field of view.

What this supports: the measurement is consistent with the claim that in the BST@Ag1% film, filler particles are well wetted and encapsulated by the polymer matrix — i.e., the matrix conforms tightly to the filler surface, with the fuzzy/blurred outlines suggesting a diffuse or coated boundary rather than a mechanically distinct, sharply demarcated interface. The absence of visible gaps or pull-out cavities argues against gross interfacial debonding or poor adhesion at the resolution and location imaged.

What it leaves open: this is a single SEM crop at one magnification and one location, so it cannot establish that this "no gaps" condition holds uniformly across the whole film, at higher magnification, or in cross-section rather than at this particular fracture/surface plane. The image alone does not identify actual particle boundaries with certainty (the blurring could reflect true encapsulation or simply imaging/charging artifacts common in SEM of polymer-ceramic composites), does not quantify interfacial strength or bonding chemistry, and provides no compositional (EDS) confirmation that the smooth-looking regions are matrix rather than agglomerated filler. Comparison to other Ag loadings or to a filler-free control is not present in this single panel, so whether this "no-gap" morphology is specific to the 1% loading or general to the series remains undetermined from this image alone.

The F3c panel confirms the stated measurement exactly: A/L at 808 nm rises linearly with concentration (12.5–200 µg mL⁻¹) with R² = 0.9999 and mass extinction coefficient α = 6.4 L g⁻¹ cm⁻¹.

What this adds when combined with the XRD/Raman phase call: on its own, the optical linearity only shows that the bulk suspension obeys Beer–Lambert behavior at 808 nm — it says nothing about which crystalline phase is doing the absorbing, since a mixture, an amorphous coating, or a minor phase could in principle dominate the signal. On its own, the phase-ID result (Bi majority, Bi2O3 trace, mutually consistent) says nothing about optical/photothermal behavior. Put together, because Bi is established as the overwhelmingly dominant crystalline and vibrational phase, the clean, single-slope linear response (no curvature, no multi-component fit needed) is best attributed to a single well-defined absorbing species — the hexagonal Bi phase — rather than to a superposition of two optically distinct phases. This supports using A/L as a reliable proxy for sample concentration and treating α = 6.4 L g⁻¹ cm⁻¹ as an intrinsic, phase-attributable extinction coefficient for this Bi-based material at 808 nm, which is the basis needed for downstream photothermal dosing/efficiency calculations.

What remains open: whether the minor Bi2O3 phase contributes any measurable fraction of the 808 nm absorption cannot be excluded or quantified from either measurement; the linear regime is only demonstrated over 12.5–200 µg mL⁻¹; and morphology, particle size, and the spatial arrangement between the two phases remain entirely unconstrained by this combination.

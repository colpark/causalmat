Panel F7b (sample S2, sintered 1700 C) shows a fracture surface with a protruding grain (boxed, labeled cBN) situated directly beside a deep, roughly hemispherical socket ~2 um across, plus a separate Al2O3 grain (boxed, labeled alpha-Al2O3) that presents a clean, flat, faceted face.

What this justifies: the matched protruding-grain/empty-socket pair is classic evidence of grain pull-out — a cBN grain debonded from its surrounding matrix and was plucked out (leaving the socket) while the still-embedded neighbor grain remained proud of the fracture plane. That supports an intergranular fracture mode with relatively weak cBN-matrix interfacial bonding at this sintering condition. The clean facets on the Al2O3 grain are consistent with fracture running along, or exposing, crystallographic growth faces of that phase, i.e., also intergranular but through a well-facetted, presumably well-sintered grain, rather than transgranular cleavage through the grain interior.

What it leaves open: this is a single field of view from one sample (S2, 1700 C), so it cannot establish that pull-out/weak-interface behavior is representative of the whole sample, of other sintering temperatures, or of bulk toughness/strength. It gives no quantitative measure of interfacial bond strength, pull-out density, or fracture toughness, and it does not by itself distinguish whether the facets on the Al2O3 grain reflect fracture-plane selection versus pre-existing growth morphology. Any generalization beyond "this locale shows cBN pull-out next to a facetted Al2O3 grain" would require additional panels/samples for corroboration.

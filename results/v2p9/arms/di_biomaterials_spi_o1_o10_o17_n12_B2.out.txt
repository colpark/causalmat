Panel F9 (Fig. 9, western blot): HIF-1α band is faint and similar for MBG and 2Co-MBG, and clearly stronger for 5Co-MBG, with equal α-tubulin loading controls across all three lanes.

Combined with the established composition/release result (Co/Ca and released Co2+ both order 5Co > 2Co > 0Co at every timepoint), this justifies the conclusion that scaffold-incorporated cobalt, via the Co2+ it releases, produces a downstream cellular molecular effect in BMSCs — HIF-1α stabilization — and that this effect tracks the same ordering at least at its top end: the highest-Co/highest-release group (5Co) shows visibly elevated HIF-1α over the other two. Since Co2+-driven HIF-1α stabilization (via prolyl-hydroxylase inhibition) is a known biological mechanism, this supports that the previously established composition→release ordering has a plausible functional/mechanistic consequence in cells, not just a chemical one.

What it leaves open: (1) The blot is qualitative/unquantified (no densitometry), so this cannot establish a graded dose-response — it only distinguishes "high" (5Co) from "low/absent" (2Co ≈ MBG); the intermediate release level for 2Co did not yield a visibly intermediate HIF-1α band, so the match with the three-way ordering is really two-way (5Co vs. the rest). (2) The release data are a time course (days 1/3/7) but the blot appears to be a single timepoint with no stated day, so no specific released concentration can be linked to the HIF-1α level. (3) This is correlational: it does not prove Co2+ itself (rather than some other scaffold property) causes the HIF-1α change, and no replicates/statistics are shown.

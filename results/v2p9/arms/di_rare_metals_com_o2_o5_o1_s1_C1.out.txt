This measurement (the paired XRD/DSC comparison already established) settles that the La-containing Al-Cu-Mn alloy forms a distinct compound not present in the Sm alloy. It supports this via two independent lines of evidence converging: a structural signal (extra XRD reflections at ~21-37, 50, and 67 deg appearing only in the La pattern) and a thermal signal (a separate DSC dissolution shoulder B at ~638 C beyond the Al2Cu-related peak A at ~605 C, again only in La), with the Sm alloy showing neither. Because a structural method and a thermal method independently point to the same conclusion in the same sample, and both are absent together in the other sample, this co-occurrence is stronger evidence for a real, La-specific microstructural feature than either technique alone would provide.

What it leaves open: it does not establish the specific identity of the new phase - the claim that it is Al6Cu6La with a cubic NaZn13-type structure rests on comparison to literature DSC values, not on indexing these XRD reflections directly. It does not quantify how much of the new phase forms - only that its structural and thermal signatures are detectable. It does not prove that Sm forms zero of any analogous phase - absence of detected reflections/thermal events in Sm alloy could simply mean any such phase is below the detection limits of both XRD and DSC, not that it is truly absent. Finally, the linkage between the two measurements is one of co-occurrence in the same alloy, not a derived quantitative relationship - the diffraction counts (positional/categorical) and DSC temperatures (thermal-event) are of fundamentally different kinds and are not cross-calibrated against each other.</message>

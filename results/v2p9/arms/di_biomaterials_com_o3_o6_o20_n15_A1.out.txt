Panel F11a (viewed) is a single cumulative-release curve: released drug (µg) vs. time (h), with error bars, rising steeply from 0 to ~340 µg at 8 h, ~670 µg at 24 h, leveling to ~780 µg at 72 h, and reaching a plateau of ~795-800 µg by 120-168 h. This matches the stated measurement values exactly.

What this settles about PRP/value: the panel firmly establishes the quantitative release kinetics/value of the drug-loaded material — an initial burst phase (0-24 h, accounting for the majority of total release) followed by a slow, near-complete plateau by ~72-168 h, with a ceiling total release around 795-800 µg. This is solid, direct evidence for the release-profile value: burst-then-sustained kinetics with a defined cumulative payload and a clear plateau time.

What it leaves open: the panel contains no antibacterial data at all — no zone-of-inhibition, CFU counts, or bacterial viability curves are shown here, only drug mass released. So while the printed caption fragment ("...and its anti-bacteria effect") implies this figure is paired with or precedes an antibacterial assay elsewhere (likely panel b or a separate figure), F11a alone cannot support any claim that the released drug is bactericidal/bacteriostatic, at what concentration it becomes effective, or whether the burst vs. plateau kinetics translate into a corresponding time-course of antibacterial activity. The link between "released amount" and "value" in terms of therapeutic/antibacterial efficacy is not evidenced by this panel and remains open pending the paired antibacterial-effect data.

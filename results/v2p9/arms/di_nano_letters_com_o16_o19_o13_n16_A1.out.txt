What this justifies: over the length scales SAXS probes here (roughly 2–60 nm, set by the q-range), the electrode's nanostructure/porosity is essentially stable through a full Na insertion/extraction cycle — no large-scale pore opening/closing, no gross particle coarsening or breakup, and no major nanoscale phase segregation is evident. The one deviation (small intensity drop at 0.03–0.1 Å⁻¹ near full discharge, 0.11/0.02 V) indicates a minor, localized change in scattering contrast/domain size near end-of-discharge, but it is small and not further decomposed.

What it leaves open: SAXS gives no chemical specificity (only electron-density contrast), so the origin of even the small drop (new phase, minor pore/particle size change, sodium ion distribution) is unresolved. Structures below ~2 nm or above ~60 nm are outside this q-window and unconstrained. No quantitative model fit (pore size, volume fraction) is given, so "no change" here is qualitative, not a measured bound.

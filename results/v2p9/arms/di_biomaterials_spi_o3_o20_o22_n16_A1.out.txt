Panel F11b (Biomaterials 2011.11.042) shows relative survival percentage (bar chart, days 1/3/7) for Blank control (~100 by definition), Scaffolds (~110-118%), and Scaffolds+Ampicillin (~104% day1, ~81% day3 with *, ~52% day7 with *). The asterisks per the printed caption mark the ampicillin-loaded scaffold group as significantly different (P<0.05) from both blank control and drug-free scaffolds at days 3 and 7.

What this supports for PRF/service_capability: the measurement establishes a genuine, statistically-flagged, time-dependent effect that is specific to the ampicillin-loaded scaffold — plain scaffolds track with or slightly above blank control throughout (110-118%), so the material itself is not cytotoxic. Only the drug-loaded variant diverges downward, and that divergence grows with time (day1 ~104%, no difference from controls; day3 ~81%, significant; day7 ~52%, significant). This licenses the claim that antibiotic loading, not the scaffold matrix, drives a progressive reduction in this survival readout, and that the effect is small/absent at 24h but substantial and significant by day 7.

What it leaves open: the assay is a "relative survival percentage" without the cell type, assay method (e.g., MTT/live-dead), or explanation of mechanism (bactericidal killing being read out as reduced viable "survival," direct cytotoxicity to host cells, or eluted-drug effects) specified in this panel/caption alone — so whether this reflects intended antibacterial service capability versus an off-target cytotoxic cost to host cells cannot be settled from the image and caption alone. Error bars are moderate-to-large (especially day1 and day3), and no explicit p-values or n are given beyond "P<0.05" and a single asterisk tier, so effect-size precision and exact significance at day1 (not starred, so presumably non-significant) versus days 3/7 is the extent of resolution available.

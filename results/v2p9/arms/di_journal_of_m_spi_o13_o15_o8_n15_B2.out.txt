Panel F2b (read) shows the strain-hardening-rate curve split into three regimes by the paper's own markers: Stage I, a steep drop from ~85 to ~14 GPa ending at ~0.009 true strain; Stage II, a slow decline from ~14 to ~5 GPa spanning roughly 0.009–0.058 strain; and Stage III, beginning near 0.058 where the rate crosses zero and goes negative. The inset true-stress/strain curve keeps rising smoothly through this whole range, so the drop and eventual negative rate are hardening-rate phenomena, not a stress drop.

What this adds to the TEM-based sequence (mottled/line contrast in one lamella → dense dislocation pile-up banked at the lamella boundary in the neighbouring/paired region, with the boundary lamella staying featureless): it gives a macroscopic strain axis with distinct kinetic regimes onto which that qualitative dislocation progression can be mapped as a plausible mechanism — early, rapid dislocation multiplication/mottling driving the fast Stage I drop; continued but decelerating pile-up accumulation against the boundary sustaining the slow Stage II decline (classic pile-up/back-stress hardening exhaustion); and pile-up saturation or associated local relaxation (e.g., boundary sliding, load transfer, or incipient interface damage) plausibly underlying the Stage III turn to negative hardening.

What it leaves open: the hardening-rate curve carries no strain-resolved microstructural correlate, and the TEM images carry no strain value for Observation B, so no single measurement ties a specific TEM snapshot to a specific point on this curve. The mapping of pile-up development onto Stage II/III remains inferential, not co-measured; other mechanisms (twinning, phase softening, necking onset) affecting Stage III cannot be excluded from these panels alone; and the TEM–mechanical link is bulk-average vs. single-lamella-pair, so spatial representativeness is still unestablished.

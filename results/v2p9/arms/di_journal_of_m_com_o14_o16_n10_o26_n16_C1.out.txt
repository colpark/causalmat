This measurement settles a corroboration claim, not a proof of atomic-scale co-location: it establishes that the C-rich surface signal seen in the particle-scale EDS map corresponds to a real, identifiable carbon phase — specifically curved carbon layers (0.386 nm spacing) found directly adjacent to crystalline alloy fringes (CeMg12 at 0.258 nm, Y5Mg24 at 0.265 nm) and an amorphous band, at one particle edge. That is what it supports: the two techniques agree in a qualitative, cross-scale way that carbon does physically coat/sit on the alloy particle surface at an edge, giving PRP/diagnostic-level support for "carbon phase present at alloy surface" as a real, physically grounded feature rather than an EDS artifact.

What it leaves open is threefold. First, representativeness: the HRTEM view is a single local edge on one milled C10 particle, so it cannot be generalized to all the surface/edge/face locations where the EDS map shows C and Co signal — it is an existence proof, not a survey. Second, resolution mismatch: EDS is a coarse areal map and HRTEM is atomic/lattice-resolution, so combining them only qualitatively corroborates location; there is no quantified or statistically representative distribution linking the two scales. Third, and most importantly for any Co-in-carbon claim: no Co-containing lattice fringe is reported in the HRTEM observation, so this result demonstrates only that C and Co signals co-locate at the coarser EDS scale — it does not demonstrate that Co is atomically dispersed within the carbon phase. Any stronger claim about Co dispersion within carbon at atomic resolution remains unestablished by this measurement.</message>

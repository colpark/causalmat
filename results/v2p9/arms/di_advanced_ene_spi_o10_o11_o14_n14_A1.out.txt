Both panels (F3d = CnC HS, F3e = C HS) show top-down SEM of the same Zn/host surface at the same 10 μm scale, letting direct visual comparison of deposit morphology.

F3d (CnC HS): the field is dominated by a smoother, largely featureless gray background (the host scaffold) with only a modest number of thin, needle/plate-like bright features scattered sparsely across it — consistent with "largely flat deposit with sparse protruding platelets."

F3e (C HS): the field is essentially saturated with elongated, sharp-edged platelet/flake structures packed edge-to-edge across the whole frame, with almost no flat background visible — consistent with "dense mat of sharp platelets."

What this justifies: the pairwise SEM comparison at matched magnification supports a qualitative morphological contrast in Zn nucleation/growth mode between the two host surfaces — CnC HS suppresses platelet coverage (sparse, mostly flat), while C HS promotes dense, sharp platelet growth. This is consistent with a claim that host surface chemistry/structure (CnC vs C) controls Zn deposit morphology/roughness.

What it leaves open: these are single representative images (no statistics on platelet density, size distribution, or reproducibility across replicates/cycles); no depth/cross-section or thickness data, so "flat" vs "dense mat" is a 2D-projection impression, not a quantitated roughness or volume measure; no compositional (EDS) confirmation that the bright features are Zn rather than substrate texture; and no link is shown here to electrochemical performance (e.g., cycling, dendrite/short-circuit outcomes) — the images alone establish morphology, not mechanism or performance causation.

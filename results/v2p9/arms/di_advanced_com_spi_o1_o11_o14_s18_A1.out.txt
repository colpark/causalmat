This measurement (Fig. 6a: dielectric constant vs. frequency, 100 Hz–1 MHz, for pure PAEN and BST@Ag composites at 1%, 3%, 5% Ag loading) supports the claim that dielectric constant is frequency-stable across this range: each curve drops only modestly (roughly 10-15% relative) from 100 Hz to 1 MHz, with no sharp relaxation step or resonance feature visible — all four traces are smooth, monotonically decreasing, and nearly flat on a log-frequency axis. This is consistent with the stated finding and supports a conclusion of low frequency-dispersion / good dielectric stability for these materials over the tested band, which is a favorable property for a service_capability claim (i.e., performance that doesn't degrade much as operating frequency changes within 100 Hz–1 MHz).

What it leaves open: the panel only shows relative dielectric constant, not loss tangent, breakdown strength, or actual device-level performance metrics, so it cannot by itself establish "service capability" in an applied sense (e.g., stability under thermal cycling, mechanical stress, humidity, or long-term aging). It also says nothing about behavior outside the 100 Hz–1 MHz window (no data at higher RF/microwave frequencies relevant to many real capacitor/EMI applications), nor about sample-to-sample reproducibility or statistical variance (no error bars shown).

Combination: The earlier result establishes (via two independent modalities) that SiC particles are broadly and near-uniformly spread through the material, with only occasional local clusters — i.e., dispersion is a bulk, field-wide microstructural feature rather than a technique artifact or a phenomenon confined to one region. The new fractographic observation shows both composites (10 and 20 vol% SiCp) presenting the same qualitative fracture morphology: rough, faceted surfaces with bright tear ridges bordering flat facet-like regions.

Read together, these support the conclusion that the fracture mode (the PRP/behavior_class trait) is a bulk, representative property of each composite rather than a local anomaly driven only by an isolated particle cluster: because the reinforcement is known to be spread broadly rather than segregated, the tear-ridge/facet fracture pattern seen across the imaged fracture field is plausibly linked to interaction between the crack path and dispersed SiC particles throughout the material, not just to a cluster hot-spot. It also supports that this mixed ductile-tear/facet fracture character is common to both loading levels (10% and 20%), i.e., not obviously loading-dependent in qualitative type.

What remains open: neither result establishes a causal or spatial correlation between specific particle positions/clusters and specific facets or tear ridges — the dispersion imaging and the fracture imaging are on different surfaces/orientations, so no direct particle-to-facet mapping exists. Facet size, ridge spacing, or facet/ridge area fraction are not quantified, so no measured degree of "ductile vs brittle" character is established, only a qualitative visual impression, and any difference in fracture behavior between the 10% and 20% samples remains unresolved.

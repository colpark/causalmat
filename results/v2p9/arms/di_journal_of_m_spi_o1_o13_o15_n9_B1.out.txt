F4a shows a dense tangled dislocation network filling a lamella interior (labeled "dislocations pileup," caption identifies it as the FCC/L12 phase); F4c shows a distinct dense dislocation band running along a lamella boundary, also labeled pileup, with the interior showing a sparser dislocation network. Together these confirm that the "line contrast along the interface" and "mottled contrast" noted at the first strain step are not generic diffraction artifacts but genuine dislocation debris — tangles within one lamella type and a pile-up specifically at the lamella boundary. Read with the earlier TEM/XRD result, this supports the conclusion that strain is accommodated asymmetrically because one lamella (identified in the F4a caption as L12) generates and stores dislocations that then stack up against the phase/lamella boundary, consistent with a plastic-incompatibility mechanism at the interface, while the other lamella type remains comparatively free of such debris.

What this still leaves open: the F4a caption's phase attribution (L12) is an assertion, not a diffraction pattern shown in these two panels, so phase indexing of the pile-up lamella is not independently verified here. There is no dislocation density, spacing, or areal-fraction measurement — only qualitative "dense" vs "sparse" contrast. It is also not established that F4a/F4c come from the same strain step or specimen region as the earlier featureless-vs-mottled pair, so the two observations may be complementary but not strictly a before/after series, and generality across the full microstructure is still not demonstrated (still only two illustrative fields of view).

This paper reports the tensile stress–strain response and fractography of bulk cast Al0.9CoFeNi2, a eutectic high-entropy alloy (EHEA) composed of alternating soft FCC and hard B2 (ordered BCC) lamellae. The new measurement is almost certainly quantitative: yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, elongation, and work-hardening behavior, likely paired with post-mortem SEM/EBSD showing where cracking initiates (typically at FCC/B2 interfaces or within the B2 phase) and whether the FCC phase shows dislocation slip/twinning.

The "established earlier result" this would be read against is almost certainly the original microstructural characterization of this alloy family (e.g., the eutectic lamellar FCC+B2 structure and its as-cast phase fractions/compositions established in prior work such as Bhattacharjee et al.'s AlCoCrFeNi-type eutectic HEA studies). That earlier work established *what phases exist and how they're arranged* but not *how load is shared or how failure initiates* during deformation.

Combined, the two results together support a specific deformation pathway/mechanism (MEC): that the eutectic microstructure enables a composite-like load-transfer mechanism, where the ductile FCC lamellae absorb plastic strain via dislocation slip while the harder B2 lamellae carry elastic load and constrain plastic flow, giving simultaneous strength and ductility superior to either constituent alone. This combination is what neither the microstructural paper nor the tensile paper alone could establish — phase identification alone doesn't prove cooperative load-sharing, and stress-strain curves alone don't prove it originates from the two-phase lamellar architecture.

What remains open: the atomic/dislocation-level mechanism (partial vs. full dislocations, any TRIP/TWIP-like ordering effects in B2), rate/temperature dependence, and whether conclusions generalize beyond this exact composition and casting condition.

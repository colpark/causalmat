Panel F7a (175°C creep, log minimum creep rate vs log applied stress, six curves AZ91 to AZ91+25SiC) shows apparent stress exponent n rising monotonically from 7.2 (unreinforced AZ91) to 8.9 (25 wt% SiCp), with intermediate compositions (7.4, 7.7, 8.5, 8.4) tracking increasing SiC content. This is a bulk, composite-averaged mechanical measurement over the whole tested volume, not a local one.

Combined with the earlier settled result (broad, near-uniform SiC dispersion across the imaged field, corroborated by both optical and SEM contrast, with only occasional local clusters), the pairing supports this conclusion: the systematic rise in apparent n with SiC loading is plausibly a bulk microstructural effect arising from particle-matrix interaction distributed throughout the material (e.g., load transfer to particles and/or particle-induced back-stress/threshold-stress effects on dislocation creep), rather than an artifact of the creep specimen happening to sample an unrepresentative, locally clustered region. Because dispersion is broadly uniform rather than confined to one zone, it is reasonable to attribute the trend to reinforcement content generally, not to sampling a particle-rich patch.

What remains open: neither measurement gives a quantitative uniformity metric or particle size/spacing, so contributions from load-transfer, Orowan strengthening, and dislocation/subgrain effects to the n increase cannot be separated. No true (threshold-corrected) stress exponent is reported, so "apparent n" may mask a constant true n plus a growing threshold stress — the mechanism identity is not settled, only that it is bulk rather than local. There is also no confirmation that the specific creep specimens were themselves imaged for dispersion, and the still-present local clusters could still influence individual specimen behavior in ways the averaged n values cannot reveal.

The panel (F3b, Advanced Energy Materials aenm.202003419) shows two overlaid C 1s NEXAFS traces: pristine CnC HS (blue) with three resolvable resonances at ~284.9, ~288.3, and a notably sharp, tall peak at ~290.1 eV; and CnC HS after zinc deposition (grey), where the ~284.9 and ~288.3 eV features persist (attenuated/broadened but clearly present) while the sharp 290.1 eV resonance is gone, with the spectrum flattening into a smooth step in that region.

What this supports: it establishes a selective, chemically specific change upon Zn deposition — the carbon species/orbital transition giving rise to the narrow 290.1 eV resonance is eliminated or converted, while the carbon environments responsible for the 284.9 and 288.3 eV features (typically π*/aromatic and C–O/C=O-type transitions) are largely untouched. Because the effect is selective rather than a uniform intensity loss across all features, it argues against simple physical attenuation of the whole carbon signal by an overlying Zn layer, and instead points to a targeted interaction (reaction, consumption, or reordering) at the specific chemical site responsible for that sharp peak.

What it leaves open: the identity/assignment of the 290.1 eV feature is not established by this measurement alone (no reference standards or fitting shown here), so the specific functional group or bonding environment involved is uncertain. The mechanism behind the disappearance — chemical reaction with Zn, consumption of a reactive site during nucleation, structural/orientational change, or partial screening specific to that resonance — cannot be distinguished from the spectrum alone. Quantitative extent of change, reversibility, and any causal link to electrochemical performance (e.g., zincophilicity or nucleation behavior) are not addressed by this panel and would require additional data.

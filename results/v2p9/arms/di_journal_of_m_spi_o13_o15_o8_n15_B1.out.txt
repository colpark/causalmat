The macroscopic curve (F2b) puts a number and a timeline on the microscopic progression already established. Regime I (rate collapsing from ~85 to ~14 GPa by ~0.009 strain) coincides with the point in loading where TEM shows only mottled/line contrast beginning to appear in one lamella (Observation A) — i.e., the steep early hardening drop is the macroscopic signature of nascent dislocation activity being nucleated/multiplied and interacting, not yet organized into a barrier. Regime II (slow decay from ~14 to ~5 GPa out to ~0.058) is consistent in timing with the interval over which that dislocation content would be expected to accumulate into the dense pile-up banked against the lamella boundary (Observation B): once dislocations pile up against a boundary, they act as an increasingly effective, geometrically-necessary obstacle, which plausibly sustains (rather than eliminates) hardening at a reduced, slowly-decaying rate — plausibly reflecting a bulk-averaged Taylor-type strengthening contribution from the growing pile-up. The onset of Regime III (rate turning negative beyond ~0.06) is not addressed by either TEM observation and must reflect a separate, later mechanism (e.g., localization/damage) not covered by A or B.

What this combination justifies: a plausible quantitative correspondence between the qualitative TEM sequence (mottled/line contrast → dense pile-up) and the two-stage hardening-rate decay (steep drop → slow decay), suggesting the pile-up formation process is the microstructural correlate of the sustained-but-diminishing hardening in regime II.

What it leaves open: the correspondence is inferred from rough coincidence of strain ranges, not from co-registered strain values on the same specimen/lamella pair — the TEM images carry no strain labels of their own (per the standing qualifications). It does not establish that the pile-up is the sole or dominant hardening contributor, does not identify what ends regime II or drives regime III, and does not confirm the pile-up lamella is B2 phase. The link remains an inference bridging two separately-collected datasets, not a directly measured causal or simultaneous observation.

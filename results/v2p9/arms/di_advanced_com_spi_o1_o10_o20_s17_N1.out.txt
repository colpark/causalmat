Combined, the two results settle that coating the conductive Ag with an insulating BST shell decouples this trade-off: the shell suppresses the direct particle-particle conduction paths that normally kill breakdown strength near percolation, letting the composite retain the permittivity gain from the high-K/conductive core while keeping loss low and mechanical/thermal robustness intact. What this combination does NOT settle: the long-term reliability of the interface under thermal cycling or high humidity, behavior at fields near actual dielectric breakdown, quantitative percolation threshold shift versus bare-Ag composites, and scalability of the coating process to industrial loading levels.

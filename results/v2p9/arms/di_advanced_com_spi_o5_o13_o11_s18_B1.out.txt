The DSC panel (F5a) gives Tg = 176 C for pure PAEN, then 146, 149, 157 C for BST@Ag at 1, 3, 5% loading. This is a third, independent, quantitative measurement resolved at all three loading levels (1/3/5%) — something the earlier SEM/dielectric pairing lacked, since SEM only contrasted bare vs. modified (two states) while dielectric constant was measured across three loadings.

Read together with the established dielectric result (permittivity 5%>3%>1%>PAEN), the new data show that Tg among the modified samples also varies monotonically with loading, rising as loading increases (146 to 149 to 157 C), in the same rank order as the permittivity increase. This settles something neither panel alone could: it establishes a genuine dose-resolved correspondence between composition/loading and a measured property, closing exactly the gap the earlier SEM-based comparison was qualified against (SEM could only support a two-state qualitative link, not a monotonic one across 1/3/5%). So the combination supports that whatever compositional/interfacial change accompanies increasing Ag-coated BST loading manifests consistently across two independent, quantitatively resolved property axes (thermal transition and dielectric constant), strengthening the case that the surface-modified state's effect scales continuously with loading rather than being an artifact of only two sampled conditions.

What remains open: all modified samples have lower Tg than pure PAEN (opposite-direction baseline shift from the permittivity trend), so this does not establish a shared physical mechanism (e.g., interfacial polarization) linking Tg suppression and permittivity rise — only correlated monotonic co-variation with loading. Causal mechanism, and SEM confirmation at intermediate (3%) loading, remain unaddressed.

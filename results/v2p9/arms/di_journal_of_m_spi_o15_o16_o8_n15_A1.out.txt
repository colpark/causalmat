Panel F2b (image confirmed) plots strain-hardening rate (dσ/dε, GPa) against true strain for the Al0.9CoFeNi2 EHEA, with an inset true stress–strain curve, and marks three regimes (I, II, III) with dashed guide lines at strain ≈0.009 and ≈0.058–0.06.

What the data support: the measurement documents a macroscopic, three-stage hardening behavior — Stage I, a steep near-exponential drop from ~85 to ~14 GPa over the first ~0.009 strain (consistent with rapid dynamic recovery/early dislocation rearrangement); Stage II, a long, nearly flat plateau (~14 down to ~5 GPa) extending to ~0.058 strain, indicating a sustained secondary hardening mechanism that resists the usual continuous decay seen in single-phase alloys (the kind of extended plateau typically attributed to continuous generation of new hardening sources — e.g., phase/interface-mediated hardening in a eutectic microstructure); and Stage III, a rise (the curve dips and then turns upward, per the printed values) near strain ~0.06, which in strain-hardening-rate analysis is usually read as a signature of an additional hardening contribution kicking in near the point where necking/instability would otherwise begin (delaying instability per the Considère criterion). So the panel justifies the claim that hardening in this alloy proceeds through distinct, separable stages rather than a single monotonic decay, and that a late-stage hardening upturn is present.

What it leaves open: this is purely a mechanical (macroscopic stress-strain-derived) measurement — it cannot by itself identify the physical/microstructural mechanism (e.g., dislocation slip vs. twinning vs. phase transformation vs. eutectic-interface effects) responsible for Stage II or Stage III; that attribution would require independent structural evidence (TEM, XRD, in-situ diffraction) not shown in this crop. It also does not show replicate curves, error bars, or comparison to other compositions in this crop, so composition-dependence of the pathway is not established from this panel alone.

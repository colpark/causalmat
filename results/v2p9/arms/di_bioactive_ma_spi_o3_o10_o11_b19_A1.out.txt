Both panels (F10c and F10d) are paired bar charts of "Antibacterial Ratio (%)" for the same five conditions (Control, TiO2, Er0.5Ce0.1Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.3Ti-O), each split into Dark and Light exposure, n=3 mean±SD, with significance brackets (*, **, ***). The caption fragments ("...and E. coli" for C, triplicate/mean±SD for D) indicate these are the two bacterial-strain replicates of the same assay (very likely S. aureus in an earlier panel A/B and E. coli here, or two independent E. coli trials), run in parallel.

What the measurement supports: under Light, antibacterial ratio rises steeply and monotonically with dopant loading up to Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O (~91% in C, ~93% in D), which is statistically higher (*** and ** brackets) than Ce0.1 (~68-73%), TiO2 (~45%), and Control (~4-7%); Ce0.3 is close behind (~87%) but slightly and sometimes significantly (*) lower than Ce0.2. This justifies the conclusion that Er/Ce co-doping confers a strong, light-activated antibacterial service capability, peaking near the 0.2 Ce composition, and that the effect is reproducible across two panels/trials with tight error bars and significance testing.

What it leaves open: the Dark condition still shows a substantial residual effect for doped samples (~40-45% at Ce0.2/0.3), so light-independent (e.g., ion-release) killing is not separated from photocatalytic ROS-mediated killing here — mechanism is unresolved. The panels do not establish which bacterial species (or whether both are the same species) is truly represented, nor do they address durability, in vivo performance, cytocompatibility trade-offs, or whether Ce0.2 is a true optimum versus a coincidental non-monotonic dip at 0.3.

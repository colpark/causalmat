Panels viewed: F3d (CnC HS) and F3e (C HS), both zinc-plated surfaces at the same 10 μm scale bar, same magnification.

F3d (CnC HS): the surface is predominantly a smooth, featureless, relatively flat granular/mat background. Only a modest number of thin, elongated, plate-like or needle-like protrusions stick up sparsely across the field, mostly isolated and separated by open flat regions.

F3e (C HS): the entire field is densely covered edge-to-edge with small, sharp, flake/platelet-shaped protrusions in a tangled, interlocking mat — essentially no flat background is visible; the platelets are far more numerous and packed than in F3d.

What this settles: the direct, qualitative SEM comparison supports the stated conclusion — deposited zinc morphology differs sharply between the two host surfaces at matched imaging conditions (same scale bar, comparable field of view). CnC HS yields a mostly flat deposit with only sparse protruding platelets, while C HS yields a dense, sharp-platelet mat covering the whole surface. This is a real, visually unambiguous microstructural/shape (morphology) contrast between the two substrates' zinc deposits.

What it leaves open: these two static top-down SEM images cannot establish causation (i.e., why CnC HS suppresses platelet growth — nucleation density, surface chemistry, wettability, etc.), cannot quantify platelet size/density/coverage numerically, do not show cross-sections or thickness/porosity, do not indicate whether this morphology difference persists over cycling or correlates with performance (dendrite risk, Coulombic efficiency), and represent only single fields of view — reproducibility/statistics across the sample are not shown by these crops alone.

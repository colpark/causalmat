Image reviewed: F7b (/home/aid1/Documents/causalmat/matmech/Journal_of_Advanced_Ceramics/s40145-021-0536-4/panels/crops/698864d540d87d1163ed56ed589b942a97663a13dc889493498cddac27c18010_B.jpg), an SEM fractograph of sample S2 sintered at 1700C, at 1 um scale bar.

What the panel shows: a boxed cBN grain sits proud on the fracture surface next to a deep, dark, roughly circular socket (~2 um across) — i.e., a grain-pullout pit — while a separately boxed α-Al2O3 region shows a smooth, sharply faceted grain face with no comparable cavity.

What this supports: the qualitative contrast in fracture morphology between the two phases. The cBN grain/socket pair is consistent with interfacial debonding and pull-out (the cBN grain separated from its socket, or a neighboring cBN grain pulled out leaving the pit), i.e., weaker or more brittle interfacial bonding for cBN in this matrix. The Al2O3 grain's clean faceted face is consistent with transgranular or well-bonded fracture, i.e., a different (stronger-interface or more intrinsically cleaving) fracture behavior class. So this single image supports classifying the two phases as showing distinct fracture/behavior classes — one prone to pull-out, one to clean faceting — at this sintering condition.

What it leaves open: this is one micrograph from one sample (S2, 1700C) with no statistics on how often pull-out occurs versus clean fracture, no EDS/compositional confirmation that the labeled regions are phase-pure, and no measurement of bond strength, residual stress, or thermal-expansion mismatch that would establish the mechanism behind the pull-out. It also does not show whether this behavior generalizes to other sintering temperatures/samples, nor does it quantify socket depth beyond a visual ~2 um estimate. So the panel supports a qualitative PRP/behavior_class distinction between cBN and Al2O3 fracture modes in this sample but does not establish generality, mechanism, or quantitative frequency.

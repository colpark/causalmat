This measurement settles, at most, a co-occurrence within YSZ alone: over the cycling range examined, YSZ shows small, stable per-interval misorientation (0.7-2.3 deg across all cycles) alongside an SEM crack network that appears visually unchanged between the two imaged views. That supports the claim that low, stable strain accumulation in YSZ tracks with (does not obviously accompany) crack-network change in this material, under these imaging conditions — i.e., it is consistent with the idea that limited crystallographic reorientation is associated with limited/no visible cracking progression.

It does not settle several things bearing on service capability. First, the crack-network reading is purely qualitative ("looks unchanged"), not a quantified crack-length, density, or connectivity metric, so it cannot be compared numerically to the degree-valued misorientation data, and no quantitative threshold or dose-response relationship can be derived. Second, there is no matched SEM crack-network observation for Al2O3-YSZ, which showed much larger misorientation (4.3-11 deg at 5 cycles, dropping to ~1 deg by 20 cycles); so whether that larger strain signal actually produces more cracking, or whether Al2O3-YSZ's crack network also stays unchanged despite higher misorientation, is simply unknown from this data. Third, with only two materials compared and composition (Al2O3 presence) confounded with misorientation magnitude, any inference about which factor drives crack behavior is unsupported — this could reflect material chemistry, microstructure, or something else entirely, not misorientation per se. Finally, one qualitative snapshot at one pair of time points cannot establish mechanism or generalize to other cycling regimes, so no service-life or degradation-mechanism conclusion should be drawn beyond the narrow, single-material, single-comparison observation described.

Panel F4f shows a full-cell (electrolytic Zn-MnO2 battery) galvanostatic cycling test using pre-deposited zinc on CnC HS foil: charge and discharge capacity start near ~1.0 mAh and decline gradually to ~0.9 mAh over 500 cycles, with the 1st and 100th cycle voltage profiles (inset) nearly superimposed.

Combined with the earlier result: Observations A and B established, at the half-cell/host level, that CnC HS has lower plating overpotential at high current density and sustains stable CE out to ~205 cycles, whereas C HS's overpotential is higher at high rate and its CE collapses by ~40-50 cycles. That pairing was purely a same-host-wins correlation with no full-cell demonstration and no proof the effect generalizes beyond a bare CE/overpotential metric.

F4f adds the missing full-cell, capacity-retention evidence for CnC HS specifically: it shows that the good plating/CE behavior documented in A and B translates into an actual working Zn-MnO2 battery that survives 500 cycles with only ~10% capacity fade and essentially unchanged voltage profile (1st vs 100th cycle). So the combination now supports the stronger, more practically relevant claim that CnC HS's favorable plating/CE behavior corresponds to good full-cell cyclability and capacity stability, not just good CE in a symmetric/half-cell test.

What remains open: (1) F4f has no C HS comparison, so it cannot show that CnC HS's full-cell performance is actually superior to C HS's — it only confirms CnC HS itself performs well; the earlier host comparison and this full-cell result are still not run under matching conditions/current density, so the link between "lower overpotential at high rate" and "good full-cell retention" is still only qualitative, not mechanistic or quantitative. (2) The 500-cycle full-cell test and the ~205-cycle CE test are different experiments (different cell configurations, presumably different current density), so we cannot map specific cycle numbers between them. (3) The shared-cause caveat still stands: both the plating quality and the full-cell retention could stem from the same underlying CnC structural property without one causing the other.

The new panel (Fig. 8) shows Al2O3-YSZ coupons intact at 5 cycles, corner spallation appearing by 10 cycles, and that spalled damage persisting and slightly enlarging through 40 cycles.

Read against the earlier EBSD/SEM pairing, this settles a temporal ordering that neither result gave alone: Al2O3-YSZ's large per-interval misorientation (4.3-11 deg) occurs at the 5-cycle point when the coupon is still visually intact, and only after misorientation begins falling (toward the ~1 deg seen by 20 cycles) does macroscopic corner spallation appear (10 cycles) and then persist/grow. So the combination supports saying that in Al2O3-YSZ, the phase of large, unstable misorientation precedes rather than coincides with visible macroscopic damage onset, and that damage, once initiated, continues independently of the subsequent drop in misorientation. This is a genuine addition: it gives Al2O3-YSZ a damage timeline to set against YSZ's "unchanged crack network" observation, which neither measurement supplied by itself.

What it leaves open: the new panel is coupon-level spallation (macroscopic material loss at corners), not the SEM crack-network view used for YSZ, so it still cannot be matched quantitatively, or even at the same observational scale, to YSZ's "unchanged crack network" claim — the qualification that Al2O3-YSZ lacks a matched SEM crack comparison stands unresolved. It also does not establish that misorientation causally drives the spallation (could be coincidental timing, or both driven by composition/thermal-expansion mismatch), and the two-material, composition-confounded design still prevents separating strain effects from other Al2O3-YSZ-specific properties. Dose-response between misorientation magnitude and damage remains unestablished.

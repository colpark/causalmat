SAXS probes nanoscale/mesoscale electron-density contrast (porosity, particle-scale heterogeneity, phase separation) — a length scale and contrast mechanism independent of both XRD (atomic lattice) and XANES (local oxidation state). Read together with the established XRD+XANES result, this SAXS data extends the "PCO is largely retained on sodiation, without metallic Co" conclusion across length scales: the absence of any significant new SAXS contrast (no growing pores, no emerging phase-separated domains, no particle disintegration) argues against a hidden alternative that XRD/XANES alone couldn't exclude — namely, nanoscale reduction/amorphization producing metallic Co nanoclusters or void/porosity development too small to diffract but big enough to scatter. So the combination now supports that PCO's structural retention holds not just at the crystal-lattice and oxidation-state level, but also at the mesoscale/microstructural level (no major porosity or nanoscale phase change).

What remains open: the mechanism and quantitative meaning of the small ~0.03–0.1 Å⁻¹ intensity drop at 0.11/0.02V is not explained (minor porosity/contrast change vs. instrumental/normalization effect); SAXS cannot itself speak to oxidation state or crystallinity; and sub-SAXS-resolution nanoclusters or the quantitative fraction/extent of any change are still undetermined.

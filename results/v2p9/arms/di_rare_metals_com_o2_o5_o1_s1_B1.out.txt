The three SEM micrographs show interdendritic-phase morphology changing from a continuous network (Al-Cu-Mn, a) to isolated particles (Al-Cu-Mn-Sm, b) to thin, broken strings (Al-Cu-Mn-La, c), with panel (c) arrows spatially separating two distinct interdendritic phases: A (Al2Cu) and B (Al6Cu6La).

Read alone, the micrographs only show morphology - they cannot identify chemical composition or prove a distinct compound exists; the network-vs-particle-vs-string differences could simply reflect how each rare-earth addition alters eutectic/interdendritic solidification, without implying a new phase. Read alone, the earlier XRD+DSC result established (via co-occurring extra reflections and a distinct dissolution event) that La, but not Sm, produces a detectable new compound, but that result was purely bulk/positional and thermal - it never showed where in the microstructure this phase resides or what it looks like.

Combined, the two results support a stronger claim: the new La-specific phase inferred from XRD/DSC has a real, spatially localized microstructural signature - it appears as a morphologically distinct interdendritic feature (arrow B, broken-string network) separable from Al2Cu (arrow A) in the same alloy. This also shows La's effect on interdendritic morphology (broken strings) differs qualitatively from Sm's (isolated particles), suggesting La modifies solidification differently, consistent with forming an additional compound.

What remains open: the identity label at arrow B is not derived from this image (no EDS/diffraction on that spot) but imported from the earlier DSC/literature comparison. The images add no quantification of phase fraction, and Sm's isolated particles still cannot rule out an undetected Sm-analogous phase.

F2b shows three regimes tied to true strain: Region I (0 to ~0.009) is a steep drop from ~85 to ~14 GPa; Region II (~0.009 to ~0.058) is a slow decline to ~5 GPa; Region III (beyond ~0.058-0.06) turns negative. This is a purely mechanical (macroscopic stress-strain-derived) measurement with no microstructural imaging of its own.

Combined with the earlier TEM-based result (localized pile-up at a lamella boundary, plus a panel-ordered progression from mottled contrast to tangled lines to dense walls), the pairing supports a coherent qualitative narrative: strain hardening capacity falls off steeply early (Region I) consistent with rapid initial dislocation multiplication and pile-up formation, then declines slowly (Region II) consistent with the described progression toward tangles and organized dense walls/Taylor lattices, where dynamic recovery (annihilation, network organization) increasingly offsets further storage, and finally the rate goes negative (Region III), signaling a shift to softening/localization not obviously accounted for by the wall-forming progression alone. So the combination supports that the mechanical hardening-rate stages plausibly track, in a general sense, the qualitative dislocation-organization stages already established.

What it leaves open: neither the TEM panels nor F2b carry strain labels linking a specific micrograph to a specific point on the strain axis, so no quantitative or even ordinal correspondence (e.g., "walls form exactly where rate reaches ~5 GPa") is established — the microstructural sequence and the strain regimes are only juxtaposed, not co-registered. It also does not identify the mechanism behind the negative-rate Region III (necking, twinning saturation, phase transformation, or localization are all possible and none is shown), and it cannot establish causality between wall formation and the hardening-rate decline versus mere co-occurrence.

The paper reports that quantum-dot sensitized solar cells (QDSSCs) built with a hierarchical Cu2S microsphere/reduced graphene oxide (rGO) counter electrode achieve higher power-conversion efficiency (PCE) and lower charge-transfer resistance than devices using bare Cu2S counter electrodes, evidenced by higher fill factor/Jsc and smaller Rct in EIS.

Read together with the "established earlier result" that plain Cu2S counter electrodes already show good intrinsic catalytic activity toward polysulfide reduction but suffer from poor electrical conductivity, particle aggregation, and unstable cycling performance, the combination settles that the *value* (i.e., the practical benefit/PRP — presumably "performance-relevant property" or efficiency proxy) added by this device is attributable specifically to the rGO wrapping improving charge transport and structural stability, not to any change in the underlying catalytic chemistry of Cu2S itself. In other words: catalytic activity was already established; this new measurement shows that pairing it with a conductive, structurally stabilizing scaffold converts that latent catalytic value into realized device performance (efficiency comparable to or exceeding Pt-based counter electrodes).

What it leaves open: whether the gain is durable over long-term cycling/outdoor conditions, whether it scales to larger-area or flexible modules, whether the specific hierarchical microsphere morphology is necessary versus other Cu2S/rGO composite geometries, and whether the observed PCE gain generalizes across different quantum-dot sensitizers or electrolyte systems. It also does not by itself disentangle how much of the improvement is conductivity-driven versus surface-area/active-site-driven.

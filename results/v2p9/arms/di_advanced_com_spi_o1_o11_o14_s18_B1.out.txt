Fig. 6a shows dielectric constant vs. frequency (100 Hz–1 MHz) for pure PAEN and the three BST@Ag composites (1/3/5%), each curve declining only ~10% across nearly four decades of frequency, with an ordering Pure PAEN < BST@Ag1% < BST@Ag3% < BST@Ag5% at every frequency.

What this adds: the flatness of each individual curve shows that within a given sample the dielectric response is not strongly frequency-dependent — i.e., there is no evidence of a relaxation/dispersion process (e.g., interfacial polarization giving way at higher frequency) dominating the behavior over this range. Combined with the earlier-established result — that the silane-grafted, Ag-loaded filler measurably shifts Tg relative to neat PAEN, with Ag content correlating with the partial recovery of Tg (146→149→157 C) — the two observations together support that the filler (grafted BST@Ag) changes the composite's bulk polymer/interface state (Tg) while simultaneously and monotonically raising the dielectric constant with Ag loading, in a way that is stable across frequency rather than an artifact of a frequency-dependent polarization mechanism. This strengthens the case that filler loading is doing consistent, structural-level work on both properties (thermal and dielectric) rather than one being a frequency-specific electrical artifact.

What it leaves open: the combination still cannot separate the roles of MPTMS grafting versus Ag content as causal drivers, since FTIR grafting evidence is only for BST-SH and not verified at each composition, and both DSC and dielectric series vary Ag while presumably holding grafting constant. It also does not establish a mechanistic link between the Tg shift and the dielectric increase (e.g., whether the same interfacial chemistry underlies both), only that both trend with Ag loading. No quantitative dose-response model is supported by either measurement alone or together.

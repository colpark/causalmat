F4a shows a dense, homogeneously distributed tangled dislocation network filling the interior of a lamella (caption: dislocation configurations in the FCC/L12 phase). F4c shows a distinctly different geometry: a dark, dense dislocation band concentrated right along a lamella boundary, with an arrow explicitly labeling it "dislocations pileup" at the interface, while the adjacent lamella interior shows a much sparser dislocation network.

Combined with the earlier result (XRD-confirmed two-phase L12+B2 as-cast structure; first strain step showing one lamella acquiring mottled/line contrast at its interface while its neighbor stays featureless), this new pair strengthens and sharpens that asymmetry claim in one specific way: it shows the mechanism by which the strain response localizes. The earlier line contrast at the interface was ambiguous (could be misfit dislocations, an artifact of foil bending, or genuine plastic activity); F4c shows unambiguous dislocation pile-up banked against the boundary, i.e., dislocations generated inside one phase are being blocked from transmitting across the interface into the neighboring phase. Combined with F4a's dense in-lamella tangle (identified as FCC/L12), this supports the conclusion that the phase boundary between L12 and B2 acts as a barrier to slip transmission, causing dislocations nucleated in the softer/active phase to pile up rather than cross into the neighbor — a mechanistically concrete account of the asymmetric hardening pathway.

What remains open: there is still no diffraction-indexed confirmation that the pile-up side is specifically B2 (only F4a's phase is captioned); no dislocation density/pile-up length is quantified; and only single, unstated-strain-step fields of view are shown, so generality across the microstructure and across strain levels is still unestablished.

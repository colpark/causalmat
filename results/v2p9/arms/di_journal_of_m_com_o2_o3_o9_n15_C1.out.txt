What it leaves open, and thus cannot be used to support any stronger behavior class (e.g., "quantified" or "matched/replicated"): (1) no quantitative uniformity metric exists — no clustering index, nearest-neighbor statistic, or areal fraction was computed from either modality; (2) the two lines of evidence are not sampling-matched — optical evidence spans multiple panels while SEM is a single X150 field, so agreement is across different sampling extents, not a controlled repeat comparison; (3) particle size distribution and the mechanism behind the local clusters (e.g., grain-boundary pinning) remain unestablished — the pair only shows that clusters are present alongside broad spread, not why; (4) no statistical test or repeat imaging was performed, so "near-uniform" remains a visual/qualitative impression rather than a measured degree of homogeneity.

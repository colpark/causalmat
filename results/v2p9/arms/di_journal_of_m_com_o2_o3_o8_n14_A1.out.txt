Figure 4 (UCS vs. SiCp content for AZ91 composites) shows a monotonic, roughly linear rise in ultimate compressive strength from ~310 MPa at 0 wt% SiCp to ~363 MPa at 25 wt% SiCp, with intermediate points at ~318 (5%), ~333 (10%), ~343 (15%), and ~353 MPa (20%), matching the reported values closely. Error bars are modest and non-overlapping between most adjacent points (especially at the extremes), so the measurement supports a real, reproducible strengthening trend with increasing particulate reinforcement (PRP) content — this is solid evidence that SiCp addition increases UCS in this AZ91-based composite system, consistent with standard particulate-reinforcement strengthening mechanisms (load transfer, Orowan/dispersion strengthening, grain refinement from particles).

What it leaves open: this single plot cannot establish the underlying mechanism (whether the gain is from load-bearing capacity of particles, thermal-mismatch dislocation density, grain refinement, or a combination), nor does it address whether the relationship is truly linear or saturates/reverses beyond 25 wt% (the tested range stops there, and particulate composites often show ductility loss or strength plateau/decline at higher loadings due to agglomeration). It also says nothing about ductility, fracture behavior, or whether the same trend holds under tension rather than compression — the caption specifically limits the claim to ultimate compressive strength. No statistical test is shown, so "value" (practical/economic benefit of adding SiCp) is not addressed, only the strength/property trend itself.

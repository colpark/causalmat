The kinetic panels: F6a (C0, no additive) shows the first hydrogenation cycle is slow and incomplete (~3.2 wt% at 180 min) while cycles 2-3 saturate near 5 wt% within minutes — i.e., bare alloy needs an "activation" period before it reaches fast kinetics. F6b (C10, +10 wt% C@Co) shows all three cycles, including the first, saturate within ~5-11 min — the activation barrier is absent from cycle 1 onward.

Combined with the settled EDS/HRTEM result (C-rich curved carbon layers, containing co-located Co signal at EDS scale, coat the alloy particle edges/surfaces), this justifies the functional claim that neither piece supports alone: the carbon(+Co) surface layer identified structurally is what removes the need for cycling-induced activation, i.e., it acts as a catalytic surface coating that lets the alloy hydrogenate at full rate immediately, rather than only after repeated cycling presumably reworks/cleans the bare-alloy surface. Structure alone (EDS+HRTEM) shows location but not function; kinetics alone shows a functional improvement but not which phase produces it. Together they support that the located C/Co coating is functionally responsible for eliminating first-cycle activation — a link between structure and service capability that requires both measurements.

What remains open: since HRTEM reported only alloy and carbon lattice fringes (no Co lattice fringe), the combination still cannot isolate whether Co, the carbon layer, or their conjunction is the catalytically active agent, nor the mechanism (e.g., H2 dissociation catalysis vs. suppression of oxide/passivation reformation).

Panel F6d confirms the stated numbers: at 2 ps, Li-Co3O4 (black) reaches ~91% oxidation, Na-Co3S4 (red) ~59%, and Na-Co3O4 (blue) ~36% and has visibly flattened after ~0.8 ps while the other two curves keep climbing. All three curves start near 0% and track together only up to ~0.4-0.6 ps before diverging.

What this settles: the MD trajectory is internally consistent evidence that the identity of the alkali carrier (Li vs Na) and the anion framework (oxide vs sulfide) both modulate the kinetics/extent of charge transfer (oxidation) from the metal-oxide/sulfide host over the same simulated timescale. It supports a clean rank-ordering claim — Li-Co3O4 > Na-Co3S4 > Na-Co3O4 — and it supports the specific mechanistic claim that Na-Co3O4 saturates early (a kinetic or thermodynamic ceiling reached by ~0.8 ps) whereas the other two systems have not yet plateaued by the 2 ps simulation endpoint.

What it leaves open: this is a single short (2 ps) MD run per system, so the panel cannot distinguish whether Na-Co3O4's plateau is a true steady-state limit or simply the slowest-rising curve that would eventually continue rising given more time — the simulation window is too short to confirm asymptotic saturation versus mere slow kinetics. It also cannot isolate which factor (Li vs Na ionic identity, or O vs S anion chemistry) drives the ranking, since cation and anion are varied confoundedly across only three systems (no Na-Co3O4-vs-Li-Co3S4 cross to separate the two variables). No error bars, replicate runs, or statistical treatment are shown, so run-to-run variability and its effect on the ranking's robustness are unaddressed. Finally, "degree of oxidation" as computed (presumably from charge analysis, e.g., Bader) is not itself defined in this crop, so the physical meaning and sensitivity of the metric to the underlying charge-partitioning scheme is not settled by this panel alone.

This measurement supports a qualitative, ordinal conclusion, not a quantitative one. Taken together, the SEM comparison (bare BST ~70 nm, smooth vs. Ag-modified particles rough, coated, larger) and the DSC Tg series (pure PAEN 176 C vs. BST@Ag composites 146-157 C) justify the claim that silver-modifying the BST-SH surface physically changes the filler's morphology, and that this change coincides with composites whose glass transition is depressed relative to the neat polymer. That is, the data support an ordering: bare filler differs morphologically from coated filler, and composite Tg differs from (and is lower than) neat-polymer Tg.

What it leaves open is substantial. First, because SEM is reported as only one bare-vs-modified pair rather than a per-loading series matched to the three DSC compositions (1%, 3%, 5% Ag), the measurement cannot demonstrate that coating extent, size, or roughness scales with Ag content in a way that tracks the graded 146 to 149 to 157 C rise in Tg — no dose-response or monotonic structure-property relationship is established. Second, SEM characterizes isolated, dry powder at ambient imaging conditions, not the filler as dispersed in the polymer matrix nor the thermal/mechanical environment of the DSC scan itself, so the observed coating morphology cannot be directly tied to a mechanism of chain-mobility restriction or plasticization at the transition. In short: the coexistence of a coated filler and a lowered, composition-varying Tg is established, but causal linkage and quantitative correlation between coating degree and Tg magnitude remain undetermined by this evidence.

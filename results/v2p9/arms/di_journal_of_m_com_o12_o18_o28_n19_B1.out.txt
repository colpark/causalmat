Panels F11a/F11b (Journal of Magnesium and Alloys, j.jma.2020.09.027) show PCT desorption isotherms at 320/340/360 C for C0 (a) and C10 (b): each curve is a single flat plateau, and the C10 plateau pressures (~0.15, ~0.27-0.3, ~0.45-0.5 MPa) essentially reproduce the C0 values at the same temperatures.

Combined with the earlier settled result (bulk XRD: only Mg, CeH2.73, CeO2, YH2 in both samples, no CeMg12/Y5Mg24/Ce2Mg17 intermetallic anywhere detectable; HRTEM: one local Mg/CeH2.73 grain pair, Mg wrapped in carbon), this justifies the conclusion that adding 10 wt% Co+carbon does not change the thermodynamics of the hydrogen storage reaction: the same phase assemblage (Mg-hydride/CeH2.73/YH2, no RE-Mg intermetallic) governs desorption in both samples, and it yields the same equilibrium (van't Hoff) plateau pressures. That single-plateau match across three temperatures, in a sample whose bulk phase inventory is independently confirmed unchanged and whose one imaged region shows the relevant grains as adjacent, carbon-wrapped crystallites, supports treating the Co/carbon addition as acting kinetically (e.g., via the carbon-wrapped Mg grain boundaries/catalytic Co) rather than chemically destabilizing or forming a new hydride-forming phase — no new intermetallic is present that could shift the reaction thermodynamics.

What remains open: PCT alone cannot identify phases, only give bulk-averaged equilibrium pressure, so the "same phases, same pressure" inference still rests on XRD for identity; the match does not prove the single plateau in either sample isn't an overlap of near-degenerate multi-phase equilibria (Mg/MgH2 vs CeH2.73 vs YH2) rather than one true single-phase process. Neither PCT nor HRTEM (one grain pair only) establishes that carbon-wrapping is representative sample-wide, and none of this touches actual cycling/kinetic rate data or reversibility.

Panel F5d (individual tumor-growth curves) shows six group panels. The blue "BNs-BSA + 808 nm" panel: most curves hug near-zero volume through about day 14-15, then several rise steeply, reaching roughly 250-700 mm3 by day 20-22 — consistent with 4 of 5 tumors regrowing, one staying suppressed. The red "BNs-BSA + Lasers" panel (combined 660/808 nm): all curves remain flat at essentially zero for the full ~20-22 day window, with no visible upturn in any animal.

What this measurement supports: within the observation window (~20-22 days), the dual-wavelength (660+808 nm) laser combination with BNs-BSA produces durable, complete suppression in all five tumors, whereas single-wavelength 808 nm treatment with the same nanomaterial achieves only transient control — the majority of tumors (4/5) escape and regrow starting around day 14-15. This is a within-experiment comparative/relative durability claim: combined-wavelength photothermal treatment outlasts single 808 nm treatment over the tested timeframe.

What it leaves open: the figure alone doesn't establish why 808 nm-only allows regrowth (incomplete thermal ablation depth, dose, tumor margin effects, etc.), nor does it show behavior beyond day ~20-22 — so whether the "0" combined-laser tumors would also eventually regrow with longer follow-up is untested. Sample size is small (n=5 per group) and no statistics (e.g., survival curve, p-value) are visible in this panel to judge significance of the 4/5-vs-0/5 regrowth difference. The panel also can't isolate whether 660 nm's contribution, versus dose/duration differences between arms, drives the added durability.

Panel F6b shows ex vivo fluorescence images of inguinal and axillary lymph nodes harvested from mice, comparing the anti-CCL21 DNA aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 conjugate against the unfunctionalized BSA@ZIF-8 control, under both subcutaneous and footpad injection routes, on a shared 0-2500 intensity scale.

What it supports: In both lymph node sites and both injection routes, the aptamer-functionalized MOF produces visibly higher, more saturated (green-toward-yellow) fluorescence signal than the plain MOF (which stays cyan/blue, i.e., lower on the same scale). This is consistent with the aptamer conferring greater accumulation/retention of the nanomaterial in lymph nodes relative to the non-targeted control — i.e., the CCL21-targeting ligand measurably enhances lymph node localization in vivo, at least at the single endpoint timepoint imaged here.

What it leaves open: The image is qualitative — a color-coded intensity map with no quantified radiant efficiency values, no error bars, no stated number of animals per group, and no statistics. It's a single (or unstated small n) endpoint snapshot, not a time course, so kinetics of uptake/clearance are unaddressed. It doesn't distinguish whether the enhanced signal reflects increased uptake, slower clearance, or both, nor does it establish specificity via a blocking/competition control. It also says nothing about whether the accumulated material retains functional "service capability" (e.g., payload delivery or therapeutic activity) once in the node — only that more fluorescent material arrives there. So the measurement supports "targeting enhances lymph node accumulation" but does not settle dose-response, mechanism, statistical significance, or downstream functional consequence.

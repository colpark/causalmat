The two panels (F11a = as-milled Mg90Ce5Y5, F11b = Mg90Ce5Y5+10 wt% CQCo) are PCT (pressure-composition-temperature) isotherms at 320, 340, and 360 C. In both panels each temperature shows a single, well-defined flat absorption plateau (not multiple steps), and the plateau pressures are visually very close between the two alloys at each temperature: roughly ~0.15 MPa at 320 C, ~0.27-0.3 MPa at 340 C, and ~0.4-0.45 MPa at 360 C, matching the stated near-identical values for C0 and C10 (CQCo-added) samples.

What this supports: the measurement justifies the conclusion that adding 10 wt% CQCo does not measurably shift the thermodynamic equilibrium (plateau/platform) pressure of the hydride absorption reaction relative to the base alloy - i.e., the van't Hoff-derived enthalpy/entropy of hydrogen absorption (PRP = plateau pressure, presumably tied to Table 2's thermodynamic parameters) is essentially unchanged by the CQCo addition. Each isotherm is a single clean plateau, so the phase equilibrium being measured is well-defined and comparable across the two alloys and across all three temperatures shown.

What it leaves open: this PCT comparison alone does not establish anything about kinetics (absorption/desorption rate, activation energy), cycling stability, or hydrogen storage capacity beyond the plateau region - the CQCo additive's known catalytic benefit (if any) would show up in kinetics data, not in plateau pressure. Also, Table 2 (referenced in the caption) is not shown here, so exact numeric enthalpy/entropy values and their error bars cannot be directly read off the panel; only the qualitative near-overlap of plateaus at each temperature is confirmed visually.

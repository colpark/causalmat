What this adds when combined with the settled SXRD+XANES result: SAXS probes electron-density contrast on the ~nm-to-tens-of-nm (particle/domain) length scale, distinct from both the atomic-lattice periodicity XRD measures and the local Co coordination/oxidation state XANES measures. The near-identical, straight SAXS contours and largely overlapping I(q) curves through full sodiation show that no new nanoscale phase-separated domains, pores, or metallic Co nanoparticles/clusters (which would create a strong new density-contrast feature) form during discharge. Combined with the earlier XRD (lattice retained) + XANES (Co stays oxidized, not metallic) result, this closes the specific gap that XRD/XANES alone left open: it was previously possible that a small metallic Co fraction could form below XRD's diffraction detection limit while still being locally reduced in XANES-invisible or minority domains, nucleating as nanoscale metallic clusters. The absence of any new/growing SAXS feature (only a small, transient intensity dip near 0.11-0.02V) argues against such nanoscale phase separation or metallic Co nanoparticle formation, strengthening the case that PCO undergoes at most slight, homogeneous, non-phase-separating reduction rather than local decomposition into metal + oxide domains.

What remains open: SAXS's small intensity drop at ~0.03-0.1 Å⁻¹ is not identified with any specific structural cause (subtle density/porosity change vs. minor particle rearrangement); the technique cannot rule out sub-nm-scale or highly dilute point-defect-level reduction (isolated atoms/very small clusters below SAXS contrast sensitivity); and none of the three techniques together gives a quantitative fraction of any residual reduction or a mechanism for why PCO resists reduction. The oxidation state remains only qualitatively bracketed between CoO and Co3O4, not quantified.

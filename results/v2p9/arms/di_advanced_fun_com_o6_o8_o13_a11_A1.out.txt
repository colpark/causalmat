Panel F3c shows A/L (photothermal-relevant absorbance normalized by sample length) plotted against concentration (0–200 microg/mL) at 808 nm, with a straight red fit line through five data points, R^2 = 0.9999, and a mass extinction coefficient alpha = 6.4 L g^-1 cm^-1 labeled on the plot. The points track the line very tightly across the full concentration range tested, with no visible curvature or saturation.

What this settles: the measurement establishes that Beer-Lambert linearity holds for this material's optical absorption at 808 nm over the tested concentration range, and it gives a well-determined, reproducible mass extinction coefficient (alpha = 6.4 L g^-1 cm^-1) for that wavelength. This is solid, quantitative evidence of a strong, linear NIR absorption cross-section — a standard and necessary precursor measurement for claiming photothermal conversion capability, since it shows the material absorbs 808 nm light efficiently and predictably in proportion to dose/concentration.

What it leaves open: this calibration curve alone says nothing about actual heat generation, photothermal conversion efficiency, temperature rise under laser irradiation, cytotoxicity, or in vivo/therapeutic performance ("value" in the PRP/therapeutic sense). It only characterizes optical absorbance, not conversion of absorbed light to heat, so it cannot by itself support a PRP efficacy or value claim — it only supports the narrower claim that the material has a strong, linear, well-calibrated 808 nm absorption coefficient suitable as a candidate photothermal agent. Whether that absorption translates into useful photothermal output requires separate temperature/irradiation data not shown in this panel.

This measurement settles that dislocation structures in the material become progressively denser and more organized as imaged features move from diffuse, unresolved contrast toward pile-up- and wall-type arrangements. It supports the paper's qualitative account that FCC dislocation content evolves through a sequence — mottled contrast, then tangled lines, then dense walls — with pile-ups and intersections forming as intermediate stages along the way, and it grounds this trend in a concrete, localized example: a pile-up band observed at a lamella boundary that fits within the broader panel-ordered progression.

What it leaves open is substantial. First, the ordering is inferred from the sequence of panels (F3a, F4a, F5a) across separate TEM fields/specimens, not from continuous imaging of a single region evolving in time, so no direct temporal or causal transformation from one feature type to the next has actually been observed. Second, the evidence mixes scales inappropriately: the pile-up observation is a single localized snapshot at one specific lamella boundary, while the progression claim is a general, distributed trend drawn from multiple panels across the material — so the local instance cannot be said to represent or drive the general trend. Third, both observations are categorical/descriptive (mottled, tangled, walls, pile-up) rather than quantitative, so no dislocation density rate or magnitude relationship linking the pile-up feature to the overall density rise is established. Finally, it cannot be ruled out that dense walls form through some other route that does not pass through the specific pile-up geometry captured in this observation.</message>

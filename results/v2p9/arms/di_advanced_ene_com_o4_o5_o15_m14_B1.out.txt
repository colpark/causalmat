The CV panels show Cu2S and RGO-Cu2S with large reduction peaks (~-41 and ~-43 mA/cm2 near -1.25 V) versus Pt's negligible ~-0.45 mA/cm2 (note the y-axis rescale between 6a and 6b needed just to see Pt's curve at all). This establishes that Cu2S-based electrodes are strongly catalytically active for the sulfide/polysulfide reduction reaction while Pt is essentially inert for it — a functional/electrochemical fact on its own.

Combined with the earlier TEM+XRD result (RGO sheets wrap Cu2S spheres, with XRD reflection attenuation ordering -1/-2 ~ bare Cu2S > -3, consistent with more GO giving more wrapping/masking of crystalline Cu2S), the new pairing shows that this wrapping does not come at an electrochemical cost: the RGO-Cu2S composite's peak current (~-43) is essentially the same as, or marginally larger than, bare Cu2S's (~-41), even though the wrapping is inferred to reduce exposed/detectable Cu2S surface. So the combination supports that RGO coating, despite structurally obscuring the Cu2S crystalline signal, does not electrochemically block access to the active sulfide-reduction sites — i.e., the wrap is not simply a passivating shell in the catalytic sense, and any conductivity/synergy from RGO at least offsets whatever surface occlusion occurs.

What this leaves open: the CV/measurement is for one RGO-Cu2S composition (RGO-Cu2S-2 per the caption), not the full -1/-2/-3 series, so there is no evidence on whether higher-coverage RGO-Cu2S-3 also retains activity or eventually degrades it — no dose-response for catalysis, mirroring the earlier gap for coverage itself. Mechanism (added conductivity vs. porous/thin coating vs. some synergistic catalytic contribution from RGO) is also not distinguished.

Panel F6a is the full CV overlay (Pt, Cu2S, RGO-Cu2S-2) run in the same methanol/water/Na2S/S/KCl electrolyte on the same -1.5 to 0 V (vs SCE) axis; F6b is a separate magnified plot of the Pt trace alone, spanning roughly +0.4 to -1.2 mA/cm2. Reading F6a directly confirms the numbers: Cu2S (blue) and RGO-Cu2S (red) both show a pronounced cathodic peak near -1.2 to -1.3 V reaching roughly -40 to -45 mA/cm2, while the Pt trace is visually almost flat, sitting close to zero across the whole voltage window — consistent with the ~-0.45 mA/cm2 figure, which only becomes legible at all because of the separate zoomed-in Pt-only panel (F6b).

What this justifies: the measurement solidly supports that Cu2S-based counter electrodes (with or without RGO) have vastly higher polysulfide-reduction current density — roughly two orders of magnitude — than Pt under identical electrolyte and scan conditions, i.e., Cu2S is a much more catalytically active counter-electrode material than Pt for this redox couple.

What it leaves open: the Cu2S (~41) and RGO-Cu2S (~43) peaks are close enough in the image that this single CV does not clearly establish RGO addition as a meaningful improvement over bare Cu2S — the difference could be within run-to-run or surface-area variation, since no error bars, scan-rate, or electrode-area/roughness normalization are shown. It also does not by itself demonstrate device-level benefit (cell efficiency, fill factor, stability, or charge-transfer resistance via EIS) — only that this particular electrode couple is far more catalytically active than Pt in CV. Whether that translates to superior solar-cell performance is not addressed by these two panels.

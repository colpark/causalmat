Both panels plot grain orientation variation (degrees) vs. thermal cycle number for ten tracked grains/regions (curves 1-10), one panel per coating layer.

Panel (a), YSZ topcoat: values stay in a tight band of roughly 0.7-2.3 deg across the whole 5-40 cycle range, oscillating (peaks near cycle 25 and 40) but never departing far from that band — no large early spike.

Panel (b), Al2O3-YSZ bond coat: at cycle 5 the ten curves are clustered high, roughly 4.3-11.4 deg (with the outlier curve "3" reaching ~11.4), then fall steeply and monotonically, converging to ~1-2 deg by cycle 20 and staying there through cycle 40 — essentially matching the YSZ level by the end.

This supports the stated measurement numbers exactly (5-cycle range ~4.3-11 deg falling to ~1 deg by 20 cycles for Al2O3-YSZ, versus a flat 0.7-2.3 deg for YSZ throughout), and it justifies the qualitative conclusion that the Al2O3-YSZ interlayer undergoes a large, transient grain-reorientation event early in thermal cycling that relaxes/anneals out with continued cycling, whereas the YSZ topcoat's grain orientation is comparatively stable and low-amplitude from the start.

What it leaves open: the plots give no error bars, replicate/sample-size information, or statistical test, so whether the early Al2O3-YSZ spike is significant versus measurement noise isn't established from the figure alone. The figure also does not identify the physical mechanism (e.g., interfacial stress relaxation, sintering, phase change, grain growth) driving the early high values, nor does it show whether this behavior generalizes beyond this specimen/cycling protocol, or how it connects mechanistically to coating failure (spallation, TGO growth, etc.) discussed elsewhere in the paper.

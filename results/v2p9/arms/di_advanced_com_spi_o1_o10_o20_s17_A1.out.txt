The figure (F7a) confirms the numeric measurement exactly as reported: pure PAEN = 79.3 MPa, BST@Ag 1% = 78.3 MPa, BST@Ag 3% = 74.4 MPa, BST@Ag 5% = 73.5 MPa, tensile strength in MPa, with visible error bars on each bar that are modest (a few MPa) and overlap somewhat between adjacent conditions (especially PAEN vs 1% Ag, and 3% vs 5% Ag).

What this supports: there is a clear, monotonic decreasing trend in tensile strength as Ag (silver) loading in the BST@Ag filler increases from 0 (pure PAEN) to 5%. The drop from pure PAEN to 1% Ag is small (~1 MPa, within/near error-bar overlap), while the drops to 3% and 5% Ag are larger (~5-6 MPa from baseline) and appear to exceed the error-bar range, supporting a real degradation in tensile strength at higher Ag/BST loading — consistent with a PRP-type story where increasing filler/particle content trades off some mechanical strength (plausibly for another benefit like antibacterial or dielectric function, "value" elsewhere).

What it leaves open: this single panel does not establish statistical significance (no sample size, no stated significance test, error bars only, likely SD not SE); it does not show whether the small PAEN-to-1%-Ag difference is meaningful given the overlapping bars; and it says nothing about mechanisms, other mechanical properties, or the actual "value" side of any PRP trade-off (e.g., antibacterial efficacy, dielectric constant) since only tensile strength is shown here. Any causal/tradeoff claim beyond "tensile strength decreases modestly and monotonically with Ag content" would require the paired "value" panel and statistical detail not present in this crop.

This step settles the PRP/value question at the level of qualitative, cross-modal corroboration, not quantitative proof. The value of pairing SEM (F1b–d) with XRD (F3a) is that two independent, differently-scaled measurement types — local/image-based (SEM) and bulk/sample-averaged (XRD) — point the same direction for the same sample (RGO-Cu2S-3): visually greatest RGO coverage coincides with the greatest attenuation of the Cu2S diffraction reflections, while the less-covered -1 and -2 samples resemble bare Cu2S. That convergence is what justifies the claim that increasing RGO coverage is accompanied by suppression of the Cu2S diffraction signal, consistent with sheets wrapping/burying the spheres — a conclusion neither technique could support alone, since SEM cannot speak to the bulk average and XRD cannot speak to spatial coverage.

What it leaves open: whether the SEM panel-d burial is representative of the whole sample or just a local field-of-view artifact; whether the XRD attenuation is truly caused by physical shielding of X-rays by wrapping sheets, or is instead (or additionally) a compositional dilution effect from a higher RGO mass fraction in sample 3; and any quantitative characterization — percent surface coverage, uniformity of coverage, or wrap thickness. In short, this settles that the two lines of evidence are mutually consistent and support a coverage-suppression narrative, but it does not settle the causal mechanism or provide any quantitative measure of coverage — those require further, ideally quantitative or spatially-resolved, measurements (e.g., coverage statistics across many SEM fields, or controls disentangling shielding from dilution).</message>

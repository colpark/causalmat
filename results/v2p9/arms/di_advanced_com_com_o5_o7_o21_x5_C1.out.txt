This measurement settles that the particle-level surface change visualized by SEM (bare BST, ~70 nm and smooth, versus a modified state that is rough, coated, and larger) is the same physical event that co-occurs with, and moves in the same direction as, the macroscopic color trend from white to dark brown as Ag content increases. In other words, taken together, the SEM images and the photograph support that whatever is producing the visible darkening is linked to a real, physically observable surface modification at the particle scale (coating/roughening), rather than the color change being an artifact unconnected to any structural change on the particles.

What it does not settle: because SEM here only compares two discrete states (bare vs. "modified") rather than a graded series across multiple Ag loadings, the data cannot show that the darkening scales continuously with the degree of coating or with SEM-observed particle size -- only that the two endpoints move together. The photograph alone establishes the color gradient across Ag content but contains no structural information, so it cannot independently confirm what is happening at the particle surface at intermediate loadings. Most importantly, because Ag content, particle size, and coating thickness all increase together as Ag loading rises, this combination cannot disentangle which of these correlated variables -- Ag amount itself, larger particle size, thicker coating, or some combination -- is mechanistically responsible for the color change. So the result supports co-occurrence and directional consistency between structural modification and color, but leaves both the continuous/graded relationship and the causal attribution among Ag content, size, and coating thickness open.</message>

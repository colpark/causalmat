The release curve (burst to ~340 µg by 8 h, ~670 µg by 24 h, then slow rise to plateau ~795 µg by 168 h) read together with the confirmed periodic mesostructure (single low-angle XRD reflection cross-confirmed by TEM channel/pore stripes, period ~5-6 nm, present in MBG/2Co/5Co) supports a joint conclusion that neither measurement gives alone: the material behaves as a genuine mesoporous drug carrier with a two-stage release mechanism plausibly tied to its ordered pore channels — an initial burst (release from surface and readily accessible near-surface pores) followed by a much slower, sustained release phase (diffusion-limited release from drug held within the internal periodic channel network) before saturating near 800 µg. The release curve alone would only show generic biphasic kinetics without any structural referent, and the XRD/TEM result alone would only show that a periodic channel structure exists without showing it does anything functionally relevant to drug loading/release. Together they support that this periodic mesostructure is functionally consistent with a reservoir-and-diffusion release behavior, i.e., the ordered porosity is plausibly load-bearing for the observed sustained release rather than incidental.

What remains open: the pair does not establish causation — no data here directly show drug residing inside the ~5-6 nm channels versus adsorbed on outer/macro surfaces, so the mechanistic link between channel occupancy and the slow-release phase is inferred, not demonstrated. Also unresolved from the earlier caveats: pore diameter (vs. period) is still unknown, so no quantitative diffusion-pore-size relationship can be derived; TEM's local (~200 nm) field means bulk uniformity of the channels feeding this macroscopic release curve is unconfirmed; and any dependence of release kinetics on Co content (2theta shifts) is not addressed by this single curve, which appears to represent one composition only.</message>

The UV-vis panel (F4b) shows plain TiO2 with a sharp absorption edge near 410 nm, while all three co-doped Er0.5Ce_xTi-O samples show edges extended well past 420 nm into the visible, plus two sharp superimposed peaks near ~520 nm and ~650 nm (with Ce0.2 the strongest overall absorber). Those two sharp visible peaks sit at wavelengths matching known Er3+ 4f-4f intra-configurational transitions, not broad Ce-related absorption.

Read together with the established XPS+EDS result (Er is present specifically as Er3+, at 168.8 eV, associated with the Ti-O framework), the combination supports that the sharp visible-region absorption features in the co-doped samples are attributable to that same chemically-identified Er3+ species — i.e., the optical/functional behavior (visible-light response) can now be tied to a specific confirmed oxidation-state species rather than just to "doping with an Er-containing precursor." This links the chemistry finding to a functional consequence for the first time.

What remains open: since Ce's oxidation state was never established (XPS covered only Ti, O, Er regions), the combination cannot say whether Ce3+, Ce4+, or a mixture is responsible for the additional edge broadening or for why Ce0.2 absorbs most — that trend is described only phenomenologically. Nor does either measurement resolve whether the Er3+ (or Ce) responsible for these optical features is lattice-substituted into TiO2 versus surface-associated, since XPS surface-sensitivity and UV-vis bulk optical response don't establish common spatial origin. The carbon-contamination and small-EDS-peak caveats from the earlier result also remain untouched by this optical data.</message>

This measurement settles a methodological point about how microstructure/pathway characterization (MEC) should be read across the sample series, not a claim about mechanism per se. What it supports: SEM alone is unreliable for judging whether a region is "undifferentiated/deformed" versus grain-refined, because in panel (a) SEM's surface-relief contrast is too coarse to resolve boundaries that EBSD clearly shows as fine equiaxed grains (~0.5-1.5 um). So the apparent state ordering from SEM alone (deformed → equiaxed → equiaxed) is corrected by EBSD to a continuum of grain sizes (fine, ~0.5-1.5 um in a; coarser, ~1.5-4 um in c), with panel (c) additionally cross-validated because SEM and EBSD converge on similar equiaxed, grooved-boundary microstructure and overlapping size estimates there. This justifies treating the pathway as one of progressive grain coarsening/equiaxing across the sampled panels, resolved at fine scale only when EBSD is available, rather than a transition from a truly deformed state to an equiaxed one.

What it leaves open: panel (b)'s size estimate is SEM-only and uncorroborated by EBSD, so it cannot be placed on the same footing of confidence as (a) and (c). The agreement in (c) is between two physically different boundary-detection mechanisms (topographic grooving vs. crystallographic misorientation), so it is convergence, not identical confirmation. There is no guarantee SEM and EBSD sampled the same field of view in (a), so the "deformed" SEM patch and the fine-grained EBSD patch might not be strictly the same location. Finally, with only three panels, no continuous, quantitative size-versus-processing-state relationship or mechanism/pathway model is established -- only a coarse ordering across the sampled cases.

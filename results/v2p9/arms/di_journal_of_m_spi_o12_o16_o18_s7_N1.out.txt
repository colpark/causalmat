Best-guess reconstruction (no actual figures/data were available to me, so this is inferential):

The paper's DSC trace on the Se-doped Mg3Bi2 sample most likely shows a single, well-defined thermal event (melting/phase-transition peak) at a temperature that lines up with the transition temperature already established for the parent Mg3Bi2 (or Mg3Bi2-based solid solutions) in earlier phase-diagram/thermal-stability literature. Read on its own, the new DSC scan only tells you that this particular Se-doped sample has some thermal event at some temperature — it can't tell you whether that event reflects the known Mg3Bi2 host phase or a new secondary/eutectic phase introduced by doping. The earlier established result (the known transition/melting temperature and solubility limits of Mg3Bi2), taken alone, likewise says nothing about whether the specific Se-doped composition in this paper actually stays single-phase.

Combined, the two results settle that the Se dopant is incorporated as a solid solution within the Mg3Bi2 matrix (rather than segregating as a separate phase or lowering the melting point via a new eutectic), because the DSC peak matches the pre-existing reference value rather than showing a shifted or additional peak. This supports the paper's claim that the observed enhancement in thermoelectric performance is due to genuine doping-induced electronic/band effects and good thermal stability, not an artifact of a secondary low-melting phase.

What remains open: the exact solubility limit of Se at higher doping levels, long-term/cyclic thermal stability, and the specific electronic mechanism (carrier concentration, band convergence) responsible for the ZT improvement — DSC alone cannot address these.

Fig. 14 plots average UTS vs average YS for AZ61 processed by many routes (ECAP variants, squeeze casting, multidirectional forging, friction stir processing, rolling, heat treatment, extrusion+ECAP, forging+heat treatment) alongside "this study" (the EPT-processed material). The three "this study" points (~275/380, ~295/355, ~335/448 MPa, YS/UTS) sit clearly above and to the right of every other literature point on the chart (max literature YS ~240, UTS ~318).

Combined with the settled earlier result, this justifies the conclusion that the EPT-processed alloy's property level (not just its shift relative to its own without-EPT baseline) is genuinely exceptional in the broader AZ61 literature, and that this superiority is associated with (co-occurs with) the fine sub-0.2 um dispersed-particle microstructure documented in the SEM images. Together the two observations support treating the particle dispersion as a plausible microstructural correlate of an unusually strong, high-strain combination of yield strength, UTS, and ductility relative to essentially all other AZ61 processing routes reported, not just relative to the un-treated control.

What remains open: (1) the literature comparison is with other processing routes on other AZ61 batches/compositions, not a controlled comparison against this study's own without-EPT baseline, so composition, prior thermomechanical history, or measurement-methodology differences across studies could account for some of the gap; (2) as before, particle dispersion is not quantified (no size/density/spacing data), so no dose-response or mechanistic (e.g., Orowan/grain-refinement) link between the particles and the strength/ductility levels is established; (3) only three EPT points and no intermediate EPT doses are shown, so whether more or less EPT would move the material further along this property space, or where the without-EPT baseline itself would fall on this same chart, is not shown in Fig. 14 and cannot be determined from it.

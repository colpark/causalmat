The F2b curve (strain-hardening rate dσ/dε vs. true strain for Al0.9CoFeNi2 EHEA) shows exactly the three-stage pattern described: a steep drop from ~85 GPa near zero strain to ~14 GPa by ε≈0.009 (region I), a slow decline to ~5 GPa by ε≈0.058 (region II), then a turn negative (region III) just before fracture (~ε=0.06–0.065), matching the inset true stress–strain curve that flattens and ends around there.

What this justifies: the alloy sustains only modest, gradually decaying strain-hardening capacity after an initial rapid stage-I drop, consistent with conventional dislocation-based hardening (multiplication/forest interactions) rather than a mechanism that produces sustained or rising hardening (e.g., strong TRIP/TWIP behavior, which would show a rate plateau or secondary rise). The final negative excursion (region III) is best read as the onset of necking/plastic instability rather than a genuine new hardening mechanism, since it coincides with the stress curve nearing its peak.

What it leaves open: this single macroscopic curve cannot identify the microstructural pathway responsible for the region-II plateau-like decay — whether it is dislocation-phase-boundary interaction, eutectic lamellar interface hardening, partial stacking-fault/twinning activity, or some other mechanism. Distinguishing among these (and confirming which phase, FCC or BCC, dominates) requires complementary TEM/EBSD/XRD data not present in this panel. It also reflects only one composition, so it says nothing about Al-content dependence.

The CV panel (F2c) shows overlaid curves for cycles 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, and 20, all nearly superimposed across the 0.4-1.2 V window. Three distinguishable anodic/cathodic peak pairs are visible: a small pair near 0.55/0.52 V, a broader pair near 0.89/0.87 V, and the largest, sharpest pair near 1.08/1.07 V, with peak potentials essentially unchanged and peak currents only modestly attenuated (mainly between 1st and 5th cycle, then stable) out to 20 cycles.

What this justifies: the material has (at least) three distinct, reversible or quasi-reversible redox processes active in this potential window, and this multi-step redox behavior is retained with good positional and magnitude stability over short-term cycling (20 cycles). This supports a claim of electrochemical reversibility and short-term cycling stability of the redox chemistry underlying PRP's storage mechanism — i.e., the peaks are not artifacts of a single irreversible first-cycle process, since they persist through cycle 20.

What it leaves open: the CV alone does not establish specific capacity, rate capability, coulombic efficiency, or long-term (>20 cycle) durability/capacity retention — no value in mAh/g or Wh/kg can be derived from this panel. It also does not identify which chemical species or ion each peak pair corresponds to (would need complementary ex-situ XRD/XPS or reference-compound CVs), nor does it demonstrate performance in a full device or under realistic charge/discharge rates. So this measurement supports mechanistic reversibility and short-cycle stability, but says nothing quantitative about capacity or practical "value."

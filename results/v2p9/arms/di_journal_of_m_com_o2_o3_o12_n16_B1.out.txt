Panel F7a plots minimum creep rate vs. applied stress at 175°C for AZ91 and AZ91+SiCp composites (5, 10, 15, 20, 25 wt%). The 15–25 wt% curves sit clearly below AZ91 (lower creep rate at a given stress, higher stress exponent n=8.4–8.9 vs. 7.2), while 5 and 10 wt% overlap or lie slightly above AZ91 (n=7.4, 7.7) — i.e., creep resistance improves only past a loading threshold between 10 and 15 wt%.

Read together with the earlier settled result (broad, non-uniform-but-not-segregated SiCp dispersion confirmed by two independent imaging modalities), the combination supports this: the creep improvement seen at higher SiC content is consistent with genuine particulate reinforcement acting through the bulk microstructure, rather than being an artifact of gross particle segregation or clustering in one region. Because the micrographs rule out confinement/segregation as the likely explanation, the composite's superior creep resistance can be read as plausibly load-transfer/dispersion-strengthening in origin rather than a microstructural defect effect.

What it does not settle: the imaging is qualitative and not tied to specific wt% samples or matched fields, so there is no evidence linking dispersion *quality* differences to the creep threshold observed between 10 and 15 wt%. The combination cannot explain why 5–10 wt% composites fail to outperform AZ91 (whether due to insufficient volume fraction, insufficient load transfer, or subtly poorer local dispersion at those specific loadings), nor can it establish a quantitative dispersion-to-creep-rate relationship. The strengthening mechanism itself (Orowan, load transfer, grain refinement) remains unestablished by either result.</message>

Panel F4f (aenm.202003419) shows a full electrolytic Zn-MnO2 cell built with pre-deposited zinc specifically on CnC HS foil, cycled to 500 cycles: charge/discharge capacity declines only modestly (~1.0 to ~0.9 mAh), and the 1st vs 100th cycle voltage profiles nearly overlap.

Combined with the earlier SEM/CE result, this extends the morphology-performance link established for CnC HS beyond the ~205-cycle CE window into a genuine full-cell context out to 500 cycles: the same substrate whose flat/compact zinc deposit was linked to sustained coulombic efficiency also supports stable capacity and reproducible voltage behavior in a working full cell over a substantially longer horizon. This strengthens the practical relevance of the earlier structural/CE correlation—showing the benefit isn't confined to a coin-cell CE metric or a short cycling snapshot but plausibly carries through to device-level capacity retention.

What it still leaves open: F4f contains no C HS full-cell run, so there is no paired contrast here—one cannot say CnC HS's full-cell stability is better than what C HS would give, only that CnC HS itself performs well. The data are also bin-averaged over 10-cycle intervals, so fine-grained (per-cycle) fluctuations and any late-stage divergence are smoothed out. And as before, this is an observational pairing (one substrate, one condition), so other differences between substrates besides morphology still cannot be excluded as contributors to the full-cell stability, and no causal/mechanistic claim about morphology driving capacity retention is established.

Panel F4f (galvanostatic charge/discharge cycling of pre-deposited Zn on the CnC HS foil host, electrolytic Zn-MnO2 cell) supports the stated measurement: charge and discharge capacity both start near 1.0 mAh and decline gradually to roughly 0.9 mAh over 500 cycles, with the two capacity traces (charge = filled circles, discharge = crosses) essentially overlapping throughout, indicating high, stable Coulombic efficiency. The inset voltage-vs-capacity profiles for cycle 1 and cycle 100 nearly superimpose (same plateau shapes and voltage window ~0.0–1.8 V), confirming the discharge/charge reaction mechanism is preserved with little polarization growth over the first 100 cycles.

What this justifies: the CnC HS foil host supports reasonably stable, high-Coulombic-efficiency Zn plating/stripping (i.e., functions as a working current collector/host) over 500 cycles in this electrolytic Zn-MnO2 configuration, with only modest (~10%) capacity fade and minimal voltage-profile drift through cycle 100 — a basic service-capability/PRF claim of "works over many cycles with slow degradation."

What it leaves open: the panel gives no direct measure of Coulombic efficiency percentage, no comparison to a bare-foil or bare-Zn control under identical conditions (so the improvement attributable to the CnC HS structure specifically is not isolated here), no data on rate capability, capacity retention beyond cycle 500, or failure mode (dendrite formation, corrosion) at end of life. It also does not report areal/mass loading, current density, or whether the ~10% fade is due to active material loss, electrolyte depletion, or interfacial resistance growth — so long-term degradation mechanism and comparative benefit over untreated foil remain undetermined from this figure alone.

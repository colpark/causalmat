Panel F5c shows OER LSV curves in 0.5 M KHCO3 (neutral electrolyte) for Co5Fe3Cr2, Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5, Co, and IrO2. Reading the curves: Co5Fe3Cr2 (black) rises earliest and reaches the 10 mA cm-2 threshold at the lowest overpotential, marked eta10 = 452 mV, consistent with the stated measurement. IrO2 (olive/dark yellow) rises later, needing higher potential to hit the dashed 10 mA cm-2 line, and Co (green) plateaus lowest overall, never approaching 10 mA cm-2 within the shown potential window (only reaching ~6-7 mA cm-2 by 1.8 V).

What this supports: In neutral (near-pH-neutral, bicarbonate-buffered) electrolyte, the Cr-doped ternary Co5Fe3Cr2 catalyst outperforms IrO2 (the benchmark) and the other Co-based compositions (Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5, plain Co) for reaching a practical OER current density benchmark (10 mA cm-2), with a lower overpotential (452 mV). This is a legitimate, single-condition activity comparison establishing relative catalytic activity ranking under neutral conditions.

What it leaves open: This LSV panel alone does not establish "service_capability" in the PRF sense of durability, stability, or operational lifetime — it is a single potentiodynamic sweep, not a chronopotentiometry/chronoamperometry stability test. It says nothing about long-term degradation, corrosion resistance, Faradaic efficiency, or performance under real device/service conditions over time. It also does not report iR-correction status, electrode loading, or reproducibility (error bars/replicates), so quantitative comparison precision (e.g., exact IrO2 eta10 value) can't be extracted confidently beyond "IrO2 requires meaningfully higher overpotential than Co5Fe3Cr2." Claims of practical service capability (stability, cycling, real-use durability) are not settled by this panel and would need separate stability data.

The F4c SEM image (BST@Ag1%/PAEN) shows individual particles fully enveloped by polymer matrix, with blurred, indistinct particle-matrix boundaries and no visible voids or gaps at the interface — the hallmark of good wetting/adhesion rather than a mechanically loose, debonded filler.

Read together with the earlier settled result — that MPTMS grafting (FTIR CH2/Si-O bands) co-occurs specifically with a switch from hydrophilic (BST-OH) to lipophilic (BST-SH) phase behavior — this micrograph extends that chemical/wettability finding to a physical, in-composite consequence: the surface-modified, organically compatibilized filler achieves intimate, gap-free contact with the organic PAEN matrix. Neither result alone shows this. FTIR+partitioning alone stops at liquid-phase behavior of the bare particle and says nothing about how it behaves once embedded in a solid polymer. The SEM image alone shows good interfacial morphology but, without the earlier chemistry, would not tell you why the interface looks this way. Combined, they support a plausible causal chain: siloxane grafting → lipophilic surface character → favorable compatibility with, and wetting by, the organic matrix → the observed void-free, blurred-boundary interface.

What remains open: this is one micrograph of one composition (Ag1%) with no BST-OH/PAEN control shown here for comparison, so the image alone cannot rule out that the good interface arises from some other cause (e.g., Ag loading, processing) rather than the grafting per se. It also does not resolve the earlier-noted ambiguity of which grafted feature (organic CH2 tail vs. Si-O linkage) is responsible, nor does it provide any quantitative measure of adhesion strength or a graded relationship between grafting degree and interfacial quality.</message>

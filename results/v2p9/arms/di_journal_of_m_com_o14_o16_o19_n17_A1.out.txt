The three micrographs are all polarized/etched optical or SEM grain images, but at very different scales: F7c and F9c carry a 50 µm bar, while F10d carries a 2 µm bar — a 25x difference in field of view. So the ranking "F10d (~1 µm) < F7c (~5-8 µm) < F9c (~15-30 µm)" is not something you can verify by eyeballing grain counts across the three crops directly; it depends on the stated scale bars, and F10d's much higher magnification is itself evidence that its grains are far too fine to resolve at the other two panels' magnification.

What the images do support: a monotonic image-level trend across the caption-labeled processing times (25s, 30s, 40s, all followed by a 10-min hold) — shorter pre-treatment (25s, F10d) gives the finest, most uniform equiaxed structure; 30s (F7c) gives a visibly coarser, still fairly uniform equiaxed grain structure; 40s (F9c) gives the coarsest grains, and F9c also shows elongated/lath-like internal features consistent with retained deformation-twin remnants that F7c and F10d largely lack. This is consistent with a picture in which longer nucleation/incubation time before or during the anneal permits more grain growth (and less complete twin consumption).

What it leaves open: the panels alone cannot establish the underlying mechanism (e.g., continuous vs. discontinuous dynamic recrystallization, twin-mediated nucleation vs. simple thermal grain growth) — that requires kinetic or crystallographic (EBSD/misorientation) data, not just final grain-size morphology. Nor can the magnification-mismatched images independently confirm the quantitative size ranking; that rests on the reported measurements, not visual comparison.

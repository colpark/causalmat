This measurement supports the conclusion that Se doping over x = 0.005-0.04 acts as an electron donor, raising 300 K carrier concentration n_H from ~0.5×10^19 to 3.3×10^19 cm^-3, and that across this same range the samples' 300 K Hall mobility (~125-170 cm^2/Vs) exceeds every literature value reported at comparable n_H. So the justified claim is: Se doping increases carrier density in the expected donor-like direction, and it does so without incurring the mobility penalty typically seen in other reported samples at matched carrier concentration -- i.e., these samples show a mobility advantage relative to the existing literature at similar doping-induced carrier levels.

What it leaves open is substantial. First, it does not establish the microscopic mechanism -- that Se is actually substituting on the Sb site -- since that requires structural or site-specific evidence beyond n_H/mu_H trends. Second, because the literature comparison set is heterogeneous (different synthesis routes, microstructures, grain sizes), the apparent mobility advantage could partly or wholly reflect differences in sample quality/scattering rather than being a genuine consequence of the Se-doping process itself. Third, mobility data are only reported for x=0.005-0.02 and separately for x=0.04, so no single optimal composition or peak-mobility point can be identified from this pairing. Finally, since only a few doping points/ranges were measured rather than a dense series, the result supports a directional trend/ordering across x but not a fitted, continuous n_H-mu_H relationship or precise functional form.</message>

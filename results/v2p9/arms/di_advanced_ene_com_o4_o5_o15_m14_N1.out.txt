The new paper almost certainly reports that the hierarchical Cu2S microsphere/reduced-graphene-oxide (rGO) counter electrode gives a photoconversion efficiency (PCE) in the quantum-dot-sensitized solar cell that matches or exceeds a Pt or bare-Cu2S reference counter electrode, backed by lower charge-transfer resistance (Rct) and higher exchange-current density in EIS/Tafel measurements against the polysulfide (Sx2-/S2-) electrolyte.

The "established earlier result" this is implicitly being read against is the well-known prior finding that Cu2S alone is catalytically excellent for polysulfide reduction (rivaling or beating Pt) but is structurally/chemically unstable — it dissolves, aggregates, or delaminates from the substrate, so its catalytic value (its "PRP," i.e., its practical performance-value) degrades over device lifetime even though its instantaneous efficiency is high.

Read together, the two results settle something neither settles alone: that wrapping Cu2S in rGO preserves (or nearly preserves) the high catalytic efficiency Cu2S alone can achieve while addressing the stability/durability liability that limited its standalone practical value. That is, the combination supports "Cu2S's catalytic promise can be made durable via a conductive graphene scaffold without sacrificing performance" — a conclusion about efficiency alone, or stability alone, cannot support.

What is left open: actual long-term/cycling stability under realistic operating conditions (many hours/days, temperature, humidity), scalability/cost of the rGO-wrapping synthesis, and whether gains generalize beyond the specific QD sensitizer and electrolyte formulation tested.

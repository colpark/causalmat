This measurement supports a qualitative, directional link between plating overpotential and cycling stability for PRF/service_capability: the host (CnC HS) that shows the smaller plating overpotential gap at high current density (about -0.05 V vs -0.10 V for C HS) is also the host that sustains stable Coulombic efficiency (roughly 93-100%) far longer (about 205 cycles) than the plain host, which collapses to zero CE by about 40-48 cycles and stops near 50 cycles. So the finding justifies saying that CnC HS's superior high-rate plating behavior and its markedly better cycling durability co-occur and point in the same direction, consistent with CnC HS having an overall service-capability advantage over C HS.

What it leaves open is substantial, however. First, the two observations were not measured on a shared x-axis or under a common test condition (one is a single-sweep, discrete-current-density voltage plot; the other is a per-cycle efficiency trace at presumably one fixed condition), so no quantitative rate-to-lifetime relationship can be derived - the data cannot say how much overpotential difference translates into how many extra cycles. Second, because overpotential is only sampled at a few discrete current densities rather than continuously, the result cannot explain or predict the specific cycle counts (~205 vs ~40-50) at which efficiency degrades. Third, and most importantly for causal interpretation, the pairing cannot rule out a shared-cause explanation: both the overpotential difference and the cycling-stability difference could simply stem from the same underlying structural or compositional property of CnC versus C, without plating overpotential itself being the mechanism that drives the CE collapse. So this settles correlation/co-occurrence in the same host, not a mechanistic or quantitative causal relationship between rate performance and cycle life.

F2d shows, at high magnification on the tensile fracture surface, small arrowed holes and boxed microcracks concentrated at lamella ends and edges of the fractured EHEA. This is a fractographic (post-mortem, macro-to-micro damage) observation, distinct in kind from the earlier TEM-based dislocation-substructure progression (mottled contrast to tangled lines to dense walls, Observations A/B).

Read together, the two results support a coherent damage narrative: the earlier result establishes that dislocation content organizes into increasingly dense, wall-like/pile-up structures as deformation proceeds, and F2d shows that fracture damage (voids and microcracks) nucleates preferentially at lamella boundaries and edges — the same class of microstructural sites (lamella boundaries) implicated in the pile-up observation. This combination supports the inference that the dislocation pile-ups and dense-wall structures accumulating at lamella boundaries are mechanistically linked to where damage (holes/microcracks) ultimately initiates, i.e., that boundary-localized dislocation storage plausibly seeds the fracture-initiating defects seen in F2d. Neither result alone gives this: the TEM progression alone shows substructure evolution without linking it to actual fracture sites, and F2d alone shows damage locations without any dislocation-content evidence explaining why those sites failed first.

What remains open: F2d is a post-failure fractographic snapshot, so it cannot show that the boundaries which cracked are the same boundaries where dense walls/pile-ups were imaged in F3–F5 — no single boundary is tracked from dislocation buildup to void/crack formation. The link between "dislocation-dense lamella boundaries" and "crack-initiating lamella boundaries" is therefore inferred by shared location-type (lamella boundary/edge) rather than directly observed in one specimen or region. Quantitative density-to-damage correlation, and whether all such boundaries crack or only some, are also not established.

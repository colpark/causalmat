Note: the caption text given for F11a ("and its anti-bacteria effect") does not match the panel content, which is a cumulative drug-release curve (released amount, ug, vs. time, h) — I'm answering based on what the panel actually shows.

What the release curve shows on its own: a fast initial rise (0-8h to ~340 ug), continuing rapid rise to 24h (~670 ug), a marked slowdown from 24-72h (~780 ug), and an essential plateau from 72-168h (~795 ug). Alone, this only describes release kinetics — it says nothing about where the drug was held or why release decelerates.

What the earlier mesoporosity result shows on its own: MBG, 2Co-, and 5Co-MBG each have a periodic mesoscale feature that is also genuinely accessible/porous (co-occurring low-angle XRD line and type IV hysteresis). Alone, that says nothing about drug loading or release behavior.

Combined, the two support a coherent picture: a scaffold with real, accessible mesopores is a plausible physical reservoir for the observed two-phase release — an early burst (0-24h) consistent with drug near the outer surface/pore mouths, followed by a much slower, diffusion-limited release (24-168h) as remaining drug exits through the internal mesopore network until near-saturation. This links structural porosity to a release-controlling mechanism in a way neither fact alone can.

What remains unsettled: neither measurement establishes that the drug was actually loaded inside the mesopores rather than merely adsorbed on outer/macro-surfaces; no diffusion model was fit to confirm pore-mediated transport; and since the earlier result never compared pore order/size across MBG, 2Co, 5Co, this release curve (seemingly a single composite curve) cannot show whether Co doping altered release rate, capacity, or mechanism.

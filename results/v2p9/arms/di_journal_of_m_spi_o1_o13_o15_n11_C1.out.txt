This combination of results settles that the two ordered phases present in the as-cast alloy (L12 and B2, confirmed in bulk by XRD) do not respond identically to the onset of strain: at the first strain increment, one lamella in an adjacent pair stays featureless while its neighbor develops mottled contrast and interfacial line contrast. That is enough to support a qualitative claim of phase-dependent (asymmetric) mechanical response — i.e., strain accommodation is not uniform across the two-phase microstructure, and whatever mechanism produces the mottling/line contrast (most plausibly dislocation activity or local lattice distortion) engages preferentially in one lamella type before the other becomes visibly active. This is consistent with, and lends support to, a picture in which one phase acts as the primary carrier of early plastic strain while the other remains comparatively elastic or otherwise resistant at this stage.

What it does not settle: which phase (L12 or B2) is the one going mottled, since XRD only confirms bulk phase fractions and cannot be correlated lamella-by-lamella with the TEM images, and the TEM description itself is contrast-based rather than diffraction-indexed per lamella. It also cannot establish that this asymmetry is a general, reproducible feature of the microstructure, since only one strain step and one lamella pair were examined — it could be a local or coincidental effect rather than a systematic phase-dependent pathway. Finally, because no dislocation density or areal fraction is reported, the result cannot quantify how much more active one phase is than the other, nor can it identify the specific mechanism (dislocation glide, stacking faults, twinning, etc.) behind the contrast. In short: asymmetric response is supported; which phase, its generality, and its mechanistic/quantitative basis remain open.

What the panels show: F6a plots H2 absorption vs. time at 360°C for the C0 sample across three cycles — cycle 1 rises slowly and gradually (never plateauing within 180 min, reaching ~3.3 wt%), while cycles 2–3 rise fast then plateau near 5.2 wt%; the bracket marks a 66 min diffusion-controlled second stage before the plateau. F6b shows the C10 (10 wt% C@Co-added) sample, where all three cycles nearly overlap, rising fast and plateauing by ~11–15 min, with the diffusion-controlled second stage compressed to 11 min.

What this settles: the measurement directly supports a kinetic claim — adding the C@Co catalyst shortens the rate-limiting, diffusion-controlled stage of hydrogen absorption roughly sixfold (66→11 min) at fixed temperature (360°C) and comparable capacity (~5 wt% plateau in both). This is solid evidence that the additive accelerates the hydrogenation pathway's slow step, consistent with a catalytic effect on hydrogen dissociation/diffusion, and it also explains why C10's cycle-to-cycle curves converge almost immediately (activation is largely complete by cycle 1) whereas C0 needs multiple cycles to reach the same fast regime.

What it leaves open: the panels give only bulk absorption curves, not the microstructural or chemical mechanism — they cannot distinguish whether the acceleration comes from catalytic H2 dissociation at Co sites, carbon/Co-created diffusion channels, grain refinement, or reduced particle size. No TEM/XRD/XPS evidence is shown here to confirm which specific pathway is responsible; that mechanistic attribution would require other figures/panels not included in this pair.

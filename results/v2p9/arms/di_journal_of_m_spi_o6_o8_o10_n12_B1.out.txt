Panel F5a (rho vs T, x=0 to 0.04) shows: x=0 starts near 650 microOhm-m at 300 K and falls with rising T (semiconductor-like, negative dRho/dT), while all Se-doped samples (x=0.005-0.04) sit far lower (12-23 microOhm-m at 300 K) and rise with T (metallic-like, positive dRho/dT, a hallmark of a degenerate, heavily doped semiconductor).

Read together with the Hall result (n_H rising ~0.5e19 to 3.3e19 cm-3 with x, mu_H staying high, 125-170 cm2/Vs, above literature at matched n_H), this settles that the doping is not just adding carriers in a way that shows up only in Hall coefficient/mobility numbers -- it also produces the expected bulk transport signature: an order-of-magnitude drop in resistivity plus a switch from semiconducting to metallic-like T-dependence upon doping. Since rho = 1/(n e mu), a large rho drop at essentially maintained/high mu independently corroborates that the resistivity collapse is carrier-count driven (donor doping raising n), not an artifact of a Hall-specific effect (e.g., multiband Hall anomalies) -- the two independent measurements (Hall and resistivity) are mutually consistent with genuine degenerate donor doping by Se.

What this still leaves open: the resistivity panel does not pin down the microscopic doping site (Se on Sb) any more than before; it doesn't resolve which single x is "optimal" (x=0.005 sits visibly above the tighter, nearly overlapping 0.01/0.02/0.04 curves, suggesting non-monotonic behavior with x rather than a clean trend, and the mu_H range gap at x=0.02-0.04 noted earlier is not filled); and the literature-mobility heterogeneity caveat is unaffected, since rho alone doesn't isolate mu from n.

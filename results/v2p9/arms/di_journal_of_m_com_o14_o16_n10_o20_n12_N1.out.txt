Best-guess reconstruction (no actual figures/data were provided to me, so this is inference from the title/journal and standard practice in this literature):

This paper almost certainly reports kinetic curves (absorption/desorption rate vs. time/temperature) and an apparent activation energy (via Kissinger analysis) for MgH2 milled/composited with carbon-cobalt, plus microstructural evidence (XRD/TEM/SEM) that Co is dispersed as fine particles anchored on a carbon matrix. The likely finding is a marked drop in desorption activation energy and faster sorption kinetics relative to un-catalyzed Mg, attributed jointly to (a) carbon's role in limiting particle growth/agglomeration and providing diffusion channels, and (b) cobalt's role in dissociating H2 and/or forming a transient Mg-Co hydride phase that acts as a catalytic "gateway."

Read alone, this measurement cannot separate whether the improvement is a physical (dispersion/surface-area) effect or a chemical (new catalytic phase/pathway) effect — carbon and cobalt are confounded in one composite.

Combined with an earlier established result — presumably that cobalt alone (without carbon) is known to catalyze MgH2 via formation of a Mg-Co intermediate/new-phase pathway — the new measurement's activation-energy drop and dispersed-Co microstructure together let you attribute the further kinetic gain specifically to carbon's nanoconfinement/anti-agglomeration function, additive to the already-known Co chemical pathway. So the two results jointly support a dual-mechanism (synergistic) catalytic pathway: chemical (Co, new-phase mediated) plus physical (carbon, dispersion mediated).

What remains open: the quantitative split between the two contributions, cycling/durability behavior, and whether the same mechanism holds at larger, non-milled/industrial scale.

The two panels confirm the numbers as stated: F5b (Al2O3-YSZ) shows grain orientation variation of roughly 4.3-11 deg at 5 cycles, decaying sharply to near 1-2 deg by 15-20 cycles and staying flat thereafter; F5a (YSZ) oscillates in a narrow 0.7-2.3 deg band across all cycle times (5 through 40), with no comparable early spike or monotonic decay.

Combined with the settled XRD result (YSZ = predominantly tetragonal, Al2O3-YSZ = cubic zirconia), this supports a correlation between crystal phase and grain-orientation dynamics during thermal cycling: the cubic-phase coating undergoes a large, transient burst of grain reorientation early in cycling that then rapidly stabilizes to a low, steady value, whereas the tetragonal-phase coating never shows that burst but instead sustains small, recurring orientation shifts throughout the whole cycling history. Neither observation alone shows this pattern-of-difference tied to phase identity; the XRD result only names the phases, and the orientation-variation data alone would just describe two distinct time-courses without a stated cause. Together they license the interpretation that phase (cubic vs. tetragonal zirconia) is associated with, and plausibly underlies, two different modes of microstructural response to cycling — one front-loaded and self-stabilizing, one low-amplitude but persistent.

This leaves open: no mechanism is given for why cubic symmetry would produce an early reorientation burst or why tetragonal symmetry would produce sustained low-level variation (e.g., no link to variant switching, twinning, or stress accommodation is demonstrated); the association could instead trace to confounded differences between the two coatings (grain size, porosity, Al distribution, thermal-expansion mismatch) rather than phase itself; and no causal or quantitative (e.g., stress-strain) link between phase fraction and orientation-change magnitude is established.

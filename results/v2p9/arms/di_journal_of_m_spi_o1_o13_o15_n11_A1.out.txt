Both panels are TEM micrographs from a post-deformation foil, and the label "dislocations pileup" is printed directly on each. Panel c is the cleaner match to the measurement described: a distinct dark, curved band of tangled dislocations running along a lamella boundary, with an arrow explicitly pointing to it as the pileup. Panel a (captioned "dislocations configurations in the FCC/L12 phase") shows a dense, more homogeneous dislocation tangle filling the interior of a lamella, not a discrete line at a boundary — it is labeled "pileup" but visually reads more like generalized forest/tangle density than a localized boundary array.

Together these images support the qualitative claim that dislocations accumulate and are impeded at (or near) a lamella/phase boundary during deformation, i.e., the boundary acts as an obstacle to slip — consistent with an interface-blocking mechanism contributing to local hardening or stress concentration in this two-phase microstructure.

What is left open: this is a single static snapshot, so it cannot establish the sequence of events (whether pileup precedes or follows other damage), the operative slip system or Burgers vectors, quantitative pileup density/spacing, or a causal link to macroscopic strength/ductility. Sample thinning/relaxation artifacts also cannot be excluded, and only one region is imaged, so representativeness is unverified.

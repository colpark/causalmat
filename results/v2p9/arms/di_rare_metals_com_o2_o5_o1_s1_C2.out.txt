This measurement (taken together, the XRD and DSC panels) settles that the La-containing Al-Cu-Mn alloy hosts a distinct compound absent from the Sm alloy. The support is convergent: an independent structural signal (extra XRD reflections near ~21-37, 50, and 67 degrees, seen only with La) coincides with an independent thermal signal (a separate DSC dissolution shoulder B at ~638 C, beyond the Al2Cu-related peak A at ~605 C, again only with La). Because these are two different kinds of measurement - diffraction geometry and thermal-event temperature - agreeing on "La but not Sm," the case for a La-specific phase is stronger than either measurement alone would provide, since co-occurrence across independent methods is harder to explain by artifact or coincidence than either one in isolation.

What it leaves open is substantial. It does not establish the identity of the new phase - the Al6Cu6La / cubic NaZn13-type assignment comes from matching the DSC peak to literature values, not from indexing the XRD pattern against this pairing, so the structural identification is external to this measurement. It gives no quantification of how much of the phase forms - only that its signatures are detectable. It cannot rule out that Sm forms the same or an analogous phase in an amount below the detection limit of both XRD and DSC; absence of signal is not proof of absence of compound. Finally, the link between the two panels is only co-occurrence in the same sample, not a derived or calculated relationship between diffraction intensity and thermal energy - so this is corroboration of presence, not a joint quantitative measurement.

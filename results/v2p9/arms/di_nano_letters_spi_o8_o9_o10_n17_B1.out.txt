The ex vivo fluorescence result adds a functional, whole-organism readout that the zeta-potential/EDS data could not provide on their own: both draining lymph nodes (inguinal and axillary), under both subcutaneous and footpad injection routes, show markedly brighter (green, higher-intensity on the shared 0-2500 scale) signal for the anti-CCL21 DNA aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 conjugate than for the neat BSA@ZIF-8 (cyan, lower intensity). Read together with the earlier charge-reversal/EDS evidence that phosphorus-bearing aptamer is physically associated with the cationic particle, this combination supports the conclusion that the aptamer is not just adsorbed onto the particle in vitro but remains attached (or otherwise co-localizes with the MOF's fluorescent cargo) through delivery in vivo, and that this attachment confers an enhanced, CCL21-receptor-mediated targeting/accumulation advantage in lymph nodes relative to the unmodified, non-targeted particle — i.e., the surface-bound aptamer is functionally active and biologically consequential (service_capability), not merely a static surface-charge artifact.

What this leaves open: the fluorescence readout measures net accumulated signal in the whole node, not the location, density, or integrity of aptamer-particle association at the moment of imaging — so it cannot confirm that the aptamer is still bound (versus having dissociated en route and acting merely to have altered initial biodistribution), cannot quantify surface coverage, and cannot distinguish electrostatic from covalent/coordination attachment (the same qualifications noted for the zeta/EDS data persist). It also does not establish the mechanism as specifically CCL21-receptor binding rather than some nonspecific consequence of altered surface charge/size from aptamer coating; no receptor-blocking or free-aptamer competition control is shown here. Quantitative brightness values, node numbers per group, and statistical comparison are also not given in this panel.

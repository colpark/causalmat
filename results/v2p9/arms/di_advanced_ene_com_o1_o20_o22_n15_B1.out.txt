The panel (F5c) shows a neutral-electrolyte OER LSV comparison across five catalysts (Co5Fe3Cr2, Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5, Co, IrO2): Co5Fe3Cr2 is the leftmost curve to cross 10 mA cm-2 (eta10 = 452 mV), ahead of the Fe-only, Cr-only, Co-only, and IrO2 benchmark curves.

Read together with the established DFT+EELS result, this settles that the composition combining Fe and Cr — the same class of material for which DFT computed the most favorable (lowest Delta G) thermodynamics for Co oxidation, and for which EELS corroborated rising Co oxidation state with anodic potential — is also the composition that delivers the best measured catalytic outcome (lowest overpotential to reach a fixed OER current density, beating even IrO2). So the combination supports a three-way convergence: computed thermodynamic ease of Co oxidation, spectroscopic evidence that oxidation state actually rises, and a macroscopic activity benefit, all pointing the same direction for the tri-metallic formulation.

What it leaves open: the LSV panel uses stoichiometric labels (Co5Fe3Cr2, Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5) that are not obviously identical to the DFT/EELS material set (CoCrOOH, CoFeOOH, CoFeCrOOH, Co(OH)2), so exact one-to-one correspondence between the activity ranking and the computed/spectroscopic ranking is not established here. The LSV is also run in neutral electrolyte, whereas the EELS measurements were taken at 1.4-1.5 V (electrolyte/condition matching to the LSV is not shown in this panel). Finally, activity is a lumped functional outcome; the panel cannot show that the advantage is mechanistically caused by the Co2+->Co3+ or Co3+->Co4+ step specifically rather than other factors (surface area, conductivity, Fe/Cr electronic effects).

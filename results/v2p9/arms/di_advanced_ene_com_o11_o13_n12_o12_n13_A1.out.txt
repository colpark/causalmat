The spectrum (F3b) shows pristine CnC HS with a sharp, resolved resonance near 290.1 eV superimposed on the C 1s edge, alongside features near 284.9 eV (sp2/aromatic C=C π*) and 288.3 eV (C–O/C=O-type). After zinc deposition, the 290.1 eV peak is gone while the 284.9 and 288.3 eV features persist essentially unchanged in position (with overall lower normalized intensity in the post-Zn trace, consistent with the plotted normalization/offset).

What this measurement supports: the disappearance is selective, not a uniform intensity drop across the whole spectrum — if zinc simply formed an inert overlayer that screened or attenuated the carbon 1s signal (a purely physical, non-chemical attenuation), you would expect all three features to shrink together, not one to vanish while two survive intact. This selectivity is evidence for a change at the specific carbon site or bonding state responsible for the 290.1 eV resonance during/after zinc deposition — i.e., some chemical or electronic modification localized to that species, distinct from the aromatic carbon and the oxygenated carbon that remain.

What it leaves open: this does not by itself identify the chemical nature of the 290.1 eV state (what functional group or bond it corresponds to), nor does it establish the mechanism — whether zinc chemically bonds to/consumes that site, reacts with it, or whether the loss instead reflects a subtler surface-sensitivity/probing-depth effect specific to that resonance's cross-section. It also does not, from this panel alone, establish causality (that zinc deposition specifically, rather than any accompanying process step, is responsible) or generalize beyond this one sample pair.

F14 is a YS-UTS scatter plot placing "this study" (three red points at ~275/380, ~295/355, ~335/448 MPa) against a wide literature set (ECAP variants, squeeze casting, multidirectional forging, friction stir processing, rolling, heat-treated, etc.), all of which cluster at lower UTS-for-given-YS, topping out near 240 YS/318 UTS. Every literature point lies below and/or left of the "this study" cluster, so this figure establishes only that the EPT-processed material (as a set of three conditions) occupies a combined strength region not reached by any prior processing route in the compiled literature — a population-level, cross-study benchmarking claim.

Combined with the earlier SEM/tensile-curve result, the two pieces together support a two-tier causal-adjacent story: (1) within-study, refinement from EPT co-occurs with a specific, non-uniform property shift (yield down, UTS and elongation up) for one paired condition, and (2) across-study, the resulting EPT strength combination as a whole exceeds all prior literature processing routes. Combining them lets you argue the EPT route is not just internally associated with refinement but externally competitive/superior in the YS-UTS trade-off space compared to established alternatives — i.e., the qualitative co-occurrence found in-house is paired with a demonstrated external benchmark advantage.

What remains open: F14 does not by itself validate the mechanism (feature-size refinement) as the cause of the superior YS-UTS position — it is a different-samples, different-studies comparison, so processing parameters, alloy composition/impurity differences, testing protocols, and specimen geometry are not controlled for. Neither panel establishes a dose-response (how much refinement produces how much of the YS-UTS shift), nor rules out confounding microstructural changes (dislocation density, texture, precipitate state) as the true driver. So the combination supports "EPT produces a distinctively favorable YS-UTS combination relative to the field, and this favorable shift co-occurs with grain/feature refinement in a within-study pair," but it leaves open whether feature refinement specifically (versus other concurrent EPT effects) causes the literature-beating position, and whether the advantage would hold under matched composition/testing conditions.

This combined EDS/HRTEM result supports the claim that the C10 material's carbon component physically functions as a surface coating on the Ce/Y–Mg alloy particles — i.e., it justifies a "service capability" of carbon at the particle-surface/edge location: EDS shows C (and Co) signal concentrated on surfaces and densest along edges/faces, and HRTEM at one such edge confirms an actual carbon phase (curved carbon layers, 0.386 nm spacing) sitting directly adjacent to crystalline alloy fringes (CeMg12, 0.258 nm; Y5Mg24, 0.265 nm) with an intervening amorphous band. Together these justify saying the surface C signal is real carbon material physically situated at the alloy particle boundary, not an EDS artifact — supporting a coating/interface role for carbon relative to the alloy grains.

What it leaves open: (1) representativeness — only one local edge region of one milled particle was imaged at atomic resolution, so the coating morphology and edge/amorphous-band structure are not shown to hold across all the surface/edge/face locations where EDS detects C; (2) scale mismatch — EDS gives only a coarse areal map, so the pairing is qualitative spatial corroboration, not a quantified or statistically representative account of coating extent or thickness distribution; (3) Co's role specifically — no Co-containing lattice fringe was observed in HRTEM, so while Co and C signals co-locate at the EDS map scale, this does not demonstrate that Co is atomically dispersed within or bonded to the carbon phase — that remains an open, unconfirmed inference beyond coarse co-location.</message>

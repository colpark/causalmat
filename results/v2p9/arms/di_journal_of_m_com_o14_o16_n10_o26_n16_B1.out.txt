Both JMAK plots (F9a for C0, F9b for C10) are strongly linear (R2>0.98) across the four desorption temperatures, yielding Avrami exponents eta = 1.59 (uncoated C0) and 1.32 (carbon/CQCo-coated C10). On its own this kinetic result only shows that desorption in both samples obeys a single JMAK regime and that the nominal exponent shifts downward with the carbon additive — it says nothing about what physical feature causes that shift, since an Avrami exponent is a phenomenological fitting parameter compatible with several nucleation/growth pictures. The earlier EDS+HRTEM result, by itself, is purely structural: it shows carbon (curved graphitic layers) sitting at alloy-particle edges/surfaces in C10, without linking that structure to any measured reaction behavior.

Read together, the two results support a mechanistic inference that neither gives alone: because the carbon phase is independently shown to reside at the particle surface (the diffusion/desorption interface) in C10, and because C10's desorption kinetics show a genuinely different (lower) Avrami exponent than the uncoated C0 baseline, the combination justifies attributing the change in desorption mechanism/rate-limiting step to the presence of that surface carbon layer, rather than treating the kinetic shift as an unexplained numerical difference.

What remains open: the HRTEM view is one edge of one particle, so whether that same coating geometry is representative of the bulk powder that underwent the macroscopic JMAK measurement is still an extrapolation; no Co-containing lattice fringe was resolved, so any catalytic role for Co (versus carbon alone, versus milling-induced particle-size/defect changes) is not distinguished; and the eta values themselves remain ambiguous among multiple diffusion/interface-controlled growth models, so the specific mechanistic pathway is inferred, not proven.

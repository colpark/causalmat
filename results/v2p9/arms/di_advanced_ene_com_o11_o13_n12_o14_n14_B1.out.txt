Panels confirmed: F3d (CnC HS, N-doped host) shows a largely flat, low-relief surface with only a few sparse, thin protruding platelets after Zn plating; F3e (C HS, undoped host) shows the same field of view completely covered by a dense mat of sharp, interlocking platelets.

Read together with the settled XAS/Raman result, this SEM comparison lets you connect the nitrogen-associated chemistry change to a morphological consequence. The earlier result shows that zinc loading coincides with a shift in nitrogen electronic state and a weakened ~1200 cm-1 vibrational feature on the N-containing host — i.e., something at/near N sites responds chemically to Zn. The new micrographs show that on that same class of N-rich host (CnC HS), zinc deposits flat and sparse, whereas on the N-poor/undoped host (C HS) it grows as a dense mat of sharp platelets. Combined, this supports the claim that the nitrogen-linked chemical interaction with zinc (detected spectroscopically) is associated with, and plausibly promotes, more uniform/suppressed zinc nucleation and growth, while its absence corresponds to rough, dendrite-prone platelet growth — a structure–function link (chemistry at N sites → deposit morphology) that neither the spectroscopy nor the SEM alone establishes.

What it leaves open: the pairing is still correlational across two different host materials (CnC vs C), which likely differ in more than nitrogen content (porosity, surface area, defect density, conductivity), so confounding structural factors cannot be excluded as drivers of the morphology difference. It does not establish that the specific chemistry detected by XAS/Raman is mechanistically responsible for nucleation behavior, nor does it resolve whether the effect is direct Zn–N bonding versus an indirect lattice perturbation, nor whether morphology differences reflect a bond-formation process versus two static end states.</message>

Panel F7a (tensile strength bar chart) shows a monotonic decline with Ag-modified BST loading: Pure PAEN 79.3 MPa, BST@Ag1% 78.3 MPa, BST@Ag3% 74.4 MPa, BST@Ag5% 73.5 MPa. Note the 1% loading (the same composition, BST@Ag1%, whose SEM showed blurred, gap-free particle-matrix outlines) causes only a small drop (~1.3%) from neat resin, well within/near the error bars, whereas higher loadings (3%, 5%) show progressively larger losses (~6-7%).

Combined with the earlier FTIR/SEM result, this quantitative, dose-resolved measurement adds what neither settled alone: it shows that at the specific loading (1% Ag) where SEM indicated intimate, gap-free filler-matrix contact, bulk mechanical performance (tensile strength) is nearly preserved relative to the unfilled polymer -- consistent with the interfacial-bonding interpretation actually translating into a macroscopic property, and giving the first magnitude/dose information tying interface quality to a measurable mechanical outcome. It also establishes a dose-response trend (strength falling as filler content rises) that FTIR/SEM alone could never show, since those were categorical/single-composition observations.

What remains open: tensile strength is a bulk composite property, not a direct interfacial measurement (e.g., no fracture-surface SEM, pull-out test, or interfacial shear strength is reported), so the modest drop at 1% is consistent with, but does not prove, good bonding -- it could also reflect low filler volume fraction alone. The decline at 3%/5% is most simply attributed to agglomeration/dispersion effects at higher loading rather than a change in the silane chemistry itself, and no SEM was shown at those higher loadings to confirm interfacial quality degrades there. Causality (grafting → improved interface → retained strength) is still inferential, not directly demonstrated by fracture-surface or bond-density evidence.

Panel F5c (LSV in 0.5 M KHCO3, neutral) shows Co5Fe3Cr2 reaching 10 mA/cm2 first, at eta10 = 452 mV, ahead of IrO2, Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5, and plain Co.

Read alone, this panel only ranks catalytic activity/service performance: the Fe+Cr-containing ternary outperforms the industry-standard IrO2 benchmark and the binary/unary controls. It says nothing about why.

The earlier DFT+EELS result, alone, only established that Co oxidation (Co2+->Co3+ then Co3+->Co4+) is thermodynamically favorable and does occur under anodic potential, with DFT ranking CoCrOOH best for the first step, CoFeOOH best for the second, CoFeCrOOH intermediate on both, and Co(OH)2 worst — a mechanistic/thermodynamic result disconnected from any measured performance number.

Combined, the two support a specific claim: the composition that is not the single-step DFT optimum for either oxidation transition, but combines Fe and Cr (i.e., is intermediate on both steps rather than extremal on one), is the one that empirically delivers the best OER activity, surpassing IrO2. This licenses the interpretation that facilitating both Co oxidation steps together (rather than maximizing either alone) plausibly underlies the ternary's superior service capability — a link neither observation could support by itself, since the DFT/EELS work had no activity data and the LSV panel had no mechanistic data.

What remains open: (1) whether "Co5Fe3Cr2" in this panel is the same material as "CoFeCrOOH" in the DFT/EELS work is inferred from naming, not shown directly; (2) causation is not established — activity differences could equally reflect conductivity, surface area, or active-site density rather than the oxidation-state chemistry; (3) the EELS measurement's composition/step-resolution limitations noted earlier still apply, so the mechanistic story remains a plausible corroboration, not a proof, of why this composition wins.

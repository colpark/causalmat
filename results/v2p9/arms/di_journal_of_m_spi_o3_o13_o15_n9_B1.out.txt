F4a shows a dense, mesh-like dislocation network filling the interior of a lamella identified as the FCC (L1₂) phase — a bulk-tangle configuration, not organized at any boundary. F4c shows a distinctly different arrangement: a dark, continuous band of dislocations lying along a lamella interface (arrowed), with a sparser dislocation network in the adjacent grain interior — the "pile-up" label marks accumulation specifically at the boundary rather than throughout the phase.

Read together with the earlier result (baseline sharp lamellar interfaces plus early-strain evidence that one lamella deforms preferentially while its neighbor stays featureless, i.e., deformation onset is phase-selective and interface-localized), this measurement supports that the interface itself continues to act as the operative site as deformation proceeds: dislocations generated in the active phase are not transmitted across the boundary but stack up against it, so the earlier "interface-localized" asymmetry is not just a transient first-step effect — it persists (and here appears to intensify) into a discrete barrier/pile-up configuration alongside continued bulk tangling within the active phase. This is the first evidence that the lamella boundary functions mechanically as a slip-transfer obstacle rather than merely as a site of early contrast asymmetry.

What remains open: this is still a single local, qualitative snapshot (one interface, one strain state), so it does not establish how general or reproducible the pile-up behavior is across interfaces, nor where in the strain sequence this sits relative to F3a.

This combined XRD+SEM result supports a co-location/association claim, not a causal or mechanistic one for PRP/behavior_class. What it justifies: within this single sintered sample, the bulk phase inventory (SiAlON species Si4Al2O2N6 and Si1.8Al0.2O1.2N1.8, alongside alpha-Al2O3 and cBN) is consistent with, and spatially compatible with, the gap-free, cleaved-facet interfaces observed by SEM between cBN grains and the surrounding matrix. That is, the data are coherent with a "SiAlON formation correlates with well-bonded (gap-free) cBN/matrix boundaries" behavior class — the two observations do not contradict each other and can reasonably be described together as reflecting a well-integrated microstructure.

What it leaves open, plainly: (1) XRD is a bulk measurement, so it cannot confirm that the SiAlON phase is actually present at the specific boxed grain boundaries shown in the SEM image — the "SiAlON region" label there is positional/visual, not chemically verified at that spot; (2) SEM only shows morphology (no gaps, cleaved facets), not composition, so it cannot itself confirm SiAlON identity; (3) there is no second sample (e.g., a binder without SiAlON formation) for comparison, so the result cannot show that SiAlON formation specifically — as opposed to some other sintering variable — causes gap-free bonding; (4) both measurements are static, single-sample endpoint characterizations, so co-occurrence of phase and morphology is established, but a causal or mechanistic (process-level) link between SiAlON formation and interfacial bonding is not. In short: supports association/plausibility of a favorable bonding-related behavior class; does not settle localization, composition confirmation at the interface, specificity, or causality.

The DFT panel (F1a) plots computed ΔG for two sequential Co oxidation steps across four model (oxy)hydroxides. CoFeOOH has the lowest ΔG for the Co3+→Co4+ step (~6.2 eV) and CoCrOOH the lowest for Co2+→Co3+ (~1.05 eV), with CoFeCrOOH intermediate on both axes and Co(OH)2 highest on the Co3+→Co4+ axis (~6.7 eV). Read together with the settled XPS/EELS result (Fe-containing samples show higher measured Co3+ character and blue-shifted L3 edge), the combination supports a thermodynamic rationale for that experimental trend: the Fe-containing composition is computed to require the least energy to push Co into its higher valence state, which is directionally consistent with why the Fe-containing samples were spectroscopically observed to sit at higher Co oxidation state than the Cr-only or bare Co sample. This strengthens the case that the effect is a genuine, energetically-grounded compositional phenomenon rather than a spectroscopic artifact, since an independent theoretical calculation points the same direction as two independent experimental probes.

It leaves open several things: the DFT is computed on idealized model structures, not verified to structurally match the actual measured samples; ΔG (eV, a reaction free energy for an OER-relevant proton-coupled step) is yet another distinct quantity from the XPS fraction and EELS edge shift, so this is directional agreement only, not quantitative cross-validation. Critically, CoFeCrOOH's intermediate/mixed behavior (not lowest on either axis) still does not cleanly isolate whether Fe alone, or Fe-Cr jointly, drives the effect — the same ambiguity flagged earlier persists. No absolute oxidation state or electron-transfer mechanism is established.

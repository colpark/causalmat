The pH trace adds a third, independent line of evidence, and reading it together with the polarization+immersion-rate result narrows what can be claimed.

What the pH panel shows on its own: Mg-Zn-Y-Nd drives the Hank's solution alkaline in a sustained, monotonic way (7.4 to ~10 by day 22, then plateau), consistent with continuous hydrogen-evolving Mg dissolution reaction (Mg + 2H2O -> Mg(OH)2 + H2, which consumes H+ and raises pH). 317L SS instead depresses pH (falls to ~6.1 by day 20) before partially recovering, and the blank control stays near neutral/slightly basic before drifting down — i.e., 317L SS's chemical signature is different in kind, not just weaker.

Combined with the earlier settled result (polarization: Mg-Zn-Y-Nd's corrosion potential ~-1.5 V vs -0.25 V for 317L SS, currents 2-3 decades higher; immersion mass-loss rate: Mg-Zn-Y-Nd 0.13-0.38 mm/y vs 317L SS <0.07 mm/y at every timepoint), this pH curve now gives a third, chemically distinct measurement channel that agrees with the same ranking — Mg-Zn-Y-Nd is far more chemically active in solution than 317L SS. Because pH is a solution-chemistry signature rather than an electrochemical or gravimetric one, its concordance strengthens the case that the faster-degradation conclusion is not an artifact of any single measurement modality.

What it still does not settle: the mechanism of degradation (though the sustained pH rise is consistent with Mg hydroxide formation and hydrogen evolution, this is suggestive, not proven, since no direct measurement of H2 evolution or hydroxide/corrosion-product identity is shown here); a quantitative link between pH rise, current density, and mass-loss rate; whether the tests share identical media/conditions; and what 317L SS's pH drop then partial recovery mechanistically reflects.

F7b shows breaking elongation falling monotonically across all four samples: 6.9% (pure PAEN) → 5.8% (BST@Ag 1%) → 4.5% (3%) → 4.4% (5%).

Combined with the earlier SEM/photograph result, this settles that the property change tracking Ag content is graded across the whole compositional series, not just a bare-versus-modified jump. The SEM comparison alone only contrasted two discrete states (bare vs. "modified" BST), so it couldn't show a continuous trend; the photograph showed a graded color change but carried no structural or mechanical information. Elongation now supplies a second macroscopic, quantitatively graded measurement (across 0/1/3/5% Ag) that moves monotonically in step with the color-darkening/coating trend already established. That agreement between two independently measured, continuously graded macroscopic properties (color and ductility) — both ordered the same way as Ag loading — supports that whatever the surface-modification event is, it (or its consequences) scales progressively with Ag content across the full range, rather than being an all-or-nothing effect confined to the two SEM endpoints.

What it still leaves open: causal attribution is unresolved. Ag content, particle size, and coating thickness remain confounded by construction, so the elongation decline cannot be pinned on the coating/roughening specifically rather than on particle-size growth or simple filler-loading effects. Also, breaking elongation is a bulk mechanical response, not direct structural evidence — a monotonic mechanical decline is consistent with, but does not independently confirm, that it is caused by the SEM-visualized coating rather than some other correlate of increasing Ag (e.g., agglomeration or interfacial stiffening unrelated to surface chemistry).

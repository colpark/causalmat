The DSC panel (F5a) shows a discrete Tg-related transition that shifts and broadens across the series: pure PAEN 176 °C, then BST@Ag1/3/5% at 146, 149, 157 °C — a large, monotonic-with-loading depression/shift relative to neat polymer, distinct from PAEN's own step.

Read alone, DSC only shows that adding the modified filler changes a bulk thermal transition of the composite; it says nothing about what chemical species causes this or whether any new component is really present versus measurement artifact/mixing effect. The FTIR+TGA result alone (established) shows a new CH2/Si-O species is chemically present and corresponds to a real, quantifiable mass-loss component, but says nothing about whether that species has any effect on the polymer matrix itself.

Combined, the three measurements settle that the FTIR/TGA-identified surface species is not chemically and thermogravimetrically inert with respect to the PAEN matrix: its presence coincides with a systematic, loading-dependent perturbation of PAEN's own thermal transition, consistent with an interfacial interaction (e.g., plasticization, altered chain mobility/free volume, or filler-matrix coupling) between the new species and the host polymer — something neither the DSC data alone nor the FTIR/TGA pairing alone could establish, since DSC alone couldn't be tied to the identified species and FTIR/TGA alone couldn't be tied to a matrix-level effect.

Left open: whether this interaction is due to covalent Si-O-BST grafting versus physisorbed/adsorbed silane species (DSC does not distinguish bonding type); no quantitative link between the ~1% TGA mass and the magnitude of the Tg shift; no mechanistic explanation (plasticization vs. confinement vs. crosslinking) for the shift; and no direct spectroscopic/mechanical bonding verification.

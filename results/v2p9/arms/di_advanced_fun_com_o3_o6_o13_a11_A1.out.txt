Panel F3c shows A/L at 808 nm plotted against concentration (0-200 microg/mL), with a linear fit yielding slope alpha = 6.4 L g-1 cm-1 and R2 = 0.9999, based on five data points.

What this settles: it confirms that the sample obeys the Beer-Lambert relationship over the tested concentration range at 808 nm - i.e., mass-normalized absorbance (A/L) scales linearly with concentration, with no visible saturation, aggregation-induced deviation, or scattering artifact across 0-200 microg/mL. This linearity is what licenses treating alpha = 6.4 L g-1 cm-1 as a well-defined, concentration-independent mass extinction coefficient at 808 nm for this material under these measurement conditions (solvent, cuvette path length, dispersion state as prepared for this plot).

What it leaves open: the measurement says nothing directly about photothermal conversion efficiency, photothermal response, or "value" in a performance sense - it is purely an optical absorption calibration. It does not establish behavior outside 0-200 microg/mL, does not address photostability, does not compare this alpha to other materials to claim superiority, and does not by itself demonstrate any therapeutic, heating, or device-level outcome. It also does not specify uncertainty/error bars on alpha beyond the reported R2, nor confirm reproducibility across independent batches. In short: the data support a solid, well-calibrated extinction coefficient at one wavelength over a limited concentration window, but any claim about photothermal efficacy or comparative "value" of the material requires additional measurements (e.g., temperature-rise/photothermal conversion efficiency experiments) not shown in this panel.

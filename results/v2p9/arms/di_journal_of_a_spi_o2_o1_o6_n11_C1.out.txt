This result settles a phase-identification claim, not a "value" or performance metric: taken together, the two XRD observations support that the YSZ coating retains a predominantly tetragonal zirconia (t-ZrO2) structure while the Al2O3-YSZ coating is predominantly cubic zirconia (c-ZrO2). The support comes from convergence within a single measurement: Observation A's phase labels are corroborated by Observation B's diagnostic peak-shape evidence — the small (012) peak near 43 deg and the split peaks near 35, 60, and 74 deg appear only in the YSZ pattern and are absent in the Al2O3-YSZ pattern, and these are exactly the features that distinguish a lower-symmetry tetragonal reflection pattern from a higher-symmetry cubic one. So B explains the basis for A's phase call. Both patterns also show no marked or visible Al2O3 reflection.

What this leaves open: (1) no mechanism is established for why the two coatings stabilize into different zirconia phases (tetragonal vs. cubic) — that requires additional explanation beyond these two observations; (2) there is no quantification of the fraction of tetragonal vs. cubic phase in either sample, so this is a qualitative phase call, not a phase-purity or phase-fraction measurement; (3) the absence of an Al2O3 XRD peak in both patterns does not indicate where the aluminum went (e.g., amorphous phase at grain boundaries) — Al distribution is simply not addressed by either observation; and (4) both observations derive from the same XRD panel (F1) on the same samples, so they represent two levels of description (summary label vs. underlying peak detail) of one measurement rather than two independent corroborating measurements. In short, the result justifies a confident qualitative phase assignment but not a mechanistic, quantitative, or Al-localization conclusion.

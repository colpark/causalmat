Panel F4b (UV–vis) shows TiO2 with a sharp absorption edge near 410 nm and near-zero absorbance beyond it, while all three Er0.5CexTi-O co-doped samples show edges extended well past 420 nm into the visible, plus small sharp shoulder peaks near ~525 nm and ~650 nm, with Ce0.2 absorbing most strongly overall.

What the combination settles: The earlier XPS/EDS pairing already established that the Er detected is a confirmed chemical species — Er3+ (168.8 eV) — sitting in a Ti-O oxide framework, not merely an unconfirmed EDS peak. UV-vis alone only shows that co-doped particles absorb more visible light than TiO2; it cannot on its own say whether this reflects a real, chemically verified dopant or an artifact/impurity. The earlier chemical result alone establishes dopant identity but says nothing about any functional/optical consequence. Together, they support that a chemically confirmed dopant (Er3+, embedded with a Ti-O oxide) is associated with a genuine electronic/optical change — extension of light absorption into the visible relative to undoped TiO2 — i.e., a link from confirmed composition to a measurable optical property relevant to applications like photocatalysis.

What remains open: (1) Ce's oxidation state is still unconfirmed (XPS never covered Ce), so the finding that Ce0.2 gives the strongest absorption cannot be tied to a verified Ce chemical species — the composition-dependent trend in optical strength has no confirmed chemical explanation. (2) Whether Er/Ce sit in the TiO2 lattice or on the surface is still unresolved, so it's unclear if the edge shift reflects true bandgap narrowing versus surface-state absorption. (3) The role of the unaddressed dominant carbon signal in the optical response is unknown.

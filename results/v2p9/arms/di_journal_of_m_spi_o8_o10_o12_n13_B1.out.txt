Panel F5c (power factor vs T) shows all doped samples (x=0.005-0.04) far above undoped x=0 (~23-29 µW/cmK^2 at 300 K falling to ~19-22 at 623 K, versus x=0 rising only from ~2 to ~5), with x=0.01 highest across the whole range and x=0.005 lowest of the doped set.

Read together with the established result that Se doping simultaneously raises 300 K Hall mobility and cuts resistivity across this same doping range, the combination supports the conclusion that the doping-driven mobility enhancement/resistivity reduction actually pays off as useful thermoelectric output: since PF = S^2/rho, the large, uniform PF gain across all doped x confirms the resistivity drop is not offset by a collapsing Seebeck coefficient, i.e., the low-resistivity, high-mobility state produced by Se doping is genuinely beneficial rather than merely a resistivity artifact.

What it leaves open: (1) the PF ranking (x=0.01 highest, x=0.005 lowest) does not match the mobility/resistivity ranking already noted (which lumped x=0.005-0.02 together and singled out x=0.04 separately at similar n_H), so PF cannot be attributed to mobility alone — Seebeck coefficient, which neither panel reports, must also vary with x and could be driving the discrepancy. (2) Because only the x=0.04 resistivity/mobility comparison was carrier-density-matched, the PF gain at other x values still cannot be split between mobility rise and concurrent carrier-density change from doping. (3) Everything remains a 300 K-anchored, few-point (x=0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04) comparison, supporting an ordering/co-occurrence pattern but no continuous dose-response or mechanistic claim.

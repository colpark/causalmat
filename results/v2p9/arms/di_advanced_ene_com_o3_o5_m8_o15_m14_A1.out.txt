What the measurement settles: In panel (a), the CV traces for Cu2S and RGO-Cu2S counter electrodes show a clear cathodic (reduction) peak near -1.2 to -1.25 V vs SCE with peak current densities in the mid-to-high 30s to low 40s mA/cm2 (visually, RGO-Cu2S's blue trace peaks slightly higher, around -43, than Cu2S's red trace near -41), while the Pt trace on the same axes is essentially a flat line near zero. Panel (b), the magnified Pt-only CV (note different y-axis scale, 0 to 1.2 mA/cm2), confirms Pt's reduction branch reaches only about -0.4 to -0.45 mA/cm2 near the negative voltage limit, roughly two orders of magnitude smaller than Cu2S/RGO-Cu2S. This supports the conclusion that both Cu2S-based electrodes have far greater electrocatalytic activity toward the polysulfide (S2-/S) redox reaction than Pt, consistent with their being superior, low-cost counter-electrode catalysts for this redox couple.

What it leaves open: the figure does not establish device-level performance (Voc, Jsc, fill factor, PCE) of a full cell using these electrodes, nor stability/durability over repeated cycling. The RGO-Cu2S vs. Cu2S-alone peak difference (~43 vs ~41 mA/cm2) is small and read visually off a low-resolution crop, so it is not strong quantitative evidence that RGO meaningfully improves on Cu2S alone — only that both greatly outperform Pt. Peak potential shifts (overpotential), electrode area/normalization details, and reproducibility across replicate scans are not addressed by these two panels.

This settled result justifies the conclusion that the lamellar two-phase architecture is not a passive backdrop but the actual template that governs where and how plasticity first appears: strain is partitioned unequally from the very onset of deformation, with one lamella type remaining featureless while its neighbor develops mottled contrast plus line contrast concentrated at the interface. In short, the first measurable defect activity (STR) is phase-selective and interface-localized rather than spatially uniform — this is a structural/topological claim about where deformation initiates, tied directly to the lamellar substrate (the "extended" architecture) at the earliest strain step.

What it leaves open is substantial. First, phase identity is unresolved: nothing here shows which lamella (A or B) is B2 versus FCC — the asymmetry is established, not its chemical/crystallographic labeling. Second, the mechanism behind the asymmetry is undetermined — it could reflect intrinsic differences in slip resistance between the two phases, or purely geometric/constraint effects from the interface itself, and this pair of observations cannot distinguish between those causes. Third, only two strain states are compared (undeformed baseline and first strain increment), so this supports only a before/after ordering, not any trend, rate, or scaling of defect accumulation with strain. Finally, whether this phase-selective, interface-localized pattern persists unchanged, intensifies, or reverses at higher strains is entirely open — later steps would be needed to know if the asymmetry is a transient onset effect or a stable feature of the deformation pathway.</message>

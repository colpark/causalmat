This result supports the conclusion that the two ordered phases confirmed present in the as-cast alloy (L12 and B2, via bulk XRD) do not respond identically to the onset of strain: at the first strain step, one lamella stays featureless while its neighbor develops mottled contrast plus line contrast concentrated at the interface. Taken together, this justifies saying the microstructure shows an asymmetric, phase-partitioned response to early strain, with defect-like contrast appearing preferentially in one phase and the interface acting as a locus where that contrast is expressed — a reasonable first indication of heterogeneous (rather than uniform) plastic accommodation across the two-phase system.

What it does not settle: which specific phase, L12 or B2, is the one acquiring contrast — XRD only establishes bulk phase content and cannot be tied to individual TEM lamellae, and the TEM description is contrast/morphology only, not diffraction-indexed to a phase. It also does not establish generality: a single lamella pair at a single strain step cannot show that this asymmetry holds across the broader microstructure or persists, grows, or reverses at higher strains. Finally, since no dislocation count or areal fraction is reported, the finding is a qualitative contrast difference, not a quantified defect density comparison — so claims about how much more "defected" one phase is than the other, or whether the mottled/line contrast truly reflects dislocation/planar-slip content versus another diffraction artifact, remain open.</message>

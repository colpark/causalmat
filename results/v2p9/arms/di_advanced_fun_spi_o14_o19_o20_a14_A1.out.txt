Panel F5d (viewed directly) shows six spaghetti plots of individual tumor-volume curves over ~20 days: PBS, BNs-BSA, Lasers, and Lasers alone all show every animal's tumor rising steadily to 750-1500+ mm3 with no regressions. BNs-BSA+660nm looks similar to the untreated controls (steady growth to >1000 mm3). BNs-BSA+808nm shows tumors suppressed near zero through roughly day 14, after which four of the five curves turn upward and regrow to 250-700 mm3 by day ~20-22 (one curve stays flat). BNs-BSA+Lasers (the dual 660/808 combination) shows all curves flat near zero volume for the full ~20-22 day observation window, with no visible regrowth in any animal.

What this justifies: the measurement directly supports that under 808 nm alone, tumor control is transient/incomplete (durable in 1/5, relapsing in 4/5 after ~day 14), whereas under the combined 660+808 nm laser regimen with BNs-BSA, tumor control is complete and durable in all five animals for the entire monitored period. This licenses a claim of superior and more uniform photothermal/PRF efficacy for the dual-wavelength combination versus 808 nm monotherapy, at the level of individual-animal curves (not just group means), i.e., the effect is consistent across animals, not driven by one responder.

What it leaves open: the panel only covers ~20-22 days, so durability beyond that window (long-term cure vs. delayed relapse) is untested; mechanism (why 808 nm alone fails in most mice) is not addressed by this panel; and no statistical comparison (e.g., significance test between arms) is shown here, only raw individual trajectories.

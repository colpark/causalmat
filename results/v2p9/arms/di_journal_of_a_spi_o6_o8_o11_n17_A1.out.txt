Panel F8 (Fig. 8, Al2O3-YSZ coatings during thermal cycling) shows six coupon photos labeled 0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40 cycles. At 0 and 5 cycles the coating surface is intact and unbroken along the edges. By 10 cycles a corner region shows clear spallation (loss of coating, exposing a darker/rougher substrate patch at one corner). At 20, 30, and 40 cycles that same corner-edge spallation persists and appears to enlarge somewhat, while the bulk of the coated face remains intact.

This image directly supports the stated measurement: the coating survives to 5 cycles without visible damage, first visible corner spallation appears at 10 cycles, and that spalled region persists and grows slightly out to 40 cycles rather than healing or being confined to a single event. So the photographic record is consistent with a service-capability conclusion that this coating maintains adhesion/functional coverage for only a handful of cycles (on the order of 5) before edge-driven, cumulative spallation initiates, after which damage is progressive but gradual (not catastrophic) through 40 cycles.

What is left open: this single figure gives no quantitative measure of spalled area, no data on whether the substrate is exposed/oxidizing beneath the spall, no information on coating composition or process variant differences (only one coupon type is shown, not a comparison series), no mechanistic explanation (thermal expansion mismatch, sintering, phase transformation) for why corners fail first, and no statistics across multiple coupons/replicates — it is a single representative specimen's visual record, so variability and failure-mode generality cannot be assessed from this panel alone.

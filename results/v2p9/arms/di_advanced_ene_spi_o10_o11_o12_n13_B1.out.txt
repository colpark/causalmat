F3b shows: pristine CnC HS has a sharp, tall peak at 290.1 eV plus features at 284.9 and 288.3 eV; after zinc deposition the 290.1 eV peak vanishes entirely while 284.9 and 288.3 eV persist (with reduced but still-present shape). Crucially, the caption/plot legend explicitly names both traces as "Pristine CnC HS" and "CnC HS following zinc deposition."

Combined with the earlier N 1s result, this settles two things neither panel settles alone. First, it resolves the earlier ambiguity about sample identity: the N 1s zinc-deposited spectrum (previously only inferable via elimination through panel A) is now directly confirmed to be CnC HS, since this C 1s panel explicitly labels the zinc-treated sample as CnC HS. Second, it shows that zinc deposition is associated with selective, feature-specific spectral change rather than uniform degradation: one distinct carbon environment (290.1 eV) disappears completely while others (284.9, 288.3 eV, the generic sp2/oxidized-carbon features) survive, mirroring the earlier pattern in which one nitrogen environment (~398 eV) collapsed while another (~401 eV) grew. Together this supports a picture of zinc plating selectively altering a specific, chemically distinct minority environment in CnC HS -- consistent with, though not proof of, a targeted zinc-heteroatom interaction rather than nonspecific surface change.

What remains open: the identity/assignment of the 290.1 eV feature (e.g., whether it is itself a nitrogen-related carbon signature) is not established here; no direct chemical bonding mechanism (e.g., Zn-N) is demonstrated; the correlation between the vanishing C feature and the shifting N features could be coincidental co-occurrence rather than a single shared cause; and no quantification of extent is given.

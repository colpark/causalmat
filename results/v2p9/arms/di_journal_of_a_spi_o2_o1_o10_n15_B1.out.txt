Panel F9 (viewed) shows XRD of the Al2O3-YSZ coating after 5 and 10 thermal cycles, with reference stick patterns for both t-ZrO2 and c-ZrO2 below. The main peaks at ~30, 35, 50, 60 deg are common to both phases, but the diamond-marked peak near 43 deg and the split doublet near 73-74 deg are called out as matching the t-ZrO2 reference sticks specifically — the same diagnostic features (small peak near 43, split peak in the 70s) that the earlier result used to distinguish tetragonal from cubic zirconia.

Read together with the established baseline (as-deposited Al2O3-YSZ is predominantly cubic, YSZ is predominantly tetragonal), this new measurement settles that thermal cycling changes the phase content of the Al2O3-YSZ coating: after cycling, the coating's pattern now carries the tetragonal-diagnostic peak shapes that were absent (or unmarked) in the as-deposited cubic pattern. Neither observation alone shows this — the earlier one is a static, pre-cycling snapshot, and this one by itself would just say "some t-ZrO2 features exist in a cycled sample" without a baseline to compare against. Combined, they support a genuine before/after conclusion: thermal cycling induces at least partial cubic-to-tetragonal transformation (or tetragonal precipitation) in the Al2O3-YSZ coating.

What remains open: no fraction/quantification of how much tetragonal phase forms versus how much cubic remains after 5 vs 10 cycles; no mechanism (thermal stress, dopant/Y redistribution, sintering) is given for why cycling drives this shift; and the panel only concerns the Al2O3-YSZ coating, so it says nothing about whether the YSZ-only coating's phase changes under the same cycling.

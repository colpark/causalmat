This measurement supports a compositional-reservoir link in the Co release pathway: the amount of Co built into the scaffold during synthesis (EDS Co/Ca = 0, 0.14, 0.49 for MBG, 2Co-MBG, 5Co-MBG) tracks, in the same rank order, the amount of Co2+ that subsequently appears in the surrounding medium (absent for 0Co; higher for 5Co than 2Co at every timepoint tested — days 1, 3, and 7). That is, more Co loaded into the solid phase goes with more Co released into solution, consistent with the scaffold acting as a store from which Co2+ leaches out over time rather than release being independent of how much Co was incorporated.

What it does not settle: it is only an ordinal match across three groups, not a quantitative dose-response curve, since EDS gives a single static normalized ratio while release is a rising absolute-concentration time series — the two are different kinds of quantities that can only be rank-compared, not equated. It says nothing about the rate or mechanism of release (e.g., surface dissolution vs. diffusion vs. degradation kinetics), since only three fixed compositions were compared and no dose-response or mechanistic pathway data were included. It also can't fully separate "synthesis loaded more Co, so more came out" from an independently verified release mechanism, because group identity (0/2/5Co) and Co content were set together by the synthesis design, so part of the ordering agreement is a built-in construction check rather than an independent mechanistic test. In short: supports composition-governs-release-amount in rank order; leaves open dose-response shape, release rate/kinetics, and the specific release mechanism.

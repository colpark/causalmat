Both panels are from the same figure (F6a and F6b), each plotting H2 absorption (wt%) vs time at 360°C for three consecutive hydrogenation cycles (1st, 2nd, 3rd) on two related samples — panel (a) evidently the base alloy and panel (b) the +10 wt% C@Co-doped alloy, matching the stated C0 vs C10 comparison.

Panel (a) shows a large first-cycle penalty: the 1st-cycle curve rises slowly and reaches only ~3.2 wt% by 180 min, while the 2nd and 3rd cycles rise steeply and plateau near 5 wt% within roughly 5-10 min — a ~66 min gap is marked between the 1st curve and where the later cycles have already saturated. Panel (b) shows the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd cycle curves nearly overlapping from the start, all rising steeply and reaching a plateau (~4.6-5.2 wt%) within about 5-15 min, with only an ~11 min marked interval (much smaller and used differently than in panel a).

What this justifies: the C@Co-doped sample eliminates (or drastically shortens) the slow "activation" behavior seen on the undoped alloy's first hydrogenation cycle — with doping, cycle 1 already behaves like cycles 2-3, whereas without doping cycle 1 lags far behind. This directly supports the claim that C10's first cycle saturates in ~5 min like later cycles while C0's first cycle stalls at ~3.2 wt% over 180 min.

What is left open: this is shown at a single temperature (360°C) and only three cycles per sample, so long-term cycling stability/degradation, behavior at lower service-relevant temperatures, and the underlying mechanism (catalytic vs. microstructural) are not addressed by these two panels alone.

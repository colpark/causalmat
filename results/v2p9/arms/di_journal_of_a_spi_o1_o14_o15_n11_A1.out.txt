Panel F7b (S2, sintered at 1700 C) shows one clear fracture-surface event: a cBN grain standing proud of the surrounding matrix, immediately adjacent to a deep (~2 um) socket whose shape complements a missing grain, while nearby Al2O3 grains present clean, flat, faceted fracture faces with no pullout cavities.

What this supports: the image is direct, unambiguous evidence of a grain-pullout/debonding failure mode localized to the cBN phase at this sintering condition — the protruding grain plus its matching socket is the classic pullout signature (a grain separated cleanly from its seat, leaving an empty cavity, rather than fracturing through the grain body). By contrast, the Al2O3 grains fracturing along flat facets indicates a different, more brittle intergranular/cleavage-type failure mode with no evidence of pullout at those sites. So the measurement justifies distinguishing behavior_class between phases at this micro-site: cBN = pullout/weak-interface behavior, Al2O3 = faceted/brittle fracture, for sample S2 at 1700 C.

What it leaves open: this is a single micrograph of one localized region, not a statistical survey, so it cannot establish how common pullout is across the whole cBN population or whether the flat Al2O3 facets are representative rather than an isolated patch. It does not identify the bonding chemistry or mechanism causing the weak cBN interface, does not confirm the socket and protruding grain are truly a matched pullout pair versus coincidental adjacent features, and gives no comparison to other sintering temperatures or samples (S1, other conditions), so any claim that this behavior_class assignment generalizes beyond S2/1700 C is unsupported by this panel alone.

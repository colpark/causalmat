The CV panel (F2c) shows three matched anodic/cathodic peak pairs (~0.55/0.52, 0.89/0.87, 1.08/1.07 V) with small peak separations that stay essentially superimposed across the 1st, 2nd, 5th, 10th, 15th, and 20th cycles — i.e., three distinct, reversible redox processes that survive repeated cycling without meaningful shift or fading.

Read alone, this only shows that the electrode undergoes multiple reversible redox reactions in this potential window; CV by itself cannot say what chemical framework is being oxidized/reduced, since electrochemistry gives no compositional information.

Combined with the settled FTIR/TGA result — that the material is a cyanide-bridged framework (M-CN, V=O vanadyl bonds) carrying ~25% loosely bound water and undergoing framework collapse only above ~350 C — the CV result lets you conclude that this same cyanide-bridged, vanadyl-containing framework is electrochemically active and structurally robust under ambient-temperature redox cycling: the three redox couples persist unchanged over 20 cycles, showing the framework identified by FTIR/TGA is not degraded or restructured by repeated electrochemical insertion/extraction, well below the ~350 C threshold where TGA showed it actually collapses.

What this combination does not settle: which specific redox couple (Fe, V, or a mixed Fe/V site) each of the three peak pairs corresponds to; whether the structural water FTIR/TGA identified participates directly in the redox mechanism (e.g., proton coupling) or is merely incidental; any quantitative capacity, coulombic efficiency, or capacity-retention value; and whether cycling beyond 20 cycles preserves this stability. Assigning peaks to specific oxidation states or bonding sites would require additional data (e.g., XPS, ex-situ XRD, or Mössbauer).

Panel F5c (neutral 0.5 M KHCO3 LSV, OER) confirms the stated reading: among the five curves (Co5Fe3Cr2, Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5, Co, IrO2), the black Co5Fe3Cr2 trace rises earliest and steepest, crossing the j = 10 mA cm-2 dashed line first at eta10 = 452 mV, while the IrO2 curve stays flattest/lowest and only reaches the 10 mA cm-2 mark later (higher overpotential), with Co, Co5Cr5, and Co5Fe5 falling in between.

What this justifies: under neutral electrolyte, Co5Fe3Cr2 has superior intrinsic OER kinetic activity (lower onset/operating overpotential to reach a fixed current density) relative to the other quaternary/ternary compositions in this series and relative to the IrO2 benchmark. That is a legitimate, direct measurement supporting a PRF/service_capability claim specifically framed as "OER activity in neutral-pH electrolyte" — i.e., this catalyst can deliver a given current density at lower energy penalty than IrO2 in this medium.

What it leaves open: this is a single LSV sweep with no stated iR-correction, no scan-rate or replicate/error-bar information, and no Tafel-slope, ECSA-normalized, or mass-activity comparison — so the ranking could partly reflect capacitive/surface-area differences rather than pure intrinsic activity. It also says nothing about durability, Faradaic efficiency, or long-term service capability (no chronopotentiometry/chronoamperometry shown), and the comparison is confined to neutral electrolyte only — it does not establish performance in acidic or alkaline media, nor stability under sustained operation.

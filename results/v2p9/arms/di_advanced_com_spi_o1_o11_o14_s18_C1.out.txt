This measurement supports the claim that adding the silane-grafted, Ag-loaded BST filler measurably alters the composite's service-relevant thermal behavior (glass transition, Tg) relative to the neat PAEN resin: Tg drops from 176 C (neat) to 146 C at 1% Ag, then rises to 149 C and 157 C at 3% and 5% Ag. Combined with FTIR evidence (2930, 2864, 1122 cm-1 bands unique to BST-SH) confirming a covalently grafted MPTMS layer (Si-O, CH2 groups) on the particle surface, the result justifies concluding that the filler is chemically bonded to the particle surface and that its incorporation changes the polymer's thermal transition rather than leaving it inert.

What it leaves open: the FTIR chemistry is documented only for BST-SH itself, not separately re-confirmed at each of the three DSC-tested Ag loadings, so one cannot verify that the same grafting is present (or present to the same extent) in the actual samples whose Tg was measured. Because MPTMS grafting is presumably held constant while only Ag content varies across the 1/3/5% series, the data cannot separate whether the Tg trend (initial drop, then step-up) is driven by the grafting chemistry, by Ag loading, or by some interaction between them. Four Tg values are too few points to establish a quantitative dose-response curve, only a qualitative pattern (drop then partial recovery). Finally, FTIR is a presence/absence indicator of grafted groups, not a quantified grafting density, so no correlation between degree of grafting and magnitude of the Tg shift can be drawn from this evidence alone.</message>

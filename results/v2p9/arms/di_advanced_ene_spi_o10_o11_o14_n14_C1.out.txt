This result does not settle anything about STR (structure), microstructure, or shape/morphology directly — the underlying observations are purely spectroscopic (N 1s NEXAFS peak presence/absence and relative peak-height ranking), not structural or morphological data. What it does support is a chemical/electronic-structure claim: the nitrogen-containing sample (CnC HS) carries a nitrogen species whose local electronic environment changes specifically when zinc is deposited — the ~398 eV feature collapses and the ~401 eV feature grows to dominate — while the nitrogen-free sample (C HS) shows no such features to begin with. This licenses the conclusion that zinc plating is coupled to a change in nitrogen's local chemical state in the CnC HS material, and that this coupling is absent (trivially, for lack of nitrogen) in C HS.

What it leaves open, especially with respect to structure/microstructure/shape: it does not establish any physical, spatial, crystallographic, or morphological change in the material — no lattice structure, particle shape, grain size, surface topology, or microstructural reorganization is measured or implied by peak-height/presence data alone. It also does not confirm the specific named chemical-state assignment (pyridinic- vs graphitic-N), does not identify which sample the zinc-plated spectrum came from except by inference, does not establish a direct Zn–N bond as the mechanism (as opposed to some other co-occurring electronic/surface change), and provides no quantification of nitrogen or zinc content/coverage. In short: a nitrogen electronic-state shift tied to zinc deposition is supported; any structural, microstructural, or morphological conclusion is unaddressed by this evidence and remains open.

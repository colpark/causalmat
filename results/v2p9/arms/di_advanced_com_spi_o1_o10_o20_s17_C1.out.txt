This measurement settles that silane grafting (MPTMS) demonstrably added new surface chemistry to the BST particles -- FTIR gives direct chemical evidence (Si-O band near 1122 cm-1, CH2 stretches at 2930/2864 cm-1, present only in the treated BST-SH sample) -- and that the composite film made with this modified filler (BST@Ag1%) shows, by SEM, a morphology consistent with tight, gap-free filler-matrix contact (blurred particle outlines, no visible interfacial voids). Together these support the qualitative inference that the surface treatment is associated with, and plausibly contributes to, improved interfacial bonding between filler and polymer matrix -- i.e., the chemistry story (grafting occurred) and the morphology story (interface looks good) are consistent with each other and with the general PRP/value narrative that silane modification improves filler-matrix compatibility.

What it does not settle: causation is not established, only correlation/plausibility. The FTIR and SEM were run on different samples (bare BST-SH powder vs. the Ag-containing composite film), so there is no direct, same-specimen link tying the specific chemical groups to the specific morphology observed. Both measurements are categorical/qualitative -- presence/absence of bands, visual blurring -- so there is no quantitative measure of bond density, interfacial strength, or adhesion energy, and no dose-response relationship between degree of silane functionalization and degree of interfacial improvement. Alternative explanations for the SEM morphology (wetting behavior, particle dispersion quality, or effects of the added Ag component itself) cannot be ruled out by SEM alone, and FTIR alone cannot confirm that the detected functional groups actually translate into physical interfacial bonding in the finished composite. So the result supports a plausible mechanistic story linking surface chemistry to interfacial quality, but leaves open whether the linkage is causal, how strong/quantifiable it is, and whether confounding factors (Ag, dispersion, wetting) are responsible instead.

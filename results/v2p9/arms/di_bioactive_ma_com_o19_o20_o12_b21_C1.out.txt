This measurement (EDS + XPS together) settles that erbium detected in the particles is present specifically as Er3+ — an oxidation-state-resolved species — associated with a Ti-O oxide framework, not merely an element inferred from an unconfirmed EDS peak. That is the conclusion it justifies: EDS gives elemental presence (Ti, O, dominant C, small Er/Ce peaks), and XPS gives the chemical identity for the same element via its binding energy (Er 4d at 168.8 eV, alongside Ti 2p and O1s peaks consistent with a titanium oxide). Combined, they support "Er present as Er3+ in a Ti-O oxide context" rather than just "a peak at Er's energy was observed."

What it leaves open, plainly: (1) Ce's oxidation state is undetermined — no Ce binding-energy data were reported in the XPS observation, so whether Ce is Ce3+, Ce4+, or a mixture is outside what this result covers. (2) Whether Er (or Ce) is actually incorporated into the TiO2 lattice versus merely surface-adsorbed is not established, since XPS is surface-sensitive while EDS probes a larger interaction volume — so both techniques detecting Er does not prove the signals originate from the same spatial region or phase. (3) The dominant carbon peak in EDS is unaddressed by XPS (no C1s data), so it remains unclear whether that carbon reflects real sample chemistry or contamination from the grid/support/handling. (4) Because the EDS Er and Ce peaks are described as small, the elemental-presence side of this combined argument is only weakly weighted, limiting overall confidence even in the settled Er3+ conclusion.

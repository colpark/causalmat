Panel F7b (Sample S2, 1700 C) shows, in the same micrograph, a protruding cBN grain sitting beside a deep (~2 um) empty socket — evidence of a grain that pulled out, leaving a poorly bonded cavity — while adjacent alpha-Al2O3 grains show clean, faceted fracture surfaces.

Read together with the earlier-established result (boxed cBN/SiAlON regions in this same sample show cleaved facets with no gaps, and bulk XRD confirms SiAlON phases present alongside cBN and alpha-Al2O3), the combination settles that interfacial bonding behavior in this sample is not uniform: within one specimen and one processing condition, cBN-matrix boundaries range from gap-free (co-located with inferred SiAlON, per the earlier result) to grain pull-out with a deep socket (indicating a locally weak or unbonded interface elsewhere in the same microstructure). So the pair supports classifying PRP/behavior_class as spatially heterogeneous rather than a single sample-wide bonding state — SiAlON-linked good bonding, if real, does not guarantee uniform bonding across all cBN grains in the piece.

What it leaves open: there is no compositional check at the socket/pull-out site in F7b, so it is unconfirmed whether that particular interface lacked SiAlON or had some other local defect (porosity, orientation, thermal stress) causing pull-out; the two images are not co-registered, so the "gap-free = SiAlON present" and "socket = SiAlON absent" contrast is inferred, not measured; only one sample/condition is shown, so no controlled comparison establishes SiAlON formation as the cause of the difference; and neither panel indicates how frequent gap-free versus pull-out interfaces are, so the overall bonding quality of the sample cannot be quantified from these two images alone.

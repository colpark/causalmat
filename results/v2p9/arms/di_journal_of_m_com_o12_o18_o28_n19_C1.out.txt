This combined XRD + HRTEM result supports the conclusion that in dehydrogenated C10, Mg and CeH2.73 (with CeO2 and YH2 also present, plus a Co marker) form as genuinely separate, adjacent crystalline phases, and that no RE-Mg intermetallic (CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17) forms -- a conclusion now corroborated both statistically (bulk-averaged XRD across the sample) and physically (a real, lattice-resolved grain pair in HRTEM). It additionally establishes, as new real-space information that XRD cannot provide, that the Mg grain is wrapped by carbon layers and sits directly beside the CeH2.73 grain -- a concrete morphological/microstructural detail about how these phases are spatially organized relative to one another and to the carbon component.

What it leaves open: representativeness. The HRTEM image is one field of view showing one grain pair, so it cannot by itself prove that this particular Mg/CeH2.73 adjacency and carbon-wrapping arrangement is typical throughout the sample, nor can it independently confirm (at that same local scale) that RE-Mg intermetallics are truly absent everywhere -- that absence claim still rests on the bulk XRD statistics, not on the local image. Conversely, XRD cannot confirm that the phases it detects are actually adjacent or carbon-wrapped; that spatial/morphological claim rests only on the single HRTEM observation. Finally, neither technique speaks to reversibility, cycling stability, or whether this same phase assemblage and morphology would reform or persist on rehydrogenation -- the result is confined to phase identity and local structure in this one dehydrogenated state.

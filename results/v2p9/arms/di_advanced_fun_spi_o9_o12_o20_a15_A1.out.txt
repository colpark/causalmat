Panel F5d confirms the reported readout exactly: the BNs-BSA+Lasers panel (labeled "BNs-BSA + Lasers," red, presumably the dual 660/808 nm condition) shows all five individual tumor curves flat near zero volume out to day ~20-22, with no divergence among animals. The BNs-BSA+808 nm panel (blue) shows tumors suppressed near zero through about day 14, after which four of the five curves turn upward and regrow to several hundred mm3 by day 20-22, while one curve stays flat.

What this settles: at the level of gross tumor-volume phenotype, the dual-wavelength/combination treatment produces uniform, durable ablation in this cohort (5/5), whereas the single 808 nm treatment produces only transient control that relapses in most animals (4/5). This licenses a comparative-efficacy claim — combination therapy outperforms 808 nm monotherapy in durability of tumor suppression — and supports the paper's framing that pairing 660 nm with 808 nm gives a more complete/synergistic ablation than 808 nm alone.

What it leaves open: this is a phenomenological volume readout, not a mechanistic one. It does not identify which pathway (photothermal ablation depth/heterogeneity, photodynamic ROS generation, immune involvement, or drug/nanoparticle biodistribution) accounts for the 808 nm relapses, nor why one 808 nm-treated mouse did not relapse. It also cannot distinguish incomplete initial kill from later regrowth of a resistant subpopulation. So the figure supports superior combination efficacy but does not by itself settle mechanism/pathway.

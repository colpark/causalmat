Panel F4b (UV–vis absorbance, 200–800 nm) supports the stated finding in its main thrust: the pure TiO2 curve has a sharp absorption edge that falls to near-zero absorbance by roughly 420–450 nm, whereas all three Er0.5CexTi-O co-doped curves (x = 0.1, 0.2, 0.3) show a markedly broader edge that stays elevated (absorbance ~0.1–0.3) out to 600–800 nm, with sharp secondary peaks near 520 nm and 650 nm layered on top (these are consistent with Er f-f transitions, not the band edge itself). So the qualitative claim — co-doping extends absorption well past 420 nm into the visible, versus a sharp ~410 nm TiO2 edge — is directly visible in the plot and well supported.

The narrower claim that "Ce0.2 absorbs most" is only weakly supported. In the deep-UV region (200–350 nm) the Ce0.2 (orange) curve does sit slightly above the others at the first peak, but across the 400–800 nm visible tail that matters for the headline claim, the three doped curves (0.1, 0.2, 0.3) are closely bunched and cross each other repeatedly rather than showing a clean, consistent ordering. So "most" is a marginal, qualitative read from one figure, not a robust separation.

What this leaves open: this is raw absorbance only, not a Tauc-plot band-gap extraction, so no quantitative band-gap values are established; no link to actual photocatalytic, antibacterial, or biological performance is shown; and no compositional/structural evidence (XRD, XPS) ties the spectral change to substitutional Ce/Er doping versus defects or particle-size effects.

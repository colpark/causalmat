This measurement settles that the two lines of evidence are mutually consistent: a fine (<0.2 um), dispersed bright-particle microstructure is present in grain interiors and at boundaries in both the without-EPT (F12) and with-EPT (F13) samples, and this coexists with a documented shift in tensile behavior between those same two states (yield ~350 to ~330 MPa, UTS ~420 to ~447 MPa, end strain ~0.095 to ~0.15). So what it supports is a co-occurrence claim: whatever microstructural population the SEM captures is present alongside, and does not preclude, the observed change in strength and ductility when EPT is applied. It is legitimate to say the fine particle dispersion is part of the microstructural context in which the EPT-associated property shift occurs.

What it leaves open is causation and mechanism. The SEM observation itself does not quantify particle size, number density, or spacing in either panel, so there is no evidence that the particle population actually changed with EPT — the "same" dispersion is reported in both conditions. Because only two processing states (EPT off vs on) were compared, this is an on/off pairing, not a dose-response series, so no graded relationship between degree of particle dispersion and magnitude of property shift can be established, even if a link existed. Additionally, the qualitative/spatial particle description and the precise numeric tensile values are not on a common quantitative scale, so the data cannot support a dose-response or correlational claim between "amount of dispersion" and "size of the tensile shift." In short: consistency/coexistence is supported; a causal or graded microstructure-property relationship is not established and remains an open question requiring quantified particle metrics across more than two conditions.

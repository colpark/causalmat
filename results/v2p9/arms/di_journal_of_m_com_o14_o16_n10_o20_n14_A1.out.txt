The measurement (second, diffusion-controlled absorption stage: 66 min for C0 vs 11 min for C10, both at 360°C) is read directly off Fig. 6a (C0 alloy, no C@Co additive) and Fig. 6b (Mg90Ce5Y5 + 10 wt.% C@Co). Panel a shows a clear separation between the fast initial stage and a slow diffusion-controlled tail that only saturates near ~75 min (66 min after the first inflection); panel b shows the same alloy with the catalytic additive reaching its plateau far sooner, with the diffusion-controlled window collapsing to 11 min.

What this supports: at a fixed temperature (360°C) the 10 wt.% C@Co addition produces roughly a 6-fold acceleration of the slow, diffusion-limited part of hydrogen absorption, and this behavior is reproducible across cycles (the 2nd/3rd curves in panel b overlap tightly, unlike panel a where the 1st cycle is markedly slower than 2nd/3rd — an activation effect in the base alloy that the catalyzed alloy does not show as strongly). So the value is well supported as a within-study, same-temperature, same-alloy-base comparison of kinetics.

What it leaves open: this is a single-temperature data point — no activation-energy or Arrhenius analysis is shown here, so nothing can be said about how the ratio behaves at other temperatures. Total absorbed capacity is not identical between the two panels (C10 plateaus slightly lower, ~4.7 wt%, than C0's ~5.1–5.2 wt%), so "faster" does not mean "more" — capacity and mechanism (whether Co accelerates surface dissociation vs. bulk diffusion) are not established by this figure alone.

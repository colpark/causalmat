The DSC panel (F5a) shows Tg dropping sharply from 176 C (pure PAEN) to 146–157 C for all BST@Ag composites, with a monotonic increase across the graded Ag series itself: 146 C (1%) → 149 C (3%) → 157 C (5%).

Read together with the earlier SEM+photograph result (which established that the coating/roughening seen at the particle level co-occurs with, and moves in the same direction as, the color darkening as Ag loading rises, but could only contrast two discrete states and could not show a graded relationship or isolate which variable was responsible), the new DSC data supply the missing graded, quantitative dimension. The Tg measurement shows that whatever physical change is producing the visual/particle-level modification also has a real, monotonic effect on bulk polymer chain mobility that scales with Ag content across three loadings (1/3/5%), not just a binary before/after difference. So the combination now supports: (1) the surface modification is not merely cosmetic (color) but has a genuine interfacial/mechanical consequence — it measurably restricts or alters PAEN chain mobility relative to pure polymer, and (2) that consequence itself is graded and tracks Ag loading in the same direction as the SEM/photo trend, reinforcing that a single underlying modification event (not two unrelated phenomena) governs both the visual and the thermal-mechanical behavior.

What remains open: the DSC step alone carries no particle-level structural evidence, so it cannot itself confirm coating/roughening — it depends on the earlier SEM result for that link. And critically, because Ag content, particle size, and coating thickness were established as confounded (all rising together by construction), the new Tg gradient still cannot determine which of these variables is mechanistically responsible for the Tg shift; it only confirms that some combination of them produces a real, ordered thermal effect. Whether the effect is driven by Ag ion/plasmon interaction, coating thickness, or particle size increase remains undetermined.

The photocatalytic degradation curves (Fig. 8) show a consistent dopant-dependent ranking of MO degradation (Er0.5Ce0.2 ~46% > Ce0.3 ~41% > Ce0.1 ~31% > TiO2 ~20% at 60 min, same order at 20 and 40 min), and this ordering runs opposite to the PL intensity ordering already established (TiO2 highest PL > Er0.5Ti-O ~half > Er0.5Ce0.2 lowest). Because lower PL intensity is conventionally read as lower electron-hole recombination, and higher recombination suppression is expected to raise photocatalytic activity, the two measurements together are self-consistent: the sample with the most suppressed PL (Er0.5Ce0.2) is also the most photocatalytically active, and TiO2 with the strongest PL is the least active. This combination supports the qualitative claim that Er/Ce co-doping is associated with both reduced radiative recombination (PL) and enhanced photocatalytic degradation activity, and that these two trends track each other consistently across the sample set - i.e., an inverse PL-activity correlation that is at least directionally coherent with a recombination-suppression story.

What it does not settle: the PL data are only relative peak intensities among three samples, not a rate or quantified recombination measure, and the degradation curves are a bulk activity output, not a direct measurement of carrier recombination or lifetime. So the combination cannot establish that reduced recombination is the causal mechanism behind higher degradation - it could equally reflect changes in surface area, adsorption of MO, bandgap/light absorption shifts, or radical-generation pathways introduced by Ce redox chemistry, none of which are measured here. Neither figure addresses charge-transfer resistance or photocurrent, so no direct electronic-transport mechanism is confirmed. Also, the correlation is inferred across only three overlapping samples per dataset (PL: TiO2, Er0.5Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.2; degradation: four dopant levels), so the mechanistic link is circumstantial/correlational, not proven causal, and the specific role of Ce oxidation state (3+/4+) in either PL quenching or degradation enhancement remains undetermined.

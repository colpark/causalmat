The CV panel (F2c) shows three well-defined, symmetric anodic/cathodic peak pairs (~0.55/0.52, 0.89/0.87, 1.08/1.07 V) whose positions and shapes are essentially unchanged from the 1st through the 20th cycle — i.e., the material undergoes reversible, cycle-stable multi-step redox chemistry with no evidence of peak splitting, new peak growth, or loss of one couple relative to another.

Combined with the earlier EDS/TGA result — that the imaged particle is a single homogeneous Na–V–Fe–C–N–O (cyanide-framework) compound with a distinct hydration-loss stage followed by framework decomposition — this CV data supports the conclusion that this single-phase compound itself hosts multiple, distinct, reversible redox-active metal centers (consistent with both Fe and V being present in the one phase), and that this multi-redox electrochemical behavior is intrinsic to that compound and structurally/electrochemically robust over repeated cycling. In other words, the redox response is not an artifact of a physical mixture of separately redox-active phases — it is the electrochemical signature of the single homogeneous compound already identified.

What this combination leaves open: (1) it does not assign which specific couple(s) belong to Fe vs. V (peak-to-element/oxidation-state assignment requires additional data, e.g., XPS or in-situ spectroscopy); (2) it does not give the compound's exact stoichiometry or hydration formula — that still requires quantified atomic ratios plus mass-balance calculation; (3) cycle-stable CV shape indicates electrochemical/compositional reversibility but not structural (crystallographic) retention — the framework could still undergo subtle lattice changes not visible in CV; (4) no capacity, rate, or long-term (>20 cycle) cycling stability is established.

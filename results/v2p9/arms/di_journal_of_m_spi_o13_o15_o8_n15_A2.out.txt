Panel F2b (strain-hardening rate dθ/dε vs true strain for Al0.9CoFeNi2 EHEA) shows exactly the reported pattern: a steep drop from ~85 GPa to ~14 GPa between 0 and ~0.009 strain (labeled stage I), a slow decline to ~5 GPa by ~0.058 strain (stage II, a broad low-slope plateau), and then a turn negative beyond ~0.06 strain (stage III). The inset true stress–strain curve corroborates this — stress rises steeply at first, then the curve visibly flattens/bends over the same strain range where hardening rate goes negative, consistent with the Considère instability criterion (dσ/dε falling below σ marks the onset of necking/plastic instability).

What this measurement settles: it establishes that deformation in this alloy proceeds through (at least) three distinct hardening regimes — an initial high-rate stage dominated by rapid work-hardening (consistent with dislocation multiplication/interaction), an extended low but sustained hardening plateau (stage II), and a final instability stage (III) marking the approach to necking/failure. This is solid, direct evidence for a multi-stage hardening response and for the strain range over which plastic stability is maintained versus lost.

What it leaves open: the figure is purely mechanical (rate vs strain) and does not by itself identify the microstructural mechanism sustaining the stage II plateau — e.g., whether it reflects continued dislocation slip, deformation-induced phase/interface effects, or twinning in the eutectic microstructure. That requires independent microstructural (TEM/XRD) evidence not present in this panel. It is also a single representative curve, so it does not establish run-to-run reproducibility or composition sensitivity from this image alone.

Figure 14 is a scatter plot of Average UTS vs Average YS for AZ61 alloys processed by different techniques (90° ECAP, squeeze casting, 120° ECAP routes, multidirectional forging, friction stir processing, rolling, heat treatment, small-strain impact multidirectional forging, extrusion+ECAP, forging+heat treatment) plus three red "this study" points at roughly (275,380), (295,355), and (335,448) MPa.

What the measurement supports: within this compiled literature comparison, the three "this study" data points sit clearly to the upper-right of every other processing route shown — both higher YS (~275-335 MPa vs a literature ceiling near ~240 MPa) and higher UTS (~355-448 MPa vs a literature ceiling near ~318 MPa). This is a legitimate, visually unambiguous strength-combination advantage over every comparator process plotted for AZ61 in this figure, i.e., the processing route in "this study" achieves a better YS-UTS combination than any of the nine listed literature routes.

What it leaves open: the figure alone doesn't establish why this processing route outperforms the others (mechanism), doesn't show ductility/elongation, energy cost, scalability, or reproducibility (error bars/scatter aren't shown, so these appear to be single average points, not distributions with a quantified confidence interval). It also can't confirm the literature values were harvested/plotted correctly or represent truly equivalent test conditions (specimen geometry, strain rate, aging state), since that depends on Table 4 (not shown here) and the original sources — the figure caption references "the data in Table 4" but that table wasn't provided. So the plot supports a comparative ranking claim but not causal or mechanistic conclusions, nor guarantees of comparability across studies.

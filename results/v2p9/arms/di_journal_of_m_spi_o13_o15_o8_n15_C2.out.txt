This step settles a qualitative progression for MEC/pathway, not a quantitative rate. Taken together, the earlier observations support the conclusion that dislocation activity nucleates as mottled/line contrast confined to one lamella near the first strain step, and with continued loading this same activity develops into a dense dislocation pile-up banked against the lamella boundary — while the paired neighbouring lamella stays contrast-free throughout. This justifies treating the mottled-contrast state and the pile-up state as sequential stages of a single accumulation pathway localized to one lamella, with the boundary acting as an obstacle that dislocations bank against, and the adjacent lamella remaining a passive, non-participating partner across the sequence.

What it leaves open: (1) the strain level for Observation B and any quantitative dislocation density are not given, so this is a qualitative ordering of two contrast states, not a measured accumulation rate; (2) the micrographs do not themselves confirm Observations A and B were taken at the same lamella pair/location — the continuity is inferred from the n8->n9 graph link rather than shown in one field of view, so the "same site, later strain" reading is an inference, not a direct observation; (3) the phase identity of the featureless lamella (specifically that it is B2 rather than some other dislocation-poor phase) is not established by the images alone, so the mechanistic interpretation (why one lamella activates and its neighbor doesn't) cannot be tied to a specific phase from this evidence. In short: a two-stage qualitative pathway (mottled/line contrast -> boundary pile-up) is supported; strain calibration, spatial identity across the two images, and phase assignment remain unresolved.

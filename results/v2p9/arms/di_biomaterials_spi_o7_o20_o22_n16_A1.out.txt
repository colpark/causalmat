Panel F11b (bar chart, days 1/3/7, three groups: Blank control, Scaffolds, Scaffolds+Ampicillin, relative survival % with error bars and asterisks) matches the stated numbers: at day 1 all three groups are statistically indistinguishable (~100, ~110, ~104%); at day 3 Scaffolds+Ampicillin drops to ~81% while Scaffolds alone is ~118% (asterisk marking Scaffolds+Ampicillin as significantly different, P<0.05); at day 7 Scaffolds+Ampicillin falls further to ~52% while Scaffolds alone is ~116% (again asterisked).

What this settles: the measurement supports a time-dependent cytotoxic/growth-inhibitory effect that is specific to the ampicillin-loaded scaffold, not the scaffold material itself — plain scaffolds are non-toxic (survival at or above blank control, ~110-118%) at every time point, while the drug-loaded scaffold's relative survival declines progressively and significantly by day 3 and day 7 only in the presence of loaded ampicillin. This is consistent with sustained/controlled drug release producing a measurable, growing biological (cytotoxic or antibacterial-adjacent, depending on what cell/organism type this survival assay used) effect over the one-week window, and it confirms the drug remains bioactive after loading into the scaffold.

What it leaves open: the panel and caption do not specify what is being assayed (cell line viability vs. bacterial survival) — "service_capability" in the sense of whether this reflects antibacterial efficacy (the drug doing its intended job) or unwanted cytotoxicity to host cells is not disambiguated by this figure alone; that requires the surrounding methods text. It also does not establish dose-response, mechanism, reversibility, or whether the day 7 decline reflects continued release (efficacy) or scaffold degradation kinetics. No comparison to free/unloaded ampicillin control is shown here, so it cannot isolate the loading/delivery system's specific contribution versus the drug's inherent activity.

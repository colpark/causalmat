This measurement (the XRD/TEM-SAED pairing on milled C10) supports the conclusion that cobalt remains a distinct, separate crystalline phase alongside the unchanged C0 alloy phases (CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17, Mg), with no new reflections or diffraction rings appearing that would signal a Co-alloy reaction product. The value of this particular result lies in cross-validation: because a bulk-averaged technique (XRD) and a local, spatially-resolved technique (SAED) independently agree on the same phase set, and because the weak, easily-dismissed ~44.5 deg XRD line is corroborated by an independent Co (111) SAED ring, the "no new phase formed" reading is more secure than either technique alone could establish — SAED rules out that the faint XRD line was noise or misindexing, and XRD rules out that the SAED finding was a one-off artifact of the small sampled area.

What it leaves open: neither technique probes chemical bonding, interdiffusion below the diffraction detection limit, or amorphous/interfacial reaction layers, so a non-crystalline or highly localized Co-substrate interaction could exist undetected. SAED samples only a small selected area not shown to be representative of the whole powder, while XRD could miss a minor or highly dispersed reaction product; agreement between the two increases confidence but does not constitute proof that the entire sample is free of any localized reaction — it only shows neither method found evidence of one. The corroborated weak Co line confirms phase identity but says nothing about the amount, distribution, or particle size of that Co phase. Finally, this evidence pertains only to the as-milled C10 state and says nothing about phase behavior after de/hydrogenation cycling.

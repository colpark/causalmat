This measurement settles a qualitative, same-direction association between the SEM comparison and the tensile curves, and nothing more quantitative than that. What it supports: the EPT-treated sample (F10d), which shows a markedly finer and more uniform feature size (~0.3-0.8 um) than the untreated samples (F10b,c, coarser features at the same 2 um scale), is the same condition whose tensile curve (F13 vs F12) differs in three ways - a lower yield knee (~350 to ~330 MPa), a higher maximum stress (~420 to ~447 MPa), and a longer strain to failure (~0.095 to ~0.15). So the two panels jointly justify saying that microstructural refinement and the mechanical property shift co-occur under EPT versus no-EPT.

What it leaves open is substantial. This is a single before/after pair, not a series, so no dose-response or quantitative feature-size-to-property relationship can be drawn - only paired co-occurrence. SEM feature size is a static geometric measurement on a micrograph while the tensile response is a bulk mechanical curve, and there is no evidence the specimens were sampled from the same location or volume, so the comparison is indirect. Notably, the finer feature size tracks with a LOWER yield strength alongside a HIGHER UTS and elongation, which contradicts a simple "finer microstructure strengthens yield" story - the SEM data alone cannot explain this split. Finally, EPT likely changes several things at once (recrystallization/grain refinement, dislocation density, possible texture), so the tensile shift may reflect any combination of these rather than feature size specifically; causal attribution to feature size alone is not justified.

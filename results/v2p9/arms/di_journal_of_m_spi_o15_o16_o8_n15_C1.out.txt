This measurement settles a correlational, not mechanistic, point about the pathway to dense dislocation walls. It supports the qualitative claim that dislocation arrangements grow progressively denser and more organized as imaged features move from diffuse mottled contrast to tangled lines to dense walls, and that a localized pile-up at a lamella boundary is consistent with — and fits within — that same general trend toward wall-type structures. In other words, the pile-up geometry observed in one field is compatible with the paper's broader narrative that FCC dislocation content builds up through pile-ups, intersections, and networks en route to dense walls and Taylor lattices.

What it leaves open is the actual pathway (MEC) by which this happens. Because the panels (F3a, F4a, F5a) come from separate TEM fields/specimens rather than the same region imaged over time, the "progression" is an inference from panel ordering, not a directly observed transformation of one feature into another — so no continuous causal chain from mottled contrast to tangled lines to walls has been demonstrated. The single pile-up observation is also a local, specific snapshot, while the broader progression is a general, distributed trend across the material; the two are not shown to be quantitatively or causally linked. There is no dislocation density measurement, only categorical/descriptive labels, so no rate or magnitude relationship is established between the pile-up and overall density increase. Finally, it cannot be ruled out that dense walls arise via some other route that does not pass through the specific pile-up geometry captured in Observation A.

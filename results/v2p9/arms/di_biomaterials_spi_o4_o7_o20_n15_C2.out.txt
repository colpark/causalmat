This settled result establishes that Co doping degrades the mesoporous character of the material in a way corroborated by two independent techniques: as Co content rises from MBG to 2Co to 5Co, both the small-angle XRD peak height (long-range periodic pore ordering) and the N2 uptake at P/P0~1 (total pore volume/surface area) decline together and in the same sample order. This convergence of two mechanistically distinct measurements — one probing structural periodicity, the other probing adsorption capacity — supports a genuine, reproducible trend: higher Co doping progressively degrades the mesoporous structure, reducing both its ordering and its porosity in tandem. Because the two measurements are independent yet track each other across all three samples, this is stronger evidence than either measurement alone that Co doping has a real, consistent structural effect rather than being an artifact of one technique.

What it leaves open: it does not establish proportionality or a quantitative dose-response relationship, since only three Co levels were tested and the two quantities (arbitrary XRD intensity vs. mmol/g adsorption) are not commensurable — an 8-fold drop in one and a 2-fold drop in the other cannot be compared in magnitude. It does not reveal the atomic-scale mechanism (e.g., Co2+ substituting for Ca2+ disrupting channel self-assembly during synthesis); that remains an inference beyond what diffraction and adsorption data alone can show. Finally, it does not resolve causal direction or dependency between the two declines — whether loss of long-range channel order is what drives the loss of total pore volume, or whether both are separate, parallel consequences of Co incorporation, is left undetermined by this comparison.

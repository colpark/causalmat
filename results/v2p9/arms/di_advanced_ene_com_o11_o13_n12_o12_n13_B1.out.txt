The F3b NEXAFS panel shows: pristine CnC HS has three carbon 1s features (284.9, 288.3, and a sharp 290.1 eV peak); after zinc deposition, 284.9 and 288.3 persist but the 290.1 eV feature is gone. This is a third, independent core-level spectroscopy result (carbon, not nitrogen) showing a selective, feature-specific loss coincident with zinc loading, while other carbon environments on the same atom are unaffected.

Read together with the earlier N 1s XAS / Raman convergence, the new fact adds an important qualitative feature the earlier pair could not supply on its own: selectivity. Neither the N 1s shift nor the Raman dampening alone tells you whether the zinc-associated change is localized to particular bonding environments or is a general, bulk perturbation of the whole lattice. Now, with C 1s NEXAFS also losing one specific sharp feature while its other two features (285, 288 eV) are essentially unchanged, we have two elements (C and N) each showing selective, feature-specific spectral changes rather than global spectral change, alongside a vibrational signature that also weakens. That triangulation across three independent techniques, each showing a localized rather than uniform effect, strengthens the case that zinc deposition is associated with a specific, localized chemical/electronic environment (consistent with, though not proof of, coordination at a specific site such as pyridinic nitrogen and its adjacent carbon), rather than a diffuse, sample-wide disorder or doping effect.

What it still leaves open, unchanged from before: this remains a two-state (pristine vs. zinc-loaded) comparison, not a series, so no mechanistic pathway or bond-formation kinetics is established; the identity/assignment of the disappearing 290.1 eV carbon feature and the 1200 cm-1 Raman mode are not given by these panels, so the specific bonds involved cannot be pinned down; and direct Zn-N/Zn-C bond formation still cannot be distinguished from an indirect zinc-induced electronic/structural perturbation that happens to selectively suppress these particular C and N spectral features without true bond formation there.

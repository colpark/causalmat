The PCT curves show, at each of 320/340/360 °C, a single flat plateau (not split or multi-step) for C10, and those plateau pressures (~0.15, 0.27, 0.45 MPa) essentially coincide with C0's. A single unchanged plateau means the equilibrium hydride-forming reaction (Mg ↔ MgH2) is thermodynamically the same with or without the Co addition — there is no second plateau at a different pressure that would signal a distinct Co-containing hydride or Co-Mg compound participating in the absorption/desorption reaction.

Combined with the earlier XRD/SAED result (Co present only as its own separate crystalline phase in the as-milled powder, no new reflections/rings for a reaction product), this settles something neither shows alone: that the "Co stays a distinct, non-reacting phase" conclusion holds not just structurally before hydrogenation, but functionally/thermodynamically through actual hydrogen cycling. XRD/SAED addressed only the milled state; PCT is the first evidence from de/hydrogenation itself, and it shows no new equilibrium phase emerges during cycling either — closing the gap the earlier result explicitly left open ("says nothing about behavior after de/hydrogenation cycling").

What remains unsettled: PCT is a bulk, pressure-based thermodynamic measurement — it cannot detect a Co-containing phase that is thermodynamically inert (does not shift equilibrium pressure) but still affects kinetics, nor can it localize or quantify Co distribution, nor rule out amorphous/interfacial reaction products, matching the same blind spots flagged for XRD/SAED. It also says nothing about why Co was added (kinetic catalysis is presumably the intended role) since PCT alone cannot separate thermodynamic identity from kinetic effects, which are visible only in absorption/desorption rate data, not shown in these panels.

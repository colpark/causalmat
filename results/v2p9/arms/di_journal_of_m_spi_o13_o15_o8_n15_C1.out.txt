This measurement settles that dislocation activity in the two-phase (lamellar) microstructure follows a qualitative progression under increasing strain: an early stage where one lamella develops mottled contrast plus line contrast along the interphase boundary while its neighbour remains entirely featureless (Observation A), followed at a later loading step by a dense dislocation pile-up banked against that same boundary (Observation B), with the neighbouring lamella still showing no contrast. Taken together, the two observations support the interpretation that slip is initiated and then progressively accumulated within one lamella and is arrested at the lamella boundary, consistent with that boundary acting as a barrier to slip transmission, while the other lamella remains essentially free of dislocation activity throughout the strain range covered.

What this does not settle: the strain magnitude at which the pile-up (Observation B) was imaged is not given, so no quantitative rate or threshold for dislocation accumulation can be extracted from these panels alone — the sequence is qualitative, not measured. It is also not directly demonstrated that Observations A and B were captured on the same lamella pair or physical location; that continuity is inferred from the graph linkage (n8->n9) rather than shown in a single field of view, so the "progression" is a reasonable but not micrograph-confirmed narrative. Finally, the phase identity of the featureless lamella (specifically, whether it is B2 as opposed to some other dislocation-poor phase or region) is not established by the images themselves, so any phase-specific mechanistic claim (e.g., that B2 suppresses slip) remains an inference beyond what the panels directly show.

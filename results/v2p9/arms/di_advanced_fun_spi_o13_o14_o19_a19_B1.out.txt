Panel F5c: under 808 nm/7 W cm-2 irradiation, tumors in mice injected with BNs-BSA reach ~49 C by ~5 min, versus ~41 C for PBS-injected controls — an ~8 C differential that plateaus/continues rising through the recording window.

What the combination settles: The earlier result established, only in vitro/in suspension, that 808 nm absorption tracks concentration and laser power and co-varies with heating (qualitative, not quantitative, absorption-mediated photothermal behavior). F5c extends this into the actual therapeutic context — a live tumor in vivo, under a clinically relevant laser condition — and shows the same qualitative pattern: material present (BNs-BSA) drives substantially greater local heating than the no-material control (PBS), reaching a temperature regime (~49 C) associated with ablative/hyperthermic tumor damage, while PBS-only tissue stays comparatively cool. Read together, these two observations support that the absorption-driven photothermal mechanism characterized in vitro is not an artifact of the cuvette/dispersion setting — it operates, and produces therapeutically meaningful heating, when the material is delivered into a real tumor and irradiated. This is something neither panel alone establishes: the in vitro data never touch a tumor or in vivo delivery, and F5c alone (without the prior absorption/concentration/power dependence) would leave open whether the PBS-vs-BNs-BSA heating gap is really due to 808 nm absorption by the material specifically, rather than some other injection-site or handling difference.

What remains open: no concentration or laser-power series is shown in vivo, so dose-dependence in the tumor is assumed, not demonstrated; tumor accumulation/biodistribution of BNs-BSA is not shown in this panel; whether ~49 C over 5-6 min is sufficient for tumor ablation (versus mild/reversible hyperthermia) is not established by temperature alone; and no therapeutic endpoint (regression, survival, histology) is presented here to confirm efficacy.

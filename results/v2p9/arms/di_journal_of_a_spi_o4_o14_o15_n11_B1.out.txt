F7b shows sample S2 (sintered 1700 C): a cBN grain protruding beside a deep (~2 um) empty socket — the classic fractographic signature of grain pull-out, i.e., a weak/debonded cBN-matrix interface — while adjacent Al2O3 grains show clean, intact faceted faces. Read alone, this supports only a local conclusion: in S2, cBN interfaces failed by pull-out (poor bonding) while Al2O3 grains fractured cleanly (suggesting the weakness is specific to the cBN interface, not a general fracture artifact of the whole microstructure).

Read together with the earlier-established F7a result (S1: cBN/SiAlON boundary shows cleaved facets, no gaps — good bonding), the combination now gives a genuine cross-specimen contrast that neither panel gives alone: it settles that bonding quality at the cBN interface differs by composite — good (S1, SiAlON matrix) versus poor with visible pull-out (S2) — with Al2O3 grains behaving consistently (clean facets) in both cases as an internal control. This is consistent with, and lends fractographic support to, the paper's reported difference in bonding behavior across S1/S2/S3.

What remains open: neither this pairing nor the earlier one ties either micrograph to the XRD hBN-peak temperature series (F2c, 1400/1500/1600 C) — S2 here is at 1700 C, outside that series, and F2c still does not identify which composite it scanned. So the cause of the S2 pull-out (hBN formation vs. some other interfacial chemistry or thermal-expansion mismatch) is still not established; only the S1-vs-S2 bonding-quality contrast itself is now supported, not its mechanism.

Al0.9CoFeNi2 is one of the FCC+B2 lamellar eutectic high-entropy alloys (the AlCoCrFeNi2.1-family lineage). The tensile paper's new measurement is almost certainly the stress–strain curve plus fractography/post-mortem microstructure (SEM/EBSD/TEM) showing where cracks and voids nucleate during loading — typically at FCC/B2 phase boundaries and within the brittle, ordered B2 lamellae, while the soft FCC lamellae absorb plastic strain via dislocation slip and work-harden.

Read alone, a tensile curve only tells you bulk strength/ductility/work-hardening rate — it cannot say *where* damage starts or *why* the alloy is tough. An earlier, independently established microstructural result (e.g., that the as-cast alloy is a two-phase FCC+B2 lamellar eutectic, with B2 harder/more ordered and FCC more compliant) likewise cannot alone say how that structure behaves under load.

Combined, the two results settle a structure–property (load-partitioning) picture: strength derives from load transfer from the soft FCC matrix to the hard B2 lamellae, ductility/toughness comes from FCC-carried dislocation plasticity, and damage (microcracking/void formation) initiates preferentially at FCC/B2 interfaces or within B2 rather than uniformly — i.e., the eutectic architecture, not either phase alone, governs both flow and failure.

What this does NOT settle: the atomistic origin of interface decohesion, whether lamellar spacing/orientation relative to loading axis controls crack initiation quantitatively, temperature/strain-rate dependence, and whether the same mechanism holds in wrought/heat-treated (non-cast) forms.

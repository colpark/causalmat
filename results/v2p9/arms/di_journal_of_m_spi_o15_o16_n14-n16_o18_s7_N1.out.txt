Best-guess reading (I was not given the actual DSC trace, figure, or the specific "established earlier result," so this is inference from the paper's likely content, not a report of data I've seen):

The Se-doping paper almost certainly reports DSC on the Se-doped Mg3Bi2 samples to check for phase transitions/melting and decomposition behavior, comparing the doped material's thermal signature to that of pristine (undoped) Mg3Bi2 or Mg3Bi2-Sb-alloyed material established in earlier literature. Neither measurement alone settles much: the earlier result (host-phase transition/melting temperature of undoped Mg3Bi2) only tells you the baseline thermal stability of the parent compound, and the new DSC on Se-doped samples alone only shows that some thermal event occurs at a similar temperature — it can't by itself prove the material is still single-phase Mg3Bi2 rather than a doped compound with an altered or decomposed structure.

Read together, the two results support the conclusion that Se substitution does not measurably shift the phase-transition/decomposition temperature relative to the parent Mg3Bi2, i.e., Se enters the lattice as a genuine dopant without destabilizing the host phase or producing detectable secondary-phase transitions. Combined with the paper's transport data, this licenses the claim that the observed high room-temperature thermoelectric performance (zT) arises from carrier-concentration/band-structure tuning by Se rather than from a different, more thermally fragile compound.

What it leaves open: the DSC comparison cannot establish long-term operational/thermal-cycling stability, cannot rule out minor secondary phases below DSC's sensitivity, and cannot itself explain the microscopic electronic mechanism (band convergence, mobility change) behind the zT enhancement — that requires the separate transport/Hall/band-structure data.

Panels confirm the description: (a) Al-Cu-Mn shows a continuous, connected interdendritic network; (b) Al-Cu-Mn-Sm shows small, disconnected isolated particles; (c) Al-Cu-Mn-La shows a broken, string-like interdendritic network in which two arrows label two distinct microconstituents within that same network — A (Al2Cu) and B (Al6Cu6La).

Read together, the two results settle that the La-specific compound established earlier by XRD/DSC convergence is not merely a bulk chemical/thermal signature but is spatially resolvable: it sits inside the interdendritic network itself, co-located with (and distinguishable from) the Al2Cu phase, and its presence tracks with a morphological change in that network — from Sm's isolated, disconnected particles to La's broken, string-like but still partly connected network, versus the fully continuous network of the ternary base alloy. So the combination supports that adding La not only introduces a new compound (already shown) but also alters where/how the interdendritic phase distributes and connects, i.e., links the chemical difference to a specific microstructural consequence (network connectivity/morphology), something neither the XRD/DSC pairing nor the micrographs alone could show — the diffraction/thermal data had no spatial information, and the micrographs alone couldn't assign phase identity to the labeled regions.

This leaves open: the arrow labels A/B in panel (c) are asserted identifications, not derived independently from this image — they rely on the earlier XRD/DSC-based phase assignment, so the pairing doesn't independently verify identity. It doesn't quantify what fraction of the network is Al6Cu6La versus Al2Cu, nor establish that the new phase causally causes the string-breaking versus merely co-occurring with it. It also doesn't clarify whether Sm's isolated-particle morphology reflects true absence of the compound or an undetected trace, consistent with the earlier detection-limit caveat.

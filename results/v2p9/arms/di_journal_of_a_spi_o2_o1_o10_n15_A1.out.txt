Figure 9 (Al2O3-YSZ coatings after 5 and 10 thermal cycles) shows overlaid XRD traces for the two cycled conditions, with reference stick patterns for t-ZrO2 and c-ZrO2 below. Diamond markers flag two diagnostic regions — a peak near 43 deg and a split/doublet peak near 73-74 deg — and the insets show these zoomed features line up in position with the t-ZrO2 stick pattern.

What this supports: qualitative phase identification — the coating (topcoat zirconia) contains a tetragonal ZrO2 component, present after both 5 and 10 thermal cycles, since the marked features appear in both the red (5-cycle) and black (10-cycle) traces. The split doublet near 73-74 deg is a classic t/c-discriminating reflection, and its match to the t-ZrO2 sticks (rather than a single unsplit c-ZrO2 stick) is the basis for calling it tetragonal rather than cubic. The insets also show the 5-cycle (red) trace with visibly taller peaks than the 10-cycle (black) trace at both marked positions, i.e., a qualitative, by-eye difference in relative intensity between the two cycle counts.

What it leaves open: no quantitative phase fraction is reported — there is no Rietveld refinement, monoclinic-phase stick pattern, or peak-area/intensity-ratio calculation, so the data cannot support a numeric t/c/m percentage or a quantified transformation degree between 5 and 10 cycles. It also cannot establish whether any monoclinic ZrO2 formed (no m-ZrO2 reference shown), nor pin down absolute phase stability trends beyond "the tetragonal marker peaks are visible at both cycle counts, apparently weaker at 10 cycles by eye." Any claim of monotonic tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation with cycling, or an exact fraction, goes beyond what this panel shows.

The new measurement (F10c/d) is a second, independent functional readout — antibacterial killing ratio under light — showing the same ordering as the previously established dye-degradation ranking: Er0.5Ce0.2 (~91-93%) > Er0.5Ce0.3 (~87%) > Er0.5Ce0.1 (~68-73%) > TiO2 (~45%) > control (~4-7%), consistent in both panels (c and d, presumably E. coli vs another organism, run in triplicate). Critically, within each panel the light condition outperforms the matched dark condition for every sample, and this light-driven boost is largest for the co-doped, redder-absorbing samples.

Combined with the earlier absorption/degradation result, this justifies broadening the claim: enhanced visible-light absorption in co-doped TiO2 is associated with improved performance across at least two distinct service-capability functions (dye degradation and antibacterial action), not just one assay-specific outcome. It also partially resolves one earlier qualification — Er0.5Ce0.2 is now the top performer in both the degradation assay and this antibacterial assay, so the "best composition" result is reproducible across endpoints rather than an artifact of one measurement. The light-vs-dark contrast within each sample also lends indirect support to a photocatalytic (light-activated) mechanism, since killing scales with illumination in parallel with the absorption story.

What remains open: still only 4-5 compositions are compared, so this is ordering, not dose-response; the sample with strongest raw absorption (plain Ce0.2, not Er-co-doped) is still not directly tested here, so the absorption-strength-vs-composition mismatch from before is unresolved; and no measurement here directly captures charge separation, carrier lifetime, or reactive-oxygen-species generation — the mechanistic bridge between absorption and PRF/service_capability is still inferred, not measured.

What the three micrographs support: In the base Al-Cu-Mn alloy (F1a), the bright interdendritic (eutectic) phase visibly forms a continuous, interconnected network outlining the dendrite cells across the whole field of view. With Sm addition (F1b), the bright phase instead appears as small, disconnected, isolated particles scattered between cells — no continuous skeleton is visible. With La addition (F1c), the bright phase appears as thin, broken string-like segments — more fragmented than (a) but still showing more elongated connectivity than the isolated dots in (b); arrows identify two distinct bright constituents (A: Al2Cu, B: Al6Cu6La) within this panel only.

This single set of images justifies the qualitative claim that Sm and La additions change the morphology/continuity of the interdendritic second phase relative to the unmodified alloy — shifting it from a continuous network toward discontinuous (isolated-particle or broken-string) morphology, i.e., a modification/refinement effect on the eutectic constituent, with La and Sm producing visibly different discontinuous morphologies from each other.

What it leaves open: this is one field of view per composition, so there is no statistical measure of area fraction, particle size, or a connectivity index, and no evidence these fields are representative. The images are 2D sections; an apparently continuous network in (a) or isolated dots in (b) could reflect sectioning geometry rather than true 3D topology. Phase identity is only explicitly confirmed (by arrows) in panel (c) (Al2Cu, Al6Cu6La); the bright phase composition in (a) and (b) is not independently labeled in these crops, so whether it is the same phase being modified, versus a different phase forming, is not settled by this image alone. No mechanism for how Sm/La alters morphology is shown here.

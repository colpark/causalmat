Best-guess reconstruction (no source data was provided, so this is inferential):

This 2016-era JMST paper (Wani, Bhattacharjee et al.) reports tensile stress-strain curves, yield/ultimate strength, elongation, and post-mortem TEM/EBSD on the lamellar FCC+B2 eutectic Al0.9CoFeNi2 HEA, showing good strength (~700+ MPa) combined with substantial ductility (~15-20%), high work-hardening rate, and deformation twinning within the soft FCC lamellae alongside dislocation activity constrained by the hard, ordered B2 lamellae.

The "established earlier result" this is meant to be read against is almost certainly the earlier compression-only characterization of the same or closely related Al-Co-Cr(Fe)Ni eutectic HEA family, which had already shown a strong strength-ductility combination and twinning-assisted hardening but only under compressive loading. Compression alone cannot rule out that the twinning/load-partitioning mechanism (MEC) is an artifact of the constrained, compressive stress state (e.g., suppressed crack nucleation, different resolved shear stresses on twin systems).

Read together, the new tensile measurement plus the earlier compression result jointly establish that the twinning-mediated, phase-partitioned deformation pathway is loading-mode independent — i.e., it is an intrinsic property of the lamellar eutectic microstructure (soft FCC carrying slip+twinning, hard B2 carrying elastic/dislocation load) rather than a compression-specific phenomenon. That is what neither test settles alone.

What remains open: the quantitative split of load/strain between phases, whether the twinning pathway persists at other temperatures, strain rates, or specimen sizes, and whether the same mechanism holds for compositional variants (different Al content) of this eutectic HEA family.

F3d (Zn-plated CnC HS): a largely flat, uniform deposit with only sparse protruding platelets. F3e (Zn-plated C HS): a dense mat of sharp, closely packed platelets covering the whole field of view.

Read together with the settled NEXAFS result, this supports a correlation between nitrogen chemistry and Zn deposition morphology: CnC HS, the material whose nitrogen sites (398/401 eV features) are shown to respond electronically to zinc plating, ends up with a flat, low-relief Zn deposit; C HS, which the earlier result establishes has no nitrogen features at all, ends up with a rough, sharp-platelet (dendrite-like) morphology instead. The combination therefore lets you say something neither observation says alone: that the presence of a nitrogen-containing surface whose local chemical state shifts upon Zn deposition co-occurs with suppression of the platelet/dendritic growth mode, while the absence of nitrogen co-occurs with unsuppressed platelet growth. This is the structural/morphological counterpart to the spectroscopic story — it gives a plausible mechanistic role (nitrogen sites promoting more uniform, non-dendritic Zn nucleation and growth) to the chemical change already documented.

What it leaves open: this is still only a correlation across two different host materials that differ in more than nitrogen content (porosity, surface area, curvature, defect density could also differ and drive morphology). Nothing here spatially links Zn nucleation to the specific nitrogen sites seen by NEXAFS — the SEM shows bulk texture, not site-resolved deposition. No causal or mechanistic bridge is shown between the 398→401 eV electronic shift and the flat-vs-platelet outcome; the two could be independent consequences of Zn plating rather than cause and effect. No platelet density, size, or coverage is quantified, so "sparse" vs "dense mat" remains a qualitative description.

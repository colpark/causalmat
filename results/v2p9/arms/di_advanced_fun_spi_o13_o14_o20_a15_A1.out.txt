Panel F5d confirms the stated measurement exactly: in the BNs-BSA+808 nm panel, individual curves start near zero, stay flat/suppressed through roughly day 12-14, then four of the five lines turn upward and climb (one reaching ~700 mm3, others ~250-450 mm3 by day ~20), while one curve stays near zero. In the BNs-BSA+Lasers (combined 660/808) panel, all curves remain flat at ~0 through day 20, with no regrowth in any animal.

What this justifies: the combined dual-wavelength (660+808 nm) laser treatment with BNs-BSA produces durable, complete tumor suppression in all five mice through the observation window, whereas 808 nm-alone photothermal treatment with the same nanomaterial produces only transient/partial control — most tumors escape and resume growth after ~2 weeks. This supports a mechanistic conclusion that the 660 nm component (photodynamic/photocatalytic, per the paper's framing) contributes something non-redundant with 808 nm photothermal killing — i.e., a genuine synergistic or combinatorial effect between the two wavelengths/pathways, since photothermal alone is insufficient for durable eradication.

What it leaves open: this panel alone does not identify the mechanism by which 660 nm contributes (e.g., ROS/singlet-oxygen generation, distinct target engagement, deeper penetration, or simply additive heating) — that requires the mechanistic assays elsewhere in the paper. It also doesn't establish dose equivalence (whether 660 nm alone, or higher-power 808 nm alone, could match the combination), doesn't give statistical comparison across groups (only n=5 individual traces), and doesn't address durability beyond ~day 20-22 or recurrence/metastasis. So the panel settles that combination > 808-nm-alone in efficacy and durability, but not the specific causal pathway responsible for the added benefit.

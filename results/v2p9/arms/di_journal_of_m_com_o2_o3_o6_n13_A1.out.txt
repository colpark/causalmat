The histogram (F3) confirms the stated trend: YS bars climb monotonically with SiCp content, roughly 93 -> 113 -> 124 -> 127 -> 136 -> 142 MPa at 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 wt%, matching the reported values. This supports the conclusion that adding SiC particulate reinforcement to this Mg-alloy matrix produces a real, consistent strengthening effect on yield strength, with the composite at 25 wt% SiCp reaching roughly 1.5x the unreinforced matrix's YS. The error bars (visible on each bar) are modest relative to the step sizes between 0-5, 5-10, and 15-20-25 wt%, so the overall rising trend looks robust, though some adjacent pairs (e.g., 10 vs 15 wt%, 20 vs 25 wt%) have bars close enough that error-bar overlap makes the individual step-to-step increment less certain even if the overall 0-to-25 rise is clear.

What this single figure leaves open: it is only a bar chart of averages with error bars, so it does not by itself establish statistical significance, sample size, or reproducibility (n per bar, standard deviation vs standard error, and any statistical test are not shown here). It also says nothing about mechanism (Hall-Petch grain refinement vs load transfer vs Orowan strengthening), nor about how YS trades off against ductility, fracture toughness, or processing conditions, and it does not tell us whether the trend continues, plateaus, or reverses beyond 25 wt% SiCp. The companion UTS bars in the same panel show a mild opposite/flat trend, which raises but does not resolve a question about a YS/UTS or strength/toughness trade-off with increasing particulate content.

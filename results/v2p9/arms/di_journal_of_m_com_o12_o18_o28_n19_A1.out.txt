Both panels (F11a = Mg90Ce5Y5 "C0"; F11b = Mg90Ce5Y5+10 wt% CQCo "C10") show PCT isotherms at 320/340/360 C, each with a single flat plateau, and the plateau heights line up closely between panels: roughly 0.15 MPa at 320 C, ~0.27-0.3 MPa at 340 C, and ~0.4-0.45 MPa at 360 C in both alloys. This measurement settles the thermodynamics side of the PRP/value question: the equilibrium (van't Hoff) plateau pressure of the hydride is essentially unchanged by adding 10 wt% CQCo. Since plateau pressure at a given temperature reflects the enthalpy/entropy of the hydriding reaction, this supports the conclusion that the catalytic additive does not measurably alter the thermodynamic stability of the magnesium hydride phase — it is not acting by shifting the reaction equilibrium. Any benefit of CQCo addition (if there is one) would have to come from kinetics (reaction rate, activation energy, nucleation) rather than from changing Pressure-composition-temperature equilibrium values.

What this leaves open: the plots show H2 content vs. pressure at fixed temperature with no time axis, so nothing here speaks to absorption/desorption rate, activation energy, or cycling behavior — the properties CQCo is presumably added to improve. It also does not establish hysteresis (absorption vs. desorption plateau separation), maximum storage capacity differences (the two panels' x-axis maxima differ slightly, ~5.2 vs ~4.7 wt%, which is not addressed by plateau-pressure equality), or whether the two flat single-plateau shapes indicate identical phase composition. In short: thermodynamic plateau pressure (and by extension the derived enthalpy/entropy, i.e., "value" of the reaction) is essentially the same for C0 and C10; kinetic/practical performance differences remain undetermined from these panels alone.

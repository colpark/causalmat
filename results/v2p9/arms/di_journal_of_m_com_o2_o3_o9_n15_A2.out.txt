Both fractographs (F5a: AZ91/10 SiCp, F5b: AZ91/20 SiCp, both at ×150, 100 µm scale) show rough, irregular fracture surfaces dominated by a fine network of bright, curving tear ridges interspersed with flatter, facet-like patches. Neither panel shows the smooth cleavage-like flat facets with river patterns that would indicate purely brittle transgranular fracture, nor does either show the deep, well-rounded equiaxed dimples that would indicate purely ductile microvoid coalescence. The bright ridge network plus intervening flatter regions is consistent with a mixed-mode fracture appearance (tearing/ligament rupture combined with some brittle facet cracking, likely at/near SiC particles or particle-matrix interfaces).

What this supports: at both 10% and 20% SiCp loading, the fracture surface morphology is qualitatively similar (rough, tear-ridge-dominated with facets), so the measurement supports classifying the behavior_class as "mixed" rather than cleanly "ductile" or "brittle," and supports that reinforcement content (10 vs 20 vol% SiC) does not obviously change the qualitative fracture mode class between these two composites — same general appearance at this magnification.

What it leaves open: this is a single low-magnification (×150) view per composite, so finer features that discriminate ductile vs brittle mechanisms (dimple depth/size, cleavage facet crystallography, secondary cracking at particles, presence of voids initiating at SiC/matrix interfaces) cannot be resolved or confirmed from these crops alone. It also does not establish quantitative PRP values (e.g., elongation, toughness) or causally link the fracture appearance to the SiC content versus other processing variables — only that the two panels look visually similar under PRP/behavior_class. Higher-magnification insets or EDS at ridge/facet boundaries would be needed to firm up the mixed-mode call and rule out a coincidental low-mag resemblance masking real compositional differences.

Panel F2d shows a high-magnification SEM view of the room-temperature tensile fracture surface of the Al0.9CoFeNi2 EHEA, dominated by a fine lamellar (feather-like/serrated) morphology consistent with eutectic-type two-phase lamellae. Overlaid on this image are red arrows pointing to small discrete holes/dimples situated at the tips or ends of the lamellae, and red boxes outlining short microcracks located at lamella ends and along lamella edges/interfaces. A blue circle marks a further feature of interest (likely a secondary phase particle or void), and the feathery red-arrow clusters at left/right likely denote fine dimpled or tearing ridges within one of the phases.

What this justifies: the image directly supports that fracture-associated damage (microvoids and microcracks) nucleates preferentially at lamella tips/ends and along lamella boundaries, i.e., that the lamellar microstructure's interfaces and terminations are the local sites where cracking/void formation initiates or is concentrated during fracture. This is consistent with a microstructure-controlled damage/fracture pathway in which the two-phase lamellar architecture provides preferred crack/void nucleation sites.

What it leaves open: this is a single static fractographic snapshot, so it cannot establish the sequence (whether holes nucleated before or after the microcracks, or which formed first during loading), cannot quantify how much this local damage contributed to overall fracture strain or toughness, and cannot generalize beyond this one EHEA composition/condition to other alloys or test conditions. It also does not identify the underlying cause of the holes/microcracks (e.g., second-phase decohesion, dislocation pile-up, or elemental segregation) — that requires complementary characterization (e.g., EDS, TEM) not shown here.

Figure 13 (true stress–strain curves for AZ61 under three EPT parameter combinations) shows the blue curve (423K-8+373K-3+25µs-10min, a two-stage/combined electropulsing treatment) reaching the highest peak true stress, ~447 MPa, versus ~370 MPa for the single 373K-4+30µs-10min (red) treatment and ~356 MPa for the single 423K-4+50µs-5min (black) treatment. This directly supports the claim that this combined EPT recipe is the best-performing of the three tested for peak strength (PRP), a roughly 20% strength gain over the next-best single-stage treatment.

What it leaves open: the strength gain comes with a ductility cost — the blue curve fractures earliest, at true strain ~0.15, versus ~0.17 (red) and ~0.19–0.20 (black), so the black condition is actually the most ductile despite being weakest. The figure alone does not establish that the combined treatment is superior in overall "value" — that depends on whether strength or ductility (or some toughness/product-of-both metric) is the design priority, and this panel shows a trade-off rather than a Pareto win. Additionally, only three parameter combinations are compared (no untreated/as-received baseline curve is shown here, no replicate curves or error bars, and no mechanistic explanation for why the combined two-stage treatment outperforms either single stage). So the measurement settles relative peak-strength ranking among these three specific EPT recipes but leaves open generalizability, ductility trade-off severity, and whether "best" changes under a different performance criterion.

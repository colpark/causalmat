This measurement (the combined EDS+XPS pairing) settles that erbium detected in the particles is chemically identifiable, not just spectrally inferred: XPS's Er 4d binding energy of 168.8 eV pins the species as Er3+, and because EDS independently confirms elemental Er is present at all (via the ~1.4 keV peak), the two together justify the specific claim "Er is present as Er3+ associated with the Ti-O oxide framework" — a claim neither technique alone could support (EDS alone would only show an energy peak with no oxidation-state information; XPS alone would show a binding energy with no confirmation that bulk/elemental Er exists to begin with). The Ti 2p and O1s peaks further support that this Er3+ signal sits within a titanium-oxide chemical environment rather than as some unrelated compound.

What it leaves open, plainly: (1) Cerium's oxidation state is undetermined — no Ce binding-energy XPS data were reported, so whether Ce is 3+, 4+, or mixed remains outside this result's scope, even though EDS shows a small Ce peak. (2) Whether Er (or Ce) is truly incorporated into the TiO2 lattice versus merely adsorbed on the surface is not established, since XPS is surface-sensitive while EDS probes a larger volume — agreement between the two techniques on "Er is present" does not prove they are sampling the same spatial phase. (3) The dominant carbon peak in EDS is unaddressed by XPS (no C1s reported), so it's unclear if carbon is genuine sample chemistry or contamination/support artifact. (4) Because the Er and Ce EDS peaks are described as small, the elemental-presence side of the argument is relatively weak, limiting overall confidence in the combined claim. In short: oxidation-state identity of Er is settled; lattice incorporation, Ce chemistry, and the carbon signal's origin are not.

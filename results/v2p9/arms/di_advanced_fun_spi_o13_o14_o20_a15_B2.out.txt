Panel F5d confirms the stated pattern: all five BNs-BSA+660/808nm (dual-laser) tumors stay flat near zero through day ~20-22, while four of five BNs-BSA+808nm-alone tumors are suppressed only until ~day 14, after which they regrow (volumes rising to 200-700 mm3 by day 22).

Read together with the established result (absorbance at 808 nm scales linearly with concentration and IR-camera spot temperature rises with both concentration and laser power, supporting absorption-mediated photothermal conversion rather than solvent heating), the combination supports that the 808-nm photothermal pathway is biologically active in vivo: the initial flattening of the 808-nm-alone curves is consistent with photothermal heating being sufficient to arrest tumor growth for roughly two weeks, linking the material's absorption-driven heat generation to a real therapeutic effect rather than just an in vitro/optical curiosity. It further shows this pathway alone is not durable — heat generation from 808 nm sufficient to stop growth transiently does not eradicate the tumor, since 4/5 regrow, whereas adding 660 nm (dual-laser) sustains complete suppression in all five animals.

What remains open: neither dataset measures actual intratumoral temperature or thermal dose in vivo, so it cannot be determined whether regrowth reflects insufficient heat penetration/dose at depth, residual heat-tolerant tumor cells, incomplete photothermal coverage, or a distinct contribution from the 660-nm component (e.g., a photodynamic/ROS mechanism) that the 808-nm-only arm lacks. The mechanistic link between the bench photothermal-conversion evidence and the specific biological cause of regrowth vs. durable ablation is not established by these panels.

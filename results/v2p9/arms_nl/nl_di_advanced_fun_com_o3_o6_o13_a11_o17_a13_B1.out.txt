Panel F3h (photothermal heating/cooling curve at λex = 808 nm, with -ln(θ) vs. time linear fit yielding τs = 133.4 s and η = 19.4%) demonstrates that irradiating the material at the same 808 nm wavelength used in the bulk-absorbance measurement (B) actually converts absorbed light into measurable heat, with a quantified time constant for heat dissipation and a numeric photothermal conversion efficiency.

Read together with the earlier settled result — that the two-phase Bi/Bi2O3 composite established by TEM+XRD (A) is the same bulk material whose NIR absorbance is linear and concentration-proportional at 808 nm (B) — this new measurement extends the story from "absorbs light" to "converts absorbed light to heat with quantifiable efficiency." That is, the combination supports that the as-prepared two-phase reduced material is not just optically absorbing but functions as an active photothermal agent, giving a concrete, reportable PRP value (η = 19.4%, τs = 133.4 s) for that whole composite as prepared.

What it leaves open: F3h is itself a bulk solution/pellet measurement, exactly like B, so it does not resolve which phase — metallic Bi, Bi2O3, or their interface — is responsible for the heat generation any more than B resolved which phase drives absorbance. It also does not link the TEM-imaged particles to the heat-generating entity, does not establish that η = 19.4% is stable over repeated heating/cooling cycles or under different conditions, and says nothing about therapeutic or applied performance (e.g., in vivo efficacy). The bulk-vs-local evidence gap carried over from A/B remains unresolved by this heating/cooling curve.

Panel F2a (read) shows three composite curves (discharge capacity, left axis, and coulombic efficiency, right axis, vs. cycle number) for three synthesis routes: co-precipitation w/ HCl (red), co-precipitation w/o HCl (blue), and simple mixing w/o HCl (black). The w/ HCl (red) trace starts near ~90 mAh/g at cycle 1 and decays gradually to ~55 mAh/g by cycle ~250, with coulombic efficiency near 100% throughout — consistent with the stated ~91 mAh/g first discharge capacity.

Read together with the earlier CV result, the combination supports that the ~91 mAh/g first-discharge capacity is a genuine electrochemical output of the single homogeneous Na-V-Fe-C-N-O compound's own multi-electron redox chemistry (the same three reversible couples seen by CV), not a capacity contributed by an unrecognized secondary/impurity phase. In other words, the service capability measured here can now be attributed with more confidence to the intrinsic framework redox sites already shown to be stable over repeated cycling, rather than being an artifact of phase mixture or surface/side reactions.

What this combination still leaves open: it does not say which fraction of the ~91 mAh/g is delivered by Fe-based versus V-based redox (CV gave no elemental assignment), nor whether the first-discharge capacity reflects full or only partial engagement of all three redox couples. It does not establish why the realized capacity is well below typical multi-electron theoretical capacities, nor connect the capacity fade seen out to 250 cycles in F2a to the CV persistence data (which covered only 20 cycles) — that longer-term fade mechanism remains unaddressed. Finally, neither result speaks to rate capability at higher C-rates, nor to how the TGA-defined thermal stability limits interact with electrochemical service life.

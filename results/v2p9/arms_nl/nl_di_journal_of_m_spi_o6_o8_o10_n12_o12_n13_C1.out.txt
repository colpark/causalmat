This measurement settles that the Se-doping series is internally consistent on a purely electrical-transport level: as x rises from 0.005 to 0.04, carrier concentration n_H climbs roughly sevenfold (0.5e19 to 3.3e19 cm-3) while mobility mu_H does not degrade below (and in fact stays above) matched-n_H literature values, and over the same range resistivity rho collapses from ~650 to ~12-23 micro-ohm m at 300 K. Combining these trends justifies the conclusion that the large PRP-relevant resistivity drop is adequately explained by carrier-concentration-driven conductivity gain and is not being masked or offset by a mobility penalty from doping -- i.e., the doping is "clean" from a transport standpoint, with both n_H and mu_H trends pointing the same direction (toward lower rho).

What this leaves open is substantial. It does not establish the microscopic mechanism (e.g., Se substituting on the Sb site) -- that requires structural or site-resolved evidence, not transport trends. The mobility-advantage claim rests on comparison to a heterogeneous literature set with different synthesis and microstructure, so retained mobility could reflect sample quality rather than the doping process itself. The mu_H data do not cover x=0.01, the composition where the resistivity minimum actually occurs, so the specific minimum cannot be tied to a measured mobility value there. The divergent temperature dependence (doped rho rising with T, undoped rho falling with T) is entirely unaddressed. Finally, with only a handful of doping points, this supports an ordering/trend across composition, not a fitted quantitative relationship among n_H, mu_H, and rho.

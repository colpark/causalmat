Best-guess reading (no actual figure/table was supplied, so this is inferential):

The paper reports that different binder systems (e.g., Al-based, Ti-based, or ceramic binders such as TiN/Al2O3 mixtures) used with cBN powder, densified by spark plasma sintering (SPS), yield measurably different sintering behavior (shrinkage/densification rate, relative density) and correspondingly different microstructures (grain size, binder phase distribution, reaction-phase formation at cBN/binder interfaces) and mechanical properties (hardness, fracture toughness, sometimes flexural strength) of the resulting PcBN composite.

Read together with an earlier established result — presumably that binder chemistry/reactivity governs densification and phase formation in PcBN systems fabricated by conventional (non-SPS) high-pressure/high-temperature or hot-pressing routes — this new SPS-based measurement settles that the same qualitative dependence (binder system controls densification behavior and downstream mechanical performance, i.e., a real "PRP" — property-response-to-processing — relationship) holds even under the very different, faster, lower-pressure SPS regime. In other words, the combination supports that the binder-dependent property/value trend is not an artifact of the traditional high-pressure process but is reproducible under SPS conditions, strengthening the causal link between binder chemistry and PcBN mechanical outcome across processing methods.

What it leaves open: the specific magnitude, ranking of binders, and whether the mechanism (reaction-layer formation vs. mere densification aid) is identical between SPS and conventional processing; also unresolved is whether the trend extends to other cBN content levels, larger sample/tool geometries, or long-term wear/thermal-stability performance, since only sintering/microstructure/short-term mechanical data are addressed.

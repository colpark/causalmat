Panel F7c plots device-relevant conversion efficiency eta(%) vs hot-side temperature Th (Tc fixed at 300 K) for x=0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04. At Th=623 K, x=0.01 tops out near 11.8%, clearly above the other three compositions, which cluster at 9.5-10.6%, and this ordering (x=0.01 best) holds across most of the Th range shown, not just at the endpoint.

Read together with the earlier settled PF result (x=0.01 has the highest power factor at both 300 K, ~29, and 623 K, ~22, among the doped samples), this combination supports the conclusion that the PF advantage at low-Se doping (x=0.01) is not merely a materials-metric artifact but actually propagates into higher device-level conversion efficiency across the full operating temperature span up to 623 K. That is, the same composition that leads in power factor also leads in the efficiency that a real thermoelectric generator would deliver, strengthening the case that x=0.01 is the practically favorable doping level, not just the one with the best isolated transport number.

What this combination leaves open: conversion efficiency depends on the full figure of merit ZT = S^2*sigma*T/kappa, and thermal conductivity is not reported in either panel, so the eta ordering cannot be attributed to the PF advantage alone versus a possible favorable kappa at x=0.01. It also does nothing to resolve the earlier unresolved questions: whether mobility or carrier density (n_H) sets the conductivity term behind the PF/efficiency peak at x=0.01, and whether Seebeck coefficient rather than conductivity drives the PF magnitude. The undoped (x=0) sample is also absent from this efficiency panel, so the doped-vs-undoped efficiency comparison remains unaddressed.

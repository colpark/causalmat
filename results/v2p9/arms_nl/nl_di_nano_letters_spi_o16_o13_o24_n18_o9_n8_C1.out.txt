This measurement (the joint A+B result) supports attributing Na capacity fading in Co3O4 primarily to poor sodiation reactivity rather than to volume-change-driven structural failure. It does so by combining two lines of evidence: the electrode's crystalline lattice and pore network survive intact through the first cycle with no metallic Co formed (ruling out the volume-change/structural-collapse explanation), while the computed oxidation degree for Na-Co3O4 stalls at only ~36% after 0.8 ps -- far below Na-Co3S4 (~59%) and Li-Co3O4 (~91%) at 2 ps -- showing the sodiation reaction itself is intrinsically limited even absent structural damage. Together these are mutually reinforcing: structural integrity without full conversion points to a chemical/reactivity bottleneck as the fading mechanism, not mechanical degradation.

What it leaves open: (1) whether this limited-conversion behavior actually persists across the full real electrochemical cycle, since B is a short picosecond-scale AIMD simulation while A is measured over the true cycling timescale -- the correspondence in timescales is assumed, not shown; (2) whether the low oxidation degree is specifically a sodium effect or partly a host-lattice/anion effect, since the B comparison varies cation and anion together across the three materials; (3) whether the modest structural change or the limited reactivity originates at particle surfaces or in the bulk; (4) no shared causal mechanism is established -- the two findings are shown to be consistent, not mechanistically linked; and (5) B is only a computed proxy for reaction extent, so no quantitative link is drawn between a given oxidation degree and a specific measured capacity loss.

The ZT panel (F6a) shows: x=0 near ~0.05 at 300 K (rising only to ~0.22 by 623 K), while all doped samples sit far higher (0.63-0.84 at 300 K, peaking 1.1-1.25 around 450-550 K, comparable to the n-Bi2Te3 reference ~0.88 at 300 K). At 300 K specifically, the doped ordering is x=0.005 (~0.84) > x=0.01 (~0.78) > x=0.02-0.04 (0.63-0.69).

Read together with the earlier PF result, this settles that the doping-driven gain is not confined to the power factor: doping also delivers a large ZT gain over the undoped sample, consistent with the doping regime being genuinely beneficial to thermoelectric performance rather than the PF rise being offset by some compensating figure-of-merit loss.

But the combination also exposes a mismatch neither piece shows alone: the PF ordering at 300 K has x=0.01 highest and x=0.005 lowest of the doped set, while the ZT ordering at 300 K has x=0.005 highest and x=0.01 second - the two rankings invert. Since ZT = PF*T/kappa, this discordance implies thermal conductivity must differ across doping levels and is exerting enough influence to override the PF ranking when producing ZT, something the PF-only or mobility-only data could not reveal.

This leaves open: no kappa values are given here, so the ZT/PF mismatch cannot be attributed to a specific thermal-conductivity mechanism (e.g., point-defect/alloy or bipolar scattering); the mobility-to-PF link remains unresolved as before; and how the ZT ordering behaves at higher temperature versus 300 K is not addressed by the 300 K-focused earlier results.

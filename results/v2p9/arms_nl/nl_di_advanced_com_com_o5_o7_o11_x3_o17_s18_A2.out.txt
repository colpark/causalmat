Panels F6c and F6d confirm the stated measurement: for pure PAEN and all three BST@Ag-loaded composites (1%, 3%, 5%), dielectric constant (F6c) and dielectric loss (F6d) are nearly flat and low from room temperature up to roughly 130-135 C, then rise sharply thereafter. Pure PAEN's own transition (rise in loss/constant) appears a bit later, near 155-160 C, consistent with the stated PAEN knee of ~155 C, while the composites' upturn occurs earlier, around 130-135 C. Dielectric loss (F6d) shows a peak for each curve near 165-175 C before declining slightly at the highest temperatures measured (180 C), rather than monotonically rising to the plot's edge.

This supports the claim that PRF/service_capability (stable dielectric behavior, i.e., usable temperature range before property degradation) is defined by a knee/onset around 130-135 C for the Ag-doped BST composites, with pure PAEN remaining stable to a higher temperature (~155 C). It also supports that loading with BST@Ag filler increases the absolute dielectric constant and loss but lowers the onset temperature of the sharp rise compared to neat PAEN.

What it leaves open: the panels only run to 180 C, so the ultimate ceiling of service capability (where properties become unacceptable, not just rising) is not shown. The mechanism for the earlier onset in composites (filler-matrix interface, moisture, or intrinsic BST/Ag effects) cannot be determined from these curves alone, nor can any correlation with mechanical or thermal (Tg) service limits — only dielectric response is measured here. No error bars or repeat runs are visible, so precision/reproducibility of the knee temperature is uncertain.

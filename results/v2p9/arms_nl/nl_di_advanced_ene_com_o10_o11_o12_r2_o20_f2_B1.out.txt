Figure 4A shows a rate-capability test: discharge capacity steps down from ~94–98 mAh/g at 55 mA/g through intermediate rates to ~54 mAh/g at 3520 mA/g (a ~64x current increase), then recovers to ~78 mAh/g when the rate is returned to 55 mA/g.

Read together with the earlier CV result (that the three reversible redox couples belong to a single homogeneous Na-V-Fe-C-N-O compound, not a mixture of phases), this combination supports the conclusion that the rate performance itself is a property of that one intrinsic multi-electron redox system: the same homogeneous phase sustains reversible charge/discharge across the whole 55–3520 mA/g range, and the partial capacity recovery on return to low rate confirms the fade with increasing current is not due to loss/decomposition of a separate impurity or conductive additive phase, but reflects kinetic limitations (e.g., polarization, ion/electron transport) intrinsic to this single compound's own redox chemistry.

What remains open: (1) which couple(s) — the ~0.55/0.89/1.08 V pairs — dominate capacity at high vs. low rate, and whether Fe- or V-centered sites are more rate-limiting, since neither CV nor this rate plot gives elemental assignment; (2) the mechanistic origin of the fade (diffusion-limited kinetics vs. incipient structural/interfacial change) is not distinguishable from a capacity-vs-cycle plot alone; (3) the incomplete recovery (78 vs. 94–98 mAh/g) indicates some rate-induced degradation, but the combination cannot say whether this is fully reversible, transient polarization, or a small permanent loss, nor how it relates to the earlier TGA thermal-stability regime (which remains a separate, uncombinable stability claim); (4) no stoichiometry, formula, or hydration state is established by either measurement.

What this settles: the measurement supports that this specific Na/PCO cathode material, under these specific cycling conditions (half-cell vs. Na metal, 0.1C rate), suffers pronounced and rapid capacity fade - losing roughly 90% of its initial discharge capacity within the first two cycles and continuing to fade to a low, largely stabilized capacity near 40 cycles. It also supports that Coulombic efficiency improves with cycling, consistent with initial irreversible side reactions (e.g., SEI formation, electrolyte decomposition, or irreversible structural/phase change) that diminish after the first cycle or two.

What it leaves open: the panel alone does not identify the mechanism of fade (structural collapse, side reactions, electrode/electrolyte interface degradation, etc.), does not establish behavior at other rates or in full cells, does not speak to cycling life beyond 40 cycles, and does not by itself support any general claim about PRF or "service_capability" as a durable, rate-independent, or application-relevant property - it only characterizes this one cell's capacity retention trajectory under one fixed low-rate protocol.

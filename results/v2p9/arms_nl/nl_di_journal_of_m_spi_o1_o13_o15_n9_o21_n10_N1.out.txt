Read together with an earlier established result — that single-phase FCC high-entropy alloys (e.g., CoCrFeMnNi) have low stacking-fault energy and therefore deform via extended (dissociated) dislocations bounding stacking-fault ribbons (STR) rather than compact dislocations — this new measurement would show that the same extended-dislocation/stacking-fault (STR) defect mode persists inside the FCC phase of a two-phase eutectic composite during tensile straining. Neither result alone settles this: the earlier work only shows the mechanism in a single-phase alloy, and the new tensile/TEM data alone would only show generic deformation defects without confirming they are the same low-SFE-driven extended dislocation type. Together they support generalizing the STR/extended-dislocon deformation mechanism from single-phase FCC HEAs to the FCC constituent of composite/eutectic HEAs, i.e., that it is an intrinsic phase property robust to the presence of a second (B2) phase and interfaces.

What remains open: whether the B2 phase hosts analogous defects, the alloy-specific stacking-fault energy value, quantitative fault spacing/density versus theory, and whether stacking-fault density causally drives the observed work-hardening/ductility rather than merely correlating with it.

F4b (before cycling) and F4c (after 5 thermal cycles) show the same field of view in the YSZ coating, and the crack network - fine branching cracks plus the dark globular pores/inclusions - appears essentially unchanged in density, length, and pattern between the two panels; no new cracking or coarsening is visually apparent.

Read together with the earlier phase/orientation-change result, this adds a second, independent line of evidence pointing the same direction for YSZ specifically: Observation B already showed YSZ's per-interval orientation change (micro-strain proxy) staying low and steady (0.7-2.3 deg) throughout cycling, unlike Al2O3-YSZ's large early change. The crack-network micrographs corroborate this at the microstructural/morphological level - the tetragonal-phase YSZ coating not only accumulates little incremental strain by diffraction but also shows no visible crack-network evolution in real-space imaging after 5 cycles. Combined, this supports that YSZ's mechanical/microstructural quiescence is consistent across two different measurement modalities (strain proxy and direct crack imaging), reinforcing that the tetragonal coating is comparatively stable early in the thermal-cycling life.

What this leaves open: the micrograph comparison is qualitative ("looks unchanged"), a single field of view, and only two timepoints (0 and 5 cycles) - no crack-density/length quantification, and no imaging at higher cycle counts where damage might eventually appear. There is no matching crack-network image pair for Al2O3-YSZ at these same cycles here, so the apparent phase-linked divergence in strain behavior cannot be confirmed to extend to crack damage on the other coating. Finally, "no visible change" does not rule out sub-resolution microstructural change underlying the small measured orientation shifts, and no mechanism links the orientation-change proxy to crack morphology - the agreement is a co-occurrence, not a demonstrated causal or quantitative relationship.

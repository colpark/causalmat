The panel (Fig. 2a) plots discharge capacity and coulombic efficiency vs. cycle number for three synthesis routes: co-precipitation w/ HCl (red), co-precipitation w/o HCl (blue), and simple mixing w/o HCl (black). At cycle 1, the red (w/ HCl) trace starts at approximately 90-91 mAh/g, consistent with the stated measurement of ~91 mAh/g first discharge capacity for the w/ HCl cathode. This is visibly the highest starting capacity of the three routes (w/o HCl starts near 78-80 mAh/g, simple mixing near 68-70 mAh/g), and coulombic efficiency for all three sits near 95-100% from the first cycles onward.

What this supports: the measurement is directly confirmed by the chart — the HCl-treated co-precipitation cathode delivers the highest initial discharge capacity among the three synthetic procedures tested, and it does so with high (>95%) coulombic efficiency, i.e., it is not achieving that capacity at the cost of poor reversibility. This justifies a claim that acid treatment during co-precipitation synthesis improves initial delivered capacity relative to the other two routes.

What it leaves open: the panel only shows capacity at one (unspecified) current rate/C-rate over ~250 cycles, so it says nothing about rate capability, high-power/service performance under varied loads or temperatures, or the mechanistic reason (e.g., morphology, crystallinity, impurity removal) for the HCl advantage. It also shows capacity fades to roughly half by cycle 250 for all three, so long-term "service capability" beyond initial capacity is not settled by this single number — only the first-cycle value is confirmed here.

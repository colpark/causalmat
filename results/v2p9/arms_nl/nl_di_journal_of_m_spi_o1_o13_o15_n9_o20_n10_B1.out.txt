F5a shows, within the FCC/L12 phase, a dense array of parallel dislocation walls organizing into cells (labelled "Taylor lattice"), with an arrow marking a pile-up at what appears to be a boundary/interface region — a bulk TEM image explicitly indexed to the L12 phase.

Read together with the earlier result: the earlier step established that the mottled/line contrast in the "responding" lamella reflects a real dislocation pile-up mechanism at the lamella boundary, but left unresolved which of the two ordered phases (L12 or B2) is the one developing that response, since the earlier TEM views were morphological/undexed. F5a supplies exactly that missing phase attribution: it demonstrates that the L12 phase specifically is the one that develops dense, organized dislocation substructure (Taylor lattice walls/cells) including a pile-up feature, i.e., L12 is dislocation-active under strain, consistent with L12 being the responding (mottled/line-contrast) lamella and B2 remaining the comparatively featureless one. The combination thus upgrades "one lamella shows pile-up, phase unknown" to "the pile-up-developing lamella is plausibly the L12 phase."

What it leaves open: F5a is a separate field of view from F4a/c, so this is an inference by phase identity/consistency rather than a direct, same-band correlation between the imaged pile-up in F4 and the Taylor lattice arrow in F5a. It still does not confirm B2 is dislocation-free (no B2-phase image is offered here for contrast), gives no dislocation density/count or strain-dependence, and rests on a single field of view, so generality across the microstructure and any boundary-geometry-specific origin of the pile-up remain unsettled.

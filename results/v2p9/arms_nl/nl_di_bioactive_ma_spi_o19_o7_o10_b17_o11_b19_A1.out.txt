Both panels (c) and (d) are bar charts of "Antibacterial Ratio (%)" for five samples — Control, TiO2, Er0.5Ce0.1Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.3Ti-O — each tested under Dark and Light conditions, n=3, mean±SD, with significance stars (*, **, ***) marked between group pairs.

Under Light, the measured values match the stated numbers closely: Control ~4-7%, TiO2 ~45%, Ce0.1 ~68% (c) to ~73% (d), Ce0.2 ~91-93% (the tallest bar in both panels), and Ce0.3 ~87% in both. Significance brackets in Light confirm Ce0.2Ti-O is statistically distinct (***/**/*) from Control, TiO2, and Ce0.1, supporting a real, dose-dependent, light-enhanced antibacterial effect that peaks at the 0.2 Ce doping level and clearly exceeds undoped TiO2 and the blank control.

This justifies the conclusion that the Er/Ce co-doped titanium oxide has a genuine, statistically supported photo-enhanced antibacterial capability against the tested bacterium, with an apparent optimal composition near Ce0.2, and that doping (versus plain TiO2 or control) materially improves antibacterial performance in both dark and light conditions (Dark values are lower overall but follow the same ranking).

What it leaves open: the caption fragments ("and E. coli" / triplicate note) do not clearly state whether panel (c) and (d) represent two different bacterial species (e.g., E. coli vs. S. aureus) or the same one — this bacterial identity per panel is not confirmed by the crops alone. The figure also does not establish mechanism (photocatalytic ROS vs. ion release), whether the Ce0.2 vs Ce0.3 difference is robustly significant (that comparison shows only a single star or is sometimes not bracketed), or long-term/in-vivo relevance.

This settled result establishes, jointly from prior XRD/XANES data (crystalline oxide phase retained, Co not reduced to metal on sodiation) and the new Na SAXS data (contours stay nearly straight, I(q) curves overlap through discharge/charge, with only a small drop at ~0.03-0.1 A^-1 at 0.11/0.02 V), that sodiation of PCO produces neither an appreciable metallic-Co-forming (conversion) reduction nor an appreciable volume/morphology change. Because both a chemical-reduction pathway and a volume-change/mechanical (e.g., pulverization) pathway are thereby ruled out as drivers, the observed Na capacity fading is left consistent with limited or poor intrinsic sodiation (conversion) activity of the oxide, rather than with structural/mechanical degradation. This is the conclusion the combined measurement supports: fading is attributable to the material's own limited electrochemical (conversion) activity, not to bulk chemical reduction or to volume/morphology-driven mechanical degradation.

What it leaves open: it does not quantify how much Co is reduced/oxidized or the extent of the modest XRD intensity loss; it does not quantify the magnitude of the small SAXS drop at ~0.03-0.1 A^-1 in real volume/morphology terms, nor identify its physical origin; it does not explain the mechanism behind why sodiation/conversion activity is limited in the first place; and it does not establish whether that small SAXS drop and the modest XRD intensity loss share a common structural cause. Additionally, this conclusion depends on combining both techniques—XRD/XANES alone cannot rule out SAXS-scale volume/morphology changes as a fading driver, and SAXS alone cannot rule out chemical reduction to metallic Co—so the joint inference, while reasonably supported, rests on qualitative rather than quantitative agreement between the two data sets.</message>

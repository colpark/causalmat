F6a shows dielectric constant vs. frequency (100 Hz–1 MHz) for pure PAEN and BST@Ag composites at 1/3/5% Ag: all four traces are nearly flat, each dropping only ~10% across the six-decade frequency sweep, while the absolute levels rise monotonically with Ag loading (pure PAEN ~3.2 down to ~3.0; then ~5.3→4.7, ~5.65→5.0, ~6.05→5.35 for 1/3/5% Ag).

Read against the earlier result (confirmed interfacial silane/Si-O species at the BST surface, paired with a Tg trend that dips then rises with Ag content, 146/149/157 °C vs. pure PAEN's 176 °C), this new panel adds a second composition-graded trend—dielectric constant magnitude—that tracks Ag loading in the same direction as the Tg values (both rise from 1% to 5% Ag), and shows that this magnitude trend is accompanied by uniform, composition-independent frequency stability (~10% roll-off in every sample, including pure PAEN). Together these support the conclusion that whatever interfacial/filler chemistry is driving the Ag-dependent property shifts (Tg and now dielectric constant magnitude) does not compromise a basic service-capability requirement—stable, frequency-independent dielectric response—since that stability is preserved across all loadings and is not itself graded by Ag content or by presence/absence of the confirmed interfacial species.

This leaves open: whether the confirmed interfacial species (versus Ag itself, BST filler, or dispersion) causes either the dielectric magnitude trend or the frequency-stability behavior—no silane-only, Ag-free sample is reported; whether the two Ag-graded trends (Tg, dielectric magnitude) share a common mechanistic origin or are independently caused; and no dose-response linking mass-loss fraction, Tg shift, or dielectric magnitude has been established.

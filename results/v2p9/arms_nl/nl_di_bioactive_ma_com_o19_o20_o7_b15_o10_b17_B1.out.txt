Combined with the earlier result: the sample already chemically confirmed to carry Er3+ anchored on the Ti-O framework and to show the most suppressed 390-420 nm recombination-linked PL among the three-sample series (Er0.5Ce0.2) is also the top photocatalytic performer here, and every Er/Ce-containing composition outperforms undoped TiO2 at all three time points. This supports a coherent, internally consistent structure-property-performance chain: confirmed Er3+ presence co-occurs not just with suppressed recombination-linked PL but also with enhanced measured photocatalytic activity, strengthening the case that the PL-suppression trend is functionally relevant to (not just numerically parallel to) the material's actual degradation performance, rather than being an isolated optical artifact.

What it still leaves open: (1) Ce's chemical role remains unattributed — XPS never covered Ce binding energies, and the degradation data actually show a non-monotonic dose-response with Ce loading (0.1 < 0.3 < 0.2), a pattern the PL data (only one Ce-doped point, Ce0.2) cannot explain or corroborate. (2) This figure has no degradation curve for plain Er0.5Ti-O (no Ce), so Er's contribution to activity still cannot be isolated from Ce co-doping. (3) Lattice-incorporation vs. surface-adsorption of Er3+ is still unresolved. (4) The link between PL suppression and higher degradation remains correlational, not mechanistic — other factors (surface area, adsorption capacity, band gap shifts) could equally drive the activity differences.</message>

Panel F5d (individual tumor-growth curves, mm^3 vs day, 5 mice per group) confirms the stated readout for PRF/service_capability: in the BNs-BSA + Lasers (combined 660/808 nm) panel, all five curves sit flat near 0 for the full ~20-day window — complete tumor suppression with no regrowth. In the BNs-BSA + 808 nm panel, curves are near-zero through about day 10–14, after which four of the five curves visibly rise (one reaching ~700 mm^3, others to ~250–450 mm^3 by day ~20), while one curve stays low; this matches "four of five regrow after ~day 14." All other groups (PBS, BNs-BSA alone, Lasers alone, BNs-BSA+660 nm) show unchecked, near-linear or accelerating growth to >1000–1500 mm^3 by day 15–20, so the panel establishes these as non-suppressive controls/monotherapies by contrast.

What this justifies: the dual-wavelength (660/808 nm) combination with BNs-BSA achieves durable, complete ablation across all treated animals, whereas single-wavelength 808 nm photothermal treatment with the same nanomaterial produces only transient suppression that fails in the majority of animals — i.e., the 660 nm component (or the combination) is necessary for durable service capability, not just initial tumor knockdown.

What it leaves open: the panel does not show what happens beyond ~day 20–22 for the combined-laser group, so long-term (e.g., >3–4 week) durability/cure fraction is not established beyond the plotted window; it does not report survival, histology, or mechanism explaining why 808 nm alone relapses (incomplete thermal ablation vs. resistant subpopulation vs. dosing); sample size is small (n=5/group) with no statistics (means/SEM/p-values) shown in this crop, so significance of the 4/5 vs 5/5 difference is not quantified here; and it does not address toxicity, generalizability to other tumor models, or reproducibility across replicate cohorts.

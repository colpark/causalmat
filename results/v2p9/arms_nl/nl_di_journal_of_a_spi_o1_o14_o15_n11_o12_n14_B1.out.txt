F6b shows K_IC rising steeply with sintering temperature for S1 (~2.4→5.6 MPa·m^1/2, 1400–1600°C), staying flat for S3 (~3.7–4.0 across its range), and staying comparatively low for S2 (~2.0–3.5, 1550–1700°C) — i.e., a real, temperature- and composition-dependent spread in bulk fracture toughness across these PcBN composites.

Read with the established fractographic result (gap-free, cleaved cBN/SiAlON boundaries at one site vs. a cleanly detached cBN grain with an open ~2 µm socket elsewhere on the same sample), the combination supports treating variable interfacial bonding quality (reaction-bonded/gap-free vs. debonded) as a plausible microstructural mechanism consistent with why bulk K_IC differs across these samples/temperatures — the macroscopic toughness spread and the microscopic bonding heterogeneity are compatible observations, both showing non-uniform, condition-dependent load-transfer behavior in the same material system.

It does not settle that bonding quality is the cause of the K_IC trend. The fractography came from one unspecified sample/condition and was never tied to a specific S-series or sintering temperature on the F6b plot, so there is no direct correspondence between "where gap-free bonding was seen" and "which K_IC value was measured." No fractography was done across the temperature series to show bonding improves as K_IC rises (e.g., for S1) or stays flat (S3) or lags (S2). Other contributors to K_IC (grain size, porosity, phase fraction changes with temperature, crack-deflection/transformation toughening) are not excluded. So the pairing gives associative plausibility for a bonding-mediated process-property link, not a demonstrated causal or even correlated one across the actual K_IC dataset.

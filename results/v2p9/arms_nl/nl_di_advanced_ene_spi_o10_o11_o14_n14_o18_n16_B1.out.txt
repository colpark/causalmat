Panel F4d shows the initial-discharge (nucleation) voltage dip for C HS and CnC HS, with the overshoot below the plateau marked as 43 mV (C HS) and 39 mV (CnC HS) — i.e., CnC HS nucleates zinc with a modestly lower overpotential than C HS.

Combined with the settled F3d/F3e + n12 result (CnC HS = nitrogen-shifted sample = flat, sparsely-platelet-covered Zn deposit; C HS = no nitrogen shift = dense mat of sharp platelets), this new electrochemical measurement gives an independent line of evidence pointing the same direction as the morphology: the sample that deposits Zn more smoothly (CnC HS) also shows a smaller nucleation barrier, while the sample that deposits Zn as sharp, dense platelets (C HS) shows a larger one. Together this supports treating "easier/lower-barrier nucleation" and "flatter, more compact deposit morphology" as two consistent, corroborating manifestations of the same underlying difference between CnC HS and C HS — strengthening confidence that the CnC HS/C HS distinction is real and multi-modal (spectral, morphological, and now electrochemical), not an artifact of any single measurement.

What it leaves open: the 4 mV gap is small, and F4d shows only single traces per sample with no stated replicates or error bars, so its statistical robustness is not established here. Neither this panel nor the earlier result shows that the nitrogen-species change causes the lower nucleation overpotential or the flatter morphology — all three observations (spectral, morphological, electrochemical) remain correlational, and other structural/compositional differences between the two hosts could independently drive both the nucleation-overpotential difference and the deposit morphology. No mechanistic chain (nitrogen state → nucleation-site density/surface energy → morphology) is established by the panels themselves.

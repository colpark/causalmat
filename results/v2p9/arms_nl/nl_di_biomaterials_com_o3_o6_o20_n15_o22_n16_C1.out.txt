This pairing supports a modest, plausibility-level conclusion: 2Co-MBG's independently established periodic, accessible mesoporosity is a structurally reasonable candidate mechanism for its observed reservoir-type release behavior, because the timescale over which release proceeds (~340 ug at 8 h rising to a plateau near ~780-795 ug by 72-168 h) roughly coincides with the window over which an accessible pore network was shown to exist, and the release curve's shape (rapid rise then plateau) is consistent with a finite reservoir being emptied rather than an open-ended process. That is the full extent of what can be claimed: a timing-level consistency that makes "release via the mesopore reservoir" a credible hypothesis, not a demonstrated fact.

What it leaves open is substantial. It does not show that drug actually transits or resides in the mesopores as opposed to being released via surface dissolution or matrix/network degradation, since no direct evidence (drug localization, pore-volume depletion, diffusion pathway) links the two data sets. It establishes correlation/coincidence in timing only, not causation - no pore size, ordering degree, or pore volume is available to explain why the plateau occurs at ~72 h specifically. Because the porosity observation is identical and non-comparative across MBG, 2Co-MBG, and 5Co-MBG while release data exist only for 2Co-MBG, the pairing cannot rule out that composition (Co content) rather than porosity governs the release profile.

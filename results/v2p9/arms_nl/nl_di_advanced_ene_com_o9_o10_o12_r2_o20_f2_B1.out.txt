The rate-capability panel (Fig. 4A) shows the discharge capacity stepping down monotonically as current density is raised from 55 to 3520 mA/g (~94-98 → ~54 mAh/g) and then largely recovering to ~78 mAh/g when the current is returned to 55 mA/g. This measurement, on its own, only shows the material can deliver capacity across a wide range of rates with substantial (though not complete) recovery on return to low rate — it says nothing about mechanism.

Combined with the earlier CV/STEM-EDS result — that the single homogeneous phase hosts three distinct, reversible redox couples (0.55/0.52, 0.89/0.87, 1.08/1.07 V) persisting over 20 cycles — the rate data now supports a stronger, joint conclusion: the multiple redox events attributable to that one phase are not merely an artifact seen only in slow-scan CV, but are collectively responsible for delivering usable, cycle-persistent capacity over a broad range of current densities (55–3520 mA/g), and that capacity is largely restored when the rate is reduced again. In other words, the same homogeneous compound that shows multi-couple reversibility in CV also functions as a rate-tolerant, at least partially reversible capacity source in galvanostatic cycling — supporting that the redox chemistry identified by CV is functionally relevant to real (dis)charge performance, not just a spectroscopic curiosity.

What this combination leaves open: it does not attribute the capacity loss at high rate, or the capacity gap on return (~78 vs original ~94-98), to any specific one of the three redox couples — there is no couple-resolved rate or capacity-retention data here. It does not give electron counts, site/element assignment (still unresolved from the earlier caveats), or a mechanism for the stepwise fade (kinetic limitation vs. structural change vs. polarization). It also does not extend the "single homogeneous phase" claim's cycle-window (only 20 cycles established) to the ~65+ cycles shown in this rate test, since capacity fade over many cycles could in principle reflect processes CV never sampled.

Panel F5d (six-panel tumor-volume-vs-time individual mouse curves) confirms the stated measurement exactly. In the "BNs-BSA + 808 nm" panel, four of the five individual tumor curves are near zero through ~day 14-15 and then turn upward, reaching ~250-700 mm3 by day 20-22 (one curve stays flat near zero). In the "BNs-BSA + Lasers" panel (the combined 660/808 nm dual-wavelength group), all five individual curves remain flat at essentially zero volume for the full ~22-day observation window, in contrast to every other group (PBS, BNs-BSA alone, Lasers alone, BNs-BSA+660nm), which show monotonic tumor growth to >1000 mm3.

What this supports: single-wavelength 808 nm photothermal treatment with BNs-BSA achieves only partial, non-durable tumor control — most tumors escape and resume growth after roughly two weeks. Full, durable suppression (service capability at the "complete/durable" level) requires the combined dual-laser (660+808 nm) modality, which held all five tumors flat with no regrowth observed. So the measurement supports a claim that 808 nm-only treatment has a lower/incomplete service capability tier than the combined-laser treatment for this endpoint (durable tumor suppression).

What it leaves open: sample size is small (n=5 per arm) and the observation window is short (~3 weeks), so it's unclear whether the flat 660/808 combo curves would eventually show regrowth with longer follow-up, whether the one non-regrowing 808 nm mouse reflects biological variability or something else, and the panel alone doesn't isolate mechanism (i.e., whether 660 nm's contribution is additive/synergistic vs. 808 nm's, or which wavelength drives the durability difference).

Panel F6a plots ZT vs. T (300-625 K) for Mg3.2Bi1.4Sb0.6-xSex with x = 0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04, plus a dashed n-Bi2Te3 reference curve. At 300 K the undoped x=0 sample sits near zero (~0.05, rising only to ~0.2 by 625 K), while every Se-doped sample is far higher: x=0.005 ~0.84-0.85, x=0.01 ~0.78, and x=0.02/0.04 ~0.63-0.69 — matching the stated numbers closely. All doped compositions rise with temperature to a broad peak of ~1.2-1.3 around 450-525 K, then fall off toward 600-625 K (down to ~0.6-1.1), tracking or exceeding the n-Bi2Te3 reference curve above roughly 420-450 K, though the reference is comparable or slightly higher near 300-400 K.

What this justifies: the measurement supports that Se substitution for Sb is the operative lever raising ZT versus the undoped baseline across the whole temperature range, that ZT is non-monotonic (peaking mid-range rather than at either endpoint), and that the best-performing doped composition(s) rival or surpass the classic n-Bi2Te3 benchmark in the 450-600 K window — a legitimate figure-of-merit claim for that material class in that window.

What it leaves open: this is a single ZT-vs-T curve set per composition with no error bars or repeat runs shown, so sample-to-sample reproducibility and measurement uncertainty are unaddressed. ZT here is the standard combination of separately measured S, sigma, and kappa; the panel itself doesn't show whether those were measured on the same pellet/run, so propagated error is unclear. Long-term/thermal-cycling stability of ZT and behavior beyond 625 K are not shown, and the n-Bi2Te3 reference is a literature curve (refs [8,22]) from a different study/setup, so the comparison is not a matched, side-by-side controlled measurement.

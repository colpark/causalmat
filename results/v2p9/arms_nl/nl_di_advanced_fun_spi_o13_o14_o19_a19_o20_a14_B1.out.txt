Panel F5d (individual growth curves) shows: BNs-BSA+808nm alone controls tumors initially but 4/5 regrow after ~day 14 (partial, transient suppression), while BNs-BSA+Lasers (660+808 combined) holds all five at 0 (durable, complete suppression). PBS, BNs-BSA-alone, and lasers-alone groups all grow unchecked, and BNs-BSA+660nm alone also fails to control growth.

Read together with the earlier-established result (BNs-BSA under 808nm light raises tumor temperature to ~49C vs ~41C for PBS at 5 min, confirming real functional heating at the tumor site rather than just in-dish absorption), the combination supports that this demonstrated in vivo heating does translate into an actual biological/therapeutic consequence: tumor growth is measurably suppressed in the BNs-BSA+808nm arm relative to every non-808, non-BNs-BSA control. So the photothermal effect is not merely a temperature readout — it does something to the tumor. It also shows that 808nm-driven photothermal heating alone is necessary but not sufficient for durable control: the same material/heating pathway that produced the ~49C reading still permits regrowth in 4 of 5 tumors, and only the combined 660+808nm treatment achieves complete, durable ablation in all five.

What remains open: growth curves give no temperature data of their own, so whether the day-14+ regrowth in the 808-only arm reflects insufficient heat dose/duration, incomplete tumor coverage, material clearance/redistribution over time, or a separate mechanistic contribution from 660nm (photothermal, photodynamic, or synergistic) cannot be distinguished. The accumulation confound noted earlier (material reaching/retained at tumor) is also unresolved by growth data alone, and no dose- or power-response relationship is established.

This measurement settles a correlation, not a causal or mechanistic link, between the nitrogen-species chemistry and zinc deposit morphology.

What it supports: it establishes that CnC HS and C HS differ consistently on two independent axes — a spectral one and a morphological one — and that these two axes align on the same sample. Specifically, CnC HS is the sample that (a) contains the nitrogen species whose 398/401 eV NEXAFS features shift upon zinc deposition, and (b) is the sample where plated zinc forms a largely flat, sparsely-platelet-covered deposit. C HS is the sample that lacks both: no nitrogen spectral features/shift, and a dense mat of sharp zinc platelets instead. So the result justifies saying that the presence of the nitrogen species (and its zinc-induced spectral shift) co-occurs with the more planar, compact zinc morphology, while its absence co-occurs with dense, sharp-platelet growth — a paired distinction between the two samples on both fronts simultaneously.

What it leaves open: it does not show that the nitrogen chemical-environment change causes the flatter morphology — only that the two differences occur together in the same sample comparison. It cannot rule out that some other structural, compositional, or textural difference between CnC HS and C HS (unrelated to nitrogen) is the real driver of the morphology difference. The specific pyridinic/graphitic assignments and any Zn-N bonding are the paper's interpretive labels, not something the panels themselves demonstrate, so no mechanistic pathway from nitrogen state to morphology can be read off this data. Finally, neither side is quantified — the spectral evidence is peak presence/ranking and the morphology evidence is a qualitative density/sharpness description, with no measured coverage, amount, or platelet size on either side.

What the images show: F4e (before cycling) shows the boxed diagonal band with a moderate network of fine, mostly short, disconnected micro-cracks running through the splat/lamellar microstructure, with a couple of pores/particles visible. F4f (after five thermal cycles) shows the same boxed band with visibly longer cracks that now link up into continuous paths running along the band, and a distinct wide horizontal crack has opened near the bottom of the image, cutting across the microstructure.

What this justifies: The paired before/after micrographs support the qualitative claim that thermal cycling drove crack growth and coalescence within the marked region of the Al2O3-YSZ coating — cracks that were short and isolated pre-cycling become longer and interconnected post-cycling, and a new (or newly opened) horizontal crack appears near the base of the field of view. This is consistent with cycling-induced microstructural damage accumulation, i.e., progressive cracking/delamination-type damage localized to the boxed band.

What it leaves open: The images alone do not establish quantitative crack length, density, or width changes, nor crack depth or through-thickness extent. They do not show intermediate cycle counts, so the rate or onset cycle for coalescence is unknown. They cannot confirm whether the "new" horizontal crack is truly new versus a pre-existing feature outside the original field of view, nor do they establish the mechanism (thermal expansion mismatch, phase transformation, sintering) or link this local damage to bulk mechanical/thermal-barrier performance (e.g., failure life, thermal conductivity change). No statistics across multiple samples/locations are given, so generality beyond this one imaged region is uncertain.

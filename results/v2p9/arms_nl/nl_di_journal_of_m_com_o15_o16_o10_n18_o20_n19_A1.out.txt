Fig. 14 plots average UTS vs. average YS for AZ61 alloys across processing routes (this study, ECAP variants, squeeze casting, multidirectional forging, friction stir processing, rolling, heat-treated, small-strain impact multi-directional forging, extrusion+2-pass ECAP, forging+heat treated), overlaying Table 4's literature data.

What the measurement supports: the three red "this study" points sit clearly in the upper-right region of the plot, at roughly (275,380), (295,355), and (335,448) MPa (YS,UTS) — all above every other technology's cluster. The next-highest points (90° ECAP, extrusion+2-pass ECAP, multidirectional forging, rolling, squeeze casting) all fall in a tight band around 190-240 YS / 260-320 UTS, and none reach above ~240 YS or ~318 UTS. So the figure directly supports the claim that this study's processed AZ61 achieves a simultaneously higher yield strength and higher ultimate tensile strength than the comparator routes shown, i.e., a favorable strength combination relative to the assembled literature set, with no strength-strength trade-off visible among the three "this study" points themselves (they scatter but all dominate the pack).

What it leaves open: the plot gives no error bars, sample counts, or scatter within each "this study" condition, and no ductility/elongation axis, so nothing here bears on toughness or strength-ductility trade-offs. It also does not indicate processing conditions distinguishing the three red points, testing orientation/temperature, or statistical significance of the offset from the literature cluster — it is a compiled comparison of averages, not a controlled head-to-head experiment. Composition/microstructure differences between "this study" and the literature alloys are not addressed, and generalization beyond AZ61 or beyond the specific literature set plotted cannot be inferred from this panel alone.

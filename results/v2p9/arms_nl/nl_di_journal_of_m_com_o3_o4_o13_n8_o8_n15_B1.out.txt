Panel F2b shows three macroscopic hardening regimes: Stage I, a steep drop from ~85 to ~14 GPa completed by ~0.009 true strain; Stage II, a slow decline from ~14 to ~5 GPa out to ~0.058 strain; Stage III, hardening rate turning negative (onset of softening) beyond that.

Read alone, this curve is purely mechanical/macroscopic: it delineates three strain-hardening stages but says nothing about which microstructural feature or which lamella phase drives them. The earlier microstructural result, read alone, is purely local/qualitative: it establishes that at the "first strain step" one lamella type develops mottled/interface contrast while its neighbor stays featureless, i.e., defect accumulation is phase-selective rather than simultaneous—but it carries no strain-quantitative anchor and doesn't say what mechanical stage that step belongs to.

Combined, the two pieces jointly support locating this lamella-selective plasticity onset inside a defined macroscopic regime: if the imaged "first strain step" falls in the low-strain range, it aligns with Stage I, the steep, rapid loss of hardening capacity below ~0.009 strain. That supports a mechanistic hypothesis—Stage I's rapid hardening-rate collapse reflects one lamella phase alone beginning to accommodate plastic strain (via defects) while the other remains elastic/undeformed, rather than both phases co-deforming from the start.

This leaves open, and is not settled by the combination: which phase (FCC(L12) or B2) is the active one; whether the strain value of the imaged "first step" actually corresponds numerically to Stage I on this stress-strain curve (different specimens/techniques may not share a strain axis); whether the same selective mechanism persists through Stage II's slower decline or explains Stage III's softening; and any quantitative link between defect density and hardening-rate magnitude.

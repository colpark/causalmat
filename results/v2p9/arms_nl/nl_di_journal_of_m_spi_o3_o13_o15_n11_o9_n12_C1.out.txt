This measurement settles that the interface-localized contrast seen at the first strain step (mottled/line contrast along one lamella's interface, versus a featureless neighbour) has a concrete microstructural identity: it is consistent with a dislocation pile-up accumulating against the lamella boundary, not an imaging artifact or a diffuse feature spread through the bulk of the lamella. Taken together, the two observations support the conclusion that the phase boundary itself -- not just one phase considered alone -- is an active site of dislocation accumulation at this strain step, and that the asymmetry noted earlier between the two lamellae has a specific, boundary-localized dislocation signature rather than being an unexplained contrast difference.

What it leaves open is substantial. It does not identify which lamella (A or B) is FCC and which is B2, so it cannot say whose boundary the pile-up sits against. It does not establish causation: whether lattice-mismatch internal stress drives the pile-up, or whether the pile-up is what raises yield strength, remains unproven by these panels. It cannot distinguish an interface/pile-up-driven mechanism from an intrinsic phase slip-resistance mechanism -- the pile-up could simply be a downstream consequence of one lamella deforming more easily, rather than an independent boundary effect causing the asymmetry. The comparison is still limited to one location (F4a, F4c) and one strain step against the undeformed baseline, so generality across the microstructure and evolution with further straining are unknown. Finally, both observations are qualitative micrograph descriptions -- no dislocation counts, densities, or quantitative link to strength or accumulated deformation can be drawn from them.

At Th = 623 K, the curves separate as claimed: x=0.01 (red) reaches the highest η, about 11.8%, while the other three compositions (x=0.005 black, x=0.02 blue, x=0.04 magenta) cluster lower, roughly 9.5-10.6%, with x=0.01 visibly above the pack across most of the mid-to-high temperature range, not just at the endpoint.

What this supports: within this single-panel comparison, the measurement justifies the narrower claim that Se-doping at x=0.01 gives the best measured maximal conversion efficiency among the four Se-substitution levels tested, at a fixed cold-side temperature of 300 K and hot-side up to 623 K. It is a real, non-trivial gap (~1.2-2.3 percentage points at the top of the sweep) and the ordering (x=0.01 best) is consistent over much of the curve, not a single-point artifact.

What it leaves open: this is device-level conversion efficiency (η), not a direct measurement of "service capability" in the PRF sense — no error bars, sample count, or reproducibility data are shown, so the magnitude of the x=0.01 advantage relative to measurement uncertainty is unknown. The panel does not show behavior at Th above 623 K, does not report power output or figure of merit (zT) separately, and gives no information on long-term stability, cycling durability, or performance under real service conditions (thermal cycling, mechanical stress, oxidation). It also only tests four discrete x values, so whether x=0.01 is a true optimum or just the best of these four sampled compositions is not established — a finer composition scan could reveal a different optimum. So the data support "x=0.01 gives higher measured maximal conversion efficiency in this lab-scale Th sweep" but not a general service-capability or durability claim.

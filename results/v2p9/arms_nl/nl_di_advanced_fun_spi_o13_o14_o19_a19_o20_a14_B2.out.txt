Panel F5d confirms the stated pattern: all five BNs-BSA+660/808nm (dual-laser) curves stay flat near 0 through day ~20; the BNs-BSA+808nm-alone curves show four of five tumors staying near 0 until roughly day 14-15 and then regrowing to ~250-700 mm3 by day 20, with only one curve remaining suppressed.

Read together with the established in vivo temperature result (BNs-BSA drives tumor to ~49C at 5 min under 808 nm, vs ~41C for PBS), the combination supports: the photothermal heating shown to be functionally engaged at the tumor site is biologically consequential, not just a thermometer reading — it produces an initial growth arrest in the 808nm-alone arm that tracks the timing of treatment, which is the expected downstream signature of a real local hyperthermic effect on tumor tissue. But the growth curves also show that this 808nm-driven heating alone is sub-curative in most animals (4/5 regrow), whereas adding the 660nm laser converts the same photothermal platform into a durable, complete regression (5/5) in F5d. So the pairing supports: the in vivo temperature rise is functionally real and tumor-engaging, but by itself (single 808nm arm) it is often insufficient for lasting ablation.

What remains open: because the 808nm-only and dual-laser arms differ by more than heat dose (660nm is added), the data cannot isolate whether durable ablation requires more/longer 808nm heating or a genuinely separate 660nm-mediated mechanism (e.g., photodynamic) acting synergistically with heat. There is also no per-animal temperature-to-outcome correlation (was the one non-regrowing 808nm mouse the hottest?), no dose/duration series in vivo, and regrowth could still reflect nanomaterial clearance rather than insufficient thermal dose. Small n (5/arm) limits generalization.

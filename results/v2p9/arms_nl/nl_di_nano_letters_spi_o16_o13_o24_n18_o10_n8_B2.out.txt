The F2c curves add the quantitative electrochemical trajectory that the earlier structural/computational pair (A: intact lattice+pores; B: only ~36% computed oxidation, stalling early) lacked. Read together:

What it settles: The 1st-cycle numbers (discharge ~865 mAh/g down to a low ~0.15 V plateau, but charge only ~470 mAh/g) show a large irreversible capacity loss already within the very first cycle — exactly the cycle over which A verified the structure stays intact (no lattice collapse, no pore destruction). Because A rules out volume-change/structural failure as the cause of that first-cycle loss, and B shows the sodiation reaction itself only proceeds partway (~36%) before stalling, the combination supports that the ~865→470 mAh/g first-cycle irreversibility is consistent with (not caused by) an intrinsically incomplete conversion reaction rather than particle fracture or pore collapse — the discharge capacity is inflated by a reaction that doesn't fully/reversibly convert back on charge. The subsequent collapse to ~60 mAh/g by the 40th cycle further confirms the fading is severe and progressive, not a one-time formation-cycle artifact.

What it leaves open: F2c is a bare electrochemical measurement — it carries no structural or oxidation-state information itself, so it cannot show whether the structure remains intact or whether oxidation degree stays similarly limited at cycle 2, 10, or 40. A's structural-retention claim and B's oxidation-degree claim were both scoped to the first cycle (A) or a picosecond simulation window (B); neither was re-established at later cycles, so the continued fade to 40 cycles could equally reflect late-onset structural degradation, SEI/electrolyte side reactions, or other mechanisms outside what A and B tested. The pair still doesn't decompose how much of the 1st-cycle irreversible loss is poor sodiation reactivity versus SEI formation, and it doesn't localize any of this to bulk versus surface.

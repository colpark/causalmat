Based on the paper's title alone (no figures/measurements provided), my best-informed reading is this:

The paper reports that Se-doping of n-type Mg3Bi2-based (Mg3(Bi,Sb)2-type) alloys pushes their thermoelectric figure of merit (zT) to a high value specifically near room temperature, by using Se substitution to optimize carrier concentration/band alignment and suppress lattice thermal conductivity, rather than only at the elevated temperatures where earlier Mg3Bi2 n-type work had shown gains.

Read together with the "established earlier result" that Bi2Te3-based alloys are the incumbent room-temperature thermoelectric benchmark (zT ~1) and that good p-type Mg3Sb2/Mg3Bi2-based legs already exist, this combination settles that a matched, all-antimonide/bismuthide n+p thermoelectric couple can now plausibly rival Bi2Te3 at the specific temperature range (near 300 K) that matters for practical Peltier cooling and waste-heat harvesting — i.e., it supports the claim that Mg3Bi2-family materials have real "practical value" (PRP) as a Te-free, more earth-abundant/lower-cost alternative to Bi2Te3 for near-room-temperature devices, not just as a high-temperature power-generation material.

What it leaves open: neither the new n-type measurement nor the prior benchmark result, alone or combined, establishes device-level performance — i.e., actual module conversion efficiency, mechanical/chemical stability of n-p junctions under thermal cycling, long-term oxidation/degradation resistance, reproducibility at scale, or cost-competitiveness in manufacturing. Single-leg zT improvements do not guarantee that a fabricated couple reaches comparable practical efficiency to commercial Bi2Te3 modules, so the commercial viability claim remains unsettled pending device-integration data.

Note: I was given no actual figures, numeric zT values, or the specific "earlier result" referenced, so this is a domain-informed inference from the title and known Mg3Bi2/Bi2Te3 thermoelectric literature, not a reading of the paper's actual data.

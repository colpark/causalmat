The panel (F5b, bright-field TEM, "dislocation configurations in the B2 phase") shows a field crossed by many closely spaced, straight, parallel dark lines all running along a single diagonal orientation, with some finer tangled dislocation debris connecting them, at a scale of 0.2 micron. This image directly supports the measurement's claim: the dislocations are not isolated or randomly oriented but form a coherent array along one slip trace, consistent with classifying this defect population as "extended" — i.e., a planar/linear array of dislocations aligned on a single slip system, as would be produced by glide on one set of slip planes (a slip band), rather than a localized point-defect or a disordered dislocation tangle/forest.

What it settles: the morphology criterion for "extended" is met — the lines are long, straight, mutually parallel, and confined to one slip trace across the imaged field, which is the signature of an extended planar defect structure (a slip-band-like dislocation array) in the B2 phase.

What it leaves open: the image alone does not establish the dislocation type (edge vs. screw), Burgers vector, or which specific slip system is active; it does not quantify dislocation density or line length beyond this one field of view; it cannot confirm whether this configuration is representative of the bulk material or a locally deformed/thinned region near the foil edge; and it says nothing about whether similar extended arrays occur in other phases besides B2, since the caption and field are restricted to the B2 phase only.

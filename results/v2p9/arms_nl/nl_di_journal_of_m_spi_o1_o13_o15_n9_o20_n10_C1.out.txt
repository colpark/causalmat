This result justifies moving from a purely qualitative "asymmetric contrast" observation to an asymmetric defect response: it establishes that the mottled/line contrast developing in one lamella of the pair, while its neighbor stays featureless, is consistent with a dislocation pile-up accumulating at the lamella boundary, rather than being a non-defect (diffraction or strain-field) artifact. In other words, the STR (structural response to strain) is now supported as being defect-mediated and spatially localized to one lamella's interface, extending the earlier finding from "contrast differs between neighboring lamellae" to "one lamella develops a dislocation pile-up at its boundary while the other does not."

What it leaves open: (1) it does not say which phase — L12 or B2 — is the lamella that piles up, since the XRD is not spatially resolved and the TEM description is morphological rather than diffraction-indexed to a phase; (2) it rests on a single strain step and a single lamella pair/pile-up band, so whether this asymmetric pile-up response generalizes across the microstructure is untested; (3) no dislocation count or areal density is reported, so the mechanism is qualitative only — it shows pile-up occurs but not how much, nor how it evolves with further strain; (4) the pile-up could plausibly be driven by local boundary geometry (e.g., a favorably/unfavorably oriented interface) rather than by an intrinsic difference in slip behavior between the two ordered phases, and this measurement cannot distinguish those possibilities. So the conclusion supported is narrowly mechanistic (dislocation pile-up as the defect basis for the asymmetric contrast) but not yet phase-specific, quantitative, or generalized.

Panel F2c (galvanostatic voltage profiles, 1st/2nd/40th cycles) shows: 1st discharge ~865 mAh/g with a plateau near 0.15 V, 1st charge only ~470 mAh/g (roughly 54% first-cycle Coulombic efficiency, so ~395 mAh/g is irreversibly lost already in cycle 1), and by the 40th cycle the curve has collapsed to ~60 mAh/g with no discernible plateau — a >90% capacity loss relative to the first discharge.

Read together with the earlier XRD/XANES + SAXS result (no metallic-Co reduction, no appreciable volume/morphology change), this settles that the severe, rapid capacity fade actually observed electrochemically is not attributable to either of the two structural/chemical pathways that bulk diffraction, absorption, and scattering could detect. In other words, the combination shows the fading is real and large in magnitude (quantified here for the first time: from 865 down to 60 mAh/g by cycle 40, with substantial first-cycle irreversibility), while the earlier bulk-structural probes show the material's crystalline framework and Co oxidation state and overall particle size/shape are essentially unchanged. Jointly, this strengthens the "limited/poor intrinsic sodiation activity" interpretation by showing the fade is not merely modest or within measurement noise — it is nearly total — yet still occurs without bulk conversion or bulk volume change.

What remains open: the actual mechanism producing this large irreversible and progressive capacity loss (e.g., SEI growth, loss of electrical contact/particle isolation, surface passivation, or kinetic/interfacial limitations rather than bulk structural change) is not established by either the voltage curves or the structural probes; none of these techniques localizes the degradation to a specific interfacial or kinetic process.

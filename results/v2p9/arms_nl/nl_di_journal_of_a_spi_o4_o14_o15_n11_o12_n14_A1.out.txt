Panel F6b (fracture toughness vs. sintering temperature for three PcBN composite series, S1/S2/S3) supports a modest, temperature- and composition-dependent effect on K_IC, not a clean monotonic PRP relationship.

What it supports: S1 rises sharply with sintering temperature, from ~2.4 MPa·m^1/2 at 1400°C to ~2.9 at 1500°C to ~5.6 at 1600°C — the largest and clearest trend in the data, and error bars at 1600°C are wide, meaning that jump is real but its magnitude is uncertain. S3 is essentially flat across 1400-1600°C (~3.7 to ~3.95 to ~3.95), i.e., toughness in that series is insensitive to sintering temperature in this range. S2, measured over a different (higher) temperature window (1550-1700°C), shows a mild increase from ~2.1/2.0 at 1550/1600°C up to ~3.0 and ~3.5 at 1650/1700°C. So the data support: (a) sintering temperature can raise K_IC substantially in some compositions (S1), (b) in other compositions the effect is negligible (S3), and (c) a third composition shows a smaller, more gradual rise over a hotter range (S2).

What it leaves open: because S1, S2, and S3 differ in composition (not just processing), the panel cannot isolate whether the toughness gain is driven by sintering temperature per se, by composition, or by an interaction between the two — no single series spans the full temperature range, so direct comparison across the three "at a fixed temperature" is not really possible. The mechanism behind S1's steep rise (grain growth, phase change, binder redistribution, etc.) is not addressed by this plot, nor is reproducibility beyond the shown error bars. So it justifies a composition-specific PRP claim, not a general PRP/value law for PcBN.

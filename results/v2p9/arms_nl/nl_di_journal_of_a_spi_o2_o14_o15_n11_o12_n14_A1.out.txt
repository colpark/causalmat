I reviewed panel F6b (KIC vs. sintering temperature for PcBN composite series S1, S2, S3).

What the panel shows, consistent with the given values: S1 rises steeply with temperature (~2.4 → 2.9 → 5.6 MPa·m^1/2 at 1400/1500/1600°C); S3 is essentially flat across 1400–1600°C (~3.7, 3.95, 3.95); S2 is low and flat at 1550–1600°C (~2.1, 2.0) then climbs at higher temperature (~3.0 at 1650°C, ~3.5 at 1700°C). Error bars are present on all points and are non-trivial relative to the point-to-point differences, especially for S1 and S3.

What this justifies: the measurement supports that fracture toughness in these PcBN composites is both composition-series-dependent and sintering-temperature-dependent, and that the three series respond differently to temperature — S1 is the most temperature-sensitive and reaches the highest toughness value measured (~5.6 MPa·m^1/2 at 1600°C), S3 is comparatively insensitive to temperature over its tested range, and S2 needs a higher temperature window before toughness rises appreciably. This is enough to support a qualitative ranking/trend claim (toughening is achievable by raising sintering temperature, but the effect size and threshold differ by composition/series).

What it leaves open: (1) the mechanism (grain size, binder phase, phase transformation, etc.) behind the differences is not shown in this panel — no microstructural evidence is included here; (2) the series are tested over non-overlapping/partial temperature ranges (S1 and S3 only to 1600°C, S2 only from 1550°C up), so behavior outside each series' tested window is unknown; (3) given the visible error bars, some adjacent points (e.g., S3's three points, S2's two low points) may not be statistically distinguishable; (4) the caption and image make no reference to "PRP" — that term does not appear here, so I cannot say what this measurement settles about PRP specifically, only about KIC vs. temperature/series as plotted.

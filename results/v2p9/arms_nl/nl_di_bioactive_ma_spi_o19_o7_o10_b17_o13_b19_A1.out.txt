Both panels are qualitative plate-culture photos, not quantitative counts. Panel F10a (S. aureus) and F10b (E. coli) each show a 2x5 grid: rows Dark/Light, columns Control/TiO2/Er0.5Ce0.1Ti-O/Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O/Er0.5Ce0.3Ti-O.

What the images support: under Dark conditions, Control and TiO2 plates show confluent/streaked bacterial lawns for both strains, and the doped Er-Ce-Ti-O plates in the dark do appear visibly less dense than Control/TiO2 dark plates (consistent with "dark plates stay covered, sparser for doped samples" — sparser relative to controls, but still substantially covered, not clean). Under simulated-sunlight (Light), colony counts drop sharply across all TiO2-containing samples relative to Control, and among the doped compositions, Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O shows the fewest, most sparsely scattered colonies for both S. aureus and E. coli — visibly less than Er0.5Ce0.1Ti-O and Er0.5Ce0.3Ti-O under light. This supports a conclusion that the Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O composition delivers the strongest light-activated antibacterial service capability among the doped variants tested, and that antibacterial action is light-dependent (dark plates retain much more growth than light plates for the same sample) rather than a simple contact-killing effect.

What it leaves open: there is no CFU quantification, replicate count, or statistical test visible in the crops — "fewest" and "sparser" are visual impressions only, not measured reduction percentages or log-kill values. The mechanism (ROS generation, ion release, etc.) is not shown. Why 0.2 Ce outperforms 0.3 Ce (a non-monotonic dose effect) is not addressed by these images. Durability, reusability, and performance beyond a single 20-minute exposure are untested here.

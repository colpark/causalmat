Panel F5c (power factor vs T) shows: x=0 lowest throughout (~2 to ~5 µW/cm·K²); x=0.005 next-lowest of the doped set; x=0.01 highest at all T (~29 at 300K, ~22 at 623K); x=0.02 and x=0.04 intermediate. All doped curves fall with increasing T; the undoped curve rises with T.

Read together with the established rho/n_H/mu_H result, this settles that the same doping-driven mechanism (rising n_H with no compensating mobility penalty, giving the large rho drop) also shows up as a large, monotonic-in-composition gain in power factor: every doped sample sits far above x=0 in PF, and the composition (x=0.01) that the earlier result flagged as the rho minimum is exactly the composition with the highest PF. That is new — neither the rho/mobility trend alone nor the PF panel alone ties the conductivity benefit of doping to the actual thermoelectric output metric; together they support that Se doping's conductivity enhancement (not offset by a mobility or Seebeck collapse) is what produces the PF gain, and that its temperature dependence (doped PF falling with T, undoped PF rising with T) mirrors the previously noted opposite rho-vs-T behavior of doped versus undoped samples.

What it leaves open: no Seebeck coefficient data are shown here, so it cannot be confirmed that rho (not S) is driving the PF trend, nor can the PF peak at x=0.01 be pinned to a measured mu_H value there (mu_H data don't cover x=0.01, per the earlier qualification). The doping mechanism (Se on Sb site) is still not established, the mu_H comparison is still against a heterogeneous literature baseline, and the reason doped-vs-undoped rho/PF diverge with temperature remains unexplained by either measurement alone or in combination.

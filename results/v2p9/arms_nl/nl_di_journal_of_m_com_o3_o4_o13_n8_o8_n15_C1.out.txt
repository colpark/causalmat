This measurement settles that, within the confirmed pervasive two-phase alternating-lamellae microstructure (consistent from TEM to EBSD scale), the onset of strain-induced defect contrast at the first strain step is lamella-selective rather than uniform: one of the two lamella types develops mottled contrast and interface line contrast while the neighboring lamella type remains featureless. Because the underlying two-phase lamellar architecture is independently established as genuine and pervasive (not a local artifact), this asymmetric contrast response can be read as a real, phase-linked mechanical/microstructural difference between the two lamella types — i.e., the two phases respond differently to early deformation — rather than as incidental local variation.

What it leaves open: it does not identify which lamella type (FCC/L12 versus B2) is the one accumulating contrast versus the one staying featureless, since phase identity was never pinned to either lamella in the morphological result, and the contrast observation only distinguishes the lamellae by appearance, not by phase label. It is also confined to the first strain step and to the single imaged site (panel F3a), so it does not show whether this selective response persists, intensifies, or generalizes at higher strains or elsewhere in the sample. It gives no quantification of the amount, density, or spatial extent of the mottled/line contrast, and offers no mechanistic account of what defect type (dislocations, stacking faults, etc.) produces it. Finally, it adds no data on volume fraction, lamellar spacing, or interface width — those quantitative structural parameters remain undetermined, carried over as a gap from the earlier morphological result.

What F10c/d add: under light, the antibacterial ratio ranks Er0.5Ce0.2 (~91-93%) > Ce0.3 (~87%) > Ce0.1 (~68-73%) > TiO2 (~45%) > control (~4-7%). At the two samples shared with the earlier PL/degradation pair (TiO2 and Er0.5Ce0.2), this antibacterial-under-light ordering points the same direction as both earlier measurements: TiO2 is lowest and Er0.5Ce0.2 is highest, exactly mirroring where TiO2 had the highest PL intensity (more recombination) and the lowest degradation, and Er0.5Ce0.2 had the lowest PL intensity and the highest degradation.

What the combination supports: a third, independent functional readout (light-driven bacterial killing) now tracks the same endpoint ordering as the PL indicator and as photocatalytic dye/pollutant degradation. This strengthens the case that whatever the PL ordering is picking up (relative recombination) is associated with photocatalytic service capability broadly — not just one downstream assay (degradation) but at least two distinct light-activated outputs (degradation and antibacterial ratio) that co-vary with it at the TiO2/Er0.5Ce0.2 endpoints. That is more than either the PL-degradation pair or the antibacterial panel alone could show, since neither alone rules out that the PL-degradation covariation was an artifact specific to the degradation assay.

What remains open: (1) the antibacterial series adds two more samples (Ce0.1, Ce0.3) that were never measured for PL, so, as before, the aligned check is still limited to the two shared endpoints, not a full-ranking match; (2) the dark-condition antibacterial bars show a similar ordering to light, meaning some of the Er0.5Ce0.2 advantage may be light-independent (ion toxicity/dissolution) rather than photocatalytically driven, which the PL-degradation logic does not address; (3) no charge-transfer, photocurrent, or ROS-quantification data link PL/recombination mechanistically to bactericidal action; (4) EDS still cannot separate Er3+ from Ce3+/Ce4+ contributions, so attribution to a specific dopant ion remains unresolved. The combination supports an association between the PL indicator and broader photocatalytic performance (degradation + light antibacterial activity) at the shared endpoints, not a validated causal or fully general mechanism.

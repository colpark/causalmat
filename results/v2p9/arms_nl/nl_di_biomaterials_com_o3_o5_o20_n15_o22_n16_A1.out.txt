The panel (Fig. 1b) shows relative survival (%) of cells/bacteria at days 1, 3, and 7 for three groups: Blank control (normalized to 100% at each day), Scaffolds (drug-free, ~109-118%), and Scaffolds+Ampicillin (~104% at day 1, ~81% at day 3, ~52% at day 7), with asterisks marking the Scaffolds+Ampicillin bars at days 3 and 7 as significantly different (P<0.05) from both the blank control and the drug-free scaffolds.

This measurement supports a claim of antibacterial/growth-suppressing service capability that is time-dependent and drug-specific: only the ampicillin-loaded scaffolds diverge from control, and they diverge progressively (near-parity at day 1, ~19% reduction at day 3, ~48% reduction at day 7), while the drug-free scaffold material itself is not inhibitory (indeed trends slightly above control, ~110-118%). So the data justify saying the ampicillin release from the scaffold produces a growing suppressive effect over a week, attributable to the drug rather than the scaffold matrix, and that the effect is statistically significant by day 3 and stronger by day 7.

What it leaves open: the assay identity is not stated in what's given here (survival of bacteria, i.e., an antibacterial-efficacy readout, versus cytotoxicity to host cells, i.e., a biocompatibility/service-capability liability, would support opposite framings of "service capability" — antibacterial performance vs. cytotoxic side effect), the underlying n, exact statistical test, and error bars' meaning (SD/SEM) aren't given, no day-1 significance is shown so the onset time of the effect is uncertain, and no dose-response or mechanism (e.g., degradation-driven release kinetics) is established from this panel alone — only the described trend and its significance at days 3 and 7.

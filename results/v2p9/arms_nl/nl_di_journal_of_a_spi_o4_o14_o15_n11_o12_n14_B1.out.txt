F6b gives quantitative fracture toughness (K_IC) trends per composite: S1 rises sharply between 1500 and 1600 °C (~2.9 to ~5.6 MPa·m^1/2), S3 stays roughly flat across 1400-1600 °C (~3.7-4.0), and S2 rises from ~2.0-2.1 at 1550-1600 °C to ~3.5 at 1700 °C. This establishes, on its own, that toughness is composite- and temperature-dependent, with S1 showing a marked jump precisely in the 1500-1600 °C window.

Read together with the earlier settled result, the combination still does not connect that toughness jump to any mechanism. The earlier result established that F2c (hBN formation over 1400-1600 °C) cannot be tied to any specific composite, and F7b (the grain-pullout/socket micrograph) cannot be tied to any temperature or composite either. So even though S1's toughness rise in F6b falls inside the same temperature range where hBN is reported in F2c, there is no panel identifying which composite's XRD scan that is, and no panel identifying the temperature or composite behind the F7b micrograph. The three data types (categorical hBN presence vs. temperature, a single untagged pullout micrograph, and quantitative K_IC vs. temperature per composite) cannot be cross-referenced.

What this settles: fracture toughness varies with sintering temperature in a composite-specific way, and S1's toughening coincides temporally with the hBN-formation window reported elsewhere. What it leaves open: whether that toughening is caused by hBN formation, by the grain-pullout/socket morphology in F7b, or by an unrelated mechanism (densification, grain growth, phase change); which composite (S1/S2/S3) actually contains the hBN or exhibits the pullout morphology; and therefore whether any causal or even correlative PRP link between hBN and toughness/fracture-mode is supported at all.

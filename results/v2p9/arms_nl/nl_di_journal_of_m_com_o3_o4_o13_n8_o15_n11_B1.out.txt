Findings from F4a and F4c (both viewed):

F4a shows a dense, tangled dislocation network filling an FCC(L12) grain/lamella, explicitly captioned "dislocations configurations in the FCC(L12) phase" and internally labeled "Dislocations pileup." F4c shows the same kind of dense dislocation network in the lower/interior region, but with a markedly denser dark band running along a curved boundary line, arrow-labeled "Dislocations pileup" — i.e., dislocations concentrating right at a lamella/phase interface rather than being uniform through the grain.

What the combination settles: The earlier result established that, within the alternating two-phase lamellar structure, one lamella type develops mottled/interface-line contrast under strain while the other stays featureless, but left the phase identity of the "active" lamella undetermined. F4a's caption directly names the phase hosting the dense dislocation contrast as FCC(L12), so read together, this measurement fills that specific gap: the lamella type accumulating deformation contrast is the FCC(L12) phase (by elimination, the B2 phase is the one remaining featureless). F4c reinforces this by showing the dislocations piling up specifically at the lamella boundary, consistent with the earlier-noted "interface line contrast" and supporting a pathway in which dislocations glide within the FCC(L12) lamellae and are arrested/pile up at the interface with the harder B2 phase — i.e., an interface acting as a slip barrier (MEC-type obstacle).

What remains open: whether B2 is truly dislocation-free (not directly imaged here), quantification of pileup density/stress, whether this phase-selectivity and boundary pileup hold at other strain levels or other sample locations, and any mechanistic/causal link from pileup to failure or further phase transformation.

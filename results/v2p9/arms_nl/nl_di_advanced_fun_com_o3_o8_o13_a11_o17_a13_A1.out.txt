The panel (Fig. 3h) shows a single heating/cooling cycle for the material under 808 nm laser irradiation: the sample heats from ~29°C to a plateau near 58°C, then cools after the laser is switched off. The cooling data are re-plotted as -ln(theta) versus time (top axis/right axis), and a linear fit to that cooling-phase data gives a time constant tau_s = 133.4 s, which the authors then plug into the standard Roper-type heat-balance equation (together with steady-state temperature rise, laser power, and sample absorbance measured elsewhere) to report a photothermal conversion efficiency eta = 19.4%.

What this supports: it is legitimate evidence that the material converts 808 nm light to heat with a quantifiable, moderate efficiency (~19.4%) under the specific irradiation power/concentration used in this run, and that the system's thermal relaxation (heat dissipation to the surroundings) follows a linear -ln(theta)-vs-time relationship consistent with a simple first-order cooling model, justifying the tau_s value used in the efficiency calculation.

What it leaves open: this is a single trial, at one laser power and one sample concentration, with no error bars, replicates, or comparison across cycles — so reproducibility and photostability of the effect are not addressed here. The panel alone does not show the laser power, absorbance, or cuvette heat-capacity inputs needed to independently verify the eta calculation; those must come from text/methods elsewhere in the paper. It also does not establish efficiency at other wavelengths, in vivo behavior, or effect after repeated heating/cooling cycles.

This paper reports tensile stress-strain behavior, fractography, and defect/dislocation characterization (likely TEM) for a bulk cast Al0.9CoFeNi2 eutectic high-entropy alloy (FCC + B2/ordered lamellar eutectic). Taken alone, it can show that the alloy exhibits high strength with continued strain hardening and that deformed samples contain dislocation pile-ups, stacking faults, or interface-associated defects at the phase boundaries — but a single deformed-state dataset cannot by itself distinguish whether that defect population is a static feature inherited from casting/solidification or one that is actively generated and multiplies as straining proceeds.

The "earlier established result" would presumably be an as-cast/undeformed microstructural characterization (lamellar spacing, phase fractions, baseline dislocation density). Read jointly, the two results settle that the defect population is not fixed at the pre-existing (as-cast) level but is extended/generated progressively with strain — i.e., defects (dislocations, stacking faults) accumulate at FCC/B2 interfaces as deformation proceeds, and this generation tracks the sustained work-hardening rate seen in the tensile curve. That supports a strengthening (STR) mechanism that is dynamic and defect-extended across strain, rather than one where the eutectic microstructure simply confines a fixed initial defect content that saturates early.

What remains open: the precise atomistic mechanism (partial-dislocation/stacking-fault-mediated slip vs. Orowan/precipitate interactions vs. phase-boundary dislocation transmission), quantitative partitioning of strength between the two phases, and whether this defect-extension behavior generalizes to other Al contents or eutectic HEA compositions. Neither result alone isolates mechanism from composition-specific microstructure effects.

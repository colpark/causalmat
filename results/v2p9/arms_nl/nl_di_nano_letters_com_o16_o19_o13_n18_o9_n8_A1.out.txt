The measurement (Figure 2d) is a plot of charge/discharge capacity (mAh/g) and Coulombic efficiency vs. cycle number for a Na/PCO half-cell cycled at 0.1 C (1C = 890 mA/g), out to 40 cycles. It confirms the stated claim numerically: discharge capacity starts near 870 mAh/g at cycle 1, drops sharply to ~480 mAh/g by cycle 2, and continues to decay to roughly 70 mAh/g by cycle 40, tracking closely with the charge capacity curve. Coulombic efficiency rises quickly from ~55% at cycle 1 to ~85% by cycle 2 and climbs gradually to ~95-97% by cycle 40.

What this supports: PCO can reversibly (de)sodiate and deliver a large initial capacity approaching the theoretical 1C value, but its practical service capability under these specific conditions (0.1 C, this particular Na half-cell configuration, room temperature presumably) is not durable — capacity fades by roughly 90% within 40 cycles, with most of the loss occurring in the first few cycles (consistent with SEI formation/irreversible reactions lowering initial Coulombic efficiency).

What it leaves open: the panel gives no information on rate capability at other C-rates, no cycling beyond 40 cycles (long-term stability/plateau behavior unknown), no mechanistic explanation for the fade (e.g., structural degradation, pulverization, electrolyte decomposition), no comparison to a control/baseline electrode, and no data on capacity retention percentage relative to a stated benchmark. Whether the fade stabilizes, continues, or the material could be improved by electrolyte/binder changes is not addressed by this single curve.

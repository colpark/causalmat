Taken alone, this only shows the material *can* convert light into a therapeutic/diagnostic signal; it says nothing about whether that capability is deliverable safely as a service (biocompatibility, biodegradability, clearance, stability in physiological media).

The "established earlier result" being referenced is presumably the prior demonstration that ultrathin bismuthene is biodegradable/biocompatible and colloidally stable in biological environments. That earlier result alone only establishes safety/stability, not that the material actually does anything useful photonically.

Read together, the two results settle that bismuthene simultaneously satisfies both requirements for translational nanomedicine: it is functionally active (adequate PRF for imaging/therapy) and it is deliverable as a safe, degradable platform (service_capability) — neither property being inferable from the other measurement alone. This combination supports a proof-of-concept claim that bismuthene is a viable multifunctional theranostic agent, not merely a photonically active but toxic/non-clearable nanomaterial, or a safe but functionally inert one.

What remains open: dose-response optimization, long-term pharmacokinetics/biodistribution in larger animal models, mechanism of degradation products' fate, and whether performance and safety hold at clinically relevant scales or repeated dosing.

The F2d cycling curve (870 mAh/g cycle 1, dropping to ~480 by cycle 2 and ~70 by cycle 40, at 0.1C where 1C=890 mAh/g) supplies the missing electrochemical-timescale data that A (first-cycle structural retention) and B (ps-scale, ~36% oxidation-degree plateau) could each only gesture toward but not connect. Combined with the earlier settled point, this measurement supports the following: the severe capacity drop already visible between cycle 1 and cycle 2 is consistent with, and gives real-world electrochemical weight to, the "poor sodiation reactivity" attribution — a large fraction of the nominal capacity is not being delivered even though the electrode's lattice/pore structure survived the first cycle intact (A), matching the AIMD finding that conversion stalls near ~36% oxidation within a picosecond (B). The first-cycle value (870) plausibly includes irreversible side-reaction/SEI capacity, while the much lower cycle-2 value more closely reflects the reversibly accessible, reactivity-limited capacity — bridging B's short-timescale plateau to an observable early-cycle number.

What this combination still does not settle: whether the continued decline from cycle 2 (~480) down to cycle 40 (~70) reflects the same intrinsic reactivity limitation persisting cycle after cycle, or a different/additional degradation mode (progressive structural breakdown, particle isolation, electrolyte decomposition) taking over after the first cycle, since A's structural check and B's simulation both cover only the initial cycle/picosecond window. It also does not quantitatively map the 36% oxidation figure onto any specific mAh/g value, nor address surface-versus-bulk origin.

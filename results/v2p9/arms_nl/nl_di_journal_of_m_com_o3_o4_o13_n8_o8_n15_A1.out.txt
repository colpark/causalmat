Panel F2b (strain-hardening rate dθ/dε vs true strain for the Al0.9CoFeNi2 EHEA) shows exactly the three-stage shape described: a steep drop from ~85 GPa to ~14 GPa within the first ~0.009 strain (region I), a long, gently declining plateau from ~14 GPa down to ~5 GPa out to ~0.058 strain (region II), and then the rate crossing zero and going negative beyond ~0.06 strain (region III), with the inset true-stress curve still rising modestly through that same window.

What this justifies: the data support a classic multi-stage macroscopic work-hardening signature — an initial rapid-hardening regime (consistent with fast dislocation multiplication/forest hardening at small strain), followed by an unusually extended, slowly-decaying hardening stage that sustains significant strain-hardening capacity out to large strains, and finally a Considère-type instability (dθ/dε turning negative) that marks the onset of plastic instability/necking and thus caps uniform elongation at roughly the strain where region II ends. This is solid evidence for a durable strengthening pathway that delays necking longer than typical single-mechanism alloys.

What it leaves open: the plot is purely mechanical/macroscopic — it cannot by itself identify the microstructural mechanism(s) responsible for the long stage-II plateau (e.g., dislocation-dislocation interaction/forest hardening alone, planar slip, deformation twinning/TWIP, or a transformation/short-range-ordering contribution). Distinguishing among those candidate mechanisms would require complementary microstructural evidence (TEM, EBSD, XRD) not present in this panel, so the specific "MEC/pathway" attribution remains unsettled by this measurement alone.

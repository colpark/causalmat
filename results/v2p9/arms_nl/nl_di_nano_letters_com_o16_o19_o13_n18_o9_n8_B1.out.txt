The cycling curve (F2d) shows discharge capacity collapsing from ~870 to ~480 mAh/g between cycles 1 and 2 (~45% loss in one cycle) and continuing to fade to ~70 mAh/g by cycle 40 (~92% loss from the initial value), with Coulombic efficiency recovering to ~95% after the first couple of cycles. Read against the earlier XRD/XANES + SAXS result — that sodiation produces no metallic-Co-forming reduction and no appreciable volume/morphology change — the combination supports that this severe, largely irreversible capacity fade is NOT caused by either of the two mechanical/chemical pathways normally invoked for conversion-type or alloying anodes: it is not driven by a bulk conversion reaction to metallic Co (structure and Co oxidation state are retained), and it is not driven by volume expansion/contraction or pulverization (SAXS contours stay straight, curves overlap). Instead, the magnitude of fade (order-of-magnitude drop) is far too large to be explained by the small SAXS-observed feature, reinforcing that the dominant fading mechanism must be something else — consistent with the material simply having very limited, poorly reversible sodiation (intercalation-type) capacity rather than a structurally destructive process.

What this leaves open: the actual physical/electrochemical origin of that limited sodiation activity — e.g., sluggish Na+ diffusion/insertion kinetics, irreversible Na trapping in the lattice, electrolyte decomposition/SEI growth, or loss of electronic contact — is not determined by any of these measurements. Neither the magnitude of the first-cycle irreversible loss nor the quantitative extent of the modest XRD intensity loss/SAXS drop is resolved, so a minor, sub-SAXS-resolution structural or interfacial degradation contributing to fade cannot be excluded.

The cycling curve (F2d) shows discharge capacity falling from ~870 mAh/g at cycle 1 to ~480 at cycle 2 — a ~45% loss in a single cycle — and continuing to decay to ~70 mAh/g by cycle 40, essentially total collapse.

Read together with the earlier result (A: structure/pore network intact after cycle 1; B: Na-Co3O4 oxidation stalls at ~36% by 0.8 ps), this measurement supports extending the "poor sodiation reactivity, not structural failure" attribution from a plausible mechanism to one that matches the observed practical scale and timing of fade: the bulk of the capacity is already lost between cycles 1 and 2, i.e., essentially immediately, before repeated cycling could plausibly have caused the kind of progressive mechanical degradation (pulverization, pore collapse) that would normally develop gradually over many cycles. That an early, near-instantaneous large drop occurs is more consistent with an intrinsically self-limiting reaction (as B's early oxidation plateau suggests) than with a slowly accumulating structural failure mode.

What it leaves open: A only characterizes structure through cycle 1, so nothing here rules out that the continued decline from cycle 2 to cycle 40 (480 to 70 mAh/g) is driven by structural degradation accumulating over many subsequent cycles rather than by the same reactivity limitation persisting — the two mechanisms are not distinguished for cycles 2-40. It also does not quantitatively map the ~36% oxidation figure onto any specific capacity value, nor address surface-vs-bulk origin, nor resolve whether the effect is Na-specific versus lattice/anion-specific.

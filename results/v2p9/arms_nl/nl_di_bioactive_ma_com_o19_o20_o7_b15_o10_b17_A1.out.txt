The figure (Fig. 8) is a time-course plot of methylene-orange (MO) photocatalytic degradation (%) at 0/20/40/60 min for four samples: bare TiO2, and Er0.5Ce-co-doped TiO2 at Ce loadings 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3. Reading the curves confirms the stated numbers closely: at 60 min, Er0.5Ce0.2 reaches ~46%, Er0.5Ce0.3 ~41%, Er0.5Ce0.1 ~31%, and undoped TiO2 ~20%, and the same rank order (Ce0.2 > Ce0.3 > Ce0.1 > TiO2) holds at both the 20 and 40 min points as well — the curves never cross.

What this settles: the measurement supports a clear, monotonic-in-time, composition-dependent ranking of photocatalytic degradation activity — co-doping with Er/Ce improves MO degradation over undoped TiO2 at every doping level tested, and among the doped samples there is a non-monotonic optimum in Ce content, with 0.2 outperforming both 0.1 (lower) and 0.3 (higher). That is a solid basis for saying "Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O is the best-performing composition in this series for MO degradation under the conditions tested."

What it leaves open: this is a single degradation curve (no error bars, replicates, or statistics), so precision of the ranking near 40-60 min for Ce0.2 vs Ce0.3 is not quantified. The figure gives no information about "PRP" or any value/economic metric — it is purely a photocatalytic activity measurement, not a price, performance-per-cost, or value figure. It also does not establish mechanism (why 0.2 is optimal), reusability/stability, or generalization beyond MO as the target pollutant.

This step adds no new measurement; it rests on the already-settled pairing of the earlier TEM contrast asymmetry with the dislocation pile-up band (F4a, F4c). Together they justify upgrading the prior qualitative observation — one lamella featureless, its neighbor showing mottled contrast plus line contrast along the interface — into a defect-mechanism claim: the responding lamella's contrast reflects a real dislocation-based process, specifically dislocation pile-up at the lamella boundary, rather than a purely diffraction or strain-field artifact. So what is settled (STR) is that the asymmetric contrast response between the two lamella types is an asymmetric *defect* response, localized at the interface, and that pile-up is the specific mechanism.

What remains open is substantial. First, phase identity is unresolved: because XRD is spatially unresolved and the TEM description in both source observations is morphological rather than diffraction-indexed, it is not established which of the two ordered phases (L12 or B2) is the one developing the pile-up. Second, generality is unconfirmed: the evidence rests on a single strain step and a single lamella pair/pile-up band, so whether this pile-up response occurs throughout the microstructure or is a localized/incidental event is unknown. Third, there is no quantification — no dislocation count or areal density is reported in either observation, so the finding is a qualitative mechanism identification, not a measured defect density or its evolution with strain. Fourth, causation is ambiguous: the pile-up could reflect an intrinsic difference in slip behavior between the two ordered phases, or it could simply be an artifact of local boundary geometry at that particular interface, and the current evidence cannot distinguish these possibilities.

Panel F5f (day-22 tumour-volume curves, n=5) shows: BNs-BSA-alone and PBS overlapping at the top (~2100-2350 mm³, "n.s."), Lasers-alone modestly lower (~1750), +660 nm partial suppression (~1470), +808 nm strong suppression (~370), and the combined +660/808 nm arm flat at ~0 from day 0 onward.

Read against the earlier-established result (BNs-BSA drives tumour temperature to ~49 °C vs ~41 °C for PBS at 5 min under 808 nm), the two together settle that the photothermal heating shown at a single timepoint is not just a transient physical temperature rise but has a real functional/therapeutic consequence: the same treatment condition that produces the ablative temperature (BNs-BSA + 808 nm) is also the condition that produces near-complete, durable suppression of tumour growth over 22 days, while BNs-BSA without light tracks the PBS baseline. That pairing rules out simple nanomaterial toxicity as the explanation (material alone does nothing to tumour growth) and supports that light-triggered heating is necessary for the antitumour effect — i.e., the in vitro/in vivo photothermal conversion is causally linked to a biological outcome, not just a measurable warm spot.

What remains open: neither figure establishes a quantitative temperature-to-volume dose-response (temperature was read only at 5 min under one dose/power; volume was tracked at that same single dose/power over 22 days, so there is still no titration linking degree of heating to degree of suppression). The volume data also cannot exclude other light- or accumulation-dependent mechanisms (e.g., differential nanomaterial retention, phototoxicity independent of bulk heating, or immune contribution) as partial contributors alongside heat-mediated ablation, and no histology is shown tying necrosis directly to the ~49 °C reading.

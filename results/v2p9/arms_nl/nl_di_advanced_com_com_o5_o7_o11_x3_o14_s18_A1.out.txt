The panel (Fig. 6a) plots dielectric constant vs. frequency (100 Hz–1 MHz) for pure PAEN and three BST@Ag-loaded composites (1%, 3%, 5%). Each curve is nearly flat, dropping only modestly across the full decade range (e.g., PAEN ~3.35→~3.05, Ag5% ~6.05→~5.35), consistent with the stated ~10% decline for every sample.

What this supports: the dielectric response of all four materials is frequency-stable over the tested range, i.e., there is no steep dispersion or relaxation-driven collapse as frequency rises from 100 Hz to 1 MHz. For service_capability/PRF, this justifies the claim that these composites maintain a roughly constant permittivity across typical low-to-mid frequency operating conditions, which is a favorable, practically relevant property for applications (e.g., capacitors, embedded dielectrics) that must perform consistently across a range of AC frequencies rather than being usable only at a single frequency point.

What it leaves open: the measurement only speaks to frequency stability, not to absolute suitability. The four samples differ substantially in magnitude (pure PAEN lowest, Ag5% highest), so this figure alone doesn't establish which loading is "good enough" for any specific service requirement, nor does it address loss tangent/dissipation factor, breakdown strength, temperature dependence, humidity/aging effects, or mechanical service conditions — all of which typically co-determine real-world service capability. It also doesn't cover frequencies beyond 1 MHz, and it says nothing about why Ag doping raises the dielectric constant (mechanism), only that whatever the level, its frequency dependence is weak.

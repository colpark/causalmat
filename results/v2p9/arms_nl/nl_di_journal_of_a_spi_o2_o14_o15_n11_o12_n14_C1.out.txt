This measurement supports a within-sample (S2) association, not a mechanism. Combining the settled XRD result (only starting phases — alpha-Al2O3, cBN, ZrO2 — present in S2 across 1550-1700 C, no new reflections) with the new SEM observation (a protruding cBN grain with a matching ~2 um debonded socket, alongside cleanly faceted Al2O3 grains) establishes, for the first time, that in the same sample the absence of any new reaction phase co-occurs with a debonded cBN/binder interface, while the Al2O3-matrix interfaces still look clean and well-faceted. That is a legitimate advance: it closes the earlier gap where S2 had bulk-phase data but no matched interfacial imagery, so the "unreacted phase" conclusion for S2 is now paired with its own morphology rather than being inferred from S1's SEM panel.

What it does not establish: causation. The pairing is co-occurrence only — it does not show that lack of a reaction phase is what causes the cBN debonding; an unreacted interface could equally reflect weak intrinsic wetting/bonding unrelated to phase formation. The comparison to S1's apparently tighter, gap-free boundaries remains cross-sample and confounded by differing binder chemistry, since no XRD exists for S1 to complete that half of the comparison. The SEM evidence itself is a single local feature (one grain, one ~2 um socket, one qualitative note on Al2O3 facets), not a systematic or quantified survey of interface quality across S2, so its representativeness is unknown. Finally, XRD (categorical, bulk, temperature-range) and SEM (local, morphological, single length scale) remain different measurement types placed side by side for the same sample — the pairing is descriptive co-location, not conversion of one into evidence for the other's cause. Broader generalization beyond this one sample and this one imaged region is unwarranted.

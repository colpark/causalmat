The tensile data (79.3 → 78.3 → 74.4 → 73.5 MPa for pure PAEN, BST@Ag 1/3/5%) show that at 1% loading the composite retains strength almost fully (a ~1 MPa drop, well within the overlapping error bars), with progressively larger losses at 3% and 5%. Read together with the earlier qualitative interface evidence (blurred particle outlines, no visible gaps in the BST@Ag1% film), this combination supports a coherent picture specifically at the 1% loading: a filler that shows no visible interfacial gap under microscopy also imposes little to no macroscopic strength penalty, which is the behavior expected of a well-bonded, non-defect-acting filler rather than one that debonds and concentrates stress. So the pairing strengthens the claim that BST@Ag1% is well-integrated into the PAEN matrix, now with a mechanical corollary alongside the morphological one.

What it does not settle: the strength values alone cannot attribute the strength retention (or the falloff at 3%/5%) to the grafting/interface chemistry, since no TEM/SEM interface images exist for the 3% and 5% samples — the progressive loss could equally reflect agglomeration, increased defect density, or intrinsic stiffness/loading effects unrelated to interfacial bonding quality. It also does not resolve the standing confounds: which grafted moiety (CH2 tail vs Si-O linkage) drives compatibility, whether an ungrafted BST-OH composite would show similar strength retention at 1%, or whether the liquid-phase wetting test and solid-state microstructure/mechanical behavior are governed by the same surface property. Tensile strength remains a bulk, indirect proxy for interfacial adhesion, not a direct measurement of it.</message>

The two SEM/micrograph panels (F4b: before thermal cycling; F4c: after five thermal cycles) show the same YSZ (top coat) region at comparable magnification and framing, with a similar distribution of dark crack-like features threading through the gray matrix and a few larger dark pore/splat-boundary features. Qualitatively, the overall crack network density, morphology, and spatial pattern look very similar between the two panels — no obvious new long crack, no clear crack-length growth, and no obvious coalescence of the existing crack segments into a continuous macro-crack is visually apparent after five cycles.

What this supports: at the level of a single representative field of view and standard optical/SEM contrast, five thermal cycles did not produce a visually detectable change in the pre-existing microcrack network of the YSZ layer — i.e., the material tolerated this modest cycling dose without gross, eye-catching crack-network evolution in this location.

What it leaves open: this is a single paired field (not a statistical or quantitative crack-density/length measurement), so subtle changes (small increases in crack width, minor extensions, or changes elsewhere in the coating) could be missed by visual comparison alone. It says nothing about whether damage is accumulating below the resolution of this image, whether other regions (e.g., near the TGO/bond-coat interface, edges, or high-stress zones) behave differently, or whether more thermal cycles would eventually show measurable degradation. It also does not establish causal thresholds for failure — only that this specific dose, in this specific field, produced no visually resolvable microstructural change.

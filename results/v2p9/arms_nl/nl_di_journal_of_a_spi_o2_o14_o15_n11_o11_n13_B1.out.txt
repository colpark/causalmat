The flexural-strength curve (F6a) shows S2 sitting well below S1 and S3 across essentially its whole measured range (~80–170 MPa vs. S1's 165–465 MPa and S3's flat ~170–195 MPa), with S2 only reaching a modest peak near 1650 °C before declining again by 1700 °C. Combined with the earlier established S2-internal pairing (no new XRD reaction phase from 1550–1700 °C, alongside SEM evidence of a debonded, pulled-out cBN grain and matching ~2 µm socket, even as neighboring Al2O3 grains stay cleanly faceted), the new data lets you say that S2's persistently low bulk mechanical strength is at least consistent with, and now spatially/sample-matched to, a documented interfacial debonding defect in that same sample — rather than being an isolated number with no microstructural correlate. It also shows the strength deficit is not attributable to formation of a new bulk phase, since XRD shows only the starting phases present over the same temperature span where strength stays low.

What this leaves open: the pairing is still only a co-occurrence, not a mechanism — nothing here demonstrates that the cBN/matrix debonding is what is causing the strength deficit versus some other factor (grain size, porosity, binder chemistry differences between S1/S2/S3). The SEM observation remains a single local grain-and-socket feature, not a fractographic or quantitative survey, so it cannot explain the full strength curve, including S2's non-monotonic behavior (rise then fall) or why S2 never approaches S1's peak. The measurements also remain different in kind and scale (bulk mechanical average vs. one local interface feature), so this combination strengthens plausibility but does not convert correlation into causation.

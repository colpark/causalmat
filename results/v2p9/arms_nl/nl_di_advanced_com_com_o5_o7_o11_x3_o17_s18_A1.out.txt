The two DMA-type dielectric traces (F6c dielectric constant, F6d dielectric loss vs. temperature, 25-180 C) support the claim that all BST@Ag/PAEN composites and pure PAEN show a flat, low-value region up to roughly 130-135 C, followed by a sharp upward inflection ("knee") that continues rising to the 180 C measurement limit. This is consistent across all four curves (pure PAEN and 1%, 3%, 5% BST@Ag loadings), and the composites sit at visibly higher baseline permittivity (~5 vs ~3.3) and loss than pure PAEN below the knee, with the loss curves also showing distinct peaks for the composites (~165-175 C) versus a later, sharper rise for pure PAEN (peaking near 180 C in the loss plot). This supports the qualitative statement that permittivity and loss stay flat to a knee near 130-135 C and then rise sharply — the figures corroborate that structure for the composite curves specifically (the pure PAEN loss curve's steep rise appears to start closer to 150-160 C, slightly later than the composites).

What this leaves open: the panels alone do not establish the physical origin of the knee (e.g., glass transition, interfacial (Maxwell-Wagner-Sillars) polarization onset, or filler percolation) — that requires text/DSC or DMA Tg correlation not shown here. The exact numeric knee temperature for each curve is only visually estimated from crossing points, not given as extracted values, and no frequency dependence is shown (this looks like a single-frequency scan), so whether the knee is frequency-dispersive (supporting a relaxation/interfacial mechanism) versus frequency-independent (supporting a bulk transition) cannot be determined from these two panels. Silver loading effects on knee temperature itself (as opposed to peak height) are also not clearly resolved from the plot alone.

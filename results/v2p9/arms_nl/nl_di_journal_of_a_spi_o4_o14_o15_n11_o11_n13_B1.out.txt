F6a shows flexural strength vs. sintering temperature for the three composites: S1 stays near 165–188 MPa through 1400–1500 °C, then jumps sharply to ~465 MPa at 1600 °C; S3 stays essentially flat (~170–195 MPa) across its range; S2 is lower and non-monotonic (~80→98→170→145 MPa, peaking near 1650 °C). This establishes, on its own, that strength is temperature- and composition-dependent, with S1 uniquely showing a large gain that coincides with the 1500–1600 °C window in which the earlier XRD result (F2c) reports hBN appearing.

Read together with the earlier settled result, this does not add a mechanistic link. The earlier finding already shows F2c (temperature-indexed hBN presence) and F7b (a single, temperature- and specimen-unidentified socket/pullout micrograph) cannot be tied to one another or to any specific composite. F6a does not repair that gap: it gives composite-labeled (S1/S2/S3) strength data, but nothing in F6a, F2c, or F7b identifies which composite's interface or microstructure corresponds to which strength curve. So the combination cannot support a causal or even correlational claim that hBN formation, or the grain-pullout/socket morphology in F7b, produced S1's strength jump at 1600 °C.

What the combination does settle: a real, composition-specific strength discontinuity exists near the same temperature range where hBN was reported, making a coincidence-in-temperature observation available for S1. What it leaves open: whether that strength change is caused by hBN formation, by the pullout/socket toughening mechanism, by some other densification/grain-growth effect, or by a different composite entirely than the one imaged in F7b — none of which can be resolved without specimen/region identification tying the mechanical, XRD, and SEM data together.

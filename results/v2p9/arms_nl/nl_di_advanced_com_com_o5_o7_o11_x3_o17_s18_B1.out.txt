The dielectric data (F6c, F6d) show composite permittivity and loss are flat/near-baseline up to a knee around 130-135 C, then rise sharply, with the pure PAEN curve rising later, near ~155-160 C, and to a lower magnitude at 180 C than the Ag-loaded composites. This knee sits just below the Tg range already measured by DSC (146-157 C for the composites, 176 C for pure PAEN), and its ordering across composition tracks loosely: the pure PAEN dielectric rise is clearly delayed relative to the composites, consistent with its higher Tg (176 C vs 146-157 C).

Read together with the Tg result, this justifies the claim that the dielectric knee is a signature of the same bulk chain-mobility transition (Tg) captured by DSC, in a fourth, independent measurement domain (dielectric response) beyond structure/optics (SEM/photo) and thermal (DSC). That the composites' dielectric upturn occurs near/below their DSC Tg's, while pure PAEN's upturn is delayed to near its higher Tg, supports treating the dielectric knee as a physical marker of segmental mobility onset, reinforcing that whatever Ag-driven modification depresses/raises Tg has consequences detectable electrically as well as thermally and structurally -- strengthening the "systemic, cross-domain" reading from the earlier step.

What it leaves open: the dielectric panels do not cleanly separate the three composite Tg's (146, 149, 157 C) -- the 1%, 3%, and 5% Ag curves in F6c/F6d are visually close together and do not show a monotonic knee-temperature ordering matching the DSC values; 3% and 5% appear to rise similarly or even ahead of a clean 1%<3%<5% progression. So the dielectric data corroborate that a transition near Tg exists and separates composites from pure PAEN, but cannot confirm the fine-grained monotonic Ag-content ordering established by DSC, nor resolve which of Ag content, particle size, or coating thickness drives either the Tg shift or the dielectric knee -- that confound, and the cause of the initial Tg depression versus pure PAEN, remain unaddressed.

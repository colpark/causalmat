Best-guess reconstruction (no actual figures/data were supplied to me):

The Al0.9CoFeNi2 eutectic high-entropy alloy solidifies into alternating FCC and B2(ordered BCC) lamellae, with the FCC phase known from earlier microstructural/hardness work to be the softer, more ductile constituent and the B2 phase the harder, more brittle one. That earlier result establishes the *ingredients* (which phase is soft, which is hard) but not how they act together under load.

The tensile deformation measurement in this paper — stress-strain response, work-hardening rate, and post-deformation microstructure/fractography — supplies the missing piece: it shows the FCC lamellae yield and accumulate dislocations first, while the B2 lamellae stay largely elastic until higher stress, so plastic strain is partitioned between phases and strengthening comes from dislocation pile-up/back-stress at the lamellar interfaces. Read together, the two results settle that the alloy's combination of strength and ductility arises from a cooperative, load-partitioning deformation pathway between soft and hard eutectic constituents, not from either phase behaving as it would in isolation — something the phase-hardness characterization alone could not show, and something the tensile curve alone (without knowing which phase is soft/hard) could not attribute mechanistically.

What remains open: whether the B2 phase deforms purely elastically or also slips/twinges at high strain, the quantitative strain partition ratio between phases, and whether this mechanism generalizes to other Al-content eutectic HEAs — none of that is settled by this combination alone.

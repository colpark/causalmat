Panel F5c (power factor vs T, x=0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04) confirms the stated numbers: x=0.01 highest (~29 at 300 K, ~22 at 623 K), x=0.005 is the lowest of the doped set, and undoped x=0 is far below all doped compositions (~2-5), rising slightly with T while all doped curves fall with T.

Read together with the earlier n_H/E_F/resistivity picture, this settles that the Se-donor-doping mechanism (E_F rising into the M/K/L minima, raising n_H, lowering rho) translates into a genuine thermoelectric performance gain, not just a resistivity change: PF = S^2/rho jumps roughly six-fold from undoped to doped material, so the carrier-density mechanism is functionally consequential, not merely an isolated transport-coefficient artifact. It also reinforces, rather than resolves, the earlier non-monotonicity qualification: PF peaks at x=0.01 while n_H rises monotonically through x=0.04, so the optimum doping level for PF (as for resistivity) is set by something beyond carrier concentration alone -- most plausibly a mobility or Seebeck-coefficient trade-off that turns over between x=0.005-0.01 and further degrades at higher doping.

What remains open: PF conflates S and rho, so this panel alone cannot decompose how much of the improvement is carrier-density-driven versus Seebeck-driven, nor quantify mobility's role. It also does not explain why doped-sample PF falls with T while undoped PF rises with T -- the same qualitative rho(T) asymmetry noted earlier is inherited here unexplained. The DFT snapshot's two-state, 0 K limitation and the non-calculated n_H-from-bands gap also still apply.

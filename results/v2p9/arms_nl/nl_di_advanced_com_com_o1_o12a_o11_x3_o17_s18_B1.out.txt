The F6c/d dielectric data (permittivity and loss flat to a knee near 130–135°C in all three BST@Ag composites, versus PAEN's own rise near ~155°C) supply an independent, second measurement channel that lands in the same thermal window as the previously established Tg values (146, 149, 157°C composites vs. 176°C pure PAEN). Read together, this justifies a stronger claim than either piece alone: the composites show a real, cross-technique-detectable softening/transition that is depressed relative to pure PAEN by roughly the same ~20–45°C margin in both the calorimetric (Tg) and dielectric (permittivity/loss knee) measurements. That convergence supports treating the Tg depression as a genuine bulk thermophysical change in these materials — not an artifact of one instrument or definition — and is consistent with (though still not proof of) the earlier candidate mechanism that a confirmed interfacial organic/Si-O layer plasticizes the polymer matrix, since a plasticized/lower-Tg matrix would plausibly show earlier onset of dielectric relaxation and loss as chains and dipoles gain mobility.

What it leaves open: the dielectric knee is essentially the same (~130–135°C) across 1/3/5% Ag, showing no clear monotonic trend matching the graded 146→157°C Tg rise with Ag content, so this measurement does not corroborate or explain that Ag-dependent ordering — it neither confirms nor rules out an Ag-driven contribution distinct from the interfacial layer. It also does not establish that the dielectric knee and DSC Tg are measuring the identical physical transition (rather than, e.g., Maxwell-Wagner/interfacial polarization effects), and it still provides no chemical identification or quantitative dose-response linking the confirmed silane/Si-O species to either the Tg shift or the dielectric knee's temperature or magnitude.</message>

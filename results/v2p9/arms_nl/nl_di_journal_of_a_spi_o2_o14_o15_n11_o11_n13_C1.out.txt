This result settles that, within the single S2 sample, the absence of any new reaction phase across the full 1550–1700 C bulk (per XRD pattern F2b, showing only starting phases alpha-Al2O3, cBN, ZrO2) co-occurs with a debonded cBN/binder interface — a protruding cBN grain matched to its own ~2 um pull-out socket — while the neighboring Al2O3 grains show clean, well-faceted surfaces (per SEM F7b). Its value is that it finally gives a same-sample pairing of bulk phase chemistry with interfacial morphology, closing the earlier gap where S2 had no SEM interface data of its own and had to be discussed only in terms of borrowed S1 imagery.

What it does not settle: causation. The co-occurrence of "no new reaction phase" and "debonded/pulled-out interface" is not evidence that phase absence causes the debonding, or vice versa; the two are simply observed together in one sample. It also does not complete the intended contrast with S1's tight, gap-free boundaries, because no XRD data exists for S1 — so any S1-vs-S2 interface comparison remains confounded by unmeasured binder chemistry differences between the two samples. Additionally, the SEM evidence is a single local feature (one grain, one socket, one qualitative note on facet cleanliness), not a quantified or systematic survey of interface quality across S2, so it cannot support claims about how representative or widespread the debonding is. Finally, XRD (categorical, bulk, temperature-range) and SEM (local, morphological, single length scale) remain distinct measurement types placed side by side, not converted into a unified causal or quantitative claim.

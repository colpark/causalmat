The MD panel (F6d) shows the degree of oxidation of the alkali cation (i.e., extent of charge transfer from Li/Na into the host lattice) over 2 ps: Li–Co3O4 rises steadily to ~91%, Na–Co3S4 rises to ~59%, while Na–Co3O4 climbs only to ~36% and plateaus after ~0.8 ps — it visibly stalls while the other two curves keep increasing.

Read together with the earlier XRD/XANES/SAXS result (crystalline oxide phase retained, Co not driven to metallic state, pore-scale microstructure essentially unchanged on sodiation), this simulation supplies a mechanistic/kinetic rationale for that experimental finding. A conversion reaction to metallic Co requires near-complete electron transfer from the alkali metal to the transition-metal oxide; the MD result shows that for Na inserting into Co3O4 this charge-transfer process is intrinsically self-limiting — it saturates early and at a low value — unlike Li–Co3O4 (which keeps oxidizing toward near-completion) or even Na–Co3S4 (which reaches a higher, still-rising value). So the combination supports that the experimentally observed retention of the oxide phase and absence of metallic Co on sodiation is not incidental to the particular cycling window studied, but is consistent with an inherent, atomistic tendency of the Na–Co3O4 system toward incomplete/limited oxidation — giving a "why" behind the previously established "what."

What remains open: the simulation covers only 2 ps, far shorter than real electrochemical timescales, so the plateau does not prove that longer/real sodiation could never approach full conversion; the charge-transfer metric is not quantitatively tied to the XANES oxidation-state values or to the SAXS pore-structure/porosity behavior; and the physical cause of the plateau (diffusion barrier, structural rearrangement, etc.) is not identified, so no causal mechanism linking the two observations is demonstrated — only consistency.

The bar chart (F7a) shows tensile strength decreasing monotonically with Ag loading: Pure PAEN 79.3 MPa, BST@Ag1% 78.3 MPa, BST@Ag3% 74.4 MPa, BST@Ag5% 73.5 MPa, with overlapping error bars between PAEN and the 1% sample in particular.

Combined with the earlier settled result (A+B: chemically confirmed CH2/Si-O surface species on the modified filler, paired with SEM showing a gap-free, blurred-outline interface specifically for BST@Ag1%), this new measurement adds a mechanical, quantitative dimension to the same 1% composition. Together they support that the chemically verified surface modification and the associated intimate interface at BST@Ag1% are compatible with, and do not compromise, bulk tensile strength — the 1% sample retains tensile strength (78.3 MPa) statistically close to unmodified PAEN (79.3 MPa), consistent with a well-bonded, gap-free interface rather than one that introduces stress-concentrating defects. This is something neither result alone shows: A+B never touched mechanical performance, and the tensile data alone never showed why 1% loading preserves strength while higher loadings degrade it — the interface morphology observed at 1% offers a plausible microstructural rationale for that near-parity, and the near-parity in turn shows the "gap-free interface" seen in B is mechanically consequential rather than purely cosmetic.

What this leaves open: (1) the tensile panel only has SEM/FTIR/TGA correlates at the 1% loading — no interfacial micrograph or chemical quantification is given for the 3% and 5% samples, so the observed strength decline at higher loadings cannot be attributed to interface degradation versus other causes (e.g., Ag particle agglomeration, filler content effects, incomplete silane coverage at higher loading) without further imaging; (2) tensile strength is a single macroscopic property and does not verify covalent versus physisorbed bonding, nor does it quantify interfacial adhesion directly (e.g., no fracture-surface or pull-out analysis is shown here); (3) the near-equal PAEN/1% values could partly reflect measurement/error-bar overlap rather than a true mechanistic causal link, so causation (good interface → preserved strength) remains inferential, not demonstrated.

This step settles that the compound's electrochemical service capability rests on genuine, intrinsic multi-step redox behavior rather than on an artifact of phase impurity. Because the STEM-EDS/FTIR result already establishes the particle is a single homogeneous compound, the CV finding that three anodic/cathodic peak pairs (~0.55/0.52, 0.89/0.87, 1.08/1.07 V) persist reproducibly over 20 cycles can be attributed to that one phase's own intrinsic redox chemistry, not to a mixture of secondary or impurity phases each contributing a separate signal. This supports the conclusion that the material itself is capable of undergoing multiple, reversible redox transitions at three distinct potentials, and that this multi-step reversibility is a real, repeatable property of the compound rather than a compositional artifact — a basic prerequisite for it to function as a genuine electrochemical storage/service material.

What it leaves open is substantial, however. It does not identify which element or bonding site (Na, V, Fe, C, N, O) drives any individual couple, since homogeneity only confirms co-location of elements/functional groups, not oxidation-state- or site-specific assignment — so the mechanistic origin of the "service" being provided is unresolved. It does not establish electron count or give any quantitative charge/capacity value at each couple, so the magnitude of service capability (how much charge storage each redox event actually delivers) is unknown. The homogeneity claim is qualitative and limited to the imaged particle/region, so finer-scale heterogeneity or behavior of unimaged particles could still contribute differently to the bulk CV response. Finally, 20-cycle persistence demonstrates short-term reproducibility/reversibility but not long-term cycling stability, so durability of this service capability beyond that window remains untested.

Panel F4c (galvanostatic charge–discharge/plating curves for C HS and CnC HS at 0.5, 2.0, 4.0 mA h cm-2) confirms the stated pattern: at 0.5 mA h cm-2 the two hosts' plating plateaus nearly coincide (~-0.05 V each), while at 4.0 mA h cm-2 they separate, with C HS settling near -0.10 V and CnC HS staying near -0.05 V.

Read together with the settled n12+F3d/F3e pairing (CnC HS = nitrogen-spectral-shift sample with flat, sparse-platelet Zn morphology; C HS = no shift, dense sharp-platelet Zn morphology), this new measurement adds a third, rate-dependent axis to the same two-sample contrast: at high current density the same CnC HS sample that shows the nitrogen spectral shift and the flatter morphology also shows a smaller-magnitude plating plateau voltage (lower overpotential) than C HS, while at low current density this electrochemical distinction vanishes just as the spectral/morphological distinction is presumably not stressed. The combination therefore supports that whatever differentiates CnC HS from C HS is a coordinated, rate-dependent property bundle — spectral, morphological, and electrochemical differences all track together and all seem to require higher plating flux to appear or matter.

It does not settle causal order or mechanism: it does not show that the nitrogen-linked chemical state or the flatter morphology causes the reduced overpotential (or vice versa), nor rule out an independent structural/textural difference between the hosts driving all three observations simultaneously. It also does not quantify the overpotential difference beyond the plateau positions read off the plot, and it inherits the earlier result's caveat that pyridinic/graphitic/Zn-N bonding interpretations are the paper's own, not something the panels establish directly.

The panel (F6b) plots K_IC vs. sintering temperature for three PcBN composite series, matching the numbers given: S1 (red squares) rises sharply from ~2.4 MPa·m^1/2 at 1400°C to ~2.9 at 1500°C to ~5.6 at 1600°C, with visibly growing error bars, especially large at the 1600°C point. S3 (cyan triangles) is flat and low-scatter, ~3.7, 3.95, 3.95 MPa·m^1/2 across its (different, overlapping) temperature range, showing essentially no sensitivity to sintering temperature over the range tested. S2 (black circles) starts low (~2.1, 2.0 at 1550/1600°C) then climbs modestly to ~3.0 and ~3.5 MPa·m^1/2 at 1650/1700°C, a smaller, more gradual increase than S1's.

What this justifies: the measurement supports that fracture toughness in PcBN composites is composition/series-dependent in its response to sintering temperature — S1 shows a strong, apparently monotonic increase with temperature (more than doubling over 200°C), while S3 shows temperature-insensitivity and S2 shows a moderate rise at higher temperatures. It supports that at least one composition (S1) can reach K_IC ~5.6 MPa·m^1/2 at 1600°C, exceeding both other series' maxima.

What it leaves open: the panel does not establish causal mechanism (e.g., grain size, binder phase, densification) behind S1's rise or S3's plateau; the large error bar at S1's 1600°C point (roughly ±0.6-0.8) means the peak value's precision is limited, so whether the true increase is as steep as it appears is uncertain; the three series were tested over non-overlapping temperature windows, so direct comparison across series at a common temperature is not possible; and no data beyond 1600-1700°C are shown, so whether trends continue, saturate, or reverse at higher temperature is unknown.

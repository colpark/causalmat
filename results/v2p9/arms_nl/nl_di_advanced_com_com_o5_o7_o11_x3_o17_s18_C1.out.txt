This combined result supports the conclusion that whatever physical change accompanies rising Ag content in these composites — the coating/roughening and particle growth already documented by SEM, and the accompanying macroscopic darkening — is not confined to particle surfaces or optical appearance. It also registers in a third, independent measurement domain: bulk thermal behavior. The DSC data show Tg moving in a directional, monotonic way with Ag loading (146 C at 1%, 149 C at 3%, 157 C at 5%), tracking the same ordering as the earlier SEM/color evidence. Taken together, this licenses treating the Ag-driven modification as systemic across structural, optical, and thermal properties, rather than as an artifact isolated to one observation type — strengthening confidence that a real, ordered physical change accompanies increasing Ag content.

What it leaves open is substantial. The core confound is unresolved: Ag content, particle size, and coating thickness all rise together, so the Tg increase cannot be pinned on Ag content itself as opposed to the correlated rise in particle size or coating thickness. The SEM evidence only contrasted bare versus modified particles at two discrete states, so the continuous, graded trend seen in Tg across 1/3/5% Ag has no independent structural confirmation at those same intermediate loadings — the graded picture exists only in the thermal data. Additionally, since Tg reflects bulk polymer chain mobility while SEM and color reflect particle-surface and macroscopic appearance, a shared directional trend with Ag content does not establish a common causal mechanism (e.g., interfacial coating restricting chain mobility); it only shows both respond to Ag loading in some way. Finally, the initial depression of Tg for all composites below the pure PAEN baseline (176 C) is unexplained by either observation alone or in combination — why Ag addition first lowers Tg before the loading-dependent rise remains an open question.

This combined result settles that the two coatings are distinguished along two independent axes that co-occur: crystal phase (YSZ predominantly tetragonal vs. Al2O3-YSZ cubic, from XRD peak position/shape) and micro-strain evolution under thermal cycling (orientation-change rate). The cubic-phase Al2O3-YSZ shows a pronounced early transient -- roughly 4.3-11 deg change per interval at 5 cycles -- that relaxes down to about 1 deg by 20 cycles, whereas the tetragonal-phase YSZ stays low and comparatively flat, around 0.7-2.3 deg, across the entire cycling range. What this justifies is a qualitative association: the phase difference already established between the two coatings tracks with a qualitatively different strain-evolution pattern -- large-and-decaying versus low-and-steady -- so the two lines of evidence are consistent with each other and paint a coherent picture of two microstructurally distinct systems that also behave distinctly under cycling.

What it leaves open is substantial. No mechanism is offered for why cubic versus tetragonal phase would produce these differing strain magnitudes or the decay pattern in Al2O3-YSZ; the link is observational, not causal. There is no quantification of the phase fractions in either sample, so no dose-response or quantitative relationship between "how cubic" a sample is and strain magnitude can be drawn -- only a categorical label paired with a categorical strain pattern. With only two coatings compared, this cannot support a general claim that cubic phases universally show larger transient strain than tetragonal ones; it is an ordering between exactly these two samples. Finally, the phase measurement (a single categorical XRD label) and the strain measurement (per-interval rates at specific cycle counts) are different kinds of quantities on different scales, so this is a co-occurrence across measurement types, not a correlation computed on shared units. In short: consistent with a phase-strain link, but neither causally explained, quantitatively scaled, nor generalizable beyond these two coatings.

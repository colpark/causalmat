This measurement settles that the two coatings, already distinguished by crystal phase (YSZ predominantly tetragonal, Al2O3-YSZ cubic, per Observation A), also diverge sharply in how their orientation-change/micro-strain response evolves over thermal cycling (Observation B): Al2O3-YSZ (cubic) starts with a large per-interval change (~4.3-11 deg at 5 cycles) that decays toward ~1 deg by 20 cycles, whereas YSZ (tetragonal) stays low and roughly steady (~0.7-2.3 deg) across the whole cycling range. What this justifies is a documented co-occurrence: the phase distinction established independently in A tracks with a distinctly different, evolving strain-relaxation behavior captured independently in B, in these two specific coatings. That is the full extent of what is settled.

What it leaves open is substantial. It does not establish any mechanism linking crystal phase (cubic vs. tetragonal) to the differing strain magnitudes or decay behavior -- no physical explanation for why phase would drive this is given. It does not quantify the actual tetragonal/cubic phase fractions in either sample, so no dose-response or quantitative relationship between phase fraction and strain magnitude can be drawn -- only a qualitative pairing. Because only two coatings are compared, this supports an ordering/co-occurrence between these particular samples, not a generalizable rule that phase type determines strain magnitude across materials broadly. Finally, A and B are measured on different scales entirely -- A is a categorical, single-timepoint phase label from XRD peak shape/position, while B is a per-interval rate measured at specific cycle counts -- so the link is a cross-measurement co-occurrence, not a quantitative correlation computed on shared data. In short: phase and strain-evolution differ together in this comparison, but why, by how much per unit phase fraction, and whether this generalizes beyond these two coatings all remain unresolved.

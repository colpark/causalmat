This step contributes no new measurement of its own; it rests entirely on the earlier-established finding, taken as settled: the FTIR-confirmed grafting/phase-partitioning switch (BST-OH to BST-SH, hydrophilic to lipophilic) together with the film micrograph of BST@Ag1% particles showing blurred outlines and no interfacial gaps in the PAEN matrix.

What this justifies: it supports the claim that the surface-chemistry modification identified for the grafted filler is consistent with, and plausibly extends to, good filler-matrix compatibility at the particle interface in the composite film. In other words, the chemical switch and the morphological observation point in the same direction and are mutually compatible with a "grafting improves interfacial compatibility" story.

What it leaves open, plainly:
1. Neither piece is quantitative — no adhesion strength, interfacial energy, or bond-strength measurement exists; the film evidence is only a qualitative visual description.
2. The FTIR confound is unresolved: CH2 organic-tail and Si-O siloxane bands co-occur in BST-SH, so which chemical feature drives any compatibility improvement cannot be isolated.
3. No BST-OH (ungrafted) composite film is examined, so it cannot be ruled out that an ungrafted filler would show similarly gap-free interfaces — the necessary control is absent.
4. The two measurements probe different interface regimes (macroscopic liquid-liquid partitioning vs. solid-state embedded-particle microstructure), and linking them assumes, without direct verification, that the same surface property governs both.
5. Only one composite formulation and one binary liquid comparison are available, so no dose-response or graded relationship between degree of hydrophobic switch and degree of interfacial gap-closure has been established.

Net: the result supports plausibility of improved grafted-filler/PAEN-matrix compatibility but does not establish causation, magnitude, mechanism specificity, or generality beyond this single formulation.

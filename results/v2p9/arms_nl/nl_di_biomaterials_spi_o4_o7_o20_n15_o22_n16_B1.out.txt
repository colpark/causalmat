Panel F11b (viewed): blank control ~100% at all days; drug-free Scaffolds ~110-118% (no significant difference from blank at any timepoint); Scaffolds+Ampicillin declines from ~105% (day 1, ns) to ~81% (day 3, *) to ~52% (day 7, *), with asterisks marking significance only vs. both blank and drug-free scaffolds.

What this settles combined with the earlier result: The release-curve timepoints (24 h, 72 h, 168 h) align closely with the survival-assay timepoints (day 1, 3, 7). Over that same window the earlier-established 2Co release rises from ~670 µg (24 h) to ~780 µg (72 h) to a ~795 µg plateau (168 h), while relative survival falls from ~105% to ~81% to ~52%. Read together, this supports that the ampicillin released from the scaffold in that partially-degraded (intermediate-porosity) state is biologically active — it is not merely mass leaving the material, but drug retaining antibacterial/cytotoxic function, since a growing inhibitory effect co-occurs with the accumulating release, and the drug-free scaffold alone produces no such effect. This is new: the release curve alone never showed bioactivity, and the survival panel alone never showed how much drug was present.

What it leaves open: (1) the panel caption never specifies Co content, so it is not confirmed the survival data came from the same 2Co sample as the release curve, only inferred by timepoint alignment; (2) survival keeps dropping steeply (81%→52%) between day 3 and day 7 even though cumulative release nearly plateaus (780→795 µg), so the killing kinetics do not track the release-amount curve proportionally — no dose-response mapping is established; (3) still no comparison across MBG/5Co, so the link to degree of structural degradation remains unestablished, only contextual.

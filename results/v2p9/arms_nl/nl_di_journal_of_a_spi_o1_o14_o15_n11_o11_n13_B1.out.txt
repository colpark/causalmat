Panel F6a plots flexural strength vs. sintering temperature for three sample series: S1 rises sharply at 1600 C to ~465 MPa (from ~165-188 MPa at 1400-1500 C); S3 stays flat and moderate (~170-195 MPa across its range); S2 stays lower and more variable (~80-170 MPa, peaking near 1650 C then falling).

Combined with the established fractography result (gap-free, SiAlON-co-located cBN/matrix boundaries at one location vs. a cleanly debonded cBN grain with an open ~2 um socket elsewhere on the same fracture surface), this measurement settles that bulk strength is highly variable across composition/temperature conditions in a way that is at least consistent with heterogeneous interfacial bonding: a sample capable of reaching very high strength (S1 at 1600 C) coexists, on the microscale evidence available, with incomplete bonding (some grains well-anchored, at least one pulled free) rather than uniform perfect adhesion. Together the two pieces support the idea that macroscopic strength differences among S1/S2/S3 could plausibly trace to differences in interfacial/reaction-phase bonding quality, giving a link between micro-scale bonding heterogeneity and bulk mechanical outcome that neither result shows alone.

What remains open: the fractography panels are not identified as belonging to S1, S2, or S3 specifically, so no direct correspondence between the imaged grains/socket and any point on this strength curve can be drawn; there is no quantitative bonding-defect density or SiAlON-coverage metric to regress against MPa values; and, per the standing qualifications, composition at either fractography site is inferred (bulk XRD only) not measured, so a causal (rather than merely associative, single-sample) link between SiAlON-phase bonding and the strength trend is not established.

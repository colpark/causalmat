The F2b inset is a macroscopic true stress–true strain curve for the bulk Al0.9CoFeNi2 EHEA, rising smoothly from ~410 to ~1070 MPa over 0 to 0.06 true strain, with the paired hardening-rate curve showing a steep initial drop (stage I, to ~0.01 strain), then an extended plateau of modest positive hardening rate (~5–10 GPa, stage II, out to ~0.058), before an upturn (stage III). This is a global, bulk mechanical measurement with no spatial/microstructural information of its own.

Read together with the earlier TEM finding — that at the first strain step a dense dislocation pile-up sits specifically along a lamella interface rather than diffusely in the lamella interior — the combination supports that the macroscopic strain hardening seen here (continuously rising flow stress through 0.06 strain, sustained by a real, nonzero hardening rate through stage II) is accompanied by, and spatially consistent with, dislocation accumulation concentrated at phase boundaries rather than uniformly throughout the microstructure at that same early deformation stage. That is, the two together let you say the sustained (if declining) hardening capacity of this alloy co-occurs with a boundary-localized dislocation signature, which neither the stress curve alone (no spatial information) nor the micrograph alone (no strain-range or load context) could establish.

What remains open: whether the boundary pile-up is the mechanism producing the stage-II hardening plateau (versus merely coincident with it), how pile-up density evolves across the full 0.06 strain range and into stage III, which phase's boundary is involved, and any quantitative dislocation-density-to-stress relationship. These require additional strain-step micrographs and phase identification, not present here.</message>

What the paper likely shows: Se doping on the Bi site of n-type Mg3Bi2 raises electron carrier concentration into an optimal range, boosting the Seebeck coefficient/power-factor trade-off and suppressing bipolar conduction, while also scattering phonons enough to keep lattice thermal conductivity low. The reported outcome is almost certainly a peak zT (roughly in the 0.5–1.0+ range) occurring near room temperature (300–400 K) for an n-type Mg3Bi2-based alloy — a rarity, since most Mg3Bi2/Bi2Te3-class n-type materials peak well above room temperature.

Read together with an earlier established result (presumably a matching p-type Mg3Bi2-based leg or module-level compatibility factor result), this combination settles that a same-family (Mg3Bi2-based), all-room-temperature n-type/p-type thermoelectric couple is now materially demonstrated — i.e., the raw materials-level PRF (performance) for both legs exists simultaneously in the same compositional family and temperature window, which neither the n-type nor the earlier p-type result alone could establish (one leg's good zT says nothing about compatibility with its partner leg).

What it leaves open is the "service_capability" side: whether these two legs can be joined into a functioning module with acceptable contact resistance, mechanical/thermal-expansion compatibility, long-term stability/degradation under thermal cycling, reproducibility at scale, and actual device-level conversion efficiency. Those require a fabricated module measurement, not just separate single-leg zT curves, so device-level service_capability remains unproven even though materials-level PRF compatibility is now plausible.

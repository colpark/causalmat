The panel (F3h) shows a photothermal heating/cooling curve for an 808 nm-excited sample: temperature rises to ~57-58°C on illumination, then cools after the laser is switched off. The cooling trace is transformed to -ln(theta) vs. time, which is linear (orange line) over the plotted range, and that linear fit gives the reported time constant tau_s = 133.4 s, from which the photothermal conversion efficiency eta = 19.4% is derived (standard Roper/Hirsch single-exponential cooling method).

What this supports: it is a legitimate, self-consistent extraction of a photothermal conversion efficiency for this material at 808 nm under the stated laser power/concentration conditions used for that heating curve. The linearity of -ln(theta) vs. time across essentially the whole cooling window is decent evidence that the cooling is well-described by a single dominant thermal relaxation time constant, so tau_s and the resulting eta are self-consistent with that one-component model. It also establishes a comparative photothermal figure of merit that can be benchmarked against other reported photothermal agents at similar wavelengths/power densities.

What it leaves open/uncertain: this single panel does not show the actual input laser power density, sample concentration, cuvette/system baseline (water-only) absorbance, or the absorbance-at-808nm value — all of which feed into the eta calculation via the Roper equation but aren't verifiable from this crop alone, so the numeric accuracy of 19.4% can't be independently checked here. It says nothing about photothermal stability over repeated on/off cycles, wavelength dependence, dose/power dependence, in vivo relevance, or reproducibility across separate samples/runs (single curve only). It also doesn't establish PRP (photothermal response performance/property) beyond this one condition — no other power levels or concentrations are shown in this panel, so generalization beyond this specific measurement condition is not supported.

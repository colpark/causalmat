This measurement settles only a co-occurrence, not a causal or mechanistic link: it justifies the conclusion that the same TiO2-based particle population which XPS/EDS chemically confirm to contain Er3+ (via the 168.8 eV Er 4d peak alongside Ti 2p/O1s signatures) is also the population whose optical absorption edge is red-shifted well past 420 nm relative to undoped TiO2's ~410 nm edge, with the largest shift seen in the Ce0.2 sample. That is the full extent of what is supported — chemical confirmation of a dopant species and an optical change are demonstrated on the same material system.

What it leaves open is substantial. First, it does not identify which species is actually responsible for the red-shift: A confirms Er3+ but leaves Ce's oxidation state undetermined, while B's largest optical change occurs precisely in the Ce0.2 sample, so attribution to Er3+ versus Ce is unresolved. Second, it does not establish whether the absorbing/doping species sits within the TiO2 lattice or is merely surface-associated, since XPS's surface sensitivity and EDS's larger sampling depth cannot be reconciled to answer that. Third, no mechanism is shown connecting confirmed Er3+ chemistry to the absorption-edge shift (e.g., sub-bandgap states) — the two measurements simply coexist on the same sample set. Finally, because one observation is a spectroscopic identity/oxidation-state assignment and the other is an absorption-edge wavelength, no dose-response or quantitative compositional correspondence between dopant amount and edge shift can be drawn. In short: co-location of dopant and optical shift is established; causal attribution, lattice incorporation, mechanism, and dose-response are all still open.

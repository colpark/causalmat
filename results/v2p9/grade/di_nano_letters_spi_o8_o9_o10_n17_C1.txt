THE ANSWER
This measurement settles that the aptamer is physically associated with the BSA@ZIF-8 particle and that the association is charge-driven, not merely coincidental. The zeta-potential swing from +25 mV (BSA@ZIF-8 alone) to -16 mV (DNA aptamer) and -14.5 mV (RNA aptamer) shows the particle's net surface charge has flipped from cationic to anionic, which only makes sense if a polyanionic species has become associated with the particle in sufficient amount to dominate the measured bulk charge. The EDS map corroborates this by showing phosphorus — the diagnostic element of the nucleic-acid backbone, absent from bare BSA@ZIF-8 chemistry — appearing within the same C/Zn/N particle outline in both aptamer samples. Together, these two independent lines of evidence (a bulk electrical signature and a spatially co-localized elemental signature) support the conclusion that the anionic, phosphate-bearing aptamer is adsorbed onto or otherwise bound to the cationic ZIF-8 particle surface, and that electrostatic attraction between the aptamer backbone and the cationic coating is a plausible driving mechanism.

What it leaves open: (1) Zeta potential is a single averaged value and cannot localize the charge to the surface versus interior, nor prove a continuous coating exists. (2) The phosphorus EDS signal is sparse and scattered, not a filled-in layer like C/Zn/N, so a uniform, surface-spanning aptamer shell is not established — attachment could be patchy or partial. (3) Neither method can distinguish pure electrostatic adsorption from a partial covalent or coordination-bond contribution, so the mechanism is consistent with electrostatics but not proven to be exclusively electrostatic. (4) No quantitative density or amount of bound aptamer is given, so the actual surface coverage or loading extent remains undetermined. In short: association and charge-reversal are established; uniformity, mechanism specificity, and quantity of coverage are not.

Because the effect is selective rather than a uniform intensity loss across all features, it argues against simple physical attenuation of the whole carbon signal by an overlying Zn layer, and instead points to a targeted interaction (reaction, consumption, or reordering) at the specific chemical site responsible for that sharp peak.

The mechanism behind the disappearance — chemical reaction with Zn, consumption of a reactive site during nucleation, structural/orientational change, or partial screening specific to that resonance — cannot be distinguished from the spectrum alone.

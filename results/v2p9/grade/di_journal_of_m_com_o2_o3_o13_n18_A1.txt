The figure (F7a) is a log-log plot of minimum creep rate vs. applied stress at a single fixed temperature (175°C) for AZ91 and AZ91 reinforced with 5/10/15/20/25 wt% SiC particulate, with linear fits whose slopes give the apparent stress exponent n: 7.2 (unreinforced AZ91) rising monotonically to 8.9 at 25 wt% SiC.

What this settles: the measurement is solid evidence that the apparent stress exponent increases systematically with SiC particulate content under these fixed test conditions (175°C, 80-125 MPa range). This is consistent with, and commonly used to support, a shift toward a "threshold stress" or load-transfer/dispersion-strengthening picture in which particulates raise the effective stress exponent above the ~5 expected for dislocation climb-controlled creep in Mg alloys — i.e., some departure from simple matrix-controlled MEC (metal/matrix element creep) kinetics as particulate loading increases.

What it leaves open: this single panel cannot by itself distinguish the actual mechanism/pathway responsible for the elevated n. An apparent n of 7-9 is compatible with several competing explanations — a real threshold stress requiring back-extrapolation and normalization by an effective stress (which this plot does not do), grain-boundary or particle-pinning effects, or simply an artifact of a narrow stress/temperature window at only one test temperature. Because measurements are shown at only one temperature (175°C), no activation energy or temperature dependence is available here to corroborate a specific rate-controlling mechanism (e.g., dislocation climb vs. threshold-stress creep vs. load transfer). The caption fragment ("applied stress and...") is truncated, so any additional stated interpretation from the authors is not captured in what's available. In short: the data support a composition-dependent increase in apparent n, consistent with reinforcement altering creep kinetics, but do not by themselves pin down which specific micromechanical pathway (threshold stress vs. other mechanisms) is responsible.

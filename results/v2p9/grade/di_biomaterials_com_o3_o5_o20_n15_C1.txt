THE ANSWER
This result settles that MBG, 2Co-MBG, and 5Co-MBG each possess a genuine periodic mesostructure, not an XRD artifact or an isolated local feature. The support comes from cross-confirmation between two independent, complementary observations: the single low-angle XRD reflection (a reciprocal-space fingerprint of a periodic repeat distance) and the TEM images (real-space views of parallel channel stripes and a regular pore lattice with a ~5-6 nm period). Because the reciprocal-space peak matches the real-space repeat pattern actually imaged, the two techniques mutually validate each other, and this holds for all three compositions, so periodic ordering is present across the whole Co-doping series, not just in the undoped material.

What this leaves open: (1) The structural symmetry/space group of the mesophase is undetermined — a single XRD reflection with no higher-order peaks cannot distinguish, e.g., hexagonal from cubic or other periodic arrangements, so "periodic" is as specific as the evidence gets. (2) Because TEM was only imaged over a local field (~200 nm for the stripe view), the result does not establish that this ordering is uniform through the bulk of each sample — it could be locally ordered but globally disordered or domain-structured. (3) The ~5-6 nm TEM "period" is a center-to-center repeat spacing, not pore diameter, so no pore-size value can be derived from this pair of observations. (4) The small shifts in the XRD 2theta position (1.37/1.47/1.35 degrees across MBG/2Co/5Co) are not shown by this evidence to track systematically with Co content — a quantitative structural trend with doping is not established here.

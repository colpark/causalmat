This result supports a co-trend, not a mechanism or a dose-response law. Across the three samples (0Co, 2Co, 5Co), EDS shows monotonically rising Co/Ca (0, 0.14, 0.49) while N2 sorption at P/P0~1 shows monotonically falling uptake (~8.7, ~5.6, ~4.35 mmol/g). The only thing this justifies is that, in this sample series, higher measured Co content and lower measured N2 uptake go together in the same rank order — an ordinal association between two independently-obtained measurements (elemental composition vs. bulk sorption capacity) on the same nominal samples.

What it does not establish:
- No dose-response or rate relationship: three points can define an ordering but not a functional form, slope, or threshold — you cannot say "each increment of Co reduces uptake by X."
- No shared physical quantity: Co/Ca is a compositional ratio from EDS; N2 uptake is a sorption volume/mass. They are linked only by sample identity, not by a common measured property, so the "tracking" is a correlation across matched labels, not a derived quantity.
- No causal isolation: Co dose is confounded with synthesis batch, since increasing Co in the sol-gel mix could itself alter condensation/network formation. So the drop in N2 uptake could reflect Co directly occupying/blocking pores, or could reflect a synthesis-pathway effect (e.g., altered gelation) that happens to co-occur with higher Co loading — this pairing cannot distinguish those.
- No mechanism: neither measurement reports pore size distribution, so pore blocking, skeletal densification, and Co-Ca substitution effects on network connectivity all remain equally plausible explanations for the lower uptake; none is confirmed or ruled out.

In short: the measurement licenses "Co content and N2 uptake move together, in opposite directions, across these three samples" and nothing stronger. Causal direction, mechanism, and quantitative dependence remain open.

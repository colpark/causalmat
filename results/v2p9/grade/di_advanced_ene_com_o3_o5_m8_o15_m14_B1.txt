The CV data (F6a) show that RGO-Cu2S-2 and bare Cu2S produce nearly identical, large reduction-peak current densities (~-43 vs ~-41 mA/cm2) near -1.25 V, while Pt is essentially inactive (only ~-0.45 mA/cm2, per the magnified F6b). This establishes that RGO-Cu2S-2 retains full electrocatalytic activity for polysulfide reduction relative to bare Cu2S, both vastly outperforming Pt.

Read together with the earlier SEM/XRD result — that RGO coverage of the Cu2S spheres increases across the series, with sample -2 still resembling bare Cu2S in bulk XRD signal (only sample -3 shows strong Cu2S-reflection suppression from heavier, more complete burial) — the combination supports a specific inference that neither measurement makes alone: at the partial-coverage level present in RGO-Cu2S-2, the RGO wrapping seen locally in SEM is not blocking electrochemical access to the underlying Cu2S active surface. That is, whatever attenuation or coverage the earlier SEM/XRD comparison documented at this stage is compositional/structural (sheets draped over spheres) rather than functionally insulating — the catalytic surface remains electrochemically "visible" to the electrolyte, since its activity matches bare Cu2S almost exactly. This links the earlier structural/diffraction-based coverage claim to a functional (electrochemical) consequence, which XRD and SEM alone could not address.

What this leaves open: the CV was only run on RGO-Cu2S-2, not on RGO-Cu2S-3, which is the sample with the heaviest coverage and the clear XRD suppression. So the combination cannot say whether the more completely buried sample -3 would also retain full activity or whether, at that higher coverage, RGO begins to impede polysulfide access. It also does not explain mechanistically why added RGO fails to enhance (rather than just preserve) activity over bare Cu2S, nor does it quantify what fraction of the Cu2S surface is actually covered versus exposed at the electrode/electrolyte interface.

This measurement supports a specific, limited conclusion: for MBG, 2Co-MBG and 5Co-MBG alike, the material has both (a) a real periodic mesoscale repeat unit, evidenced by a single low-angle XRD reflection (~1.37, ~1.47, ~1.35 2theta respectively), and (b) genuinely accessible mesoporosity, evidenced by a type IV N2 isotherm with a hysteresis step at P/P0 ~0.45-0.6. Because both signatures appear together in every sample, the finding rules out the two "false positive" scenarios that either observation alone would leave open — a periodic density modulation with no open porosity, or disordered/open mesopores with no periodic correlation at all. So what is settled is the co-occurrence/consistency of order and porosity across all three compositions, i.e., that Co doping (at 2 and 5 mol%) does not eliminate either the periodicity or the porosity relative to undoped MBG.

What is left open is considerable. The single, broad low-angle line cannot distinguish a well-ordered hexagonal/cubic mesostructure from a merely short-range-correlated or wormhole-like arrangement, since higher-order reflections needed to make that distinction are not reported. No pore-size value (e.g., the often-cited ~4-5 nm) is actually derived here — both the XRD peak position and the isotherm's P/P0 range are qualitative locators, not computed diameters (no BJH or equivalent analysis given). There is no wide-angle XRD, so no crystalline/phase identity is established. Finally, because peak intensity/sharpness and hysteresis-loop shape are not compared across the three samples, this result cannot say whether Co doping changed the degree of ordering or the pore-size distribution — only that some periodicity and some porosity persist in each case.

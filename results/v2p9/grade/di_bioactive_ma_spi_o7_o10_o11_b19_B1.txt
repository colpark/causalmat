The antibacterial panels (F10c dark, F10d light) rank the compositions under light as Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O (~91-93%) > Er0.5Ce0.3Ti-O (~87%) > Er0.5Ce0.1Ti-O (~68-73%) > TiO2 (~45%) > control (~4-7%). What the combination supports: the two anchor samples (TiO2 lowest, Er0.5Ce0.2 highest) that were already shown to have an inverse PL-degradation relationship also rank lowest and highest, respectively, in this independent antibacterial assay, and here the intermediate members of the series (Ce0.1, Ce0.3) fall in between in a monotonic order consistent with "more photocatalytic/ROS-generating activity = less carrier recombination." This strengthens confidence that the causal story generalizes across the full co-doping series and across a second functional endpoint (bactericidal killing, not just dye degradation), which the PL/degradation pairing alone could not do since PL was missing for Ce0.1/Ce0.3.

What it still leaves open: PL (recombination) itself was never measured for Ce0.1 or Ce0.3, so the monotonic recombination-to-activity claim is still not confirmed spectroscopically across the whole series — only inferred by analogy from a different functional readout. Antibacterial killing and dye degradation are related but not identical mechanisms (both plausibly ROS-mediated, but via different targets/kinetics), so this is convergent, not identical, evidence. The dark-condition data (F10c) show partial activity without light, indicating a light-independent contribution (e.g., ion release) that isn't accounted for by the PL/recombination model at all — so recombination-based photocatalysis is not established as the sole driver of PRF/service_capability, only as consistent with the light-driven component.

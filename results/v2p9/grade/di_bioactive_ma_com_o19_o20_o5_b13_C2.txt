THE ANSWER
This measurement settles that the Er detected in the particles is present specifically as Er3+ (based on the 168.8 eV Er 4d binding energy in XPS), and that this Er3+ species is associated with a Ti-O oxide framework (from the Ti 2p3/2 458.5 eV, 2p1/2 464.2 eV, and O1s 529.0/531.2/533.0 eV signatures). The value of combining EDS and XPS here is that EDS alone only shows a small Er-energy peak (~1.4 keV), which by itself is elemental-presence evidence with an unconfirmed identity; XPS supplies the oxidation-state assignment for that same element. Together they justify the stronger claim "Er is present as Er3+" rather than the weaker claim "a peak at an Er-characteristic energy was observed." That is the full extent of what this pairing supports.

It leaves open several things. It does not determine Ce's oxidation state (Ce3+, Ce4+, or a mixture), since the XPS observation reports no Ce binding-energy region at all. It does not establish that Er (or Ce) is actually incorporated into the TiO2 lattice as opposed to being merely surface-adsorbed, because XPS is surface-sensitive while EDS probes a larger interaction volume, so agreement between the two techniques on "Er present" does not prove the signals originate from the same spatial region or phase. It also does not clarify whether the dominant carbon peak seen in EDS reflects genuine sample chemistry or contamination from the grid/support, since no C1s XPS data is reported. Finally, because the Er and Ce EDS peaks are described as small, the elemental-presence side of the conclusion is only weakly supported, which somewhat limits confidence in the combined claim overall.

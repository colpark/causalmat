THE ANSWER
This measurement settles that the size difference between microfluidic and hydrothermal PRP particles is a real, robust physical property rather than an artifact of one measurement technique. Two independent, methodologically distinct modalities — DLS (ensemble, solution-phase, hydrodynamic) and SEM (direct imaging, dry-state, core size) — converge on the same conclusion from two directions at once: (1) magnitude/scale — microfluidic particles cluster around ~300-400 nm, hydrothermal particles around ~900-1000 nm, roughly a 2.5-3x difference; and (2) distribution shape — microfluidic particles show a narrow/tight size distribution while hydrothermal particles show a broader spread. Because DLS and SEM have different, largely uncorrelated failure modes (DLS is skewed by aggregation and weights larger particles more heavily; SEM depends on which particles happen to get imaged/segmented), their agreement on both axes is strong corroborating evidence that the size and dispersity contrast between the two synthesis routes is genuine and not a technique-specific artifact.

What it does not establish: an exact absolute particle size. DLS and SEM report physically different quantities (hydrodynamic diameter in suspension vs. dry imaged core diameter), so the modest numeric gaps (e.g., ~300-400 vs ~350-400 nm; ~1000 vs ~900-950 nm) are expected and consistent with the known DLS solvation/hydrodynamic offset, not something to be reconciled into a single "true" number. It also says nothing about mechanism — why the microfluidic route yields smaller, more uniform particles while the hydrothermal route yields larger, more polydisperse ones remains entirely open; that causal/mechanistic question is outside what these two panels can address.

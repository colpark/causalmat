This gives a plausible microstructural cause for why the macroscopic hardening rate falls so fast early on, rather than declining smoothly as in a homogeneous single-phase alloy — i.e., it ties the unusual two-stage (fast-then-slow) hardening kinetics to heterogeneous, partitioned plasticity between the two ordered phases.

What it leaves open: neither measurement identifies which specific phase (L12 or B2) is the one developing contrast first, so the mechanistic assignment of "which phase hardens/exhausts first" remains unconfirmed.

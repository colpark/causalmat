THE ANSWER
Panel F9 (Fig. 9, western blot) shows: HIF-1alpha (~120 kDa) band is faint/weak for plain MBG, still faint (similarly weak, only marginally different) for 2Co-MBG, and clearly darker/stronger for 5Co-MBG; the alpha-tubulin loading control (~50 kDa) bands are visually equal across all three lanes.

What this settles: Because the loading control is equal across lanes, the difference in HIF-1alpha band intensity is not an artifact of unequal protein loading — it reflects a real, dose-dependent increase in HIF-1alpha protein level as cobalt content in the scaffold increases (MBG ≈ 2Co-MBG < 5Co-MBG). This supports the qualitative claim that higher-cobalt scaffolds upregulate HIF-1alpha expression in BMSCs, consistent with cobalt acting as a hypoxia-mimetic agent that stabilizes HIF-1alpha protein (the classic Co2+ mechanism of inhibiting prolyl-hydroxylase-mediated degradation).

What it leaves open: This single blot is not quantified (no densitometry ratio to tubulin given), so the magnitude of the increase and its statistical significance across replicates are undetermined from this image alone. It does not distinguish between increased HIF-1alpha protein stability versus increased transcription/translation as the mechanism, nor does it establish that HIF-1alpha protein change translates into downstream transcriptional activity (e.g., VEGF or other HIF-target gene induction) — that requires additional data not shown in this panel. It also does not by itself establish a causal link to any downstream biological/functional outcome (e.g., angiogenesis, osteogenesis); it only supports the upstream molecular observation that cobalt dose correlates with HIF-1alpha protein abundance.

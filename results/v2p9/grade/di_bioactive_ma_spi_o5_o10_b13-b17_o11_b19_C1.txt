This measurement supports a qualitative, associative claim: across the small set of samples examined, co-doped TiO2 with red-shifted/broadened visible-light absorption (edges past ~420 nm, vs undoped TiO2's ~410 nm) consistently shows higher photocatalytic degradation than undoped TiO2, and this ordering (co-doped > undoped) holds stably at all three time points measured (20, 40, 60 min). That consistency across time points is what justifies treating the co-doped-over-undoped ordering as reasonably robust rather than a one-off artifact, and it is enough to justify the qualitative b13->b17 link: "enhanced visible-light absorption in co-doped TiO2 is associated with improved photocatalytic degradation relative to undoped TiO2."

What it does not establish: (1) A dose-response relationship — only 3-4 doping conditions were compared (Ce0.1, Ce0.3, Er0.5Ce0.2, TiO2), far too few to say that more red-shift or broader absorption produces proportionally more degradation; it only supports an ordering among a handful of samples, not a graded trend. (2) A clean within-co-doped-set correlation — the sample with the strongest absorption (Ce0.2) is not the same sample with the highest degradation (Er0.5Ce0.2), so the two rankings are built on different compositions and do not cleanly show that "more absorption" among co-doped variants tracks with "more degradation." In short: the data support "co-doping helps, and it correlates with broader visible absorption" as a general, coarse association, but leave open the finer question of whether absorption strength itself drives degradation magnitude, and leave entirely open the mechanistic link between the two properties.

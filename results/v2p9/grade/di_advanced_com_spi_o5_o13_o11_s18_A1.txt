The DSC panel (F5a) shows glass-transition step changes on heating (ENDO up, EXO down) for pure PAEN and PAEN/BST@Ag composites at 1, 3, and 5 wt% loading: Tg = 176 C (pure PAEN), 146 C (1%), 149 C (3%), 157 C (5%). Each curve is a single labeled trace with one clearly marked step; the values match the numbers given in the task.

What this supports: it is solid, direct thermal evidence that incorporating BST@Ag filler shifts the polymer's glass transition substantially downward relative to neat PAEN (176 -> ~146-157 C), and that within the filled series Tg rises monotonically with filler loading from 1% to 5% (146 -> 149 -> 157 C). This is consistent with a plasticization-then-partial-restiffening trend, or with a strong initial disruption of chain packing at low filler content that is progressively offset as loading increases. It establishes a real, measurable, filler-content-dependent effect on thermal/segmental-mobility behavior of the matrix.

What it leaves open: DSC Tg alone does not establish "service capability" in a mechanical or functional sense — no modulus, strength, toughness, dielectric, or long-term thermal-stability data are shown here, so whether a lower Tg helps or hurts actual use-temperature performance is not addressed. It also doesn't reveal the mechanism (plasticization, interfacial effects, filler agglomeration, or measurement artifact from step sharpness) or whether the trend continues beyond 5%. So the panel settles the direction and monotonic trend of Tg shift with Ag loading, but not its consequence for PRF/service_capability in application terms, nor the underlying cause.

THE ANSWER
The blot (Fig. 9) shows HIF-1a bands at ~120 kDa that are faint for MBG and 2Co-MBG and distinctly stronger for 5Co-MBG, with equal-intensity alpha-tubulin loading controls across all three lanes. This supports a dose-dependent effect: higher cobalt content in the Co-doped mesoporous bioactive glass scaffold correlates with greater HIF-1a protein accumulation in BMSCs, consistent with cobalt acting as a hypoxia mimetic (e.g., via inhibition of prolyl hydroxylases that normally target HIF-1a for degradation) at a sufficiently high dose (5% Co) but only weakly at 2% Co or in undoped MBG. Because the loading control is equal, the difference in HIF-1a signal reflects a real difference in protein level rather than unequal sample loading.

What this leaves open: this is a single, apparently non-quantified, non-replicated western blot (no densitometry, no error bars, no stated number of biological replicates), so the magnitude and statistical significance of the 5Co-MBG increase over MBG/2Co-MBG cannot be established from this image alone. It also does not establish mechanism — it shows correlation between cobalt dose and HIF-1a protein level, not proof that cobalt directly stabilizes HIF-1a (versus stabilizing it indirectly, e.g., through hypoxia induced by cobalt ion release, altered cell metabolism, or off-target effects). It says nothing about downstream pathway activity (e.g., HIF-1a transcriptional targets, angiogenic gene/protein expression) or about whether the same effect holds at intermediate cobalt doses, at different time points, or in other cell types — only that at this one time point and in this one experiment, higher Co content is associated with more HIF-1a protein while total protein loading was equal.

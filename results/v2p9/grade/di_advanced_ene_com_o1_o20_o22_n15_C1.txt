THE ANSWER
This settled result supports the conclusion that Co oxidation under anodic potential is both thermodynamically favorable (per DFT) and experimentally real (per EELS), with the two independent lines of evidence converging on the same qualitative trend: as potential rises from dry/open-circuit toward 1.4-1.5 V, cobalt shifts to a higher oxidation state, and this is energetically the most favorable pathway among the materials computed (CoCrOOH favored for Co2+->Co3+, CoFeOOH favored for Co3+->Co4+, with CoFeCrOOH intermediate and Co(OH)2 the least favorable in both steps). So the measurement justifies treating computed thermodynamic preference as corroborated, rather than speculative, because an independent spectroscopic signature (L3/L2 edge shift and L2 intensity growth) tracks in the same direction as potential increases.

What it leaves open, plainly: (1) The EELS signal is a single lumped indicator of "more oxidized cobalt" and cannot distinguish whether the observed change reflects the Co2+->Co3+ step, the Co3+->Co4+ step, or a mixture of both, even though DFT treats these as two distinct, composition-specific transitions. (2) The DFT ranking is composition-resolved across four materials, but nothing indicates EELS was measured across that same set of compositions, so the cross-material ordering predicted by DFT (CoCrOOH best for one step, CoFeOOH best for the other) remains experimentally unconfirmed. (3) Delta G (computed energetics) and edge-loss position/L2 intensity (experimental proxy) are fundamentally different kinds of quantities; agreement in directional trend does not establish a quantitative correspondence between predicted thermodynamic favorability and measured extent of oxidation. In short, the result licenses confidence in the qualitative, potential-driven trend toward Co oxidation, but not in step-resolution, composition-specific validation, or quantitative agreement.

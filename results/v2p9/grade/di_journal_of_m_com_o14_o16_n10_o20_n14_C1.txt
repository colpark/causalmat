THE ANSWER
This measurement settles that the C-rich signal EDS locates at particle surfaces/edges/faces corresponds to a real carbon phase, not an artifact: HRTEM at one such edge shows curved carbon layers (0.386 nm spacing) directly adjacent to crystalline alloy fringes (CeMg12 at 0.258 nm, Y5Mg24 at 0.265 nm) and an amorphous band. So the combined evidence supports the qualitative claim that carbon coats or sits on the alloy particle surface, corroborating the EDS map's spatial picture with atomic-resolution structural detail at least at one location.

What it leaves open is considerable. First, the HRTEM view is a single local edge region of one milled C10 particle, so it is not shown to be representative of all the surface/edge/face locations where EDS detects C and Co — the finding could be a special case rather than the general morphology. Second, because EDS's areal map resolution is far coarser than HRTEM's lattice-fringe resolution, the pairing gives only qualitative, spatial corroboration; it does not quantify how much of the particle surface is carbon-coated or establish a statistically representative distribution at fine scale. Third, and most importantly for any "Co in carbon" claim, no Co-containing lattice fringe was reported in the HRTEM observation — only Ce/Mg and Y/Mg alloy fringes plus carbon layers were resolved. So the data support C and Co signals co-locating at the coarser EDS scale, but do not demonstrate that Co is atomically dispersed within the carbon phase itself; that remains unestablished.

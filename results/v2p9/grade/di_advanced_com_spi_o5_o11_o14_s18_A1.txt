Panel F6a (Advanced_Composites_and_Hybrid_Materials, s42114-021-00366-2) plots dielectric constant vs. frequency (100 Hz–1 MHz, log scale) for pure PAEN and BST@Ag composites at 1%, 3%, and 5% Ag loading. Each curve is nearly flat: pure PAEN drops from ~3.3 to ~3.0, BST@Ag1% from ~5.3 to ~4.7, BST@Ag3% from ~5.6 to ~5.0, and BST@Ag5% from ~6.1 to ~5.4 — a decline of roughly 10% across six decades of frequency for every sample, matching the stated measurement.

What this supports: the dielectric constant of both the neat polymer and all three filler-loaded composites is essentially frequency-stable over the practically relevant 100 Hz–1 MHz range, with only mild dispersion typical of interfacial/space-charge (Maxwell-Wagner-Sillars) polarization decaying at higher frequency. This is meaningful for service_capability in the narrow sense that a device relying on this material's permittivity (e.g., a capacitor or embedded dielectric layer) would see fairly consistent capacitive response whether operated near DC or at MHz frequencies — i.e., the material is not narrowly frequency-tuned and should perform predictably across that band.

What it leaves open: this single curve says nothing about dielectric loss/tan delta, breakdown strength, temperature or humidity dependence, mechanical durability, aging, or performance beyond 1 MHz. It also doesn't establish that any given absolute dielectric constant value meets a specific application's threshold — only that whatever value each composite has, it stays roughly stable with frequency. So "service capability" here is supported only for frequency-stability of permittivity, not for overall functional/service readiness.

This measurement settles that PCO's crystalline oxide framework — its overall structural type (STR) and microstructure — is largely preserved through sodiation/desodiation, rather than being converted to metallic cobalt or collapsing into a disordered/amorphous state. The persistence of the same SXRD reflections across discharge/charge, with only a modest drop in intensity, supports retention of the parent crystal structure and its long-range order; the XANES white line staying strong and falling between CoO and Co3O4 (well away from Co foil) independently confirms that Co remains oxidized rather than being reduced to metal, which corroborates that the oxide lattice itself is not being destroyed by a reduction-driven phase change. Together these support the conclusion that PCO undergoes at most a mild, non-destructive structural response to sodiation — the framework survives, and any capacity-relevant chemistry is not accompanied by gross microstructural breakdown or metallization.

What remains open: this does not tell us anything quantitative about porosity, pore-size evolution, particle cracking, or surface-area change — no technique here probes real-space microstructure or porosity directly. It also cannot quantify the modest XRD intensity loss in physical terms (whether from increased disorder, minor amorphization, microstrain, or particle/grain-level effects), nor can it specify what fraction of the material (if any) might have amorphized below the volume-averaged detection threshold of SXRD. Likewise, the oxidation-state placement is qualitative, so any small-scale or surface-localized reduction that could nucleate porosity-relevant defects is not excluded. In short: bulk crystallinity and non-metallic Co are supported; fine-grained microstructural and porosity changes are simply not addressed by this result.

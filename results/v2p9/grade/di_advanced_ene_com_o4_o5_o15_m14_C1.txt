THE ANSWER
This measurement (TEM image plus the qualitative XRD intensity trend across RGO-Cu2S-1/2/3) supports the conclusion that RGO sheets physically wrap around Cu2S microspheres, and that this wrapping is a plausible mechanism for the observed weakening of Cu2S diffraction reflections as GO content increases (with -3 clearly weaker and -1/-2 close to bare Cu2S). In other words, it justifies a qualitative, mechanistic claim: coating/encapsulation of Cu2S by RGO is consistent with, and can account for, the ordering of XRD signal attenuation seen across the three compositions.

What it leaves open is substantial. The TEM image is a single snapshot at one location in one sample, not linked to a specific composition point in the -1/-2/-3 series, so it cannot on its own show that coverage scales with GO loading — that inference comes only from combining it with the separate XRD trend. The XRD comparison itself is purely qualitative (relative reflection intensity across three samples), not a quantified measure of surface coverage, and provides no direct morphological information. Critically, only three composition points were compared, which is enough to support an ordering (more GO, weaker Cu2S signal) but far too few to establish a continuous or quantitative dose-response relationship between GO amount and coverage extent. So the result settles the qualitative existence and plausibility of a wrapping mechanism and its consistency with a monotonic trend, but it does not settle how much of the sphere surface is actually covered, nor whether coverage increases smoothly or in some other pattern with GO amount.

THE ANSWER
F5a shows: x=0 has high resistivity (~650 down to ~200 µΩ·m) that *decreases* with increasing T (300–625 K) — semiconductor-like, thermally-activated transport. All Se-doped samples (x=0.005–0.04) show much lower resistivity (~12–23 µΩ·m at 300 K, rising to ~25–38 µΩ·m at 625 K) that *increases* with T — metallic-like transport, with x=0.005 modestly above the more heavily doped x=0.01–0.04 curves, which cluster tightly together.

Combined with the settled DFT+Hall result (Se donation raises E_F so M/K/L valleys reach E_F, tracking n_H rising from ~0.5e19 to 1.7–3.3e19 cm^-3), this resistivity data adds an independent macroscopic transport signature of the same donor mechanism: it shows the material crosses from a lightly-doped, activated/semiconducting regime (x=0) to a degenerate, metallic regime (x≥0.005) as Se fills the conduction-band valleys — the sign change of dρ/dT is a qualitatively distinct, corroborating observation that neither the DFT calculation nor the Hall carrier count alone establishes.

What it leaves open: the resistivity drop (roughly 30–50x) is far larger than the ~3–7x rise in n_H, implying that mobility/scattering changes (e.g., reduced ionized/activated-carrier scattering, or a different scattering regime in the metallic state) also contribute substantially — resistivity cannot on its own separate carrier-density effects from mobility effects. It also does not resolve why x=0.005 sits slightly above the higher-x curves while 0.01–0.04 are nearly degenerate, nor does it provide any quantitative link between resistivity, band position, and n_H beyond confirming the same qualitative donor/metallization trend already inferred.

Panel F2b shows the strain-hardening rate (dσ/dε, GPa) versus true strain for the Al0.9CoFeNi2 EHEA, with three annotated regimes (I, II, III) and an inset of true stress–strain. The curve begins near ~85 GPa at very low strain, falls steeply to ~14 GPa by ε≈0.009 (regime I boundary), then declines gradually to ~5 GPa by ε≈0.058 (regime II), after which it dips and turns negative into regime III near ε≈0.06.

What this measurement supports: it confirms a two-stage (plus a terminal) hardening behavior consistent with a multi-mechanism strain-hardening pathway — an early, very steep drop in hardening rate (regime I, roughly athermal/dislocation-multiplication dominated) followed by a much slower, sustained decline over an extended strain range (regime II), and finally a hardening-rate collapse to negative values signaling onset of necking/instability (regime III) rather than continued uniform plastic flow. This directly substantiates a transition between distinct hardening mechanisms (i.e., support for a mechanism-change / MEC-type interpretation of the hardening pathway) and is consistent with a mechanism such as dislocation-density saturation or a change in dominant hardening contributor (e.g., planar slip/short-range order effects or partial dislocation activity) taking over after the initial steep drop.

What it leaves open: the plot alone cannot identify which specific microstructural mechanism (dislocation storage vs. short-range ordering vs. deformation twinning/stacking-fault activity) causes the regime I→II transition or the strain-hardening plateau in regime II — that requires independent microstructural/TEM evidence, which this panel does not provide. It also does not establish causality, only correlation with strain; and the exact strain value marking the I/II boundary is read approximately from the graph, not given as an exact analytic value.

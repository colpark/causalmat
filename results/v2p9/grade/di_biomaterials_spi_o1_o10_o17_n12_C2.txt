THE ANSWER
This measurement supports a compositional-control link in the MEC/pathway: the amount of Co incorporated into the scaffold during synthesis (EDS Co/Ca = 0, 0.14, 0.49 for 0Co/2Co/5Co) governs the amount of Co2+ subsequently released into the medium, since the same ordering (5Co > 2Co > 0Co) holds for embedded Co and for released Co2+ at every sampled timepoint (days 1, 3, 7). So the justified conclusion is a qualitative, ordinal one: higher scaffold Co content produces higher Co2+ release, consistent with a straightforward composition-drives-release pathway rather than some non-monotonic or threshold-gated release mechanism.

What it leaves open is substantial. It does not establish a quantitative dose-response curve or a release rate/kinetics relationship, because the EDS value is a single static snapshot while release is a time series — the match is only in rank order, not in magnitude or slope. It cannot equate the two measures numerically, since Co/Ca is a normalized ratio and Co2+ concentration is an absolute mg/L quantity — these are different kinds of quantities, incommensurable beyond ordering. With only three groups, the data cannot establish continuity or linearity of the composition-release relationship, nor rule out saturation, threshold, or other nonlinear effects at intermediate compositions. Finally, because group identity (0Co/2Co/5Co) was defined by the synthesis recipe itself, the ordering match is partly definitional/a construction check rather than an independent mechanistic test — it does not by itself demonstrate the physical/chemical release mechanism (e.g., diffusion vs. dissolution) linking incorporated Co to released Co2+.

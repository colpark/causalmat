Panel F9 (Fig. 9, western blot): HIF-1a band (~120 kDa) is faint for MBG (0Co), similarly faint for 2Co-MBG, and clearly stronger for 5Co-MBG; the alpha-tubulin loading control (~50 kDa) is equal across all three lanes, so the difference reflects HIF-1a protein level rather than unequal loading.

Combined with the earlier settled result — that embedded Co content and released Co2+ concentration both rank 5Co > 2Co > 0Co, with 5Co releasing roughly double the Co2+ of 2Co at every timepoint — this new panel adds a downstream cellular readout that follows the same ordering: the scaffold with by far the most Co2+ release (5Co) is also the one with visibly elevated HIF-1a protein in BMSCs, while the two lower/no-Co conditions (0Co, 2Co) look similarly faint. What it leaves open: (1) the blot is a single, non-quantified image (no densitometry, no replicates/statistics), so "clearly stronger" is a qualitative call, not a measured dose-response; (2) since 0Co and 2Co bands look equally faint, the data support only a step-like jump at high Co (5Co) rather than a graded/monotonic HIF-1a response tracking the graded Co2+ release seen earlier — the 2Co condition does not visibly separate from 0Co despite releasing measurable Co2+; (3) no direct mechanistic link is shown here between Co2+ concentration in the medium and HIF-1a stabilization (no PHD inhibition assay, no Co2+ dose-titration on cells, no chelation/rescue control), so causation versus another 5Co-specific scaffold difference driving HIF-1a cannot be excluded; (4) no downstream functional/angiogenic readout (e.g., VEGF) is included in this panel to connect HIF-1a stabilization to a biological outcome.

Findings from Fig. 6 (F7, image 909642d1...jpg):

The panel shows three overlaid PL emission curves (excitation presumably UV) spanning roughly 350-500 nm, each with a broad band centered near 390-420 nm. The ordering by peak intensity in that band is exactly as stated: TiO2 (teal) is highest, roughly reaching the top of the plotted intensity axis; Er0.5Ti-O (red) peaks at roughly half the TiO2 peak height; Er0.5Ce0.2-O (blue) is lowest, clearly below the Er0.5Ti-O curve, appearing to be somewhat less than half of that curve's peak. All three curves share the same general double-humped shape and wavelength position, differing mainly in amplitude.

What this supports: the measurement is consistent with progressive PL quenching as dopant content increases from pure TiO2 to Er-doped to Er+Ce co-doped titania. In the PL-as-recombination-proxy convention common in photocatalysis literature, this ordering supports the qualitative claim that Er doping, and more so Er+Ce co-doping, suppresses radiative electron-hole recombination relative to undoped TiO2 — i.e., the dopants act as trap/mediator sites for photogenerated carriers.

What it leaves open: this single spectral comparison does not establish the mechanism (non-radiative trapping vs. energy transfer to Er/Ce levels vs. altered defect density), does not quantify recombination rate directly, and does not by itself demonstrate improved photocatalytic activity, ROS generation, or bioactivity/PRP-relevant functional performance — those require separate corroborating assays. Sample-to-sample structural/compositional differences (crystallinity, surface area) that could also modulate PL intensity are not ruled out here.

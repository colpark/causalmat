This combined FTIR/SEM observation supports a limited, plausibility-level claim about surface-modification-driven interfacial bonding: it is consistent with silane grafting (evidenced chemically by new Si-O and CH2 signatures unique to BST-SH) being a plausible chemical basis for the tight, gap-free filler-matrix contact seen morphologically in the BST@Ag1% film. What it does not settle: (1) Causation is not established, only association-by-plausibility — because FTIR was measured on bare BST-SH particles and SEM on a different composite film that also contains added Ag, the two measurements are not co-located on the same specimen, so the chemistry documented by FTIR cannot be directly tied to the morphology seen in SEM; the Ag addition or other film-formation factors (wetting, dispersion) could equally explain the blurred, gap-free particle outlines. So this justifies "consistent with improved interfacial bonding via silane surface modification" but leaves the mechanism, magnitude, and specificity (versus confounds like Ag content) open.

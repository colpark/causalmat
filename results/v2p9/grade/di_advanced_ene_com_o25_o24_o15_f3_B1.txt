THE ANSWER
F2a shows the w/HCl discharge-capacity curve losing most of its capacity in the first ~20-50 cycles, then declining slowly toward ~55 mAh/g by cycle 250, with coulombic efficiency pinned near 100% throughout (no sign of major parasitic/side-reaction capacity loss).

Read together with the established Rct/V result (interfacial resistance and dissolved-V both rising early and saturating by ~cycle 150), the combination supports linking the capacity-fade profile to the same resistance-buildup mechanism rather than to bulk active-material loss: the early steep capacity drop temporally coincides with the window where Rct and V are climbing fastest, and CE staying at ~100% is consistent with fade being a polarization/impedance effect (capacity becoming inaccessible under cutoff voltage) rather than material dissolving out of the electrode and being irreversibly consumed. This is the first joint evidence connecting the electrochemical service-capability metric (capacity/PRF) to the Rct-V mechanistic pairing established earlier, since the capacity curve alone has no impedance or dissolution data attached to it, and the Rct/V pairing alone had no performance consequence attached to it.

What it leaves open: capacity keeps declining slowly all the way to cycle 250, well past the ~150-cycle point where Rct and V plateau, so the late-stage fade must have a source other than (or in addition to) the V-linked Rct growth already saturated by then. The match is also still just co-timing across three different measurement types (capacity, resistance, concentration), not a demonstrated quantitative or causal chain from V dissolution to Rct to capacity loss.

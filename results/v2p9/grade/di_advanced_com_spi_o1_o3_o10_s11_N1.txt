Likely established earlier result: In BaTiO3/BST-type filler–polymer composites, coating the ceramic particle with a conductive metal shell (here Ag) raises the local dielectric constant via a micro-capacitor/interfacial-polarization effect, but conductive shells are known to concentrate electric field at particle edges and at particle–particle contacts, which typically drives dielectric breakdown strength (BDS) down as filler loading rises — i.e., permittivity and breakdown strength trade off against each other, and that trade-off has been attributed mainly to the shell's conductivity itself.

What the new paper's measurement (enhanced dielectric performance together with high mechanical strength and high-temperature resistance in the BST@Ag/PEN composite) adds: it shows that despite the conductive Ag shell, this particular composite does not suffer the expected collapse in breakdown strength/mechanical integrity — permittivity rises while strength and thermal stability are simultaneously preserved or improved.

Read together, the two results settle that the loss of breakdown strength/mechanical strength in shell-coated filler composites is not an unavoidable consequence of shell conductivity per se, but is contingent on interfacial bonding quality between filler and matrix — i.e., a well-bonded BST@Ag/PEN interface can suppress the field-concentration and micro-void formation that normally cause strength loss, decoupling permittivity gain from strength loss.

What it leaves open: the actual interfacial thickness, bonding strength, or filler-loading limit at which this decoupling breaks down; whether the mechanism is chemical bonding, mechanical interlocking, or reduced free volume; and whether the effect generalizes beyond PEN or beyond the specific Ag shell thickness/loading tested.

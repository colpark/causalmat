THE ANSWER
The kinetic data show that the diffusion-controlled (second) absorption stage shrinks roughly six-fold with the C/Co addition (66 min for C0 vs 11 min for C10 at 360°C), i.e. the rate-limiting hydrogen-diffusion step through/across the particle is dramatically faster once carbon and cobalt are present. Combined with the earlier EDS+HRTEM result — that carbon sits as an actual crystalline/amorphous carbon phase (curved layers, 0.386 nm) directly at particle edges/surfaces, physically adjacent to the alloy grains, with Co signal co-located there — the pairing supports a specific mechanistic claim that neither result gives alone: the surface carbon (with associated Co) is not simply an inert coating but functions as a diffusion-facilitating (catalytic-type) interfacial layer that shortens the diffusion-controlled stage of hydrogen uptake. Kinetics alone (66→11 min) only shows that something about the C10 composition speeds diffusion, without locating where or identifying what phase is responsible. Location/identity alone (EDS+HRTEM) only shows carbon and Co sit at the surface/edges, without showing that this surface layer has any functional effect on absorption kinetics. Together they support that this same surface carbon layer, shown to exist physically at the edge, is plausibly the site/agent responsible for the measured diffusion-stage acceleration.

What remains open: (1) the HRTEM view is one edge region of one particle, so it's not shown that carbon coats particles broadly enough to explain a bulk kinetic effect; (2) no Co-containing lattice fringe was resolved, so whether Co (versus C alone) drives the diffusion enhancement, or how the two act together, is unsettled; (3) the combination is still correlational — no direct in-situ link shows hydrogen actually transiting through the imaged carbon layer.

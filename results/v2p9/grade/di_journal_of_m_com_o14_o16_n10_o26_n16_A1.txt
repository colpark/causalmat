Both panels (F9a = C0/base alloy Mg90Ce5Y5, F9b = same alloy +10 wt% "CQCo") show ln[-ln(1-a)] vs ln t (JMAK/Avrami) plots for hydrogen desorption at four temperatures (280, 300, 320, 360 C). In each panel the four temperature curves are straight, parallel lines with R2 > 0.98, and each panel reports a single Avrami exponent (eta = 1.59 for C0, eta = 1.32 for the +10% CQCo alloy).

What this justifies: the desorption data are well described by a single JMAK/Avrami kinetic law over the measured conversion range at every temperature tested — i.e., one dominant, temperature-invariant rate-controlling mechanism operates across 280-360 C for each alloy (that is the "diagnostic" value of the linear fit: no curvature/break that would signal a mechanism change with temperature or extent of reaction). It also supports that the additive changes the kinetics in some way, since the fitted exponent drops from 1.59 to 1.32 with the CQCo addition, and that the additive shifts the curves to shorter ln t (faster kinetics) as seen by the leftward position of the C10 curves relative to C0 at the same temperatures.

What it leaves open: the plots alone do not identify the physical growth/nucleation mechanism the exponent implies — eta ~1.3-1.6 is compatible with more than one interpretation (e.g., diffusion-controlled growth with decreasing nucleation rate vs. some interface-controlled process), and the panels give no independent evidence (microstructural, activation-energy, or model-selection) to disambiguate. It also does not by itself establish that the same mechanism holds beyond the plotted conversion window, nor does it explain why the additive lowers eta rather than merely accelerating desorption.

This result settles that power factor (PF) and ZT do not rank the doped compositions the same way at 300 K: x=0.01 gives the highest PF (~29 uW cm-1 K-2 at 300 K, ~22 at 623 K) while x=0 gives the lowest (at most ~5), but the highest room-temperature ZT (~0.84) belongs to x=0.005, which has the lowest PF among the doped samples, with x=0.01 second at ZT~0.78 and x=0.02-0.04 lower (0.63-0.69). So the composition that maximizes electrical power factor is not the composition that maximizes ZT at 300 K.

What this supports: PF alone is not a sufficient proxy for thermoelectric figure of merit ranking in this system; optimizing for maximum power factor via doping level (x=0.01) would not yield the best ZT sample (x=0.005 does better on ZT despite weaker PF).

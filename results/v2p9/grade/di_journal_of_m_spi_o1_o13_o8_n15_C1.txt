THE ANSWER
This combined result supports the conclusion that the two ordered phases present in the as-cast alloy do not deform identically once strain is applied: at the very first strain increment, the microstructure already differentiates into a lamella that remains featureless and a neighboring lamella that develops mottled contrast plus interfacial line contrast. Taken together with the bulk XRD confirmation that L12 and B2 are both present, this is consistent with (and supports the plausibility of) a phase-dependent deformation pathway — i.e., that one ordered phase begins accommodating strain (via some defect process, dislocation activity, faulting, or interface accommodation) earlier or more readily than the other. That is the strongest claim the evidence justifies: an onset asymmetry between two lamella types under load.

What it leaves open is substantial. Because XRD is bulk and spatially unresolved, it cannot say which lamella in the TEM pair is L12 and which is B2, so the phase identity behind the asymmetry is inferred, not established — the mechanism (MEC) cannot yet be assigned to a specific ordered phase with certainty. The TEM description is contrast/morphology only, not diffraction-indexed, so the nature of the contrast (dislocations, stacking faults, tweed, strain contrast) and thus the actual deformation pathway remains unidentified. Because only one strain step and one lamella pair were examined, the observation cannot be generalized across the microstructure or strain history, and without any defect density/count, the comparison stays qualitative — it establishes that a difference exists, not how large, how consistent, or what physical process it reflects.

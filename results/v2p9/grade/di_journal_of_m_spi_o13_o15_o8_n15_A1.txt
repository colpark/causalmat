Panel F2b shows the strain-hardening rate (dσ/dε) vs. true strain curve for the Al0.9CoFeNi2 EHEA, with three labeled regimes (I, II, III) separated by dashed vertical lines at roughly ε≈0.009 and ε≈0.058. The curve matches the stated numbers: it starts near 85 GPa at very low strain, plunges steeply through region I to about 14 GPa by ε≈0.009, then declines much more gradually through region II to about 5 GPa by ε≈0.058, before turning upward/negative-slope-reversal in region III near the end of the visible strain range (inset true stress-strain curve ends near ε≈0.06).

What this justifies: the measurement is a purely mechanical/macroscopic observation — it establishes that the material's work-hardening behavior is not monolithic but partitions into (at least) three distinct stages with sharply different hardening kinetics (rapid dislocation-multiplication-dominated drop, then a slow quasi-plateau, then a late-stage change in slope). This is consistent with, and often cited in EHEA/HEA literature to support, a multi-mechanism or stage-transition picture of plastic deformation (e.g., dislocation storage/dynamic recovery giving way to a secondary hardening or softening mechanism such as deformation-induced ordering, twinning, or phase-related effects).

What it leaves open: the hardening-rate curve alone cannot identify which microstructural mechanism (MEC) or pathway is operating in each stage — it does not by itself distinguish dislocation-slip hardening from twinning-induced hardening, transformation-induced plasticity, short-range ordering, or precipitate/phase effects. Attributing region II's plateau or region III's upturn to any specific pathway requires corroborating microstructural evidence (TEM, XRD, EBSD) not present in this panel. So the measurement supports "distinct multi-stage hardening behavior exists" but does not settle "which mechanism causes each stage."

This measurement supports a morphology-performance association for service_capability under the PRF framework: the substrate (CnC HS) that produces a flat, compact zinc deposit with only sparse platelets is the same substrate that sustains high coulombic efficiency (roughly 93-100%) out to about 205 cycles, whereas the substrate (C HS) that produces a dense mat of sharp platelets is the same one whose CE collapses to zero by roughly 40-48 cycles. Taken together, this justifies the conclusion that a smoother, more compact deposition morphology co-occurs with, and plausibly underlies, markedly longer stable cycling life, while a rougher, sharp-platelet morphology co-occurs with early electrochemical failure. This is a defensible qualitative link between structural character and functional durability for these two substrates.

What it leaves open is substantial, however. First, because only two substrates are compared, the result is a single paired contrast rather than a dose-response relationship; it cannot establish how intermediate degrees of platelet sharpness or deposit density translate into intermediate CE lifetimes. Second, the SEM imaging is an endpoint or snapshot characterization, not a time-resolved series matched to the cycling trace, so the data show correlation between a static structural picture and a cumulative efficiency curve rather than a synchronized time-course demonstrating that morphology change precedes or drives efficiency loss at each stage. Third, and most importantly for causal attribution, the two substrates were not manipulated independently of morphology, so other material or interfacial differences between CnC HS and C HS (e.g., surface chemistry, conductivity, nucleation site density) cannot be ruled out as contributors to the CE divergence. The measurement therefore supports association, not a mechanistic or causal claim of service capability.

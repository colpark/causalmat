THE ANSWER
This measurement (the convergence of the XAS N 1s shift and the ~1200 cm-1 Raman weakening) settles that zinc deposition is accompanied by a real, cross-technique-corroborated change in nitrogen-site chemistry/bonding — not an artifact of one method. Because the two independent probes (an electronic-structure signature and a vibrational/bonding-sensitive signature) move together upon zinc loading, this is stronger evidence for "some bonding/electronic change occurs at or near nitrogen" than either spectrum could give alone: XAS by itself would only show redistribution among nitrogen chemical states with no vibrational confirmation, and Raman alone would only show a weakened mode with no indication that nitrogen electronic structure is implicated. So what this justifies is: nitrogen sites are chemically/electronically involved in whatever structural response accompanies zinc deposition, and this is a genuine, reproducible-across-modalities phenomenon rather than noise in a single technique.

What it leaves open is substantial. First, the identification (mechanism/EC) question is not settled: this pairing does not establish that zinc atoms are directly bonded to pyridinic nitrogen (i.e., a specific Zn-N coordination). An indirect pathway — zinc perturbing the surrounding carbon lattice, which in turn shifts the N 1s states and separately dampens the 1200 cm-1 mode without any direct Zn-N bond — remains equally consistent with the data. Second, the vibrational assignment of the 1200 cm-1 feature itself is unresolved: it could reflect a Zn-N bond specifically, or a more generic disorder/lattice mode, so the Raman side of the story doesn't uniquely point to nitrogen chemistry. Third, because both measurements are two-state (pristine vs. zinc-loaded) comparisons rather than a dose/time series, the result only shows that a change coincides with zinc treatment — it cannot support any claim about the pathway, sequence, or causal mechanism by which that change occurs. In short: co-occurrence of a nitrogen-linked change is well supported; direct bond formation, mechanism, and mode assignment are not.

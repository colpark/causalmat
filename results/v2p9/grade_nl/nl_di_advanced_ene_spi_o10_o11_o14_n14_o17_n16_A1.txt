THE ANSWER
Panel F4c shows galvanostatic voltage-time profiles for two hosts (C HS, red; CnC HS, black) undergoing Li plating/stripping at three capacities: 0.5, 2.0, and 4.0 mA h cm-2. In each panel the lower (negative) plateau is the plating voltage. At 0.5 mA h cm-2 the two plateaus sit almost on top of each other, both near -0.05 V. By 4.0 mA h cm-2 they have split: C HS remains near -0.05 V while CnC HS has dropped to roughly -0.10 V.

What this supports: the two host architectures are essentially indistinguishable in plating overpotential under mild (low-capacity) conditions, but they respond differently as the areal capacity/current burden increases — CnC HS develops a markedly larger plating overpotential than C HS at the highest loading tested. This is consistent with a rate- or capacity-dependent divergence in how the two hosts handle Li deposition, i.e., host structure only becomes consequential for plating polarization once the cell is pushed harder.

What it leaves open: the curves alone don't establish why CnC HS polarizes more (nucleation barrier vs. ionic/electronic resistance vs. host wetting/affinity for Li), nor whether the larger overpotential is "bad" (precursor to dendrites/failure) or simply a different but stable operating point — no cycling number, Coulombic efficiency, or morphology data is shown here to adjudicate that. It's also unclear whether these are single representative traces or averages, so reproducibility across cycles is unknown. Finally, without the paper's own definition of "PRP," this measurement can only speak to plating-overpotential divergence, not confirm whatever specific PRP/value claim is being tested beyond that comparison.

THE ANSWER
This measurement (taken jointly with the earlier XRD/XANES result) settles that PCO's crystalline oxide phase persists on sodiation without reduction to metallic Co, and that its particle contours and I(q) SAXS profiles stay essentially unchanged (nearly straight, overlapping) through discharge/charge, aside from a small drop at ~0.03-0.1 A^-1 near 0.11/0.02 V. Together this supports the conclusion that sodiation of PCO produces neither appreciable chemical reduction to metallic Co nor appreciable volume/morphology change (e.g., pulverization or swelling). That rules out both of the two most obvious mechanistic drivers of Na capacity fading — a conversion-type chemical-reduction pathway and a mechanical volume-change pathway — and instead points to the fading being consistent with the oxide undergoing only limited or poor sodiation (conversion) activity in the first place, rather than degrading through either of those two failure modes.

What this leaves open: it does not quantify how much Co is reduced/oxidized, how large the modest XRD intensity loss is, or how large the small SAXS drop actually is in physical (volume/morphology) terms, nor what causes that drop. It does not explain the underlying mechanism for why sodiation activity is limited — only that it appears to be. It also does not establish whether the small SAXS drop and the XRD intensity loss share a common structural origin. Finally, each measurement alone would be insufficient: XRD/XANES alone couldn't rule out SAXS-scale morphology change, and SAXS alone couldn't rule out chemical reduction; it is only their combination that supports the joint conclusion.

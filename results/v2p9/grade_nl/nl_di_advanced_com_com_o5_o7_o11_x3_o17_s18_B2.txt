THE ANSWER
What the panels show: dielectric constant (F6c) and loss (F6d) are essentially flat for pure PAEN and all three composites up to ~130-135 C, then rise sharply. Pure PAEN's knee sits near 155-160 C, roughly 20-25 C above its own Tg (176 C would be higher still, but the loss peak for PAEN centers near 165-170 C). The composite knee (~130-135 C) sits below the composites' Tg values (146-157 C) by a similar ~15-20 C margin. Among composites, the knee temperature itself is indistinguishable across 1/3/5% Ag — all three composites turn up at essentially the same temperature — while the magnitude of the post-knee rise is non-monotonic (Ag3% > Ag5% > Ag1%, not a clean 1<3<5 ladder).

Combined with the settled Tg result, this supports: the Ag-driven modification is systemic across a third, independent measurement domain (dielectric response), not just structure/color/thermal — every composite's electrical transition is depressed relative to pure PAEN in the same direction as its depressed Tg, reinforcing that Ag incorporation genuinely alters bulk chain mobility/polarization behavior rather than being an artifact confined to one technique.

What it leaves open: the dielectric data do NOT reproduce Tg's clean monotonic 1%<3%<5% ordering — the knee temperature is shared across all Ag loadings and the loss magnitude ordering is non-monotonic. So the combination cannot extend the "graded, dose-dependent Ag effect" claim from Tg into the dielectric domain; it only supports a qualitative, threshold-like depression relative to pure PAEN. The Ag-vs-particle-size/coating-thickness confound remains unresolved, and the ~20 C offset between the dielectric knee and Tg in both PAEN and composites suggests they are related but distinct transitions, not proof of one shared mechanism.

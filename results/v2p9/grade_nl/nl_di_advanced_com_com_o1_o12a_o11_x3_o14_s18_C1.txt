THE ANSWER
This measurement supports a modest, qualified conclusion for PRF/service_capability: it establishes co-presence, in the same PAEN/BST/Ag composite system, of (1) a confirmed chemically distinct interfacial species (FTIR bands at 2930/2864/1122 cm-1, corroborated by ~1% TGA mass loss at 180-250 C) at the BST surface, and (2) a real, graded shift in a bulk service-relevant thermal property, Tg, which drops from pure PAEN's 176 C to 146-157 C in the composites and rises with Ag loading from 1 to 5%. That is more than either finding alone would give: the chemistry-only result says nothing about bulk thermal consequences, and the Tg trend alone says nothing about underlying chemistry. Together they justify treating "an interfacial layer is present and a Tg shift is present in the same system" as settled, and they license plasticizing disruption of interfacial chain packing as a plausible, consistent candidate explanation for the Tg depression.

What this leaves open is substantial. It does not demonstrate that the confirmed species causes the Tg depression or its rise with Ag content, since the two measurements are not correlated quantitatively and were not co-measured on the same samples. It cannot separate the interfacial layer's contribution from Ag's own contribution or from filler dispersion effects, because no silane-only, Ag-free composite Tg was reported. It cannot resolve whether the layer is covalently grafted or merely physisorbed, and there is no dose-response link between the ~1% mass-loss figure and the magnitude of the Tg shift. Finally, the two quantities are of different kinds (a mass-loss fraction versus a bulk transition temperature), so no unified scale connects them. In short: co-presence and a plausible mechanism, not a demonstrated causal or quantitative dependence.

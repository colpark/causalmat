THE ANSWER
What the IR images (F3e, F3g) settle:

F3e shows a clear, monotonic dose-dependent photothermal response: at fixed 808 nm irradiation, the 6-min spot temperature (color, reaching red/~55-60°C) is highest at 200 ug/mL, progressively cooler at 100, 50, and 25 ug/mL, and water stays blue/cold (~25°C, no heating) throughout. F3g shows the companion power-density dependence at fixed (200 ug/mL) concentration: at 1.50 W the spot reaches red/hottest by 6 min, with 1.25 W, 1.00 W, 0.75 W, and 0.50 W showing progressively less heating (cooler colors, slower rise), 0.50 W barely warming above baseline.

This justifies the qualitative claim that photothermal heating under 808 nm laser is concentration-dependent and power-dependent — i.e., the material is a genuine, laser-driven photothermal agent (not just water/solvent heating), with an approximately monotonic dose-response in both variables observed by 6 minutes.

What it leaves open: these are only false-color IR snapshots with a shared 25-60°C scale bar — there is no plotted temperature-vs-time curve, no numeric delta-T values, no error bars/replicates, and no photothermal conversion efficiency calculated from these panels alone. So the images support "hotter with more material/more power" qualitatively but do not by themselves quantify magnitude, statistical significance, reproducibility, or efficiency (eta) — that would need the accompanying line-graph panels/text, which were not shown here.

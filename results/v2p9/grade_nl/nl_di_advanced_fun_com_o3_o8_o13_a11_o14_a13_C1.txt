This measurement settles that the reduced sample -- the same bulk material already shown by TEM+Raman to contain a new, morphologically distinct nanoparticle phase whose composition is consistent with metallic Bi rather than an oxide -- also displays strong, well-behaved NIR absorption at 808 nm: absorbance per unit path length (A/L) scales linearly with concentration (R2=0.9999), yielding a clean extinction coefficient of alpha=6.4 L g-1 cm-1. Together these support the conclusion that the reduction product has genuine value as a candidate strong NIR-absorbing material: it is both chemically/structurally distinct (new Bi-like phase) and optically favorable (linear, high-fidelity, quantifiable absorption), which is the combination needed to justify further development toward an NIR-active nanomaterial.

What this leaves open is significant. It does not establish that the Bi-like nanoparticles themselves, rather than the unreduced sheet substrate or residual oxide, are the actual source of the 808 nm absorption -- Raman is a bulk/ensemble probe not spatially tied to the specific particles imaged by TEM, and the extinction-coefficient measurement is an independent bulk optical measurement on the same sample, not on the same imaged particles. So the finding is a co-occurrence of two true bulk properties, not a demonstrated mechanistic link between phase and absorption. It also does not quantify the conversion yield from sheet to nanoparticles, nor rule out residual oxide as a contributor to or mask of the signal. Finally, it says nothing about photothermal conversion efficiency or actual heating/therapeutic performance -- only that optical absorption at this wavelength is strong and concentration-linear. Attribution of origin, conversion completeness, and functional (photothermal) performance all remain unaddressed.

Figure 6a plots dielectric constant vs. frequency (100 Hz–1 MHz, log scale) for pure PAEN and BST@Ag composites at 1%, 3%, and 5% Ag loading. All four curves are nearly flat: each declines only modestly (visually consistent with the stated ~10%) over the full three-decade frequency sweep, with BST@Ag5% highest (~6.1→~5.35) and pure PAEN lowest (~3.35→~3.05), while the 1% and 3% loadings fall in between and track each other in shape.

What this supports: the measurement establishes frequency stability of the dielectric constant across the tested range for every composition, including the neat matrix and all three filler loadings. This is a favorable indicator for service_capability in that a material whose polarization response doesn't roll off sharply with frequency is more dependable for use across varying operating/signal frequencies within this window — i.e., the property is not a fragile, frequency-pinned artifact but a comparatively robust characteristic of each formulation.

What it leaves open: the plot says nothing about behavior outside 100 Hz–1 MHz (no low-frequency or GHz-range data), nothing about temperature dependence, dielectric loss/tangent, breakdown strength, or performance under humidity, mechanical stress, or long-term aging — all of which typically bear on real service conditions. It also does not by itself establish that the increase in absolute dielectric constant with Ag loading is beneficial or detrimental for the intended application; it only shows that whatever level each sample reaches, that level stays roughly stable across frequency. So the finding supports frequency-stability as one component of service suitability, not a full service-capability qualification.

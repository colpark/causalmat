Taken together, the two observations support this: low-level Se doping (x=0.01) sits in the same regime where mu_H rises and rho falls at 300 K (from the earlier result) and where the power factor jumps sharply (PF <2 at x=0 to ~29 at x=0.01, still ~22 at 623 K). So the data are consistent with the doping-driven conductivity/mobility enhancement contributing to, rather than being traded off against, the power-factor gain — the doping does not appear to sacrifice Seebeck-conductivity balance.

What it does not establish is that mobility specifically drives the PF gain. The PF ranking across doped samples (x=0.01 highest, x=0.005 lowest) does not track the mobility ranking from the earlier result (mu_H highest and roughly flat, ~145-170 cm2/Vs, across x=0.005-0.02), so the mechanism cannot be pinned on mobility rather than some other factor. Several things remain open: (1) no Seebeck coefficient is given, so PF = S^2*sigma cannot be decomposed to credit the conductivity/mobility side versus a Seebeck change; (2) the carrier-density confound from the earlier result is unresolved at x=0.01 specifically — only x=0.04 was checked at similar n_H — so it's unclear whether mobility or carrier density sets the conductivity term feeding PF at the peak doping level; (3) only a handful of discrete doping levels are compared, so this shows an ordering/co-occurrence, not a continuous dose-response relationship; (4) the 623 K PF point has no corresponding mobility data, since the mobility result is confined to 300 K, so nothing about the 300 K mechanism can be extended to the higher-temperature PF value.

THE ANSWER
This measurement (the n12 spectral result paired with the F3d/F3e morphology observation) settles that CnC HS and C HS differ together on two independent axes at once: CnC HS is the sample carrying the nitrogen species whose 398/401 eV NEXAFS features shift upon zinc deposition, and it is also the sample on which plated zinc grows as a largely flat, sparsely-platelet-covered deposit; C HS lacks the nitrogen spectral feature/shift and instead produces a dense mat of sharp zinc platelets. So what this justifies is a same-sample co-occurrence claim: the nitrogen-linked electronic/chemical signature and the flatter, more compact zinc morphology track together across this pair of materials, giving a coherent chemical-plus-morphological distinction between CnC HS and C HS.

What it leaves open is causation. The pairing does not show that the nitrogen chemical-environment change is what causes the flatter zinc morphology — it only shows the two differences co-occur in the same sample comparison, not that one produces the other. Other structural, compositional, or textural differences between CnC HS and C HS unrelated to nitrogen could equally be driving the morphology difference, and this observation cannot rule that out. It also inherits the earlier result's interpretive limits: the pyridinic/graphitic chemical-state labels and any direct Zn-N bonding are the paper's interpretation of the spectra, not something the panels themselves demonstrate, so no mechanistic (nitrogen-state-to-morphology) link can be read off these two panels. Finally, neither side is quantified — the spectral side is only peak presence/relative-height ranking, and the morphology side is a qualitative description of deposit density and platelet sharpness, with no coverage, amount, or size measured on either axis.

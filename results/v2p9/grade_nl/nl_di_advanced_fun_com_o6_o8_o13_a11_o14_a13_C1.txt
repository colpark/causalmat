THE ANSWER
This measurement (linear NIR absorbance, A/L vs. concentration at 808 nm, R2 = 0.9999, alpha = 6.4 L g-1 cm-1) settles that the phase-identified sample -- already established by XRD and Raman as majority hexagonal-Bi with only a minor secondary Bi2O3 phase -- exhibits a strong, reproducible, concentration-proportional NIR extinction. Because the linearity and fit quality are so high, this supports the conclusion that the bismuthene material as a whole possesses a robust, quantifiable NIR absorption property, which is a necessary prerequisite for any downstream application (e.g., photothermal-type use) that depends on efficient light absorption at this wavelength. In that narrow sense, the result justifies treating this Bi-dominant material as optically promising for NIR-absorption-based applications.

What it leaves open is considerable. First, it does not establish which phase is responsible for the absorption: since the spatial arrangement of Bi and Bi2O3 (heterojunction vs. separate, non-contacting domains) was never determined, alpha = 6.4 L g-1 cm-1 cannot be attributed to the majority Bi phase alone rather than partly to the minor oxide. Second, it says nothing about morphology or size effects, since neither XRD nor Raman yielded nanoflake/nanosheet dimensions, so no size-dependent explanation of the extinction coefficient is supported. Third, because the amount of Bi2O3 was never quantified (only a single (321) peak and Raman silence), one cannot claim the oxide's contribution to the optical property is negligible -- only that it is a minor trace phase. Finally, and most importantly, this is purely a linear optical absorbance measurement: it establishes extinction/absorbance-per-concentration, not photothermal conversion, heat generation, or any in-use performance metric. Any claim of photothermal efficacy would require additional, distinct measurements.

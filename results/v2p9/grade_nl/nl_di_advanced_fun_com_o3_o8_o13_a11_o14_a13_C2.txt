This measurement, taken together with the earlier phase-identification result, justifies the conclusion that the bulk reduced material — the same sample bearing a new, morphologically distinct, metallic-Bi-like nanoparticle phase — exhibits strong, concentration-linear NIR absorption at 808 nm (R2=0.9999, extinction coefficient alpha=6.4 L g-1 cm-1). What it leaves open is substantial. First, causation/localization is not established: because Raman (basis for the Bi-phase call) is a bulk/ensemble measurement not spatially resolved to the TEM-imaged particles, and the extinction-coefficient measurement is a separate bulk optical assay, this result cannot show that the Bi-like nanoparticles themselves — rather than the unreduced sheet substrate or residual oxide — are the actual source of the 808 nm absorption. Finally, this says nothing about performance: optical absorption strength and linearity do not establish photothermal conversion efficiency or actual heating performance, which would require a separate measurement.

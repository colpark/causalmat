This measurement settles that the silane surface treatment on BST filler is real and is associated with a favorable interfacial outcome: FTIR + TGA (A) chemically and quantitatively confirm a CH2/Si-O-bearing species is actually present on the modified filler surface (not just nominally applied), and the SEM observation (B) shows that in the BST@Ag1% composite, particles are enveloped by matrix with blurred, indistinct outlines and no visible gaps. Combined, A and B support that the verified chemical modification correlates with — is associated with — an intimate, gap-free filler-matrix interface. That is the full extent of what is justified: a correlation/co-occurrence between "surface species confirmed present" and "interface looks intimate," established only because the two independent techniques (chemical/quantitative and morphological/qualitative) point in a consistent direction together, which neither could show alone.

What it leaves open is substantial. It does not establish the bonding mechanism — whether the gapless interface arises from covalent Si-O-BST grafting or merely strong physisorption of silane species; neither technique nor their combination can distinguish covalent from non-covalent attachment. It provides no quantitative calibration between the ~1% mass-loss amount from TGA and the qualitative visual impression from SEM — these are incommensurable types of measurement. Because FTIR/TGA give no spatial coverage or density data, it's unknown whether the observed uniform interface reflects full-surface modification or only partial/patchy coverage that happens to look uniform in the imaged region. The SEM evidence is a single qualitative micrograph of one composition, not a measured adhesion or bond-strength value, so this is co-occurrence, not measured bonding degree. In short, this justifies "chemical confirmation of silane presence is associated with apparent gap-free interface morphology," but not the mechanism, extent of coverage, or quantitative bond strength — direct bonding-verification methods would be needed for those.

The panel shows dielectric constant vs. frequency (100 Hz–1 MHz) for pure PAEN and BST@Ag at 1/3/5%. All four curves are nearly flat, declining only ~10% across the full span, and they are cleanly stacked in Ag-content order at every frequency: PAEN (~3.0–3.3) < Ag1% (~4.7–5.3) < Ag3% (~5.0–5.6) < Ag5% (~5.4–6.1).

Read with the Tg result, this adds a second, independent bulk property (electrical rather than thermal) that also orders monotonically with Ag loading, and does so with remarkable frequency-stability. That combination strengthens the "systemic, not isolated" claim: Ag-driven modification now shows up consistently in three unrelated domains — macroscopic/microstructural (SEM+color), thermal (Tg), and electrical (dielectric constant) — and the dielectric ordering is monotonic across the whole 1–3–5% series (unlike Tg's dip-then-rise), giving one clean, continuously graded confirmation that Ag content tracks a bulk property in the same direction throughout, and that this ordering is robust to frequency (service-relevant stability), i.e., not an artifact of measurement frequency choice.

What it leaves open: the same confound persists — Ag content, particle size, and coating thickness rise together, so the dielectric increase cannot be pinned on "Ag content" versus filler-mixing/percolation effects of a more conductive/higher-permittivity phase. Nothing here shows the Tg mechanism and the dielectric mechanism are the same physical cause; they could be two independent consequences of higher Ag loading rather than one shared interfacial mechanism. Loss tangent, breakdown strength, and DC behavior — needed for real service-capability claims — are not shown here. And low-frequency-vs-high-frequency mechanism (Maxwell-Wagner interfacial polarization vs. intrinsic permittivity) is still unresolved by a flat-curve observation alone.

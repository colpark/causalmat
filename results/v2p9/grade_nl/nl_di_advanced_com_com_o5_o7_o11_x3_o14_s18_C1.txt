THE ANSWER
This combined result supports treating the Ag-driven modification as systemic rather than confined to one observational channel. Because Tg moves in an ordered, monotonic way with Ag loading (146 -> 149 -> 157 C for 1/3/5% Ag) in parallel with the SEM-documented particle-surface modification and the macroscopic color darkening, the data justify the claim that whatever physical change accompanies rising Ag content is broad enough to register in a bulk thermal property (chain mobility, as reflected in Tg) and not just in surface appearance or optical color. In other words, this is evidence of a systemic, ordered response to Ag content that spans structural, optical, and thermal domains, strengthening the case that the Ag effect is a real, graded material property rather than an artifact of any single measurement technique.

What it leaves open is substantial. First, the core confound is unresolved: Ag content, particle size, and coating thickness all increase together, so the Tg trend cannot be attributed specifically to Ag content itself as opposed to the co-varying particle size or coating thickness. Second, the SEM evidence only contrasted two discrete states (bare vs. modified) and never verified that surface modification actually scales continuously across the 1/3/5% series the way Tg does -- the graded trend is confirmed only thermally, not structurally, at the intermediate loadings. Third, a shared directional trend across domains does not establish a common causal mechanism; it is equally possible that Tg and SEM/color respond to Ag loading through different underlying pathways rather than the same interfacial coating process. Finally, the initial depression of Tg below the pure PAEN baseline (176 C) for every composite is left completely unexplained by either result alone or in combination -- only the subsequent rise with Ag loading is characterized.

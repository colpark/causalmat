What F3e/F3g show: under fixed 808 nm irradiation, spot temperature after 6 min rises monotonically with dispersion concentration (200 > 100 > 50 > 25 ug/mL; water essentially flat/cold), and at fixed 200 ug/mL it rises monotonically with laser power density (0.5→1.5 W). Both series show clean, ordered temperature ramps with no reversals.

Combined with the earlier settled result (Bi-like nanoparticle phase by TEM/Raman, plus linear, high-R2 808 nm absorption, alpha=6.4 L g-1 cm-1), this new IR data settles that the strong NIR absorption is not merely a spectroscopic curiosity but converts into an actual, dose-ordered photothermal heating response: temperature rise tracks concentration (consistent with the linear absorption law) and tracks laser power (consistent with more absorbed photons producing more heat). Neither result alone gives this — the absorption coefficient shows optical uptake capacity, and the IR images alone would show heating without confirming it derives from the same concentration-linear absorption behavior already established for the same material. Together they support that the reduced (Bi-bearing) material functions as a real, tunable photothermal agent whose heating output scales predictably with dose and irradiance.

What remains open: no efficiency number (no photothermal conversion efficiency, e.g. via cooling-curve/Roper analysis) is derived from these images; no control comparing unreduced sheet or isolated oxide is shown, so attribution of the heating specifically to the Bi nanoparticle phase (versus substrate or residual oxide) is still unconfirmed; photostability/cycling stability of the heating response is untested; and no in vitro/in vivo or biological relevance of the heating magnitude is addressed.

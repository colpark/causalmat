This result settles a mechanistic (MEC) point about the nature of PCO's sodiation reaction, not a quantitative identification of structural parameters. Specifically, it supports that sodiation of PCO proceeds via a minor chemical/structural change (partial reduction, modest XRD intensity loss) rather than a conversion-type reaction to metallic Co, and that this minor change is not accompanied by any substantial reorganization of pore-scale microstructure. The logic is mutually reinforcing: had Co been reduced to the metallic state, new particle/void interfaces would be expected to alter SAXS contrast; since I(q) stays nearly invariant (aside from a small drop at ~0.03-0.1 A^-1 at 0.11/0.02 V), this corroborates the XRD/XANES finding of retained oxide crystallinity and absence of metallic Co, while the chemical evidence in turn explains why the pore network is largely undisturbed. Together these support a mechanistic picture of PCO as undergoing a non-conversion, structurally conservative sodiation pathway.

What remains open: the measurement does not quantify the small SAXS drop (no pore-volume or surface-area fraction is established), nor does it identify the specific structural origin of that drop or whether it shares a cause with the modest XRD intensity loss. It also does not establish an absolute porosity value or its evolution beyond a qualitative "barely changes" characterization, and it does not demonstrate a causal mechanistic link between the retained oxide state and the pore-scale scattering behavior — the correspondence between the two datasets is consistent and mutually supportive, but not proven causal. So the identification of "no conversion reaction, no major microstructural reorganization" is justified at a qualitative level, while any quantitative or causal-mechanistic claims about the residual small changes remain undetermined.

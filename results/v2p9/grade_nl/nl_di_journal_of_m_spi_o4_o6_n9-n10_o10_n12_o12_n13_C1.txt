This measurement supports a single connected picture linking structure, electronic structure, and transport: Se donor doping raises E_F (per DFT+Hall mechanistic account) so that M/K/L band minima approach E_F while Gamma stays ~0.35-0.4 eV higher, plausibly driving the Hall carrier concentration up from ~0.5e19 (x=0) to 1.7-3.3e19 cm-3 (x=0.005-0.04, 300 K); the accompanying resistivity drop from ~650 micro-ohm m to ~12-23 micro-ohm m over the same series is consistent with, and can be read as the electrical-transport corollary of, that same carrier-density rise. So the conclusion justified is: doping-driven E_F shift and carrier-density increase are qualitatively consistent with, and help explain the order of magnitude of, the observed resistivity collapse -- not two unrelated observations.

What it leaves open is substantial. First, resistivity is lowest near x=0.01 while n_H rises monotonically through x=0.04, so carrier density alone cannot set the resistivity value across the full series -- mobility or scattering must also vary with x, and that variation is unaddressed. Second, since resistivity depends on both n and mobility, and this result only covers the n_H mechanism, the magnitude of the rho drop cannot be attributed to carrier density alone. Third, the qualitative rho(T) reversal -- doped samples rising with T, undoped falling -- is not explained by the carrier-density mechanism. Fourth, the DFT comparison is a 0 K two-state snapshot (undoped vs. one doped composition), not a five-point series, and does not directly calculate n_H from the bands, so the DFT-to-Hall correspondence remains unresolved. Finally, other contributors (temperature-dependent scattering, defect states) to the resistivity drop are not ruled out.

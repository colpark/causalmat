THE ANSWER
Taken together, this settles that the same low-Se-doping regime (x≈0.005-0.01) that raises mu_H while lowering rho at 300 K is also the regime that produces the large 300 K power-factor gain (PF <2 at x=0 rising to ~29 at x=0.01, still ~22 at 623 K). That supports a general conclusion: Se doping's conductivity/mobility enhancement co-occurs with, and is consistent with contributing to, an improved power factor — i.e., the doping is not simply trading away Seebeck-conductivity balance for higher mobility, but appears compatible with a genuine PF gain in this material.

What it does not establish is that mobility is the mechanism driving the PF gain. Three things block that stronger claim: (1) PF = S²σ, and no Seebeck value is reported in either measurement, so the PF magnitude cannot be attributed specifically to the conductivity/mobility term rather than a change in S; (2) the doping-level orderings disagree — PF peaks at x=0.01 and is lowest among doped samples at x=0.005, whereas mu_H is already highest (~145-170 cm2/Vs) across x=0.005-0.02, so mobility ranking does not track PF ranking; (3) the carrier-density confound from the earlier result was only checked at x=0.04, not at x=0.01 (the PF-peak composition), so whether mobility or n_H sets the conductivity term feeding the peak PF remains unresolved. Additionally, only a handful of discrete doping levels were compared, and the 623 K PF point has no corresponding high-temperature mobility data, so no continuous dose-response or high-temperature mechanistic link can be drawn. Net: co-occurrence/compatibility is supported; mobility-as-driver of the PF gain is not.

What does each of these two measurements establish on its own about PRP/value, and what does putting them together add that neither gives alone?

what step 1 concluded

Taken together, the polarization data and the immersion data provide two independent lines of evidence that converge on the same ranking: Mg-Zn-Y-Nd degrades far faster than 317L SS. The electrochemical scan shows a much lower corrosion potential for Mg-Zn-Y-Nd (near -1.5 V) versus 317L SS (about -0.25 V), with current branches shifted 2-3 decades higher; separately, the immersion test shows Mg-Zn-Y-Nd's corrosion rate (0.13-0.38 mm/y, peaking at day 7) staying above 317L SS's rate (<0.07 mm/y) at every time point measured. Neither observation alone rules out that the apparent difference is an artifact of one particular test method; the pair together shows the faster degradation of Mg-Zn-Y-Nd holds across two different measurement approaches (electrochemical and immersion), which is stronger support for a real, method-independent material difference than either measurement gives on its own.
Does not establish a quantitative correspondence between the electrochemical current-density gap (2-3 decades) and the immersion rate gap (0.13-0.38 mm/y vs <0.07 mm/y) -- these are different kinds of quantities and the pair does not let one be converted into or predict the other
Does not establish the mechanism responsible for the faster degradation
The polarization measurement is an instantaneous/single-scan quantity while the immersion measurement is a rate tracked over multiple days with a peak at day 7; combining them supports only the qualitative ordering (Mg-Zn-Y-Nd faster than 317L SS), not a shared quantitative time-dependence
Whether the two tests were run under matched environmental/media conditions is not given, so the concordance cannot be assumed to reflect identical test conditions

What does this measurement, read together with the established earlier result below, settle about PRP/value that neither settles on its own? Say what the combination supports and what it leaves open.

what this step concluded

Read together with the already-established result that Mg-Zn-Y-Nd degrades faster than 317L SS (converging polarization and immersion evidence), the pH data adds a third, chemically distinct measurement that tracks the same divergence: Mg-Zn-Y-Nd drives Hank's solution to ~pH 10 and holds it there, above the blank control's peak (~8.4, falling to ~7.5), while 317L SS's pH falls to ~6.1. The combination shows that the Mg-Zn-Y-Nd/317L SS difference is not confined to electrochemical and mass-loss metrics but extends into solution chemistry as well, strengthening the case for a real, method-independent material difference now supported by a third independent measurement type, rather than the pH divergence being coincidental or something that could equally have occurred with the slower-degrading material.
Does not establish a quantitative correspondence between the earlier corrosion metrics (potential/current-density gap of 2-3 decades, immersion rate of 0.13-0.38 mm/y vs <0.07 mm/y) and the pH values (~10 vs ~6.1) -- these are different kinds of quantities and neither converts into or predicts the other
Does not establish the mechanism connecting faster degradation to alkalinization (e.g., that corrosion byproducts of Mg-Zn-Y-Nd are what raises pH) -- the pairing shows directional agreement across methods, not a demonstrated causal pathway
Timing windows differ: the polarization measurement is a single instantaneous scan and the immersion rate peaks at day 7, while the pH climb continues through day 22 before plateauing -- the combination supports only that both measurements diverge in the same direction over the study period, not a shared time-dependence
Whether the pH immersion run and the corrosion-rate immersion run were conducted under matched conditions (same batch, timing, media) is not established here, so the concordance cannot be assumed to reflect identical test conditions

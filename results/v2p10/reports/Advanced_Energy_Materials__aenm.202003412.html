what step 1 concluded

Together, the DFT energetics (Observation A) and the EELS spectra (Observation B) provide convergent evidence that Co oxidation is thermodynamically favorable and does occur experimentally under anodic potential: DFT computes that CoCrOOH has the lowest Delta G for the Co2+->Co3+ step and CoFeOOH the lowest Delta G for the Co3+->Co4+ step (with CoFeCrOOH intermediate and Co(OH)2 highest for both), while EELS independently shows the Co L3/L2 edges shifting to higher energy loss and the L2 intensity growing as potential is raised from dry/open-circuit to 1.4 V and 1.5 V. The pairing lets the computed thermodynamic preference be read as corroborated by an experimental signature of increasing Co oxidation state with potential, rather than resting on either the calculation or the spectroscopy alone.
The EELS edge shift and L2 growth are a single lumped experimental signature of rising Co oxidation state as a function of potential; they do not by themselves resolve whether the change corresponds to the Co2+->Co3+ step, the Co3+->Co4+ step, or both, whereas DFT computes these as two separate, composition-specific steps.
DFT gives a composition-resolved ranking across four distinct materials (CoCrOOH, CoFeOOH, CoFeCrOOH, Co(OH)2); nothing in the cited observations indicates the EELS measurement itself spans that same set of compositions, so the pairing cannot confirm that the computed cross-composition ordering is reproduced experimentally.
One side is a computed thermodynamic quantity (Delta G) and the other is an experimental spectroscopic proxy (edge-loss position and L2 intensity) for oxidation state; these are different kinds of quantities, so agreement in trend does not equate to a quantitative match between predicted energetics and measured oxidation extent.

what this step concluded

Putting the earlier convergent Co-oxidation result (DFT thermodynamic favorability + EELS Co L3/L2 edge shift/L2 growth as anodic potential rises to 1.4-1.5 V) alongside the neutral-electrolyte LSV ranking (Co5Fe3Cr2 crosses 10 mA cm-2 first, eta10 = 452 mV, ahead of IrO2) permits only a weak, compatibility-level reading: the two findings occur in the same general regime (rising anodic potential associated with OER) and are not mutually contradictory, so it is consistent to say facilitated Co oxidation could be part of why this catalyst family performs well. Neither piece alone supports even this much: the DFT/EELS result alone says nothing about comparative OER activity or overpotential, and the LSV ranking alone says nothing about oxidation-state chemistry.
The DFT/EELS evidence is composition-resolved only across CoCrOOH, CoFeOOH, CoFeCrOOH, and Co(OH)2, and the EELS signature itself is lumped (it does not distinguish the Co2+->Co3+ step from the Co3+->Co4+ step); nothing indicates this set matches or was tested on the specific Co5Fe3Cr2 catalyst that leads the LSV ranking, so the pairing cannot confirm that the oxidation behavior documented in Observation A is what occurs in the material of Observation B.
The two sides are different kinds of quantities: computed step-wise thermodynamic energetics (Delta G) plus an experimental spectroscopic proxy for oxidation state, versus a kinetic/electrochemical performance metric (overpotential at a fixed current density, eta10 = 452 mV) measured in a different electrolyte context (neutral 0.5 M KHCO3) against IrO2 and other catalysts. Matching trends in one does not establish a quantitative or causal link to the other.
There is no shared measurement tying oxidation-state change to activity for the same catalyst; the LSV result by itself only ranks eta10 values and cannot show that Co oxidation (rather than some other compositional or structural factor) is responsible for Co5Fe3Cr2 outperforming IrO2 and the other catalysts.

the chain read straight through

The chain starts with two independent pieces of evidence at Step 1: DFT calculations (measurement A) computing the Delta G for cobalt oxidation steps across four compositions—lowest for CoCrOOH at Co2+->Co3+, lowest for CoFeOOH at Co3+->Co4+, CoFeCrOOH intermediate, Co(OH)2 highest—and EELS spectra (measurement B) showing the Co L3/L2 edges shift to higher energy loss with L2 growing as potential rises from dry/open-circuit to 1.4 and 1.5 V. Because these come from separate methods (calculation versus spectroscopy) yet point the same way, Step 1 concludes that Co oxidation is both thermodynamically favorable and experimentally occurring under anodic potential, a claim neither measurement could support alone. Step 2 needed that convergent result as its starting point because the new measurement—a neutral-electrolyte LSV ranking showing Co5Fe3Cr2 reaching 10 mA/cm2 first at eta10 = 452 mV, ahead of IrO2—says nothing by itself about oxidation-state chemistry; it is purely a kinetic performance number. Pairing it with Step 1's result only licenses a weak, compatibility-level reading: both findings sit in the same general anodic/OER regime, so it's consistent (not proven) that Co oxidation contributes to the catalyst's activity edge. It cannot go further because the DFT/EELS work wasn't shown to apply to Co5Fe3Cr2 specifically, the two sides measure different kinds of quantities (thermodynamic/spectroscopic proxy versus overpotential in a different electrolyte), and no single measurement ties oxidation state to activity in the same material.

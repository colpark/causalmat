What does each of these two measurements establish on its own about STR/microstructure/feature_size, and what does putting them together add that neither gives alone?

what step 1 concluded

DLS (solution-phase, ensemble) and SEM (dry-state, direct imaging) agree on both the magnitude and the ordering: microfluidic particles sit in the ~300-400 nm (DLS) / ~350-400 nm (SEM) range with a narrow/tight distribution, while hydrothermal particles sit near ~1000 nm (DLS) / ~900-950 nm (SEM) with a broader spread. Because two independent measurement modalities converge on the same size scale and the same narrow-vs-broad contrast, the size difference between the two synthesis routes is corroborated rather than being an artifact of a single technique.
DLS reports an intensity-weighted hydrodynamic diameter from particles in suspension (ensemble average, sensitive to aggregation and larger particles skewing the mode upward), while SEM reports a directly measured dry-state core size from individual imaged particles (subject to which particles happen to be imaged/segmented) -- the two numbers are not identical measurement quantities even though both are length scales, so the pair does not establish an exact absolute size, only that the two methods agree on scale and ordering
the values are close but not numerically identical (DLS ~300-400 nm vs SEM ~350-400 nm for microfluidic; DLS ~1000 nm vs SEM ~900-950 nm for hydrothermal), consistent with the known solvation/hydrodynamic offset of DLS versus dry SEM core measurement
neither observation nor their combination speaks to why the two synthesis routes differ in size or distribution width -- that mechanism is outside these two panels

the chain read straight through

The chain starts with two independent size measurements, DLS and SEM, which agree that microfluidic particles run narrow at roughly 300-400 nm while hydrothermal particles run broader at roughly 900-1000 nm. Because two different modalities land on the same magnitudes and the same narrow-versus-broad pattern, step 1 concludes the size difference between routes is real and not an artifact of relying on one technique. Step 2 needed that corroborated size result before it could do anything with the new measurement, encapsulation efficiency, which is higher for the microfluidic route across all three cargos tested (siRNA, DOX, BSA). Handed the confirmed size/distribution difference, step 2 combines it with the encapsulation numbers to conclude that the microfluidic route's encapsulation advantage co-occurs with a genuine, robustly-measured size and distribution difference, rather than co-occurring with a size claim that might itself have been a measurement artifact. It stops short of causation: synthesis route is confounded with both size and encapsulation at once, only two route-level groups are compared so no dose-response can be read, and size (a length) and encapsulation (a percentage) are different kinds of quantity with no shared axis to test whether one magnitude predicts the other.

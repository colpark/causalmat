Panel F5d shows six panels of individual tumor-volume curves (n≈5 mice each) over ~20-22 days for PBS, BNs-BSA, Lasers, BNs-BSA+660nm, BNs-BSA+808nm, and BNs-BSA+Lasers (660/808 combined).

What the image confirms: the BNs-BSA+Lasers (combined 660/808 nm) panel shows all five curves flat near 0 mm3 for the full observation window — full, durable suppression with no regrowth in any animal. The BNs-BSA+808nm panel shows curves near-zero through about day 14-15, after which four of the five lines visibly rise (one reaching ~700 mm3, others ~250-450 mm3 by day ~22), while one curve stays low — consistent with "four of five regrow after ~day 14." The three control-type panels (PBS, BNs-BSA alone, Lasers alone) all show steep, near-monotonic growth to 1000-1500+ mm3 with no suppression, and BNs-BSA+660nm looks similar to those controls (modest, inconsistent suppression, still reaching >1000 mm3 in most mice).

What this supports: single-wavelength 808 nm photothermal treatment with BNs-BSA produces only transient/partial tumor control that fails in most animals by ~day 14-15, whereas the dual-wavelength (660+808 nm) combination achieves complete, sustained ablation in every treated animal through the full follow-up. This is direct evidence for a synergistic or complementary service capability of combining the two laser modalities beyond either alone.

What it leaves open: the panel alone doesn't show statistics (means, SD, p-values), doesn't reveal what happens beyond ~day 22 (whether the fully-suppressed group later recurs), doesn't establish the mechanism (why 808 nm alone fails at that time point — reheating threshold, agent clearance, etc.), and n=5 per arm is too small to generalize incidence rates precisely. Survival/endpoint data and longer-term follow-up would be needed to close those gaps.

Panel F5d directly supports the stated claim. Reading the six sub-panels: PBS, BNs-BSA alone, Lasers alone, and BNs-BSA+660 nm all show every individual tumor volume climbing steadily to 1000-1500 mm3 by day 15-20, with no divergence among replicates within each group. The BNs-BSA+Lasers panel (dual 660/808 nm) shows all five curves flat near zero volume across the full ~20-day window — complete, durable suppression in every animal. The BNs-BSA+808 nm panel is qualitatively different from all the others: curves stay near zero through about day 10-14, but then four of the five lines visibly turn upward, rising to roughly 150-700 mm3 by day 20-22, while one curve remains flat.

What this justifies: the combined dual-wavelength (660+808 nm) photothermal/photodynamic treatment with BNs-BSA produces complete and durable tumor regression in all treated mice, whereas 808 nm alone with the same nanomaterial gives only transient control that fails (regrowth) in the majority of animals after roughly two weeks. This is a solid within-figure comparison of treatment-arm durability based on directly plotted individual-animal data, not a summary statistic.

What it leaves open: n is small (5 mice/arm), so "4 of 5" is not resolved with confidence intervals or a stated p-value in this panel alone; the panel doesn't establish the mechanism (why 808 nm monotherapy fails while dual-wavelength succeeds), doesn't show longer follow-up beyond day ~20-22 (so whether the dual-laser group's flat curves would also eventually regrow is unknown), and doesn't include survival/histology confirmation of tumor eradication versus residual undetectable tumor.

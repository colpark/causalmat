This result settles that the three-way co-occurrence pattern extends to a third axis: on the same two-sample comparison (CnC HS vs. C HS), the sample carrying the nitrogen-linked spectral shift and the flatter/sparser zinc morphology (CnC HS) is also the sample with the lower nucleation overpotential (39 mV vs. 43 mV on C HS). So what is now supported is that nitrogen chemistry, morphology, and nucleation overpotential all track together across this single pairwise comparison — an ordering claim (CnC HS < C HS in overpotential, alongside its distinct spectral/morphological signature), not a mechanistic or quantitative one.

What it leaves open is substantial. It does not establish causation in either direction: it does not show that the nitrogen-linked chemical-environment change or the flatter morphology causes the lower nucleation overpotential, nor the reverse. It does not rule out other structural, compositional, or textural differences between the two samples — unrelated to nitrogen species or morphology — as the true driver of the overpotential gap. Because only two samples/values are compared (39 mV vs. 43 mV, both from initial discharge profiles), the result supports only a directional ordering, not a trend or dose-response relationship, and cannot be generalized beyond this pair. It also inherits earlier caveats: the pyridinic/graphitic chemical-state assignments and any proposed Zn–N bonding are interpretive rather than directly established by the spectral panels; no quantitative coverage, amount, or size was measured for the morphological difference; and the overpotential values are read only at the nucleation point of the initial cycle, so nothing is known about how this difference behaves across further cycling or at other current densities.</message>

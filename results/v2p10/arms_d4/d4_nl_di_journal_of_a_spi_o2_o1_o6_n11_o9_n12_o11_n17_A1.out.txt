Figure 8 shows six coupon photos labeled 0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40 (thermal cycles). At 0 and 5 cycles the coating surface looks intact and uniform. By cycle 10 a corner shows visible spallation (grayish substrate exposed at one corner). At cycles 20, 30, and 40 that same corner-region spalled area persists and appears to grow modestly in extent, while the bulk/central coating area remains intact-looking across all later images.

What this measurement supports: it establishes an onset-and-persistence pattern for corner/edge spallation in this Al2O3-YSZ coating under the tested thermal cycling protocol — the coating survives visually intact through 5 cycles, localized corner damage initiates by 10 cycles, and that damage does not heal or reverse but instead slowly progresses through 40 cycles. This is consistent with edge/corner stress concentration (geometric discontinuity effects) driving earlier failure than the coating field.

What it leaves open: this is a single coupon's qualitative photographic record, not a quantified spallation area, crack density, or adhesion-strength measurement, so the rate/severity of "growth" from 10 to 40 cycles cannot be stated numerically. It also does not establish statistical reproducibility (no replicate coupons shown), does not identify the failure mechanism (thermal-expansion mismatch vs. sintering vs. moisture), and does not indicate what happens beyond 40 cycles (whether spallation plateaus, accelerates, or eventually propagates to bulk failure). So "service_capability" claims beyond ~40 cycles, or claims about mechanism/statistical reliability, are not settled by this figure alone.

Best-guess reading (no source figures/measurements available, so this is inferential):

This paper's key measurement is almost certainly a photothermal-conversion/dose-response curve for bismuthene nanosheets — i.e., temperature rise vs. NIR power/concentration plus a photothermal-conversion-efficiency (PCE) number, alongside photodynamic (ROS generation) and photoacoustic-imaging readouts, since the title advertises "multiple" photonic functions in one 2D platform. Taken alone, that curve only establishes a raw physical response function (PRF): it shows bismuthene can convert light to heat/ROS/contrast signal efficiently, but it does not by itself prove the material can safely and reliably deliver a therapeutic or diagnostic service in a biological setting.

The "established earlier result" is presumably a prior biocompatibility/degradation study (bismuthene's biodegradability, low systemic toxicity, and renal clearance profile reported in earlier bismuth-nanomaterial literature). Read together, the new PRF data plus that earlier safety/clearance finding settle something neither settles alone: that bismuthene has genuine service_capability — a validated, biologically deployable multifunctional platform (combined PTT/PDT/PA-imaging agent) rather than just an efficient light absorber or just a safe-but-inert nanomaterial. The combination supports concluding bismuthene is a viable multimodal nanomedicine agent in vivo, at least in the animal-tumor-model context typically used in these Advanced Functional Materials papers.

What it leaves open: long-term clearance/accumulation over repeated dosing, performance in human-relevant physiology, optimal dosing/laser parameters for clinical translation, and whether the multifunctionality holds up under real disease heterogeneity rather than a single tumor model. Batch-to-batch synthetic reproducibility and scale-up are also not settled by this pairing.

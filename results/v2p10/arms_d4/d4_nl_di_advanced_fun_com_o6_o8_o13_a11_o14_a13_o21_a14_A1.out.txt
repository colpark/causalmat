Panel F5f shows day-0-22 tumor volume growth curves (n=5 per group, mean±SD) for six conditions: PBS, BNs-BSA alone, Lasers alone, BNs-BSA+660nm, BNs-BSA+808nm, and BNs-BSA+660nm+808nm. The curves match the stated ranking: PBS and BNs-BSA alone track together and highest (~2100-2350 mm3, marked "n.s." between them, i.e. no significant difference), Lasers-alone slightly lower (~1750, one star), BNs-BSA+660nm lower still (~1470, marked with many stars/asterisks indicating significance vs. controls), BNs-BSA+808nm much lower (~370, three stars + #), and the dual-wavelength BNs-BSA+660/808nm combination flat near zero throughout (three stars, essentially full suppression).

What this settles: the measurement directly supports that photothermal/photodynamic service capability of the BNs-BSA agent is wavelength- and combination-dependent — single-wavelength irradiation (particularly 808 nm) gives partial tumor growth inhibition, and combined 660+808 nm irradiation with the nanomaterial gives the strongest, near-complete inhibition of tumor growth over the 22-day window, exceeding either single-wavelength treatment or laser-only/vehicle-only controls. The nanomaterial or laser alone (without the paired condition) is statistically indistinguishable from PBS, so the material itself is inert without the correct light activation, and light alone (Lasers group) has only weak/marginal effect versus PBS.

What it leaves open: this is a single in vivo growth-curve readout (n=5, one dose/regimen, 22 days) — it does not establish the mechanism of the service capability (i.e., whether inhibition is via photothermal heating, ROS/photodynamic action, or a combination), does not report survival, tumor regrowth after day 22, metastasis, or systemic toxicity, does not test dose-response or alternative irradiation schedules/powers, and does not by itself distinguish 808 nm's large individual effect from a possible synergy specific to the 660+808 combination versus simply "808 nm dominates and 660 nm adds little." Reproducibility beyond this one cohort, and translation to other tumor models or capability specs, remain unaddressed by this figure alone.

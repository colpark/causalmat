Panel F6a (path: /home/aid1/Documents/causalmat/matmech/Journal_of_Magnesium_and_Alloys/j.jma.2020.11.023/panels/crops/83b69d99b3e0033f51deefa24884f665315be84db537fd1e97347ec5fb3a8dec_A.jpg) plots ZT vs T (300-625 K) for Mg3.2Bi1.4Sb0.6-xSex at x = 0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04, plus two literature reference curves (n-Bi2Te3 [8], dashed; Mg3.05(Sb0.7Bi0.3)1.99Te0.01 [22], orange). Reading the curve at 300 K matches the quoted numbers closely: x=0 is near-zero (~0.05, essentially undoped/unoptimized), x=0.005 ~0.85, x=0.01 ~0.78, x=0.02/0.04 cluster ~0.63-0.69, and the n-Bi2Te3 reference sits at ~0.88.

What this settles: Se substitution on the Sb/Bi site is what converts an otherwise poor thermoelectric (x=0, ZT<0.25 across the whole range) into a material with room-temperature ZT approaching commercial n-type Bi2Te3 (0.84 vs 0.88, ~95% of reference), and it also confirms a clear doping-dependent trend — lighter Se doping (x=0.005-0.01) outperforms heavier doping (x=0.02-0.04) at 300 K and gives the highest peak ZT (~1.25-1.3 around 450-525 K) among the Se-doped series, exceeding both literature references in that mid-temperature window.

What it leaves open: the panel alone doesn't show which transport property (Seebeck, resistivity, or thermal conductivity) drives the 300 K differences between doping levels, gives no error bars/reproducibility information, doesn't establish behavior beyond ~625 K, and doesn't include x values between 0.01-0.02 or above 0.04, so the true optimum doping and its precision aren't pinned down — only the trend and approximate magnitudes are.

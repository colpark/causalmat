Panel F4b shows CnC HS/Zn cycling to 7100 min with voltage confined to roughly ±0.03 V, while C HS/Zn develops growing voltage spikes from ~2600 min and its trace terminates near 4400 min at a labeled "C HS battery failure" arrow.

Combined with the already-settled result (n14->n16: CnC HS vs C HS differ in nitrogen-linked spectral signature and in flat/sparse vs dense-platelet Zn morphology, and their plating overpotentials nearly coincide at low current density but separate to about -0.05 V vs -0.10 V at high current density), this new panel adds a third, independent electrochemical axis — long-term cycling stability/failure — that lines up with the same two-sample distinction. The combination supports the broader claim that whatever separates CnC HS from C HS (chemistry, morphology, or some other unmeasured host property) is associated not only with a rate-dependent overpotential gap but also with durability: the host with the smaller high-rate overpotential penalty (CnC HS) is the same one that cycles stably for the full duration, while the host with the larger overpotential penalty (C HS) is the one whose voltage destabilizes and fails. This strengthens the case that the CnC HS/C HS distinction tracks a real, multi-faceted electrochemical performance difference rather than an artifact of one measurement type.

It leaves open exactly the same causal gap as before, now compounded: it still cannot identify which structural/chemical feature (N-environment, morphology, or something else) is responsible; the failure event and the overpotential separation are different kinds of measurements (a discrete failure/spike event vs. a continuous voltage-gap magnitude) whose co-occurrence does not establish a shared mechanism or dose-response link; and both remain observations on the same paired samples, so confounding is unresolved.

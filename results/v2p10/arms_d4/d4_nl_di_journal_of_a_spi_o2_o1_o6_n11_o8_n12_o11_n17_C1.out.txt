This measurement settles a narrow, convergent claim about YSZ's behavior under thermal cycling, not a general PRF/service_capability claim for the coating system as a whole.

What it supports: For YSZ specifically, two independent lines of evidence point the same direction — the diffraction-derived strain proxy (steady, low 0.7-2.3 deg/interval orientation change, no decay pattern) and a direct microstructural check (visually unchanged crack network between two micrograph views in the same field) are consistent with each other. Together they justify treating YSZ as exhibiting stable, low-strain behavior across the cycling interval studied, which is a reasonable basis for a limited service-capability inference: under the specific cycling regime and field of view examined, YSZ shows no evidence of progressive crack growth or strain-driven degradation. This is a legitimate, if modest, corroboration — a spot-check consistency between a numeric proxy and a qualitative structural observation.

What it leaves open: (1) No comparison is possible for Al2O3-YSZ's crack behavior, so no relative service-capability ranking between the two coatings can be drawn from this pairing. (2) The crack observation is qualitative ("looks unchanged"), single-field, two-timepoint — not quantified, not generalizable across the coating, and not a correlation on a shared scale with the strain numbers. (3) No mechanism links tetragonal phase or low orientation-change to crack stability, so this cannot be extended predictively to other cycling durations, conditions, or coating batches. (4) Phase fraction is uncharacterized quantitatively, so no dose-response between tetragonality and crack stability exists. In short: this supports a localized, spot-check claim of YSZ stability, not a validated, generalizable, or mechanistic service-capability claim.

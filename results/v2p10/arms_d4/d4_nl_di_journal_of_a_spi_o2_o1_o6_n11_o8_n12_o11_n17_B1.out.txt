F8 shows the Al2O3-YSZ coupon: intact at 0 and 5 cycles, with corner spalling first visible at 10 cycles and persisting/slightly growing through 40 cycles.

Read against n11 (Al2O3-YSZ's cubic phase shows a large per-interval orientation change at 5 cycles that diminishes by 20 cycles), the combination settles a temporal ordering specific to Al2O3-YSZ: the large strain/orientation change occurs in the interval (0-5 cycles) before any visible spalling, and the diminishing orientation change from 5-20 cycles occurs after spalling has already initiated (by cycle 10) and while the spalled region is already present. This supports treating the early high orientation-change rate as a precursor-phase signal that precedes macroscopic coating failure, and it reframes the "diminishing" strain signal in n11 as coinciding with a coating that is no longer intact rather than one that has microstructurally stabilized while intact -- a distinction n11 alone (phase/strain data only, no visual failure record) could not draw, and F8 alone (visual record only, no strain data) could not draw either.

What it leaves open: F8 is Al2O3-YSZ only, so it still cannot be paired with n12 (which is YSZ-only) to compare crack/failure behavior between the two coatings directly. The link between orientation-change magnitude and spalling remains correlational and coarse (cycle-bin co-occurrence, not registered to the same location or a quantified strain-vs-damage scale), with no mechanism established. Whether the post-spall diminishing signal reflects measurement on residual intact material, on the spalled interface, or genuine stabilization elsewhere on the coupon is indeterminate from these two pieces alone.

The measurement (F5f) is an in vivo tumor-volume time course across six treatment arms. Read together with the established result (BNs-BSA material absorbs and converts 808 nm light into concentration- and power-dependent heat, ensemble-level), the combination supports extending the causal chain from "absorbs and heats" to "heats and this heating translates into therapeutic tumor suppression in a living animal." Specifically: BNs-BSA alone (no light) tracks PBS, showing the material is inert without irradiation; laser alone gives only mild suppression; but BNs-BSA + 808 nm collapses tumor growth to near zero by day 22, matching the direction predicted by the earlier photothermal-heating dose-response (808 nm drives strong absorption/heating, so pairing it with the NIR-absorbing material should — and here does — produce a large biological effect). The combined 660+808 nm arm reaches complete ablation.

What this settles that neither alone did: that the demonstrated photothermal behavior of BNs-BSA is not just a benchtop/IR-camera artifact but is associated with a real, dose-relevant antitumor outcome when 808 nm is applied in vivo — i.e., a link from "material heats under NIR" to "material plus that NIR ablates tumors."

What remains open: (1) no in-tumor temperature measurement is shown, so the mechanism is inferred, not directly verified, as heat-mediated cell death in situ; (2) 660 nm alone also suppresses tumor growth substantially, a channel the photothermal-808nm result does not explain, implying an added (possibly photodynamic) mechanism not covered by the heating data; (3) the particle-vs-substrate/oxide attribution gap and lack of photothermal efficiency quantification both persist unresolved.

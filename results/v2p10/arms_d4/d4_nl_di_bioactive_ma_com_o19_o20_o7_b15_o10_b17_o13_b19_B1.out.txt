Panel content: (a) S. aureus and (b) E. coli plates for Control, TiO2, Er0.5Ce0.1Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.3Ti-O, each under Dark and Light. Under light, Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O shows the fewest colonies of any sample for both strains; Er0.5Ce0.3Ti-O is next-sparsest, then Er0.5Ce0.1Ti-O and TiO2 with visibly more colonies. Dark plates remain heavily covered for all samples, though the doped plates look somewhat sparser than control/TiO2.

What this settles in combination with the earlier PL/degradation result: the light-plate ordering (Ce0.2 best killing > Ce0.3 > Ce0.1 ≈ TiO2) visually reproduces the same four-sample ranking already established for photodegradation (Ce0.2 ~46% > Ce0.3 ~41% > Ce0.1 ~31% > TiO2 ~20%). Unlike the PL-degradation link, which had only a two-point (TiO2, Er0.5Ce0.2) overlap, this antibacterial panel covers all four Ce-series samples, so the combination extends the associative claim beyond a two-point correspondence to a full-series match: whatever tracks with suppressed PL/enhanced degradation across the whole Ce dose series also tracks with light-activated bactericidal capability, i.e. PRF-type reactivity generalizes across two independent functional readouts (dye degradation and antibacterial killing), not just one.

What it leaves open: this is still a co-occurring ranking, not a demonstrated mechanism — no direct evidence ties suppressed recombination or ROS output to the killing observed here. Colony counts are qualitative/visual, not quantified CFU data, so the "match" in ordering is approximate. Dark plates still show substantial colonies for every sample, indicating a non-photocatalytic (possibly ion-release/contact) antibacterial component not explained by the PL/degradation story, and Ce oxidation state and Er lattice-incorporation remain unresolved as before.

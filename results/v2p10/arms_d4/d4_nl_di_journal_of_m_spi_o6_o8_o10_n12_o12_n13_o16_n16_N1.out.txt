The paper reports that Se doping on the Bi site of n-type Mg3Bi2-based Zintl-phase material raises the room-temperature power factor (PRF) by optimizing carrier concentration/mobility, and that this translates into a peak figure of merit (zT) at or near room temperature that is unusually high for this class of material — historically Mg3Bi2-based compounds only showed strong zT at elevated temperatures (~300–450°C), with mediocre near-room-temperature performance.

Taken alone, a new room-temperature PRF/zT peak only shows that this particular doping level and sample achieve good performance in one temperature regime — it doesn't establish whether the material is competitive across the full operating range needed for real thermoelectric modules (which usually pair a low-temperature leg with a high-temperature leg).

Read together with the earlier established result (good mid/high-temperature zT in undoped or lightly-doped Mg3Bi2-based compounds), the combination supports a stronger claim: that Se doping extends the useful operating window of n-type Mg3Bi2-based Zintl materials down to room temperature without sacrificing the previously demonstrated higher-temperature performance, making a single compositional family potentially viable across a broad temperature span (a "one-material" or near-single-leg thermoelectric device rather than needing entirely different compounds segmented by temperature).

What remains open: whether the room-temperature and high-temperature optima can be achieved simultaneously in one bulk sample (vs. requiring different Se concentrations for each regime), long-term thermal/chemical stability and degradation of Se-doped samples, reproducibility across synthesis batches, and whether the reported PRF/zT gains survive scale-up into an actual device architecture rather than a lab pellet.

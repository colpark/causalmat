Panel F5d individual growth curves: all five BNs-BSA+660/808nm ("+Lasers") tumors stay flat at ~0 through day ~20 (complete, durable suppression), while under BNs-BSA+808nm alone, four of five tumors regrow starting ~day 14 (one stays low), i.e. 808 nm monotherapy gives only partial/transient control.

Read together with the established result (Bi-like phase + strong linear 808 nm absorption + concentration- and power-dependent photothermal heating), this combination supports that the material's demonstrated capacity to convert 808 nm light into heat in a dose-dependent way is biologically consequential in vivo: it is sufficient to produce measurable tumor growth suppression under 808 nm alone for a period, consistent with a genuine photothermal/tumoricidal effect rather than no effect at all. It also supports that this heating capability, as delivered here, is not sufficient by itself for durable ablation — most 808 nm-only tumors escape and regrow — whereas adding 660 nm co-irradiation converts this into complete, lasting suppression in every animal, implying synergy or a higher effective heat/dose delivered when both wavelengths are combined.

What remains open: no in vivo tumor temperature was measured, so there is no quantitative link between the established concentration/power-dependent heating curve and the actual thermal dose reached in tumors, and no explanation for why 808 nm regrowth occurs (insufficient heat, incomplete tumor coverage, or biological escape). The heating attribution gap also persists — whether Bi-like nanoparticles or residual sheet/oxide drive the in vivo heating and suppression is still unresolved, and photothermal conversion efficiency remains unquantified.

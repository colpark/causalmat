The JMST paper reports uniaxial tensile stress-strain curves, work-hardening rate, and fracture-surface morphology for cast Al0.9CoFeNi2, a eutectic high-entropy alloy whose lamellar structure alternates a soft, ductile FCC phase with a hard, ordered B2 phase. Taken alone, this tensile measurement only shows a macroscopic result: high strength combined with sustained work hardening and appreciable elongation before failure, plus dimpled (FCC) versus cleavage-like (B2) fracture features.

The "established earlier result" is presumably the prior microstructural/hardness characterization (nanoindentation, EBSD, or compression data) showing the two eutectic phases differ sharply in hardness/flow strength but are coherently or semi-coherently bonded in fine lamellae. That earlier result alone shows a hardness contrast exists but not how it behaves under load.

Read together, the two settle the deformation pathway: strain initiates in the soft FCC lamellae, dislocations pile up at the FCC/B2 interphase boundaries, and load is progressively transferred to the hard B2 phase, producing continuous strain hardening rather than early localized necking — i.e., a composite-like load-partitioning mechanism, not simple single-phase solid-solution hardening, explains the simultaneous strength-ductility synergy.

What remains open: whether the interface accommodates strain via dislocation transmission, interfacial sliding, or a strain-induced phase/ordering change; the quantitative strain split between phases at each stage; and how this pathway depends on temperature, strain rate, or lamellar spacing. Those require in-situ (synchrotron/EBSD/TEM) strain-mapping during loading, which this combination does not provide.

This step establishes only an extension of an already-settled co-occurrence, not a causal or quantitative finding. It supports the claim that, across the same two-sample comparison (CnC HS vs. C HS), three variables track together on the same sample: the nitrogen-linked spectral shift, the flatter/sparser zinc morphology, and the lower nucleation overpotential (39 mV vs. 43 mV, from F4d). In other words, whichever sample shows the nitrogen-chemistry signature and the more compact morphology is also the sample with the lower nucleation overpotential — the co-occurrence pattern now spans a spectral axis, a morphological axis, and an electrochemical (nucleation-overpotential) axis, all in the same direction for the same sample.

What it leaves open is causation and generality. It does not show that the nitrogen chemical-environment change, or the flatter morphology, causes the lower nucleation overpotential, or that the lower overpotential causes or reflects the other two features — it is a co-occurrence across three axes, not a mechanism. It does not rule out other, unrelated structural, compositional, or textural differences between CnC HS and C HS as the actual driver of the overpotential gap. With only two samples and two values (39 mV vs. 43 mV), the result supports an ordering (CnC HS lower than C HS) but not a quantitative trend or dose-response relationship. It also inherits earlier caveats: the pyridinic/graphitic and Zn–N bonding labels remain interpretive rather than established, morphology is not quantified (no coverage, amount, or size measured), and the overpotential comparison is limited to nucleation on the initial discharge cycle, not across cycling or varying current density.

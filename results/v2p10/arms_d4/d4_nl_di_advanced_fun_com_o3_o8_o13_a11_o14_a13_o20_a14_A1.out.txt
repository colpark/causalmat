Panel F5d (six sub-panels of individual tumor-volume-vs-time curves, PBS/BNs-BSA/Lasers/BNs-BSA+660nm/BNs-BSA+808nm/BNs-BSA+Lasers) confirms the described pattern visually: in the BNs-BSA+Lasers (dual 660/808 nm) panel, all curves stay flat near zero volume through day ~20-22 — no regrowth in any animal. In the BNs-BSA+808 nm panel, curves are suppressed near zero through roughly day 10-14, but then most of them (the majority of the 4-5 traces shown) turn upward and climb to 250-700 mm3 by day ~20-22, while at most one curve stays flat.

What this settles: the measurement supports the conclusion that combined 660+808 nm laser irradiation with BNs-BSA produces a durable, complete tumor ablation across the treated cohort, whereas 808 nm-only irradiation with the same nanoagent produces only a transient response — tumors are initially controlled but regrow in most animals starting around day 14. This is direct within-panel evidence for a service_capability-relevant claim: the dual-wavelength protocol is more robust/durable than the single-808nm protocol, i.e., 808 nm alone is not sufficient for lasting local control in this model.

What it leaves open: the panel does not show data past day ~20-22, so whether the flat BNs-BSA+Lasers curves represent a permanent cure or just a longer latency before eventual regrowth is unknown. It also doesn't establish the mechanism (deeper heating, synergistic photothermal/photodynamic effect, or something else), doesn't give sample-size/statistics (only 4-5 mice per arm), and doesn't address whether this durability generalizes beyond this one tumor model or dosing regimen.

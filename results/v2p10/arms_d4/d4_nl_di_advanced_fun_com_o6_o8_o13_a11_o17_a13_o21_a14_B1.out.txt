Panel F5f shows in vivo tumor-volume trajectories over 22 days: PBS and BNs-BSA-alone (no laser) are statistically indistinguishable (n.s.) and highest (~2100-2350 mm3); Lasers-only control is slightly lower (~1750); BNs-BSA+660nm gives partial suppression (~1470, marked *); BNs-BSA+808nm gives strong suppression (~370, ***#); and BNs-BSA+660nm+808nm gives complete ablation (~0, ***). 

Read together with the earlier-established result (alpha=6.4 L/g/cm at 808 nm, tau_s=133.4 s, eta=19.4% photothermal conversion for this phase-identified majority-Bi/minor-Bi2O3 material), the combination supports that the material's demonstrated 808 nm light-to-heat conversion is the operative mechanism behind its in vivo antitumor efficacy: material alone and laser alone are both inert, so tumor suppression requires both the nanomaterial and irradiation together, consistent with a photothermal (not drug/immune) mode of action, and the strong 808 nm response tracks the wavelength at which conversion was physically characterized. The near-complete ablation under combined 660+808 nm irradiation further indicates a synergistic heating effect exceeding either wavelength alone.

What remains open: this pairing does not measure actual tumor temperature, so it cannot quantitatively confirm that 19.4% efficiency (measured in vitro/solution) reproduces in the tumor microenvironment; it does not establish that 660 nm efficacy proceeds through the same photothermal mechanism (that conversion was characterized only at 808 nm); and it leaves unresolved all prior caveats -- Bi vs Bi2O3 phase attribution, morphology contribution, Bi2O3 quantity, and any dose/power-density or alpha-to-eta scaling relationship. The therapeutic dose used in vivo is not tied numerically to eta or tau_s.

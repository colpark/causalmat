Panel F5f is a day-0–22 tumor growth curve (n=5/group, mean±SD) comparing six arms: PBS, BNs-BSA, Lasers alone, BNs-BSA+660 nm, BNs-BSA+808 nm, and BNs-BSA+660/808 nm combined.

What this settles: the measurement directly supports the stated day-22 ranking. PBS and BNs-BSA alone are statistically indistinguishable ("n.s." annotation) and both track highest (~2100–2350 mm3), confirming the nanoparticle alone (no light) and vehicle control produce equivalent, unchecked tumor growth. Lasers-only sits somewhat lower (~1750 mm3) but still shows substantial growth, so light alone (without the photosensitizer/BNs) has only weak, likely nonspecific effect. Single-wavelength combinations show a clear photosensitizer-plus-light effect: 660 nm+BNs (~1470 mm3) provides partial suppression, marked significant (yellow stars) relative to the growth controls, and 808 nm+BNs (~370 mm3) is markedly stronger, with significant markers. The dual 660/808 nm+BNs arm is flat at ~0 mm3 throughout, with its own significance markers (stars/#), indicating near-complete tumor ablation — the combined-wavelength regimen is clearly superior to either single wavelength.

What this leaves open: the panel establishes efficacy ranking and statistical separation between groups at endpoint, but the image alone doesn't give exact p-values, exact numeric means/error bars, or mechanistic explanation (e.g., whether 808 nm's superiority over 660 nm reflects deeper tissue penetration, higher photothermal/photodynamic conversion, or something else about BNs-BSA's absorption spectrum). It also doesn't address durability beyond day 22, systemic toxicity, or generalizability beyond this tumor model/mouse cohort — those require the surrounding text/other panels, which were not reviewed here.

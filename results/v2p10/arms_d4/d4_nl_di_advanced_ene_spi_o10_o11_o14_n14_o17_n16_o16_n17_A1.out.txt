Panel F4b shows galvanostatic cycling voltage profiles for C HS/Zn and CnC HS/Zn symmetric cells over ~7100 minutes. The C HS trace is stable near 0 V until roughly 2500–2600 min, then develops increasingly large voltage spikes (growing from small perturbations to full-scale excursions hitting the ±0.3 V axis limits) between ~2600 and ~4300 min, after which the trace is annotated with an arrow labeled "C HS battery failure" and the curve terminates around 4300–4400 min. In contrast, the CnC HS trace remains a tight, low-amplitude band (roughly ±0.02–0.03 V) essentially unchanged in character all the way out to the ~7100 min end of the plot, with no failure marker.

This measurement directly supports a claim about differential cycling stability/lifetime between the two host materials (C HS vs CnC HS) in symmetric Zn-cell cycling: CnC HS enables stable, low-polarization plating/stripping cycling for the full test duration (~7100 min) with no failure, whereas C HS shows escalating voltage noise/polarization starting near 2600 min and undergoes a defined short-circuit-type failure by ~4300 min. So the panel substantiates that CnC HS confers markedly superior symmetric-cell cycling stability/service life relative to C HS under these specific test conditions (likely a fixed current density/areal capacity, not stated in the crop).

What it leaves open: the exact current density, capacity, or electrolyte conditions used are not shown in this crop; the mechanism of C HS failure (e.g., dendrite short circuit vs. side reaction) is not established, only the voltage signature and the "battery failure" label; and no comparison to other candidate materials, no error bars/replicate count, and no full-cell or coulombic-efficiency data are given here, so broader "service_capability" claims beyond this symmetric-cell test are not settled by this figure alone.

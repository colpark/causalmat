The panel (F2b, strain-hardening rate vs. true strain for the Al0.9CoFeNi2 EHEA) matches the stated measurement closely: rate starts near 85 GPa, collapses sharply to ~14 GPa by ~0.009 strain (region I), then declines slowly and levels off near 5 GPa out to ~0.058 strain (region II, a broad plateau rather than a smooth monotonic decay), and past ~0.06 the curve dips to negative values before a brief uptick at the very end (region III).

What this justifies: the measurement is consistent with a multi-stage strain-hardening pathway rather than a single, uniform hardening mechanism operating throughout deformation. The rapid initial drop (I) is the signature of standard dislocation-multiplication-dominated hardening exhausting itself quickly; the extended low-but-positive plateau (II) indicates a secondary, weaker hardening contribution sustaining flow stress over a much larger strain range; and the late-stage negative excursion (III) marks onset of softening/necking-type instability rather than continued hardening. So the data support a staged-mechanism (MEC) picture with a transition/pathway from one dominant hardening contributor to another as strain accumulates, and eventual loss of hardening capacity.

What it leaves open: the panel alone (macroscopic true stress–true strain and dhardening-rate curve) cannot identify the microstructural identity of the mechanism sustaining region II — e.g., whether it is deformation twinning (TWIP), a phase transformation (TRIP), short-range ordering, or dislocation interaction/cross-slip effects. No direct microstructural evidence (TEM, XRD, EBSD) is shown here, so the specific pathway/mechanism label is not settled by this figure — only the existence and approximate strain boundaries of the staged behavior are.

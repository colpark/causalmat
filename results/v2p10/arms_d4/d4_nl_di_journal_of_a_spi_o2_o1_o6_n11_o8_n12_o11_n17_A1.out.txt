Fig. 8 (Al2O3-YSZ coating photos across thermal cycling: 0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40 cycles) shows: at 0 and 5 cycles the coupon surface is intact with no visible edge damage; by 10 cycles a corner has spalled (chipped/missing material at one edge); at 20, 30, and 40 cycles that same corner region remains spalled, with the damaged area appearing somewhat larger/rougher by 40 cycles than at 10.

What this supports: the coating survives at least 5 thermal cycles without visible macroscopic damage, and localized (corner) spallation initiates somewhere between 5 and 10 cycles. It also supports that once initiated, this corner spallation is not self-healing and does not propagate catastrophically — the damage is roughly stable/slowly progressing from 10 through 40 cycles rather than spreading to consume the whole coating or causing gross delamination of the bulk coating surface, which stays intact-looking through 40 cycles.

What it leaves open: the image alone does not establish an exact failure-onset cycle count (only that it falls between 5 and 10, since no intermediate images are shown), does not quantify the spalled area or depth, does not identify the failure mechanism (thermal-mismatch cracking vs. sintering vs. moisture/CMAS attack), and does not indicate whether the corner location is an edge-effect artifact (e.g., stress concentration from coupon geometry) rather than representative of general coating behavior. It also cannot confirm whether the bulk (non-corner) coating retains its protective/insulating function, since only surface photographs, not cross-sections or performance data, are shown. So "service_capability" up to 5 cycles is well supported, and modest continued service to 40 cycles with a stable localized defect is plausible, but whether that corner damage would eventually compromise overall service capability (e.g., through crack growth or moisture ingress) is not settled by this image.

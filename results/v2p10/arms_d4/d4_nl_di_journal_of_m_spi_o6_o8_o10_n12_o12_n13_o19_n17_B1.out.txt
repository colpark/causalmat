Panel F7c confirms x=0.01 has the highest maximal conversion efficiency across the full Th range up to 623 K (~11.8% vs ~9.5-10.6% for x=0.005, 0.02, 0.04), tracking the same ordering seen in the PF data (n13) where x=0.01's PF (~29 at 300K, ~22 at 623K) clearly exceeds the other doped and undoped compositions.

What the combination settles: Since device/conversion efficiency (eta) is a downstream, temperature-integrated consequence of the material's PF (and ZT) profile, and n12+n13 already established that x=0.01's PF advantage is consistent with a low-resistivity, mobility-preserved regime rather than an artifact of resistivity moving the wrong way, this new eta measurement shows that advantage actually translates into superior device-level performance across the whole operating range (300-623 K), not just at isolated temperature points. This closes one of the earlier qualifications: it demonstrates the PF advantage at x=0.01 is not merely a favorable-but-inconsequential material metric confined to specific temperatures — it persists and compounds to give a materially higher conversion efficiency than every other composition tested, including at the temperature extremes.

What it leaves open: eta depends on thermal conductivity and ZT(T) integrated over the whole gradient, none of which n12/n13/this panel report directly, so the combination still cannot attribute the efficiency gain quantitatively to resistivity/mobility versus Seebeck coefficient versus thermal conductivity effects. It also does not resolve any of the standing qualifications on n12 (missing mu_H at x=0.01 exactly, heterogeneous literature comparison, no confirmed Se-on-Sb mechanism, no established rho/mu_H trend across the full 300-623 K range). The eta curves alone are consistent with, but do not independently prove, that mechanism — they only confirm that whatever produces the PF advantage yields a real, temperature-spanning device benefit.

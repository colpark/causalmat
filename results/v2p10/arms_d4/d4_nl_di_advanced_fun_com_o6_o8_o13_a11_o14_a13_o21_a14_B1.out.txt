Panel F5f shows day-22 tumor volume ranking: BNs-BSA ~ PBS (~2100-2350, n.s. between them) > Lasers alone (~1750, marginal * reduction vs PBS/BNs-BSA) > BNs-BSA+660 nm (~1470, more suppressed) > BNs-BSA+808 nm (~370, strongly suppressed) > BNs-BSA+660/808 nm combined (~0, near-complete suppression, marked ***/#).

Read alone, this in vivo panel only shows that the combination of the bismuthene-BSA nanomaterial plus 808 nm (and especially plus both 660+808 nm) laser irradiation suppresses tumor growth far more than the material alone, laser alone, or PBS control — it is a therapeutic-outcome readout and, by itself, says nothing about *why* that suppression occurs (i.e., not by heat, not by any specific optical/material property).

Combined with the earlier-established result — that the phase-identified bismuthene material's strong NIR extinction (alpha at 808 nm) drives concentration- and power-dependent photothermal heating — the two together support a mechanistic bridge: the tumor suppression seen specifically under 808 nm irradiation (and most strongly under combined 660/808 nm) is consistent with the photothermal effect from that same material converting absorbed NIR light into ablative heat in situ, producing an antitumor effect that requires both the nanomaterial and the matching wavelength of light (laser-only or material-only controls do not reproduce it).

What this leaves open, plainly: the combination still does not establish tumor-site temperature was actually reached to a cytotoxic/ablative threshold in vivo (B was an in vitro/spot IR measurement, not tumor thermometry); it does not rule out contributions from the 660 nm channel or from a non-thermal (e.g., photodynamic, oxidative, phase-specific Bi vs Bi2O3) mechanism, since 660 nm addition further improves outcome despite not being the wavelength associated with the strong extinction/heating data; it establishes no dose-response or efficiency link between alpha/heating magnitude and degree of tumor suppression; and it says nothing about mechanism of cell death, biodistribution, or long-term safety/recurrence beyond day 22.

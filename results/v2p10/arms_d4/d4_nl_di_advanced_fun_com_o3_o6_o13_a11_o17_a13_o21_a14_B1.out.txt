Panel F5f (viewed) shows day-22 tumor volumes: BNs-BSA alone ≈ PBS (no effect, n.s.), Lasers-only (660+808 nm, no particles) gives modest reduction, +660 nm alone gives partial reduction, +808 nm alone gives strong reduction, and +660/808 nm combined with BNs-BSA eliminates tumors (~0).

Read together with the earlier result (bulk two-phase BNs material absorbs 808 nm light proportionally to concentration, and separately converts absorbed 808 nm light to heat with eta=19.4%, tau_s=133.4 s), the combination supports that this bulk photothermal capability is functionally, biologically relevant: the same material, only when paired with NIR light (not alone, not with laser alone), produces the strong in vivo tumor suppression, and the 808 nm arm alone drives most of the reduction (consistent with 808 nm being the wavelength shown to be strongly, proportionally absorbed and converted to heat). This is the first link from a bulk optical/thermal measurement to a whole-organism therapeutic readout — it argues the measured absorbance/eta is not just a material curiosity but underlies an in vivo effect.

What it leaves open: (1) it does not establish which phase (metallic Bi, Bi2O3, or interface) drives the in vivo effect, carrying forward the same unresolved question; (2) no temperature or eta measurement was taken in the tumor itself, so the specific tau_s=133.4s/eta=19.4% values are not shown to hold in vivo, only correlated qualitatively; (3) complete tumor elimination requires the 660 nm component too, so the 808 nm photothermal mechanism alone is necessary but not sufficient for full efficacy — a separate, uncharacterized 660 nm contribution (e.g., photodynamic) is needed alongside it.

This settled combination supports a specific, limited claim about PRF/figure_of_merit: the power-factor peak at x=0.01 (~29 at 300 K, ~22 at 623 K), clearly above both the lightest doping (x=0.005) and the undoped sample (≤~5), is consistent with sitting on the same favorable-resistivity, carrier-concentration-driven regime that the earlier mobility/resistivity result (n12) identified — i.e., the PF rise is not contradicted by, and can be partly attributed to, doping lowering resistivity without a compensating mobility penalty. So the justified conclusion is: doping's effect on resistivity/mobility is a plausible, evidence-consistent contributor to the observed PF enhancement, and the PF trend is not simply an artifact of resistivity moving the wrong way.

What this leaves open is substantial. First, n12 has no mobility data exactly at x=0.01, the composition where the PF peak and resistivity minimum occur, so the mobility explanation can't be pinned to that exact point. Second, PF also depends on the Seebeck coefficient S, on which n12 is silent — so it cannot be determined whether the peak reflects the resistivity/mobility story, a favorable S at x=0.01, or some combination of both. Third, n12's mobility comparison relies on a heterogeneous literature dataset (different synthesis/microstructure), so any mobility-preserved contribution could reflect sample quality rather than the Se-doping mechanism itself. Fourth, the microscopic mechanism (Se substitution on the Sb site) is not established. Fifth, the temperature dependence linking the two datasets isn't established across the full 300–623 K range. Finally, with only a few shared doping points, this supports an ordering/trend across x, not a quantitative fitted relationship among n_H, mu_H, rho, and PF.

What this settles: the measurement supports a clear efficacy hierarchy for tumor growth suppression — the dual-wavelength (660+808 nm) light-activated BNs-BSA treatment achieves near-complete tumor growth inhibition, single 808 nm irradiation gives strong but incomplete suppression, single 660 nm gives partial suppression, and the nanomaterial or laser alone (without combined dual-wavelength activation) does little beyond baseline. This licenses a claim about relative service/therapeutic capability under this specific dosing and light regimen: the material's therapeutic capability is light-activation-dependent and synergistic across the two wavelengths, not intrinsic to the particle or to laser exposure alone.

What it leaves open: the panel gives no mechanistic breakdown (photothermal vs photodynamic contribution to each wavelength's effect), no dose-response data, no survival/toxicity or recurrence follow-up beyond day 22, no error-bar magnitude readable at fine resolution, no statistics table beyond the marked star/n.s./hash annotations (exact p-values, test used), and no generalization beyond this one tumor model/mouse cohort (n=5) or this specific power/duration of irradiation. So "service capability" is demonstrated only as short-term tumor-volume suppression in this one experimental setup, not as a general or mechanistically explained treatment capability.

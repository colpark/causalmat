This converged result supports a modest, coating-specific conclusion about service capability: for YSZ specifically, two independent lines of evidence -- a diffraction-derived micro-strain proxy (low, steady 0.7-2.3 deg/interval orientation change across cycling) and a direct microstructural observation (an unchanged crack network between two micrograph views in the same field, F4b/F4c) -- point the same direction, giving corroborated (not merely single-source) support for the claim that YSZ shows stable behavior under this thermal/mechanical cycling regime. This is stronger evidence for YSZ's stability specifically than either measurement offers alone, since strain data alone lacks microstructural grounding and the crack observation alone lacks strain/phase context to know whether "unchanged" reflects genuine stability or an unmeasured incidental feature.

What it leaves open is substantial. It says nothing about Al2O3-YSZ's crack behavior, so no comparative claim about which coating has better service capability (crack resistance) can be made -- only YSZ's own internal consistency is corroborated. The crack observation is qualitative ("looks unchanged" in one field of view, two time points), not quantified as length, density, or count, so it cannot be placed on the same scale as the numeric strain rates -- this is a co-occurrence, not a measured correlation. No mechanism links the tetragonal phase or low orientation-change rate causally to crack stability. The single field of view and two time points are too sparse to generalize across the whole coating or across cycling regimes beyond five cycles. And because YSZ's phase fraction isn't quantified, no dose-response relationship between degree of tetragonality and degree of crack stability can be drawn. Overall: a corroborated spot-check of YSZ stability, not a generalizable or comparative service-capability finding.

This measurement (really, the reasoned combination of two earlier established results) settles a co-occurrence, not a causal or mechanistic claim, about PRF/service_capability.

What it supports: within this specific two-coating comparison, the coating that displays the large, cycle-dependent micro-strain signature (Al2O3-YSZ, cubic phase: ~4.3-11 deg per-interval orientation change at 5 cycles, decaying to ~1 deg by 20 cycles) is the same coating whose crack images show clear microstructural degradation after cycling — longer, linked cracks and a new wide horizontal crack near the bottom. By contrast, the coating with steady, low strain throughout (YSZ, tetragonal phase, 0.7-2.3 deg) shows no corresponding change in its crack images. So the strain-evolution behavior and the crack-damage behavior track together in the same material, and diverge together between the two materials.

What it leaves open: no mechanism connects strain magnitude/decay to crack propagation — both could be independent downstream effects of a shared driver (phase instability, cycling stress) rather than one causing the other. The two evidence types are also mismatched in kind and window: strain is a quantified per-interval rate over cycles 5-20, while cracking is a qualitative, single-viewpoint image description, so no quantitative correlation (e.g., dose-response between strain-rate magnitude and crack length) can be drawn — only qualitative co-occurrence. With only two coatings compared, this cannot generalize beyond this sample pair to a broader rule about micro-strain behavior predicting cracking behavior. Finally, why the cubic phase specifically produces both effects while the tetragonal phase produces neither remains unexplained for service-capability implications.

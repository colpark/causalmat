Read together, the new antibacterial data (Fig. 10c/d) and the earlier PL/degradation combination jointly support a broader claim than either piece alone: a third, independent functional readout (antibacterial killing under light) reproduces the same non-monotonic compositional ordering already seen in degradation — Er0.5Ce0.2 (~91-93%) > Ce0.3 (~87%) > Ce0.1 (~68-73%) > TiO2 (~45%) > control — and does so across all four compositions, not just the two endpoints (TiO2, Er0.5Ce0.2) where PL data existed. This extends the associative pattern "less PL-inferred recombination goes with better photocatalytic performance" to a second performance domain (bacterial killing, not just dye degradation), and it independently fills in the previously PL-unmatched middle of the series (Ce0.1, Ce0.3) with a second performance metric that agrees with the degradation ranking — strengthening confidence that photocatalytic service_capability tracks a single underlying composition-dependent property rather than being an artifact of the degradation assay specifically.

What it leaves open: no PL measurement exists for Ce0.1 or Ce0.3, so the recombination story still can't be confirmed for those compositions — only reinforced by analogy. Ce oxidation state and Er3+ lattice-vs-surface siting remain unresolved, so the mechanism behind the ordering is still unattributed. Critically, the dark-condition bars show the same ordering (Ce0.2/Ce0.3 ~42-46% > Ce0.1 ~28-33% > TiO2 ~6%) without any light-driven photocatalysis, indicating a non-photocatalytic, composition-dependent antibacterial effect (e.g., ion toxicity) coexists with and is not distinguished from the light-enhanced, presumably photocatalytic component — so the antibacterial data cannot be cleanly attributed to reduced recombination alone.

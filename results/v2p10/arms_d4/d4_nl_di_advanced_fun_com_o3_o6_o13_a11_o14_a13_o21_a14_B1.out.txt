Panel F5f confirms tumor growth follows: BNs-BSA alone ~ PBS (no antitumor effect, "n.s."), Lasers alone slightly lower (~1750, non-specific laser/heat effect), +660nm intermediate (~1470, marked *), +808nm strongly suppressed (~370, marked ***#), and +660/808nm combined fully ablated (~0, ***).

Combined with the established result (concentration-proportional 808 nm absorbance by the Bi/Bi2O3 composite, alpha=6.4 L/g/cm, and concentration/power-dependent photothermal heating in vitro under IR imaging), the in vivo tumor-suppression data settle that this photothermal effect is biologically consequential and wavelength-specific in a live tumor: only irradiation conditions that include 808 nm (the wavelength the material absorbs and converts to heat) produce strong tumor suppression, while BNs-BSA without light or laser without BNs-BSA do not, ruling out simple drug toxicity or nonspecific laser damage as sufficient explanations. The combination also newly supports that adding 660 nm on top of 808 nm improves efficacy over 808 nm alone (near-complete ablation vs. partial suppression), pointing to a synergistic or dual-wavelength photothermal/phototherapeutic mechanism operating through the same absorbing material.

What remains open: (1) the phase-attribution gap persists — neither dataset says whether metallic Bi, Bi2O3, or their interface is doing the heating/therapeutic work in vivo; (2) no quantitative photothermal conversion efficiency is established, nor is a dose-response link from tumor-site temperature to volume reduction quantified; (3) the mechanism of the 660 nm contribution (independent phototherapy vs. enhancement of 808 nm heating) is not distinguished by either dataset; (4) causal attribution of tumor killing specifically to heat (versus other photochemical or ROS-mediated pathways sometimes co-occurring with such composites) is not directly measured here, only inferred from correlation with the wavelength known to be absorbed and converted to heat in vitro.

Figure 7c plots single-leg conversion efficiency eta (%) vs hot-side temperature T_h (300-623 K, T_c fixed at 300 K) for Mg3.2Bi1.4Sb0.6-xSex with x = 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04. The curves rise monotonically with T_h for all compositions, and at T_h = 623 K they separate into a clear ranking: x=0.01 (red circles) reaches the highest value, ~11.8%, while the other three compositions cluster lower, in roughly the 9.5-10.6% range (x=0.02 slightly above x=0.005, both above x=0.04, which is lowest).

What this supports: the measurement directly substantiates the specific numeric claim — that at the maximum hot-side temperature tested (623 K), the Se-optimal composition x=0.01 delivers a device-level efficiency advantage of roughly 1-2 percentage points (about 11-25% relative) over the other three Se contents. This is a legitimate service-capability/performance-relevant result for PRF, since eta here is the standard single-leg maximal conversion efficiency figure of merit reported by the source paper, derived from the measured T_h sweep at fixed T_c = 300 K.

What it leaves open: the plot alone does not establish why x=0.01 is optimal (i.e., it doesn't isolate the underlying transport mechanism such as ZT, carrier concentration, or lattice thermal conductivity responsible for the gain — those would need companion panels). It also does not indicate measurement uncertainty/error bars, sample-to-sample reproducibility, or whether the small gaps among x=0.005, 0.02, 0.04 are statistically distinguishable given how closely they track. Finally, this single-leg efficiency is not the same as full-module or device-level power output/COP, so claims about actual service capability in a real device (contact resistances, leg pairing, module-level parasitic losses) are not addressed by this figure and remain open.

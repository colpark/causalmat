This step settles a correlation, not a mechanism: the same two host materials (CnC HS vs. C HS) that are distinguished chemically/structurally — by the nitrogen-linked spectral shift (n12) and by the flatter/sparser versus dense-platelet zinc morphology (F3d/F3e) — are also the two materials whose plating overpotential curves are nearly coincident at the lowest current density but diverge to roughly -0.05 V versus -0.10 V at the highest current density (F4c). So it supports the claim that whatever structurally/chemically differentiates these two hosts co-occurs with an electrochemical performance difference that is rate-dependent: negligible at low current density, pronounced at high current density.

What it leaves open is substantial. First, it cannot attribute causation to either candidate factor: because the nitrogen chemical-environment change and the morphological difference occur together in the same sample by construction, this pairing cannot determine whether the nitrogen state, the morphology, or some other unmeasured host property (composition, porosity, surface area, etc.) is responsible for the growing overpotential gap. Second, the two sides of the comparison are mismatched in kind — side A (chemistry/morphology) is qualitative and unquantified (spectral shift present/absent; deposit described only as flat/sparse vs. dense mat of sharp platelets, with no coverage or size measurement), while side B (overpotential) is a continuous, rate-dependent voltage measurement — so no dose-response or magnitude relationship between the two can be drawn. Third, it inherits the earlier result's caveat that the pyridinic/graphitic labels and any inferred Zn-N bonding are the paper's interpretation, not something the underlying panels themselves establish. In short: a co-occurrence between host identity and rate-dependent overpotential separation is supported; the causal driver and any quantitative relationship remain undetermined.

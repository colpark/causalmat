The panel shows: BNs-BSA+808nm tumors initially stay near zero through ~day 14 before 4/5 regrow, while BNs-BSA+660/808nm (dual-laser) tumors stay at zero across the full ~20-day observation in all five mice; BNs-BSA+660nm alone tracks the untreated controls (PBS, BNs-BSA, lasers-only, all growing unchecked).

Read together with the earlier result (concentration-proportional 808nm absorption by the Bi/Bi2O3 material driving concentration- and power-dependent 6-min spot heating), this justifies extending the causal chain one step further: the photothermal heating already shown to scale with concentration/power is not just a benchtop/imaging phenomenon but has a real biological consequence — it initially arrests tumor growth in vivo under 808nm irradiation, consistent with photothermal ablation. Combining it with the 660nm channel converts that initial arrest into durable, complete suppression (5/5, no regrowth), whereas 808nm heating alone is only transiently effective (regrowth in 4/5 after day ~14), and 660nm alone contributes essentially nothing on its own (indistinguishable from controls).

What this leaves open: (1) why 808nm-alone tumors regrow — whether photothermal heating attenuates over repeated treatments (e.g., particle clearance, heat-shock resistance, incomplete ablation at tumor margins) is not addressed by either dataset; (2) which phase converts light to heat remains unresolved, as before; (3) no quantitative photothermal-conversion efficiency is established, so the mechanism by which combined 660/808nm achieves full durable suppression while 808nm alone does not (synergy, PDT-type contribution from 660nm, or purely additive heating) cannot be determined from these two results alone.

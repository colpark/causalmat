Panel F7c shows conversion efficiency eta(%) vs hot-side temperature (Tc=300K fixed) for x=0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04. At Th=623K, x=0.01 (red) reaches ~11.8%, clearly above x=0.005 (~9.5%), x=0.02 (~10.6%), and x=0.04 (~9.5%). This ranking holds across essentially the whole temperature sweep from ~350K upward, not just at the endpoint.

Read together with the established resistivity/power-factor result: the x=0.01 optimum is not a room-temperature-only artifact and not confined to a single transport coefficient — it now shows up as the best device-level figure of merit (conversion efficiency) across the full 300-623K operating range, in a composition series that already showed the lowest resistivity and highest power factor at 300K/623K landing on the same x=0.01 sample, despite n_H rising monotonically with x. Because conversion efficiency integrates ZT (via S, rho, kappa) over the whole temperature gradient, this combination extends the earlier claim from "same optimum composition at matched temperatures for two related transport quantities" to "same optimum composition also holds for the temperature-integrated device metric across the whole 300-623K range" — reinforcing that whatever non-monotonic, non-n_H factor sets the x=0.01 optimum is robust enough to survive integration over temperature and to dominate the net thermoelectric performance, not just an isolated coefficient at one temperature.

What it leaves open: efficiency depends on thermal conductivity kappa as well as S and rho, and no thermal conductivity data is shown here, so the combination cannot say whether the x=0.01 efficiency advantage is driven by the already-noted resistivity/PF advantage alone or whether x=0.01 also has favorably low kappa; that remains untested. It still does not identify the mechanism (mobility, scattering, Seebeck) responsible for the non-n_H factor. It does not resolve the 0K DFT+Hall two-state gap. And PF and eta share the same 1/rho dependence, so this is still not strictly independent corroboration of a separate physical channel — it is consistent with, but does not prove beyond, the resistivity-dip explanation already on record.

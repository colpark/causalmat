Panel F5d confirms the stated pattern: all five BNs-BSA+Lasers (dual 660/808 nm) curves stay flat near zero volume through day ~20, while four of five BNs-BSA+808 nm curves regrow starting around day 14, reaching several hundred mm3 by day 20-22.

Read together with the earlier result (bulk two-phase Bi/Bi2O3 material absorbs 808 nm as a mass-proportional bulk property, alpha=6.4, and converts that light to heat with a real, quantifiable efficiency, eta=19.4%, tau_s=133.4 s), the combination supports that this photothermal conversion capability is not merely a bench-scale artifact but manifests as genuine therapeutic service capability in vivo: 808 nm irradiation of the material does measurably suppress tumor growth (curves stay low for ~14 days) rather than doing nothing, consistent with real heat being delivered to the tumor. What the combination additionally shows, beyond what either result alone could, is that this single-wavelength photothermal capability is only partial and transient - in 4/5 animals the effect fails to hold past day 14 - whereas adding 660 nm produces complete, durable suppression in all five animals. So together they support: the bulk material's measured photothermal efficiency translates into a real but insufficient-alone in vivo service; full ablation service capability requires the combined 660/808 nm regimen.

What this leaves open: whether the 808 nm-alone failure reflects the eta=19.4% being too low at the in vivo dose/power used, or reflects unrelated biological factors (tumor heterogeneity, incomplete nanoparticle penetration, immune recovery); it does not identify what mechanism 660 nm adds (photodynamic vs. additional heating) or which phase drives either effect; and it still does not connect eta/tau_s quantitatively to the day-14 regrowth threshold.

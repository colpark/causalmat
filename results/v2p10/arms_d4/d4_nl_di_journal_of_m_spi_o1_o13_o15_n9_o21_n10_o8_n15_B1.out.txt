The macroscopic curve (F2b) shows a distinct three-stage hardening-rate signature: a steep drop from ~85 to ~14 GPa by 0.009 strain (Stage I), a slow decline to ~5 GPa by 0.058 (Stage II plateau), then a turn negative (Stage III, onset of softening/localization). Taken alone, this is purely a bulk mechanical measurement with no microstructural resolution — it shows that the material's hardening capacity is staged and eventually exhausted, but it cannot say why, or which microstructural feature (phase, dislocation arrangement, boundary) is responsible for any given stage.

The earlier TEM result establishes only that two qualitatively different dislocation morphologies (pile-up at a lamella boundary vs. ordered parallel slip-trace lines) coexist somewhere in the microstructure, implying non-uniform, phase-dependent dislocation response — but with no strain-state anchoring and no quantification, it cannot say how that heterogeneity evolves with strain or connect it to the macroscopic curve.

Combined, the two support that the sustained, extended Stage II plateau (a slowly-decaying but persistent hardening rate over a broad strain range) is consistent with a two-population deformation process: one region storing/piling up dislocations (sustaining hardening) while another region glides more freely along ordered slip traces (limiting further hardening) — i.e., a composite/dual-phase hardening mechanism plausibly underlies the staged macroscopic behavior, rather than a single homogeneous dislocation process.

This combination leaves open: which phase (L12 or B2) drives which stage; whether pile-up density actually grows through Stage II and saturates/reverses to produce Stage III softening; and any quantitative or strain-resolved link between the TEM snapshots and the specific hardening-rate stages — the compatibility is plausible, not demonstrated.

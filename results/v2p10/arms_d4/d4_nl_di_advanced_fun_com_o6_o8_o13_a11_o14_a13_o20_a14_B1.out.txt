Panel F5d shows six treatment-group tumor-growth spaghetti plots. The two relevant to this question: BNs-BSA+808nm alone shows 4/5 tumors regrow starting ~day 14 (transient suppression only), while BNs-BSA+Lasers (the combined 660/808nm dual-laser group) shows all 5 tumors held at ~0 volume through day 20+ (complete, durable ablation).

What this adds to the earlier settled result: A+B already established that this material has strong 808nm NIR extinction and converts that absorbed light into concentration/power-dependent heat in vitro (a qualitative photothermal effect), without any in vivo or efficacy claim. This new in vivo panel shows that irradiating BNs-BSA-laden tumors at 808nm does produce a measurable antitumor effect (growth is suppressed relative to untreated/laser-only/BNs-BSA-only controls, which grow uncontrolled), confirming the photothermal mechanism is biologically active and translates into tumor growth suppression in living animals — not just a bench-top heating artifact. Read together, A+B+this panel support: the demonstrated NIR-driven photothermal heating is a genuine antitumor mechanism in vivo.

What it leaves open: 808nm monotherapy is not curative — regrowth in 4/5 mice after day 14 shows the photothermal effect alone is only partial/transient, whereas full durable ablation (0/5 regrowth) requires the combined dual-laser (660+808nm) treatment. This dataset cannot say why: whether the second wavelength adds an independent mechanism (e.g., photodynamic), whether it simply delivers more total heat/dose, or whether phase heterogeneity (Bi vs Bi2O3, unresolved earlier) explains why some 808nm-only tumors resist. No temperature or heat-dose data are given for the in vivo tumors themselves, so the in vitro heating magnitudes cannot be quantitatively linked to the observed regrowth pattern.

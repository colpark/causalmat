This measurement (power factor tracking resistivity to a shared optimum at x=0.01) supports the conclusion that the x=0.01 composition is a genuine transport optimum by more than one metric, and that whatever drives it is not simply the monotonic rise in Hall carrier concentration n_H across x=0.005-0.04 predicted by the Se-donor/E_F mechanism. That is, the Se-donor doping picture alone is insufficient to explain the location of the optimum, and some additional composition-dependent factor (mobility, scattering, or Seebeck coefficient variation with x) must be operating at x=0.01.

However, this does not amount to independent corroboration in a strict sense: since PF = S^2/rho, a PF peak at x=0.01 could simply be inherited from the already-known resistivity dip at that composition, with no separate contribution from Seebeck behavior. So the finding is consistent with, but does not prove, two independent transport quantities converging on the same optimum. It also does not identify what the extra factor actually is. The match is established only at 300 K (resistivity data), while PF was measured at 300 K and 623 K, so nothing here confirms whether the x=0.01 optimum or its underlying cause persists across temperature. The DFT+Hall mechanistic account remains a two-state, 0 K snapshot that does not compute n_H from bands directly, so this addition does not close that gap. Finally, other possible contributors -- temperature-dependent scattering or defect states -- are not ruled out as (partial) causes of either the resistivity minimum or the power factor maximum. In short: the result strengthens the case that x=0.01 is special and that carrier density is not the whole story, but leaves the actual mechanism, its temperature stability, and the strict independence of the two measurements unresolved.

Panel F5d (individual tumor-volume-vs-time curves, six treatment groups) matches the described measurement: the rightmost panel (BNs-BSA+Lasers, i.e., combined 660/808 nm) shows all five curves flat near zero out to day ~20-22, with no visible upturn. The BNs-BSA+808 nm panel (blue) shows curves suppressed near zero through roughly day 14-15, after which most of the traces (visually, the majority of the five) turn upward and climb to volumes of roughly 250-700 mm3 by day 20-22, while at most one curve stays near baseline.

What this justifies: the dual-wavelength (660/808 nm) combination with BNs-BSA achieves durable, complete tumor ablation in this cohort (n=5, all responders, no regrowth within the observation window), whereas single-wavelength 808 nm photothermal treatment with the same nanoagent produces only a transient response — most tumors regrow after about two weeks. This supports a claim that the two irradiation wavelengths act synergistically (or address different tumor regions/depths) and that 808 nm alone is insufficient for lasting local control, i.e., PRF/service capability of the 808 nm-only regimen is limited to short-term suppression, not cure.

What it leaves open: the mechanism behind regrowth (incomplete thermal ablation depth, insufficient dose, or biological heterogeneity) is not addressed by this curve data alone; durability of the combined-laser group beyond day ~22 is untested; the small n (5 per group) limits statistical confidence in "4 of 5" vs "5 of 5" distinctions; and no histology/molecular data here explain why 660 nm and 808 nm differ in efficacy.

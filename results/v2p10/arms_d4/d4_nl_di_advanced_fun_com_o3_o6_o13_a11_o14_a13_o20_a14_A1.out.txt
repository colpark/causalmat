Panel F5d (5th sub-panel, "BNs-BSA + 808 nm," blue) shows individual tumor-volume curves: nearly all animals sit near zero out to about day 12-14, then most curves inflect upward, climbing to roughly 250-700 mm3 by day 20-22, consistent with the stated "four of five regrow after ~day 14." The 6th sub-panel ("BNs-BSA + Lasers," red, i.e. the dual 660/808 nm combination) shows all curves pinned at essentially zero volume through day 20, with no late inflection in any of the five traces.

What this justifies: within this cohort, single-wavelength 808 nm photothermal treatment with BNs-BSA produces an initial tumor-suppression response that is not durable in most animals — regrowth (relapse) is the dominant individual-level outcome by ~day 14-20. By contrast, the dual-laser (660+808 nm) combination with the same nanoagent produces uniform, durable suppression across all five individual tumors through the observation window. This supports a claim that combining the two wavelengths (or the associated dual-mode/synergistic treatment) confers a more robust and complete "service capability" (durable tumor control) than the 808 nm-alone arm, at least at the population level shown.

What it leaves open: the panel is descriptive (individual growth curves), not an inferential test — no statistics, hazard ratios, or significance are given here, so the true magnitude/reliability of the difference between 808-nm-alone and dual-laser arms isn't quantified. The endpoint stops at ~day 20-22, so it is unknown whether the "durable" dual-laser responses would also relapse with longer follow-up, or whether the 808 nm-alone regrowths would plateau or continue expanding. Mechanism (why 4/5 regrow at 808 nm alone but 0/5 do under dual laser) is not addressed by this figure — it could reflect incomplete photothermal ablation, tumor heterogeneity, laser penetration/dosimetry differences, or a true synergistic effect of combined wavelengths; this panel cannot distinguish between those explanations. Sample size (n=5 per arm) is small, so exact reproducibility/generalizability of "4 of 5" is not established beyond this cohort.

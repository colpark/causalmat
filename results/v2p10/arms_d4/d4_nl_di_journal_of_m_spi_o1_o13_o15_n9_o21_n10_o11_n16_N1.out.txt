The earlier established result is almost certainly the microstructural characterization showing this alloy solidifies into a fine, uniform lamellar eutectic structure of alternating soft FCC and hard B2 (ordered BCC) phases, a structure known (from prior work on similar AlCoCrFeNi2.1-type EHEAs) to give a favorable strength-ductility combination. That result alone only tells you the alloy has this two-phase lamellar architecture; it says nothing about how the phases actually behave once load is applied, or where failure initiates.

The new tensile measurement (stress-strain curves, strain-hardening rate, and likely fractography/EBSD or in-situ imaging of the deformed/fractured specimen) shows strain localizing preferentially in the ductile FCC lamellae while the B2 lamellae carry higher local stress, with cracking/void formation nucleating at FCC/B2 interfaces or within the B2 phase before propagating to failure.

Read together, the two results jointly establish that the good tensile properties arise specifically from load transfer/strain partitioning between the two lamellar phases, with damage originating at the phase boundaries — a mechanistic link that neither the microstructure paper (no mechanical response) nor the tensile data alone (no phase-resolved deformation path) could establish separately.

What remains open: the atomic-scale dislocation-interface interaction mechanism, whether the same partitioning/damage sequence holds at different strain rates or temperatures, and whether this generalizes to other Al-content variants (Alx) in the same family. This is inference, not a report of read data.

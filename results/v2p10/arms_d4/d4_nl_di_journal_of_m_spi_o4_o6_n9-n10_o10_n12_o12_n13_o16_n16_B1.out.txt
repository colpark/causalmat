The ZT data show that the composition with the lowest resistivity and highest power factor at 300 K, x=0.01, is not the composition with the highest ZT at 300 K: at 300 K, x=0.005 (~0.84) exceeds x=0.01 (~0.78), and ZT falls further and monotonically through x=0.02-0.04 (0.63-0.69). Only at higher temperatures does the ranking invert, with x=0.01 (red circles in F6a) rising above x=0.005 (black squares) from roughly 400-450 K upward and remaining the best-performing doped composition through 623 K, tracking approximately with, and even slightly exceeding, the n-Bi2Te3 reference curve near 450-500 K.

Read together with the resistivity/power-factor result, this settles that the x=0.01 transport optimum (lowest resistivity, highest PF at 300 K) does not by itself dictate the ZT ranking at 300 K -- something else, almost certainly thermal conductivity (not measured in either panel), must be more favorable at x=0.005 to overcome its transport disadvantage and produce the higher room-temperature ZT. It also shows the optimum composition is temperature-dependent: x=0.01 only becomes the ZT leader once temperature rises into the 400-623 K range, which is consistent with (though not proven by) the earlier note that its power factor advantage was also documented at 623 K.

What remains open: no thermal conductivity data are given, so the reversal at 300 K is inferred, not measured; the non-n_H factor behind the x=0.01 resistivity/PF optimum is still unidentified; and whether the same factor governs the temperature-dependent ZT crossover, or a separate mechanism (e.g., temperature-dependent kappa or scattering) does, cannot be determined from these panels.

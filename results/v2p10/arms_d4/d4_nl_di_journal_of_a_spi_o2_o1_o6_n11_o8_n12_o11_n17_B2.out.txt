The combination therefore supports a claim neither piece makes alone: Al2O3-YSZ's early microstructural instability (large orientation change) temporally coincides with the onset of macroscopic coating failure, and — importantly — the later diminishing of the diffraction signal does not track with macroscopic recovery or stabilization; the coating keeps degrading visibly after the diffraction proxy has quieted down.

What this leaves open: F8 gives no crack-network or micrograph-level corroboration for Al2O3-YSZ analogous to YSZ's n12 (F4b/F4c), so there is no microstructural (as opposed to macroscopic/photographic) confirmation. There is no mechanism linking the cubic phase or its orientation-change rate to spallation — the timing co-occurrence is not causal evidence. It's a single coupon/field, not a statistical sample. And F8's spallation is localized to corners, while n11's orientation-change measurement is not clearly localized there, so spatial correspondence between the two measurements is only inferred, not established.

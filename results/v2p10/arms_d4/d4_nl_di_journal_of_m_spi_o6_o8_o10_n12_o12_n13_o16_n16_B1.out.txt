I opened F6a: a ZT-vs-T plot for x=0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04, with n-Bi2Te3 (Ref. 8) and a Se-doped literature curve (Ref. 22) for comparison, ZT peaking near 1.2–1.3 around 450–550 K for all doped samples and falling to ~0.2 for x=0.

At 300 K the panel confirms the stated values, and critically shows x=0.005 (~0.84) sitting slightly above x=0.01 (~0.78), which is the reverse of the PF ranking established earlier (PF peaks at x=0.01, ~29, clearly above x=0.005, the lowest doped PF). Since ZT = PF·T/κ, this reversal is only possible if κ differs across compositions in a way not visible in the PF/resistivity data: x=0.005 must have a low enough thermal conductivity to overcome its PF disadvantage and out-perform x=0.01 in ZT at 300 K. So the combination settles that the ZT advantage of doped Mg3.2Bi1.4Sb0.6-xSex over undoped material — and specifically the composition with the best room-temperature ZT — cannot be explained by the electronic-transport story (n12/n13) alone; a thermal-conductivity contribution, not characterized by either earlier measurement, must be doing real work in setting the ZT ranking, at least at 300 K.

What it leaves open: no κ (lattice or total) values are given here, so the magnitude or mechanism (e.g., Se mass-fluctuation phonon scattering) of that thermal-conductivity effect is unestablished; whether the PF-led ranking (x=0.01 highest) reasserts itself in ZT at higher T, where the doped curves converge/cross, is not resolved since PF was only measured at 300 K and 623 K, not continuously; and all the earlier qualifications (S untested, sample heterogeneity, doping mechanism) still stand.

Panel F4a (Coulombic efficiency of C HS vs CnC HS at 1 mAh cm⁻² areal capacity, 4 mA cm⁻² current density) shows two cycling traces: one set of points (CnC HS) scattering between roughly 93–100% CE and persisting out to ~200-plus cycles with no sign of decline; the other set (C HS) tracks similarly for the first ~35-40 cycles, then collapses — two points read at essentially 0% CE around cycle 40 and again near cycle 48 — and no further data are plotted after ~50 cycles.

What this justifies: the measurement directly supports a claim about relative cycling durability as indexed by Coulombic efficiency — the CnC HS configuration sustains high, stable CE far longer (to ~205 cycles) than the bare C HS, which fails abruptly (CE crashing to zero) by roughly cycle 40–48 and is not reported beyond ~50 cycles. This is a legitimate, visually clear basis for saying CnC HS has markedly better short/medium-term electrochemical stability than C HS under this one test condition.

What it leaves open: this is Coulombic efficiency only, not capacity retention, rate capability, energy density, or any other component of "service_capability" — CE near 100% does not by itself prove usable capacity is preserved. The test is reported at a single areal capacity/current density combination, so generalization to other operating conditions is unverified. The panel also doesn't state cell count/replicates, so whether the C HS failure and CnC HS survival are reproducible or single-cell artifacts is unknown. Finally, CnC HS's behavior beyond ~205 cycles (the end of the plotted range) is simply not measured, so any claim of indefinite/long-term service life beyond that point is unsupported by this panel.

Combined result: The cycling panel (F4b) shows CnC HS/Zn symmetric cells running smoothly (voltage confined to roughly ±0.03 V) out to ~7100 min, while C HS/Zn cells develop growing voltage spikes from ~2600 min and fail outright near 4400 min. Read together with the earlier finding — that CnC HS is the same sample carrying the nitrogen-linked spectral shift, the flatter/more compact Zn morphology, and the lower nucleation overpotential (39 vs 43 mV) — this extends the tracked co-occurrence from three axes to four: nitrogen chemistry, morphology, nucleation overpotential, and long-term cycling stability/failure all align on the same CnC HS vs. C HS split. This combination supports the conclusion that whatever distinguishes CnC HS from C HS chemically and morphologically is associated not just with easier initial nucleation but with a durable, order-of-magnitude difference in cell lifetime and voltage stability under prolonged galvanostatic cycling — i.e., the early nucleation advantage co-occurs with (and is consistent with, though not proven to cause) resistance to the dendritic/corrosion processes that eventually short or degrade the C HS cell.

What remains open: this is still a two-sample comparison (n=1 per condition), so no causal mechanism is established — it does not show that the nitrogen species, the flatter morphology, or the lower nucleation overpotential is what prevents failure, only that all four measurements co-vary across the same CnC HS/C HS pair. Other unmeasured structural or compositional differences could be the actual driver of cycling stability. The panel gives only qualitative failure-timing/spike behavior, not a quantitative cycling-life metric, current density dependence, or coulombic efficiency. And the interpretive labels (pyridinic/graphitic N, Zn-N bonding) from the earlier step remain unverified by these panels.

Panel F2c (read directly): 1st discharge (black) descends to a low-voltage plateau near 0.15 V reaching ~865 mAh/g; 1st charge (red) recovers only ~470 mAh/g (~54% first-cycle coulombic efficiency, i.e., large irreversible loss); the 40th cycle (blue) collapses to a tiny loop of only ~60 mAh/g — roughly 87% capacity loss relative to the 1st charge.

What the combination settles: This is the first actual capacity/cycling measurement in the chain, and it supplies exactly the piece the earlier result explicitly lacked — real electrochemical performance data. Read together with the earlier finding (retained crystalline oxide, Co not driven metallic, invariant SAXS pore structure, and MD's comparatively low/early-saturating oxidation degree for Na-Co3O4), the combination now supports that Na-Co3O4's low, low-oxidation-degree, structurally-limited sodiation reaction is accompanied by — and consistent with — low reversible capacity and severe capacity fade in practice. This converges structure-side, simulation-side, and performance-side evidence onto the same qualitative picture: Na-Co3O4 is a poorly reactive, low-capacity, fast-fading electrode, with the structural/simulation data offering a plausible mechanistic rationale (limited conversion) for the poor cycling seen here.

What remains open: The combination still does not establish causation — it cannot distinguish whether poor sodiation reactivity (vs. volume change, SEI formation, or particle cracking) is what actually drives the 87% fade by cycle 40; that qualification carries forward unresolved. No structural data (XRD/SAXS) exist at cycle 40 to confirm the oxide/pore invariance still holds when capacity has collapsed. The large first-cycle irreversible loss (865→470) near 0.15 V strongly suggests SEI-type side reactions, a mechanism the structural/MD story does not address at all. The 2 ps MD timescale still cannot be equated with the 40-cycle timescale.

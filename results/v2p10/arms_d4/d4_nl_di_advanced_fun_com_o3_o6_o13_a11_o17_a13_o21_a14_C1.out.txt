This measurement settles that the bulk two-phase (metallic Bi + Bi2O3) reduced material, already shown to absorb 808 nm NIR light as a bulk, mass-proportional property (alpha = 6.4 L g-1 cm-1, R2 = 0.9999), also converts that absorbed 808 nm light into heat with a quantifiable, well-fit photothermal response: a linear -ln(theta) vs time relationship yielding tau_s = 133.4 s and a photothermal conversion efficiency eta = 19.4%. Taken together, the two results support that this specific as-prepared bulk composite is both a strong NIR absorber and an effective photothermal heat generator — i.e., it has demonstrated, characterizable photothermal conversion capability at the whole-material level, with a specific efficiency value now attached to it.

What this leaves open: it does not identify which phase (metallic Bi, Bi2O3, or the interface between them) is actually responsible for generating the heat, since tau_s/eta come from a single bulk temperature-vs-time curve with no phase- or particle-resolved readout — the same ambiguity left by the absorbance measurement. It also does not establish that the component(s) driving NIR absorption are mechanistically the same component(s) driving heat conversion; the connection is made only at the bulk-material level, not through any per-phase causal pathway. Further, alpha and eta/tau_s are conceptually different quantities (a concentration-normalized optical constant versus an efficiency/time-constant from one heating curve), so the strong linear absorbance relationship does not itself predict or guarantee this efficiency value. Finally, because eta/tau_s derive from a single measurement (F3h), the result supports only that this particular bulk material has this photothermal efficiency under these conditions — not any general relationship between eta and phase composition, concentration, or incident power density.

Panel F4a (read) shows CnC HS maintaining ~93–100% Coulombic efficiency out to roughly 205 cycles, while C HS collapses to 0% CE at ~40 and ~48 cycles, with its data series ending near cycle 50.

Read together with the settled n14→n16 result, this supports a broadened conclusion: the same host-material distinction (CnC HS vs C HS) that produces a growing plating-overpotential gap at higher current density (F4c) is also the distinction between a host that sustains stable, near-100% CE for hundreds of cycles and one that fails catastrophically after only ~40–50 cycles. So the electrochemical difference between these two materials is not confined to a modest, rate-dependent overpotential shift — it is accompanied by an outright cycling-stability/failure difference. This strengthens the case that whatever separates CnC HS from C HS (the chemistry/morphology axis established earlier) tracks a substantial, practically consequential performance gap, not merely a small instrumental voltage difference visible only at high current density.

What it leaves open: causal attribution is still unresolved — nothing here indicates whether the nitrogen chemical state, the flatter/sparser zinc morphology, some other unmeasured host property, or the failure mode itself (e.g., dendrite-driven short circuit, dead/isolated Zn, side reactions) is responsible for either the overpotential separation or the CE collapse. The CE test was run at a single current density (4 mA cm⁻²), so no dose-response link between the overpotential gap's growth with rate and the timing/severity of C HS's failure can be drawn — the two measurements remain independent electrochemical axes, not one continuous mechanistic story. Also unaddressed: why C HS fails abruptly rather than degrading gradually, and whether CnC HS would eventually show similar collapse at cycle counts beyond ~205.

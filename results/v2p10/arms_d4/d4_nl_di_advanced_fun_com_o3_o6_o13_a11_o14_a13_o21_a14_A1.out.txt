Panel F5f (tumor volume, day 0-22, n=5 per group) confirms the stated ranking: PBS and BNs-BSA alone are statistically indistinguishable (n.s. marked at day 22, both ~2100-2350 mm3), tracking essentially together as the top, fastest-growing curves. Lasers-only sits somewhat below them (~1750 mm3, single star). BNs-BSA+660 nm is markedly lower (~1470 mm3, marked with multiple stars). BNs-BSA+808 nm is much lower still (~370-400 mm3 with large error bar, stars+hash). BNs-BSA+660/808 nm combined is flat at ~0 mm3 throughout the 22 days (stars), i.e., essentially complete tumor suppression.

What this justifies: the measurement directly supports a graded photothermal/photodynamic efficacy ordering for this nanomaterial (BNs-BSA) under different light conditions — the material itself (BNs-BSA, no light) and laser-only controls do not slow tumor growth relative to PBS, single-wavelength irradiation (660 or 808 nm) with the material gives partial, dose-graded suppression (808 nm outperforming 660 nm), and dual-wavelength (660+808 nm) irradiation combined with the material gives complete tumor ablation over the 22-day window in this cohort. This is solid causal evidence for a "service_capability" of BNs-BSA as a combination-phototherapy agent whose efficacy depends on wavelength synergy, at least in this one animal model.

What it leaves open: the panel alone doesn't establish mechanism (i.e., whether 660 nm and 808 nm act via distinct physical processes such as PDT vs PTT, versus simply additive heating/dosing), doesn't quantify statistical significance between every pairwise group (only select comparisons are annotated), and n=5 per group with only one tumor model and one dosing/irradiation regimen means generalizability (other tumor types, doses, durability beyond day 22, toxicity/safety) is not settled by this figure alone.

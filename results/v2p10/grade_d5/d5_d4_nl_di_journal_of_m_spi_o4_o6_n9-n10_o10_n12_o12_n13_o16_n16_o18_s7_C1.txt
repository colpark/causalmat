THE ANSWER
This comparison settles that the electronic-transport optimum and the overall thermoelectric-performance optimum do not coincide across the doping series: power factor and resistivity are best at x=0.01 (~29 uW cm-1 K-2, resistivity minimum), but the highest ZT at 300 K occurs one composition step lower, at x=0.005 (~0.84, versus ~0.78 at x=0.01 and 0.63-0.69 at x=0.02-0.04, against a ~0.88 n-Bi2Te3 reference). This justifies the conclusion that whatever mechanism enhances power factor and suppresses resistivity at x=0.01 is not, by itself, sufficient to maximize ZT -- some additional composition-dependent factor must be pulling the ZT optimum back to x=0.005. That is as far as the comparison goes: it establishes a location mismatch between two optima, not a mechanism or a continuous trend.

It leaves open several things. First, since ZT = PF*T/kappa and no thermal-conductivity data are cited here, it is unknown whether lower kappa at x=0.005, a Seebeck-coefficient effect not captured by PF, or some other factor drives the shift. Second, both quantities are compared only at 300 K, so whether the same x=0.005-vs-x=0.01 split persists at higher temperature (e.g., 623 K, where a PF value exists without a matching ZT) is untested. Third, the underlying PF/resistivity result itself does not separate a resistivity effect from an independent Seebeck effect, and that ambiguity carries forward unresolved beneath the added unknown of thermal conductivity. Finally, the comparison covers only discrete doping levels, giving an ordering of optima rather than a continuous PF-versus-ZT relationship, and it does not test or extend the underlying 0 K DFT+Hall two-state mechanistic account.

THE ANSWER
This measurement settles that, within the same sample pair, the structural/electrochemical distinction between CnC HS and C HS (nitrogen-linked spectral shift, flatter/sparser vs dense-platelet zinc morphology, and a growing overpotential gap from about -0.05 V to -0.10 V with increasing current density) co-occurs with a sharp divergence in long-term cycling durability: CnC HS sustains roughly 93-100% Coulombic efficiency out to about 205 cycles, whereas C HS's CE collapses to zero around 40-48 cycles and its dataset ends near 50 cycles. So what is supported is a consistent directional pattern across the two hosts — the host that looks better on chemistry, morphology, and overpotential is also the host that survives far more cycles at stable efficiency — establishing co-occurrence of two favorable/unfavorable profiles in the same two materials.

What it leaves open is substantial. It does not show that the overpotential gap, the nitrogen chemistry, or the morphology causes or predicts the CE cycling outcome; it only shows the two effects appear together in this one pair. The nitrogen chemical-environment change and the morphology change remain confounded with each other and with the CE result, since all vary together in the same samples with no way to isolate which factor (if any) drives durability. The two measurements are also different kinds of quantities — a continuous, rate-dependent voltage gap at one time point versus a cycle-count failure/endurance outcome — so no dose-response or magnitude relationship between the size of the overpotential gap and the size of the cycle-life difference can be inferred. Finally, with only two materials compared, this is a single co-occurrence between two endpoints, not a trend established over multiple points or intermediate cases, so generalization beyond this pair is unsupported.

This measurement extends an already-established three-way co-occurrence (nitrogen-linked spectral shift, flatter/sparser morphology, and lower nucleation overpotential of 39 mV vs 43 mV, both distinguishing CnC HS from C HS) by adding a fourth co-occurring axis: long-term cycling behavior. C HS develops growing voltage spikes starting around 2600 minutes and fails near 4400 minutes, whereas CnC HS remains stable within about 0.03 V out to 7100 minutes. Read together, this supports the conclusion that within this single two-sample comparison, the same sample (CnC HS) that shows the nitrogen-chemistry difference, the flatter morphology, and the lower nucleation overpotential is also the one that sustains stable, low-noise cycling much longer before failure. So it is justified to say that nucleation-overpotential ordering, nitrogen-chemistry difference, morphology difference, and cycling-life ordering all track together across CnC HS vs C HS.

What this leaves open is substantial. It does not establish causation in either direction between the overpotential, the nitrogen chemistry, or the morphology and the cycling-life outcome — it only shows a fourth variable co-occurring in the same two samples. It does not rule out other unmeasured structural, compositional, or textural differences as the true driver of the cycling-life gap. With only two samples, the result supports an ordering (CnC HS outlasting C HS) but not any quantitative or dose-like relationship. The two measured quantities are also of different kinds — a single early-cycle mV value versus a cumulative long-duration failure time — so the "tracking" is a pairing of orderings, not a functional relationship. All prior qualifications carry over: pyridinic/graphitic assignments and any Zn-N bond remain interpretive, morphology has no measured coverage/amount/size, and overpotential is read only at initial-cycle nucleation, not across cycling or current density.

The photograph (F4g) shows a three-cell-in-series Zn-MnO2 stack wired through a handheld multimeter to a smartphone whose screen is lit and displaying content. This directly demonstrates one thing: the assembled cell stack can deliver enough voltage/current to power on and sustain an active display on a real consumer electronic load. That is a legitimate, if qualitative, demonstration bearing on service_capability/PRF (practical relevance/functional demonstration) — it supports the claim that the battery stack is capable of driving a real-world device, not just sourcing a bench load or LED.

What it leaves open is everything quantitative and durational: the multimeter reading itself is illegible in this low-resolution crop, so no actual voltage, current, or power value can be read off the image. There is no indication of how long the phone stayed powered, what fraction of charge was consumed, whether the phone was actively drawing significant current (e.g., under load) or just idling with a static screen, or how this demonstration compares across cycles, temperatures, or after degradation. It also says nothing about capacity retention, coulombic efficiency, or cycle life — only that at this one instant, the stack could turn on and run a phone. So the image supports a qualitative "proof of concept" claim of functional viability but does not settle any quantitative service-capability metric (voltage under load, runtime, energy delivered) and does not generalize beyond this single demonstrated instance.

This measurement settles a fourth co-occurrence in the same two-sample comparison (CnC HS vs C HS), without establishing causation.

What it supports: within this single pairwise comparison, four axes track together consistently — lower nucleation overpotential (39 mV vs 43 mV), the nitrogen-linked spectral shift, the flatter/sparser morphology, and superior long-term cycling behavior (CnC HS holding voltage excursions to ~0.03 V out to ~7100 min, versus C HS spiking from ~2600 min and failing near 4400 min). All four properties are consistently attached to the same sample (CnC HS) and consistently absent/worse in the other (C HS). This justifies the descriptive conclusion that, in this comparison, the sample with the more favorable nucleation and nitrogen chemistry and morphology is also the one that cycles longer and more stably — extending an already-established three-way co-occurrence to a fourth, cycling-life axis.

What it leaves open: it does not show that any of the three properties (overpotential, nitrogen chemistry, morphology) causes the improved cycling life, or that cycling life causes or reflects back on them — it is purely an added co-occurrence, not a mechanism. It does not rule out other unmeasured structural, compositional, or textural differences between the two samples as the true driver. With only two samples/traces, it supports an ordering (CnC HS > C HS) but no quantitative or dose-response relationship between overpotential magnitude and cycling duration. The two measured quantities are of different types — a single early-cycle value (mV) versus a cumulative long-duration outcome (minutes to failure) — so this is a pairing of orderings, not a functional relationship. It also inherits earlier qualifications: chemical-state (pyridinic/graphitic) labels and any Zn-N bond remain interpretive, morphology lacks coverage/amount/size quantification, and overpotential is only measured at nucleation on the initial cycle, not across cycling or varying current density.

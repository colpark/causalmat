This measurement settles that, within this same CnC HS vs C HS sample pair, the host already identified (via chemistry/morphology and overpotential) as the more favorable one is also the host with far superior long-term cycling durability: CnC HS sustains ~93-100% Coulombic efficiency out to ~205 cycles, while C HS collapses to zero CE by ~40-48 cycles and its data end near 50 cycles. So the justified conclusion is a co-occurrence claim: the structural/electrochemical profile tied to CnC HS (nitrogen-shifted chemistry, flatter/sparser zinc morphology, smaller overpotential penalty that grows with current density) tracks together with markedly better cycling-life outcomes in the same two materials — CnC HS wins on both axes, C HS loses on both.

What it leaves open is substantial. It does not establish causation or predictive power: nothing here shows the overpotential gap, the nitrogen chemistry, or the morphology drives or forecasts the CE divergence — only that they co-occur in this one pair. The nitrogen-chemistry vs morphology confound from the earlier step is inherited unchanged; neither can be isolated as responsible for the cycling difference. The two measured quantities are not commensurate (a continuous, rate-dependent voltage gap at a single time point vs. a cycle-count failure/endurance outcome), so no dose-response or magnitude mapping between the size of the overpotential separation and the size of the cycle-life difference can be drawn. Finally, with only two materials compared, this is a single co-occurrence instance, not a relationship demonstrated across multiple points or intermediate cases, so generalization beyond this pair is unsupported.

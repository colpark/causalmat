Panel F4f (galvanostatic charge/discharge cycling of pre-deposited Zn on the CnC HS foil electrode in a Zn-MnO2 cell) shows charge and discharge capacity tracking each other closely and declining only modestly, from roughly 1.0 mAh to roughly 0.9 mAh, over 500 cycles (points are 10-cycle-averaged, sampled every 10 cycles). The inset compares the 1st- and 100th-cycle voltage-vs-capacity curves and these nearly overlap in shape and voltage plateaus.

What this supports: the CnC HS foil host, once pre-plated with zinc, sustains stable and largely reversible Zn plating/stripping (charge/discharge) behavior over an extended cycling window (500 cycles) with only ~10% capacity fade, and the electrochemical reaction mechanism/voltage profile is preserved from early to later cycling (1st vs 100th cycle overlap), i.e. no major shift in polarization or reaction pathway develops early on. This is direct evidence for the PRF/service_capability claim of cycling stability and capacity retention under galvanostatic operation in a full Zn-MnO2 cell using this current-collector/host design.

What it leaves open: this is a single test condition (one current rate/areal capacity, one cell configuration) with no stated current density, mass loading, or number of replicate cells, so rate capability, reproducibility, and behavior under different currents or larger capacities are not established. The data stop at 500 cycles, so longer-term degradation, coulombic efficiency values, and failure mode (e.g., eventual dendrite growth or capacity cliff) beyond that point are unknown. There is no comparison to a bare/undecorated foil control in this same panel, so the specific quantitative advantage attributable to the CnC HS architecture (versus a baseline current collector) cannot be read off this figure alone. Full cell energy density, capacity retention percentage, and coulombic efficiency are not explicitly quantified in the panel itself.

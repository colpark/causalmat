Panel F4f shows galvanostatic cycling of pre-deposited Zn on the CnC HS foil in an electrolytic Zn-MnO2 full cell: charge and discharge capacity are plotted over 500 cycles (points averaged every 10 cycles), both starting near 1.0 mAh and drifting down to roughly 0.9 mAh, with charge and discharge tracking each other closely throughout (no visible divergence, i.e., near-unity, stable Coulombic efficiency by proxy). The inset overlays the 1st and 100th voltage-vs-capacity discharge/charge curve, and these two curves nearly superimpose in shape and voltage plateaus.

What this supports: the measurement directly supports a PRF (performance/service_capability) claim of cycling stability/durability — the CnC HS current collector/host sustains a working Zn anode in a full Zn-MnO2 cell for at least 500 cycles with only modest (~10%) capacity fade, and the electrochemical charge/discharge mechanism (voltage profile shape) is preserved from cycle 1 to cycle 100, i.e., no major degradation mode (e.g., new plateau, large polarization growth) emerges early in life.

What it leaves open: this is a single cell/condition (one current density, one loading, presumably one temperature) with no error bars or replicate cells shown, so reproducibility and variability are unquantified. Coulombic efficiency is not explicitly plotted, only inferred from charge≈discharge. No comparison to a bare-foil or control anode is shown here, so the specific contribution of the CnC HS morphology to this stability (versus a conventional Zn foil) cannot be judged from this panel alone. Longer-term behavior beyond 500 cycles, rate capability, and failure mode (dendrites, capacity cliff) are not addressed.

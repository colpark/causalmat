Panel F4f (galvanostatic charge/discharge of pre-deposited Zn on CnC HS in a full electrolytic Zn-MnO2 cell) shows charge and discharge capacity holding near 1.0 mAh, drifting only to ~0.9 mAh over 500 cycles, with near-identical 1st and 100th cycle voltage profiles.

Combined with the earlier-established co-occurrence (lower nucleation overpotential, N-linked spectral shift, and flatter morphology on CnC HS tracking with longer, low-noise symmetric-cell cycling to ~7100 min versus C HS failing near 4400 min), this new panel adds a device-level, capacity-based endpoint on CnC HS alone. Together they support the broader claim that whatever distinguishes CnC HS (its nitrogen chemistry, morphology, and lower nucleation overpotential) is associated not only with a symmetric-cell voltage-stability advantage but also with a full-cell that retains ~90% of its capacity and an essentially unchanged voltage profile out to 500 cycles — i.e., the favorable behavior generalizes from a symmetric-cell voltage metric to a full-cell capacity-retention metric on the same material.

What it leaves open: F4f contains no C HS full-cell comparison, so it cannot establish that CnC HS's full-cell capacity retention is better than C HS's — it only shows CnC HS performs well in isolation. The link between the earlier overpotential/morphology/chemistry co-occurrence and this capacity-retention result is therefore inferred by analogy (same material, different test), not demonstrated by a shared measurement or causal pathway. It still does not identify which structural/chemical feature drives either outcome, and the correlation across the two different metrics (symmetric-cell longevity in minutes vs. full-cell capacity in mAh over cycles) remains an association across different test types, not a validated causal or quantitative relationship.

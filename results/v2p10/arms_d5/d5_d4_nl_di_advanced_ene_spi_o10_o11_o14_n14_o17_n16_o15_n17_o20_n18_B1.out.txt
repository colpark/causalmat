The panel (4f) shows, for CnC HS in a full electrolytic Zn-MnO2 cell, that charge and discharge capacity decline only modestly (~1.0 to ~0.9 mAh) over 500 cycles, with the 1st and 100th cycle voltage profiles nearly superimposed — i.e., stable, low-fade cycling in an application-relevant full-cell format, for CnC HS alone (no C HS data shown here).

Read together with the earlier n16->n17 result, this adds a third, independent performance axis for CnC HS: (1) favorable N-linked chemistry/morphology and lower overpotential, (2) high, long-lived CE (93-100% to ~205 cycles) versus C HS's collapse by ~40-50 cycles, and now (3) durable full-cell capacity retention (~90% of initial) and stable discharge profile shape out to 500 cycles — a longer horizon and a more practically relevant test (full battery vs. the presumed half-cell/CE setup) than either prior measurement covered. The combination supports concluding that CnC HS's advantageous structural/electrochemical signature is consistent with, and extends to, sustained real-device capacity performance beyond where the CE data stopped, i.e., three separate metrics now converge on CnC HS as the more durable host over a longer cycling range.

What it leaves open: because no C HS capacity-retention curve is shown in this panel, it cannot itself establish a comparative divergence on this metric — it corroborates CnC HS's durability in an absolute sense but does not extend the direct two-host contrast the CE data provided. It also does nothing to resolve the standing confound between nitrogen chemistry and morphology, still cannot relate magnitudes across the mismatched quantity types (voltage gap, CE-percent/cycle-count, capacity/cycle-count), and remains a two-endpoint (single-material-shown) observation rather than a dose-response or multi-sample relationship.

The chain starts with XRD and Raman on the bismuthene sample: XRD alone shows hexagonal Bi (PDF#05-0519) as the dominant phase plus one weak Bi2O3 (321) peak near 33 deg (matched to PDF#45-1344), while Raman alone shows only the two Bi modes (~69, ~95 cm-1) with no Bi2O3 bands (~120-500 cm-1). Neither technique alone rules in or out the oxide's significance, but together they agree Bi is the majority phase and treat the oxide as a minor, Raman-invisible trace, fixing phase identity without settling the Bi/Bi2O3 spatial arrangement. Step 2 needed that phase call before the linear NIR absorbance data (A/L vs. concentration at 808 nm, R2 = 0.9999, alpha = 6.4 L g-1 cm-1) could be attributed to "this phase-identified bismuthene material" as a whole, giving it a quantified NIR extinction, though not saying which phase absorbs. Step 3 needed that extinction result before the IR-image heating data (higher temperature with concentration 200>100>50>25 ug/mL and with power 0.5->1.5 W, water cold) could be read as the extinction driving the heating, together supporting a qualitative photothermal effect rather than an artifact. Step 4 then needed that qualitative photothermal claim to make sense of the day-22 tumour-volume ranking (BNs-BSA~PBS highest, then Lasers, +660nm, +808nm~370, +660/808nm=0): the material-plus-808nm arm being far lower than either alone fits the established heat-generation direction, supporting photothermal conversion as a plausible contributor to tumour suppression, while leaving the added 660 nm effect, phase attribution, and any dose-response link unexplained.

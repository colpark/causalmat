The chain starts from two measurements taken together: SEM images showing bare BST particles smooth and ~70 nm versus modified particles rough, coated, and larger, and dielectric data showing modified composites ranked 5%>3%>1%, all well above PAEN at every frequency (~6.05 vs 3.35 at 100 Hz). Neither alone would show a link between structure and property, so the step combines them to conclude that the coated, modified particle state goes along with the higher permittivity - the structural change and the property change track together across the same samples.

Step 2 needed that result because the new DSC data (Tg of 176 C for pure PAEN, and 146, 149, 157 C for the 1/3/5% BST@Ag composites) only becomes informative when read against the same loading series already tied to the dielectric ranking. Combined, the two show that the loading series with elevated permittivity is also the series with depressed Tg, both varying together with loading. But the combination stops there: permittivity is a frequency-swept quantity and Tg is a single temperature per composition, so there is no shared temperature-resolved measurement showing whether the permittivity holds up near Tg; the co-occurrence along composition does not establish a shared mechanism over two independent effects; no operating-temperature window is measured; and the original SEM/dielectric correspondence stays only qualitative, not dose-resolved.

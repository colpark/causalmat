The chain starts with an EDS spectrum showing Ti, O and small Er (~1.4 keV) and Ce (~4.8 keV) peaks, which establishes that Er and Ce are physically present in the particles. Paired with a PL measurement showing the 390-420 nm band is strongest for TiO2, about half for Er0.5Ti-O, and lowest for Er0.5Ce0.2, Step 1 concludes that stepwise incorporation of Er then Ce tracks a progressive drop in PL intensity across the three samples.

Step 2 needed that PL ordering as a baseline to compare against a new degradation measurement (60 min: Er0.5Ce0.2 ~46% > Ce0.3 ~41% > Ce0.1 ~31% > TiO2 ~20%, same order at 20 and 40 min). At the two samples the two measurements share, TiO2 and Er0.5Ce0.2, lower PL co-occurs with higher degradation, so the step adds an association between the PL indicator and photocatalytic degradation performance, though this only holds where the two measurements overlap.

Step 3 needed that PL-degradation association because it is tested against a third measurement: antibacterial kill ratio under light (Er0.5Ce0.2 ~91-93% > Ce0.3 ~87% > Ce0.1 ~68-73% > TiO2 ~45% > control ~4-7%). The same TiO2-versus-Er0.5Ce0.2 endpoint contrast reappears here, so the step adds that the low-PL/high-performance pattern extends to a second, distinct performance measure (bacterial inactivation), rather than being specific to the degradation assay.

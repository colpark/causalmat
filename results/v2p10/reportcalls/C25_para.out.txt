The chain starts with SEM images showing bare BST particles as smooth, ~70 nm, versus modified particles as rough, coated, and larger, alongside a photograph showing powders and films darkening from white to dark brown as Ag content rises. Together these two support that the particle-level coating/roughening seen in SEM is the same change that tracks the color trend, both moving with increasing Ag loading. Step 2 needed that established co-tracking before it could make sense of new DSC data: Tg falls from pure PAEN's 176 C to 146, 149, then 157 C across the 1/3/5% Ag series. Read against the earlier result, this shows the Ag-driven modification isn't confined to appearance and particle surface -- it also registers as an ordered, directional shift in a bulk thermal property. Step 3 then needed that Tg series to interpret dielectric data: permittivity and loss in composites stay flat until a knee near 130-135 C (versus PAEN's knee near 155 C), then rise sharply. Compared against the Tg values, this knee sits below every composite Tg, showing the dielectric performance ceiling is reached before the glass transition itself.

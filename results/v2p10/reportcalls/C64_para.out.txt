The chain starts with two measurements handed in together: small-angle XRD showing a single low-angle reflection in all three glasses (~1.37, ~1.47, ~1.35 deg) and a release assay showing ampicillin released gradually and cumulatively (~340 to ~795 ug from 8h to 168h, plateauing). Neither alone says anything about the other, so Step 1 needed both to conclude that the material retaining ordered mesoporous channels is the same one showing sustained, non-burst release. Step 2 was handed that combined structure+release claim and added a new survival measurement (scaffolds+ampicillin surviving ~105%, ~81%, ~52% at days 1/3/7, versus blank 100% and drug-free scaffolds 110-118%). This step needed the prior release timing to make sense of the survival drop: because drug-free scaffolds showed no killing while ampicillin-loaded ones declined over the same days release was occurring, the combination supports that killing tracks released ampicillin rather than the scaffold material itself. But qualifications limit this: release plateaus after 24h while killing keeps falling, the two datasets aren't matched to the same composition, pore ordering was never tested against survival, and the mismatched units (ug vs %) block any dose-response conclusion.

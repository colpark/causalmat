The chain begins with two separate images that neither can prove the microstructure alone. TEM at fine scale shows straight, alternating A and B lamellae with sharp interfaces, but only at one imaged site, so it cannot rule out that this is a local artifact. EBSD at colony scale shows two-phase lamellar colonies radiating from colony boundaries with no single-phase regions, but its resolution cannot confirm the sharp, straight fine-scale lamellae TEM saw. Step 1 needed both together: because the two-phase lamellar character holds from TEM's nanoscale up through EBSD's colony scale with no single-phase regions anywhere, the structure is established as a pervasive, sharp-interfaced two-phase alternating-lamellae (eutectic-type) microstructure, not a local or partial artifact. Step 2 then adds a strain-step observation: one lamella stays featureless while its neighbor develops mottled contrast and interface line contrast. This needed Step 1's result, because without confirmation that the material is genuinely a pervasive two-phase lamellar structure, the contrast difference could only look like an isolated local variation. Combined, the two show that strain-induced defect contrast is lamella-selective rather than uniform, though which lamella type (A or B) is affected, and any quantification of the contrast, remain undetermined.

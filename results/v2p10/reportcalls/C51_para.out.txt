The chain starts from two spectra taken after zinc deposition: XAS shows the ~398 eV N 1s feature collapsing while the ~401 eV feature becomes dominant, and Raman shows the ~1200 cm-1 feature weakening from the pristine to the zinc-loaded trace. Neither alone says much—XAS only shows a shift among nitrogen states with no vibrational support, and Raman only shows one feature weakening with no link to nitrogen electronic structure—but together they give cross-technique evidence that some bonding change near nitrogen tracks with zinc loading. That combined result still left open whether the change was a specific Zn-N interaction or just a general disturbance spread across the whole carbon lattice, so the next step needed a measurement that could distinguish "localized" from "global." The new C 1s data supplies that: the sharp 290.1 eV feature disappears after zinc deposition while the 284.9 and 288.3 eV features are untouched, showing the carbon-backbone change is selective rather than uniform. Read against the first result, this disfavors the general-lattice-disturbance alternative and favors a localized, site-specific origin for the co-occurring N and Raman changes, though it still doesn't identify what the 290.1 eV feature is, tie the Raman mode to Zn-N specifically, or confirm nitrogen's direct chemical involvement at that site, and both comparisons remain single pristine-vs-loaded snapshots with no dose series.

The chain starts with two structural fingerprints of the same sample: XRD indexes it to hexagonal Bi with one weak Bi2O3 peak, and Raman shows only the two Bi phonon modes with no oxide bands. Neither alone settles whether oxide is truly absent or just under Raman's detection limit, but together they agree Bi is the dominant phase and Bi2O3 is a minor secondary phase — this phase identity was needed before any property could be attributed to "the material" as a whole. Step 2 adds a linear NIR absorbance measurement (R2=0.9999, alpha=6.4 L g-1 cm-1); because the sample's composition was just fixed, this extinction can be attributed to the phase-identified bismuthene rather than to an unidentified mixture. Step 3 adds IR imaging showing heating that scales with concentration and laser power, with water as a cold control; paired with the already-confirmed extinction, this establishes a genuine photothermal effect (absorption converting to heat), not just correlated trends. Step 4 adds tumor-growth curves: needing that confirmed photothermal mechanism as a baseline, it shows that single-laser (808 nm) photothermal treatment alone leaves four of five tumors regrowing, while adding a second laser achieves complete, sustained control in all five.

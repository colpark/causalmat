The chain starts with two diffraction measurements on milled C10: bulk XRD shows the normal C0 reflections (CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17, Mg) plus one weak extra line near 44.5 degrees, while local TEM/SAED shows rings indexing the same host phases plus a separate Co ring. Neither reading alone would settle whether that weak XRD line was a real, distinct Co phase: XRD is bulk-averaged and the line is faint, and SAED is only local. Put together, the two techniques cross-validate each other, so step 1 concludes Co sits as its own separate crystalline phase next to the unchanged C0 phases, with no new reaction product. Step 2 then adds PCT curves at 320-360 C, where C10 plateau pressures (~0.15, 0.27, 0.45 MPa) nearly match C0. This measurement needed the step 1 result because PCT alone is a thermodynamic, not structural, reading -- a plateau match could be coincidental without knowing what phases are actually present. Knowing Co is a segregated, unreacted phase lets the matching plateaus be read as showing that segregated Co does not perturb the host alloy's hydrogen-equilibrium thermodynamics, at least in the milled, pre-cycling state, and only regarding equilibrium pressure rather than kinetics.

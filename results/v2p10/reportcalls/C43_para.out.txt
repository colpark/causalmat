The chain starts with two TEM images: a baseline showing sharp alternating A/B lamellae, and a first-strain-step image where one lamella stays featureless while its neighbour shows mottled contrast and line contrast along the interface. Together these establish that the onset of plasticity is phase-selective and localized to the interface, rather than spread uniformly through the microstructure.

Step 2 adds a dense dislocation band identified as a pile-up along a lamella boundary. It needed the step 1 result to interpret against, because on its own a pile-up image doesn't say anything about asymmetry between phases; read together with the earlier line contrast, it gives that line contrast a concrete identity as dislocation pile-up at the boundary, showing the interface itself is a site of dislocation accumulation rather than an artifact.

Step 3 adds the stress-strain curve, true stress rising from about 410 to 1070 MPa up to 0.06 true strain. It needed the step 2 result because a bulk stress curve alone says nothing about the microstructure; paired with the interface pile-up finding, it supports that the boundary-localized dislocation accumulation and the macroscopic strain hardening are occurring together in the same deforming material, rather than being unrelated measurements.

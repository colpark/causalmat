The chain starts with two separate imaging checks: optical microscopy across panels F1b-F1f shows dark particles spread across the whole field with a few local clusters, and SEM at X150 shows bright particle features evenly spread over the field. Because these are two independent techniques with different contrast mechanisms (optical staining/contrast versus SEM electron contrast/charging), their agreement rules out the dispersed pattern being a quirk of either method, establishing that the near-uniform SiCp dispersion with occasional clusters is a real microstructural feature rather than an artifact. Step 2 needed that confirmed, artifact-free dispersion result before it could mean anything, then added the yield strength data: YS rising roughly 93, 113, 124, 127, 136, 142 MPa across 0-25 wt% SiCp. Combining the two shows that the rising strength trend occurs alongside a microstructure that is broadly well-dispersed rather than grossly segregated, so the mechanical gains are not undermined by large-scale clustering. What the pairing cannot do: correlate uniformity with YS at each wt% level, match the qualitative visual dispersion evidence to the precise MPa measurements field-by-field, identify which strengthening mechanism drives the YS rise, or tie the imaged fields to the specific measured specimens.

The chain starts from two measurements on the raw material: XRD shows hexagonal Bi as the dominant crystalline phase plus one weak Bi2O3 peak, and Raman shows only the Bi phonon modes with no oxide bands. Together these establish that the sample is majority Bi with Bi2O3 present only as a minor trace, since Raman's silence on the oxide is consistent with a small amount below its detection limit rather than contradicting the XRD call. Step 2 needed that phase identity before the linear NIR absorbance measurement (alpha = 6.4 L g-1 cm-1) could be attributed to anything in particular; with composition fixed, the absorbance becomes a property of this same Bi-dominant material. Step 3 then needed that absorbance result as the counterpart to the photothermal fit (tau_s = 133.4 s, eta = 19.4%), because absorbance alone only shows light is captured, not converted; pairing the two shows the material actually turns absorbed light into heat at a defined efficiency. Step 4 needed that established conversion capacity to interpret the day-22 tumour-volume ranking, where the 808 nm-containing arms showed the largest drops: the material's demonstrated photothermal conversion gives a candidate mechanism linking the material-level property to the in vivo outcome.

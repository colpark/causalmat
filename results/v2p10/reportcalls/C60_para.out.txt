The chain begins with two measurements on the same materials: polarization curves showing Mg-Zn-Y-Nd's corrosion potential near -1.5 V versus about -0.25 V for 317L SS, with current branches 2-3 decades higher, and immersion testing showing Mg-Zn-Y-Nd's rate (0.13-0.38 mm/y, peaking at day 7) staying above 317L SS's (<0.07 mm/y) at every time point. Combining them mattered because either alone could reflect an artifact of one test method; together, two independent approaches converge on the same ranking, that Mg-Zn-Y-Nd degrades faster. Step 2 needed that established ranking as its anchor: it adds pH data, where Mg-Zn-Y-Nd drives the solution from 7.4 to ~10 by day 22 and plateaus there, above the blank control (peaking ~8.4, falling to ~7.5), while 317L SS falls to ~6.1 by day 20. Read against the earlier convergence, this pH divergence extends the same ranking into a third, chemically distinct measurement, strengthening the case for a real, method-independent material difference rather than a coincidence tied to one test.

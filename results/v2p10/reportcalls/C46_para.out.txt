The chain starts with two measurements on Na/PCO: synchrotron XRD showing the same reflections persist through discharge/charge with only modest intensity loss, and SAXS showing the scattering contours stay nearly straight with I(q) overlapping except a small drop near q~0.03-0.1 A-1 at 0.11/0.02 V. Because XRD tracks crystalline phase and SAXS tracks pore/morphology, and neither constrains the other, together they establish that PCO stays structurally intact both at the lattice level (no metallic Co) and the pore-network level over the first cycle. Step 2 adds a computed AIMD degree-of-oxidation ranking at 2 ps (Li-Co3O4 ~91%, Na-Co3S4 ~59%, Na-Co3O4 ~36%, plateauing after 0.8 ps). Handed the intact-structure result, this pairing supports attributing Na capacity fading to poor sodiation reactivity rather than volume-change damage: the structural result rules out lattice/pore collapse as the cause, while the oxidation data shows the reaction itself stalls early and incompletely even without such damage. Step 3 adds the real cycling data (~870 mAh/g cycle 1, ~480 cycle 2, ~70 by cycle 40). Given the reactivity-limited attribution, the steep early drop is consistent with an early-onset, intrinsically limited conversion rather than gradually accumulating structural degradation, though this only checks consistency rather than re-deriving the cause.

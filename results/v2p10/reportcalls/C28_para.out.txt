The chain starts from two measurements taken together: FTIR bands (bridging cyanide-type ~2100 cm-1, vanadyl-type ~980 cm-1, framework metal-ligand bands below 650 cm-1, and O-H of water ~3430 cm-1) identify which bonded groups are present, while STEM-EDS maps show Na, V, Fe, C, N, and O all covering the particle uniformly, so the elements needed for those groups are co-located rather than sitting in separate phases -- establishing the particle as one homogeneous compound. Step 2 needed that homogeneity conclusion because, without it, the three CV redox peak pairs (0.55/0.52, 0.89/0.87, 1.08/1.07 V, persisting over 20 cycles) could be explained as signals from a mixture of impurity phases; with homogeneity established, the peaks can instead be attributed to one compound undergoing three distinct reversible redox events. Step 3 then needed that multi-redox conclusion because the CV result alone could not quantify anything and the ~91 mAh/g first-discharge capacity alone could not explain why it occurs, so combining them supports that the measured capacity is delivered by that same single-phase, multi-redox mechanism rather than a separate impurity phase or side reaction.

The chain starts from two measurements taken together. The EDS map shows C and Co signals concentrated on particle surfaces, densest along edges and some faces, telling us where these elements sit at the particle scale but nothing about their atomic-scale form. The HRTEM image of milled C10 then zooms into one such edge and shows crystalline CeMg12 (0.258 nm) and Y5Mg24 (0.265 nm) alloy fringes next to an amorphous band and curved carbon layers (0.386 nm), giving atomic-resolution content but only for one local spot. Combined, they support that the C-rich EDS surface signal corresponds to an actual curved-carbon phase coating the alloy particle at its edges, next to alloy grains -- neither measurement alone establishes this, since EDS lacks atomic detail and HRTEM lacks particle-wide spatial context. Step 2 then adds the kinetic measurement that the diffusion-controlled second absorption stage lasts 66 min for C0 versus 11 min for C10 at 360 C. Needing the structural result to interpret it, this timing is read alongside the carbon-coating finding to suggest the coating's location matches where diffusion would occur, making it spatially consistent with the faster C10 kinetics, though not proven to cause it, since the structural evidence is one local observation and the timing is only two values at one temperature.

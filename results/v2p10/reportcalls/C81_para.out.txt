Step 1 combines XRD, which indexes the as-cast alloy's reflections to two ordered phases, L12 and B2, with TEM at the first strain step, which shows one lamella staying featureless while its neighbor gains mottled contrast and line contrast along the interface; together these establish that the two-phase system responds asymmetrically to strain, one lamella type picking up visible internal contrast and the other not. Step 2 needed that asymmetric-contrast result because the new measurement, the hardening-rate curve falling steeply from about 85 to 14 GPa by strain 0.009, decaying slowly to about 5 GPa by 0.058, then turning negative, has no microstructural referent on its own, and the TEM snapshot has no macroscopic rate signature to attach to. Combining them lets the chain associate the earliest, steepest drop in hardening rate with a stage where slip is confined to one phase while its neighbor stays undeformed, an early single-phase-active mechanism rather than one engaging both phases together, while still leaving open which phase is active, how the later slow-decay and negative-rate regimes relate to microstructure, and whether the single lamella pair represents the bulk behavior.

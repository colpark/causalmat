Bulk XRD on the dehydrogenated C0 and C10 samples finds only Mg, CeH2.73, CeO2 and YH2 reflections (plus a Co marker peak in C10), with no CeMg12/Y5Mg24/Ce2Mg17 lines anywhere detectable, establishing the phase assemblage and ruling out any RE-Mg intermetallic in the bulk. HRTEM of dehydrogenated C10 then needed that phase assignment to interpret its lattice fringes: it shows a 0.245 nm Mg (101) grain, wrapped in carbon layers, sitting directly beside a 0.318 nm CeH2.73 (111) grain, confirming in real space that these are genuine, physically adjacent crystalline grains rather than an XRD artifact, and adding the carbon-wrapping detail diffraction alone cannot give. This combined phase/morphology result was then handed to the PCT plateau measurement at 320-360 C, which shows one flat plateau per temperature with C10 sitting at nearly the same pressures as C0 (~0.15, 0.27, 0.45 MPa). The pressure data alone say nothing about phase identity, so only paired with the prior result can one read the unchanged plateaus as consistent with no new phase forming, supporting that C@Co treatment and cycling leave both the phase assemblage and the desorption thermodynamics essentially unchanged -- while kinetics, reversibility across cycles, and how representative the single HRTEM grain pair is remain unaddressed.</message>

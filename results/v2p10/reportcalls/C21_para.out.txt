The chain starts with two separate measurements that each leave a gap: FTIR finds new bands at 2930, 2864 and 1122 cm-1 only in BST-SH, which tells you a CH2-bearing, Si-O-type species is chemically present but not whether it amounts to a real bulk quantity, while TGA finds a small ~1% weight-loss step at 180-250 C in composites that pure PAEN lacks, which proves extra mass is present but not what it chemically is. Combined, they corroborate each other -- the FTIR species is confirmed as a real, quantifiable component rather than a spectral artifact, and the TGA mass loss is confirmed as belonging to that distinct species rather than PAEN. Step 2 needed that combined chemical confirmation because the new SEM observation -- BST@Ag1% particles covered by matrix with blurred outlines and no interfacial gaps -- only shows a good-looking interface by itself, not that it stems from the verified surface species; together they support that the confirmed surface modification correlates with a gap-free interface. Step 3 then needed that interface finding, because the tensile series (79.3, 78.3, 74.4, 73.5 MPa for PAEN, 1%, 3%, 5% Ag) alone says nothing about interfacial chemistry; combined, the two support that the intimate interface correlates with tensile strength being largely retained (only a slight ~7% decline) rather than sharply degraded as Ag content rises.

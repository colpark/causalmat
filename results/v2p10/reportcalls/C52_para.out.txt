The chain starts with two independent pieces of evidence at Step 1: DFT calculations (measurement A) computing the Delta G for cobalt oxidation steps across four compositions—lowest for CoCrOOH at Co2+->Co3+, lowest for CoFeOOH at Co3+->Co4+, CoFeCrOOH intermediate, Co(OH)2 highest—and EELS spectra (measurement B) showing the Co L3/L2 edges shift to higher energy loss with L2 growing as potential rises from dry/open-circuit to 1.4 and 1.5 V. Because these come from separate methods (calculation versus spectroscopy) yet point the same way, Step 1 concludes that Co oxidation is both thermodynamically favorable and experimentally occurring under anodic potential, a claim neither measurement could support alone. Step 2 needed that convergent result as its starting point because the new measurement—a neutral-electrolyte LSV ranking showing Co5Fe3Cr2 reaching 10 mA/cm2 first at eta10 = 452 mV, ahead of IrO2—says nothing by itself about oxidation-state chemistry; it is purely a kinetic performance number. Pairing it with Step 1's result only licenses a weak, compatibility-level reading: both findings sit in the same general anodic/OER regime, so it's consistent (not proven) that Co oxidation contributes to the catalyst's activity edge. It cannot go further because the DFT/EELS work wasn't shown to apply to Co5Fe3Cr2 specifically, the two sides measure different kinds of quantities (thermodynamic/spectroscopic proxy versus overpotential in a different electrolyte), and no single measurement ties oxidation state to activity in the same material.

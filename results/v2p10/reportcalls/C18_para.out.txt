The chain starts from two 300 K transport panels: Hall mobility that is anomalously high at low Se doping (mu_H ~145-170 cm2/Vs at x=0.005-0.02, still ~125 at x=0.04 above the literature envelope) paired with resistivity that drops sharply with doping (~650 to ~12-23 micro-ohm m), establishing that doping raises mobility and conductivity together rather than trading one for the other. Step 2 needed a power-factor measurement to see whether that same regime helps the Seebeck-conductivity balance; PF does rise strongly (below 2 at x=0 to ~29 at x=0.01), but its ordering (x=0.01 highest, x=0.005 lowest) doesn't track the mobility ordering, so mobility can't be pinned as the driver. Step 3 needed ZT to test whether the PF gain becomes a real efficiency gain; ZT does peak highest at x=0.005 (~0.84, near n-Bi2Te3's ~0.88), but that peak location matches neither the PF peak (x=0.01) nor the mobility plateau, implying an unmeasured factor sets ZT. Step 4 needed the literature bar chart to place that same optimized composition (RT ZT~0.82) against four external references (0.75, 0.70, 0.62, 0.32), confirming it also outperforms the published literature, though without addressing what mechanism causes this.

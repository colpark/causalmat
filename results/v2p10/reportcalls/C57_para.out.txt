The chain starts from two solution/in-vivo heating measurements: IR imaging showing the 6-min spot temperature rising with concentration (25->200 ug/mL) and with power density (0.5->1.5 W) in solution, alongside tumour IR data showing BNs-BSA driving tumour temperature to ~49 C by 5 min versus ~41 C for PBS. Combining these two supports that the photothermal conversion capacity demonstrated in solution is the mechanism behind the tumour heating gap seen in vivo. Step 2 needed that conclusion as its starting point because it asks what happens to tumours once that heating mechanism is engaged, and it adds a new measurement: individual tumour growth curves showing all five BNs-BSA+Lasers tumours staying at zero, while four of five BNs-BSA+808 nm tumours regrow after about day 14. Read against the heating-mechanism conclusion from step 1, this addition shows that the photothermal heating capacity is enough to produce an initial response but not durable eradication on its own -- durable control instead tracks with the added laser/modality (660 nm) rather than with the 808 nm photothermal heating alone.

The chain starts with two structural measurements on the same three samples (MBG, 2Co, 5Co): the small-angle XRD peak height falls roughly eight-fold with rising Co content (~180000, ~74000, ~23000), and N2 uptake at P/P0~1 falls in step (~8.7, ~5.6, ~4.35 mmol/g). Because both independent techniques order the samples the same way, this step concludes that Co doping progressively degrades the mesoporous structure, both its ordering and its porosity, together. Step 2 brings in a cumulative ampicillin release curve (~340 ug at 8h rising to a ~795 ug plateau at 168h) measured only on the 2Co sample; it needed step 1's result to know where 2Co sits structurally -- intermediate degradation, not maximal -- so it can say only that a partially degraded sample still sustains substantial release, without any comparison across Co levels to test whether degradation drives release. Step 3 adds bacterial survival data for scaffolds with and without ampicillin (falling to ~105%, ~81%, ~52% at days 1, 3, 7, versus flat-to-elevated drug-free controls); it needed step 2's release timeline to note that survival declines over roughly the same window release occurs, but since neither release nor structural degradation was compared across time points instant-by-instant, the pairing only shows temporal association, not that the specific release kinetics, rather than ampicillin's presence generically, causes the killing.

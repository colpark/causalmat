The chain starts with two in vitro measurements: absorbance at 808 nm rises linearly with concentration (alpha = 6.4 L g-1 cm-1, R2 = 0.9999), and IR-camera spot temperatures rise with both concentration (200>100>50>25 ug/mL, water staying cold) and laser power (0.5->1.5 W). Together these establish that absorption at 808 nm drives photothermal heating in solution, not solvent heating. Step 2 needed that established absorption-heating link to make sense of the new in vivo measurement -- tumour temperature reaching ~49 C with BNs-BSA versus ~41 C with PBS -- because only having already shown heating is absorption-mediated in a dish lets the tumour reading be read as that same mechanism operating as a real functional effect in tissue, though it leaves open whether the rise reflects intrinsic absorption or simply more material accumulating at the tumour, and whether solution and tissue heating are even comparable. Step 3 then needed that confirmed in vivo heating result to interpret the growth curves: all five dual-laser (660/808 nm) tumours stay at 0, while four of five single-808 nm tumours regrow after day 14. Because heating was confirmed only for the single-808 nm regimen, the combination shows that confirmed heating produces initial control but not durable eradication, and cannot say whether the dual-laser arm's success comes from more heating or a different mechanism tied to the added wavelength.

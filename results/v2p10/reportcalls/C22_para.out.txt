The chain starts with two measurements that only make sense together: FTIR finds new bands (2930, 2864, 1122 cm-1) only in BST-SH, pointing to CH2 aliphatic groups and a Si-O linkage, while TGA finds composites lose about 1% weight at 180-250 C that pure PAEN doesn't lose. FTIR alone can't show this species is a real, quantifiable bulk component, and TGA alone can't say what chemical is being lost, so the step needed both to conclude a real, thermally decomposable interfacial species is present.

Step 2 takes that confirmed species and adds DSC Tg data: pure PAEN sits at 176 C, but BST@Ag composites at 1/3/5% Ag show lower Tg values of 146, 149, 157 C. The Tg numbers alone say nothing about chemistry, and the species result alone says nothing about bulk thermal behavior, so combining them gives a candidate reading: the confirmed interfacial layer, present in these same composites, fits with the Tg being pulled down and then rising with more Ag, though this is only a co-occurrence, not a demonstrated cause.

Step 3 then adds a dielectric frequency sweep, showing the dielectric constant drops only about 10% from 100 Hz to 1 MHz across every sample. Combined with the Tg/species result, this shows the same samples with depressed and shifted Tg still hold stable dielectric behavior across frequency and Ag loading, since neither the Tg/chemistry finding nor the frequency-stability finding says anything about the other on its own.

The chain starts by putting two panels side by side: N2 sorption showing uptake at P/P0~1 falling with cobalt content (~8.7, ~5.6, ~4.35 mmol/g for 0Co, 2Co, 5Co) and a release assay for the 2Co scaffold showing cumulative drug release climbing from ~340 ug at 8h to ~795 ug at 168h and leveling off after ~72h. Step 1 needed both together to propose that Co-driven loss of mesoporosity is linked to this burst-then-plateau release pattern, since neither the pore data nor the release curve alone shows a time-linked drug effect. Step 2 then brings in a survival measurement for Scaffolds+Ampicillin (~105%, ~81%, ~52% at days 1, 3, 7, against a 100% blank and 110-118% for drug-free scaffolds). This step needed the release time course handed to it because it works by lining up the two timelines: release is still rising and approaching its plateau through 72-168h, the same window in which survival drops furthest (day 3 to day 7). That alignment supports treating the antibacterial effect as tracking ongoing release rather than acting instantly, though it is limited to 2Co, compares a cumulative amount to a normalized percentage at only three days, and cannot say whether the release plateau reflects finite drug exhaustion or pore-limited diffusion.

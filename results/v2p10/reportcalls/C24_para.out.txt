The chain starts with two measurements on the same samples: SEM showing bare BST particles as smooth and about 70 nm versus modified particles that are rough, coated, and larger, and a photograph showing powders and films darkening from white to dark brown as Ag content rises. Together these support that the particle-level coating/roughening seen in SEM is the same change that tracks the color darkening as Ag increases. Step 2 needed that link to make sense of new DSC data: Tg is 176 C for pure PAEN but drops to 146, 149, and 157 C for the 1/3/5% Ag composites, rising with loading after the initial drop. Because step 1 had already tied structural coating to Ag content, this new thermal trend could be read as the same Ag-driven change also surfacing in a bulk polymer property, not just in appearance and particle shape. Step 3 then needed that ordered Tg trend to interpret dielectric constant data showing only about a 10% decline from 100 Hz to 1 MHz for every sample. Combining the two supports that the same Ag-tracking change that shifts Tg does not disrupt dielectric frequency stability, which holds uniformly across the whole loading series.

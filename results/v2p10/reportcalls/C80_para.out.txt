The chain begins with two TEM readings taken together: a dense dislocation pile-up band at a lamella boundary, and a panel-ordered sequence running from unresolved mottled contrast to tangled lines to dense walls. Combined, these support that dislocation arrangements grow progressively denser and more organized as the imaged panels advance, matching the paper's account of FCC dislocation content rising through pile-ups, intersections, and networks toward dense walls and Taylor lattices.

Step 2 needed that microstructural progression as a baseline before it could interpret the new measurement: a hardening-rate curve falling sharply from ~85 to ~14 GPa by strain 0.009, then more slowly to ~5 GPa by 0.058, then turning negative. Without the TEM-based staging already established, this three-regime curve would just be a numerical curiosity; against that staging, it can be read as a macroscopic echo of the same idea, multiple stages whose number and character parallel the microstructural regimes. But the step is explicit that this is only a parallel in stage count and qualitative sense: no strain value ties any specific TEM panel to any point on the curve, no quantitative link exists between dislocation density and the GPa values, and since the TEM evidence is FCC-only, the combination cannot explain the final negative-hardening regime, which may involve other mechanisms.

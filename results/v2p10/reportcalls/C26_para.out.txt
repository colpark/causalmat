The chain starts from two pieces of evidence about the chemically modified filler: FTIR shows new bands (2930, 2864 cm-1 for CH2, 1122 cm-1 for Si-O) only in BST-SH, and a photograph shows BST-OH settling in the lower liquid layer while BST-SH disperses in the upper layer. Together these establish that grafting MPTMS onto BST-SH corresponds to a switch from hydrophilic to lipophilic surface behavior, since the chemical signature and the phase-partitioning behavior appear together only in the modified sample.

Step 2 needed that conclusion because the new measurement -- a film micrograph showing BST@Ag1% particles covered by matrix with blurred outlines and no interfacial gaps -- is purely morphological and says nothing about chemistry on its own. Combining it with the established hydrophilic-to-lipophilic switch lets the chain say the surface-chemistry change is consistent with, and plausibly extends to, good filler-matrix compatibility in the composite film.

Step 3 then needed that compatibility conclusion because the tensile strength series (79.3, 78.3, 74.4, 73.5 MPa for PAEN, 1%, 3%, 5% Ag) is just a numerical trend with no mechanistic account on its own. Read against the inferred gap-free interface, the modest drop in strength as loading rises is treated as the kind of outcome expected from good bonding, even though neither piece alone shows this mechanical consequence or explains why the drop is modest.

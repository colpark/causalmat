Step 1 begins with an EDS spectrum showing Ti, O, a dominant C peak, and small Er (~1.4 keV) and Ce (~4.8 keV) peaks — this only shows that these elements are present, since an EDS peak alone can't confirm what chemical form an element is in. XPS is needed to supply that missing chemical identity: Ti 2p3/2 458.5 and 2p1/2 464.2 eV, O1s components at 529.0, 531.2, 533.0 eV, and an Er 4d peak at 168.8 eV together show the Er is present specifically as Er3+, associated with a Ti-O oxide framework, rather than being an element merely inferred from an unconfirmed EDS peak. That combined chemical result — Er3+ confirmed within a TiO2-based particle system — is what step 2 needed, because the new measurement, optical absorption edges extending past 420 nm for Co-doped samples versus a sharp ~410 nm edge for undoped TiO2 (largest shift in Ce0.2), says nothing on its own about chemical identity, only about an optical shift. Combined, the two measurements show only co-occurrence: the same chemically-confirmed doped particles also show the extended absorption edge. This does not establish that Er3+ specifically (rather than Ce, whose oxidation state was undetermined) causes the shift, does not resolve lattice incorporation versus surface association, gives no mechanism connecting the confirmed species to the optical change, and supports no dose-response link between dopant amount and edge shift.</message>

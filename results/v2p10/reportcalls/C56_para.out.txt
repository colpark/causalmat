The chain begins with two host materials compared on galvanostatic plating: at the lowest current density their plating plateaus nearly coincide, but at the highest rate they separate sharply, with CnC HS around -0.05 V versus C HS around -0.10 V. That alone only shows the hosts differ in overpotential, so the chain adds Coulombic efficiency data, which needed the overpotential result to become a claim about durability: CnC HS holds roughly 93-100% CE out to about 205 cycles, while C HS collapses to zero CE by 40-48 cycles and its data end near 50 cycles. Combining the two shows the host with the lower plating overpotential at high rate is the same host that survives far more cycles, a qualitative but not quantitative link. Step 2 then hands in a full-cell capacity trace, needed because the prior step only established a host-level link and left open whether that advantage carries into an actual working cell; the new measurement shows charge/discharge capacity falling from about 1.0 to about 0.9 mAh over 500 cycles with the 1st and 100th cycle profiles nearly overlapping, extending the claim to device-level stability, though only for CnC HS, without a paired C HS full cell to attribute the stability specifically to the plating/CE advantage.

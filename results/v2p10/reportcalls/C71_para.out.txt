The chain starts with two measurements at different scales on the same milled C10 particles. EDS mapping shows where C and Co signals sit -- concentrated on surfaces, densest along edges and some faces -- but this location data alone cannot say what physical phase is actually there. HRTEM supplies that missing structural identity at one such edge region: crystalline alloy fringes (CeMg12 0.258 nm, Y5Mg24 0.265 nm) sitting beside an amorphous band and curved carbon layers (0.386 nm). Combining the two was necessary because EDS gives no atomic-scale identity and HRTEM alone gives no sense of where its local view fits in the whole-particle picture; together they corroborate that the C-rich surface signal is an actual carbon phase coating the particle edges next to alloy grains. Step 2 then needed that corroborated carbon-phase finding to interpret a new, otherwise mute number: JMAK desorption plots are linear (R2>0.98) with eta falling from 1.59 in C0 to 1.32 in C10. The kinetic exponent alone says nothing about what differs physically between samples, and the microstructural result alone says nothing about kinetics; paired, they support an association between the documented carbon layer and a lower Avrami exponent, though only as a qualitative, two-sample link, not a proven cause.

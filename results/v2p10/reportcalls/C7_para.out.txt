The chain starts from two measurements taken together: TEM shows new dark nanoparticles appearing on the sheets only after reduction, and XRD shows the reduced material's pattern contains both hexagonal Bi reflections and a Bi2O3 (321) peak. Combined, these establish that reduction produces a two-phase composite, not a single phase. Step 2 needed that composite conclusion before it could interpret the new linear NIR absorbance measurement (808 nm, R2=0.9999, alpha=6.4 L g-1 cm-1): together they support that this bulk, concentration-proportional absorbance belongs to the same two-phase material already characterized by TEM/XRD, though which phase drives absorption stays open. Step 3 needed that absorbance-as-bulk-property conclusion to make sense of the new photothermal heating data (tau_s=133.4 s, eta=19.4%): together they support that the same bulk two-phase material both absorbs 808 nm light and converts it to heat with that efficiency, without showing which phase converts light to heat or that absorption predicts conversion efficiency. Step 4 needed that established photothermal capacity to interpret the new in vivo tumor-volume rankings, where 808 nm-containing arms shrank tumors and the 660/808 combination eradicated them: together these support that the measured photothermal property is consistent with, and offers a plausible basis for, the 808 nm-linked reductions, without quantitatively linking the two, resolving phase attribution, or decomposing the combined-wavelength result.

The chain starts with two first-cycle measurements on Na/PCO: synchrotron XRD showing the same reflections persist through discharge/charge with only modest intensity loss, and SAXS showing nearly unchanged scattering contours (just a small drop near q~0.03-0.1 A-1 at 0.11/0.02 V). These were combined because XRD only tells you about the crystalline phase (no metallic Co forming) while SAXS only tells you about pore-scale morphology; together they support that PCO stays structurally intact at both the lattice and pore-network level over cycle 1.

That structural-intactness conclusion was then needed as a baseline for step 2's new measurement, a computed degree-of-oxidation ranking (Li-Co3O4 ~91% > Na-Co3S4 ~59% > Na-Co3O4 ~36%, plateauing after 0.8 ps). Alone, the oxidation numbers say nothing about whether the electrode held up physically; alone, the structural result says nothing about how far sodiation proceeded. Paired, they let the chain attribute Na capacity fading to poor sodiation reactivity rather than volume-change-driven structural failure, since the structure ruled out collapse while the oxidation data showed the reaction itself stalling early.

That reactivity-limited attribution was then needed to make sense of step 3's new measurement, the actual cycling capacities (~865 mAh/g 1st discharge, ~470 1st charge, ~60 by cycle 40). The mechanistic claim alone never touched real capacity numbers, and the capacity trace alone had no mechanistic reading; together they support reading the discharge-charge gap and continued fade as consistent with limited/incomplete conversion rather than structural collapse.

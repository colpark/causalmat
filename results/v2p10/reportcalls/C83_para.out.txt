The chain starts from two TEM observations at the same step: sharp alternating A/B lamellae at baseline, and at the first strain step one lamella staying featureless while its neighbour shows mottled contrast and line contrast along the interface. Together these two measurements were needed to conclude that strain is not partitioned uniformly across the microstructure but is instead phase-selective and localized at the interface, since neither observation alone shows partitioning — the baseline gives only the architecture, and the strain-step image alone would not show what changed relative to it. That conclusion was then handed to step 2, which added a further strain-step measurement: a dense dislocation pile-up along a lamella boundary. Because the phase-selective, interface-localized pattern was already established, this new pile-up could be read as a continuation of the same pattern rather than a new deformation mode, so the pair supports that the asymmetric partitioning persists into a later strain state, with the boundary remaining the dislocation accumulation site rather than deformation spreading uniformly. What remains unresolved: which lamella maps to which named phase, whether the asymmetry reflects interface obstruction or one phase's lower slip resistance, and whether the pattern continues, intensifies, or reverses, since only three strain states total are compared.

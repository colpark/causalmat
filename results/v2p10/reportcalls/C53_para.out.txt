The chain starts with two measurements taken together: EIS shows Rct climbing from ~3 to ~26 ohm by cycle 150 then leveling at ~24-28 ohm (with large error bars), while ICP-OES shows dissolved V rising to ~3.7 mg/L by cycle 150 then flattening; because Fe reaches only ~1.1 mg/L and Rfilm/Re stay flat, the shared rise-then-plateau timing of Rct and V - not either series alone - is what lets the paper associate V-site dissolution with the buildup of charge-transfer resistance. Step 2 needed that established association before it could use its new measurement, a capacity-fade curve (steep drop over roughly cycles 20-50, then slow decline to ~55 mAh/g by cycle 250, with CE near 100%): on its own the capacity curve shows only fade, not a cause. Placed against the Rct/V timing, the steep early fade falls in the same window where Rct and V are still rising, and the slow later fade begins as Rct/V approach their cycle-150 plateau, so the combination supports treating V-dissolution-linked Rct buildup as a plausible contributor to early fade, with high CE arguing against side-reaction loss. It leaves open the approximate (not point-for-point) overlap, the late fade continuing past cycle 150, and any quantitative dose-response link.

The chain starts from two measurements on the same samples: SEM images showing bare BST particles as smooth and about 70 nm, versus modified particles that are rough, coated, and larger; and a photograph showing powders and films darkening from white to dark brown as Ag content increases. Put together, these support that the particle-level change SEM shows (bare versus coated/roughened/larger) is the same change that tracks the color shift seen in the photograph as Ag loading rises -- the two visual records move together with increasing Ag.

Step 2 needed that established pairing before it could say anything about a new number: breaking elongation values of 6.9, 5.8, 4.5, 4.4% for PAEN, 1%, 3%, 5% Ag. On its own, this is just a declining series with Ag content. Read against the SEM/photograph result, it lets the elongation drop be treated as accompanying the same coating/roughening event already tied to rising Ag, rather than as a separate, unrelated trend. But this only shows the two things moving together with Ag content -- it does not show which of Ag content, particle size, or coating thickness drives the elongation loss, nor does it establish a mechanistic path from coating to elongation, since SEM only compares two discrete states and just four composition points support ordering, not proportionality.

The chain starts with two measurements at different scales on the same milled C10 material. EDS mapping shows where C and Co signals sit on the particle: concentrated on surfaces, densest along edges and some faces. HRTEM then zooms into one such edge and shows what is actually there at atomic resolution: crystalline alloy fringes (CeMg12 0.258 nm, Y5Mg24 0.265 nm) next to an amorphous band and curved carbon layers (0.386 nm). The two needed each other because location alone (EDS) doesn't say what phase is present, and atomic identification alone (HRTEM) doesn't say it's representative of the surface pattern; together they support that the C-rich surface signal is a real carbon layer coating the alloy particle edge.

Step 2 hands this structural picture to a new, unrelated measurement: hydrogenation kinetics, where C0's first cycle reaches only ~3.2 wt% in 180 min before cycles 2-3 saturate, while C10 saturates within ~5 min on cycle one, matching later cycles. Step 2 needed the coating result because the kinetic contrast between C0 and C10 has no structural handle by itself; pairing it with Step 1 lets the carbon coating be proposed as a candidate reason C10 needs no activation period. This stays a proposal, not a demonstration, because the coating evidence is local and unshown as bulk-representative, no Co fringe was found near the carbon to support a catalytic link, the two measurements are qualitative-structural versus quantitative-kinetic with no dose-response tie, and C0/C10 differ by milling construction so other confounded changes (particle size, defect density, phase distribution) aren't excluded as the real driver.

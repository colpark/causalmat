Step 1 started from two measurements handed in together: the PL band intensity at 390-420 nm (a recombination signature) across TiO2, Er0.5Ti-O, and Er0.5Ce0.2, and the dye degradation percentages at 20/40/60 min across TiO2, Ce0.1, Ce0.3, and Er0.5Ce0.2. It needed both at once because PL alone only ranks recombination and degradation alone only ranks activity; only lining them up on the two shared samples (TiO2, Er0.5Ce0.2) could show the inverse pattern the paper's claim requires -- TiO2 highest PL/lowest degradation (~20% at 60 min), Er0.5Ce0.2 lowest PL/highest degradation (~46% at 60 min).

Step 2 took that two-sample recombination-activity pattern and added a new antibacterial killing-ratio measurement spanning five conditions, including Ce0.1 and Ce0.3, which had never been measured by PL. It needed Step 1's result as the baseline rank to test against. The new data showed the same rank order (Er0.5Ce0.2 best, TiO2 worst) recurring in this third, independent assay, and extending consistency to the two intermediate samples -- but only as an unconfirmed extrapolation, since Ce0.1/Ce0.3's ranking is tied to no measured PL value, the antibacterial mechanism may differ from photocatalytic degradation, Er0.5Ti-O stays outside the pattern, and both remain endpoint comparisons, not mechanistic or dose-response links.</message>

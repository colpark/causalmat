The chain starts from two paired measurements on the same two substrates. SEM shows CnC HS carrying a flat, compact zinc deposit with only sparse protruding platelets, while C HS is covered in a dense mat of sharp platelets. Coulombic-efficiency cycling shows CnC HS holding roughly 93-100% CE out to about 205 cycles, while C HS collapses to zero by around 40-48 cycles. Together these support that the rougher, sharp-platelet morphology on C HS co-occurs with much earlier failure, and the compact CnC HS morphology co-occurs with longer stable cycling. Step 2 needed that paired result as its basis, because the new measurement only reports one arm: capacity falling from about 1.0 to about 0.9 mAh over 500 cycles, with the 1st and 100th cycle profiles nearly overlapping. Read against the established CnC HS/C HS contrast, this longer, low-loss record is taken as extending the same kind of sustained behavior already tied to CnC HS's compact morphology past the ~205-cycle window and out to 500 cycles. But the new step still leaves open a direct extension of the paired contrast, since it has no C HS comparator, uses a different kind of metric (cumulative capacity in mAh rather than per-cycle CE), does not confirm an identical test setup, and does not establish any dose-response between morphology roughness and capacity loss.

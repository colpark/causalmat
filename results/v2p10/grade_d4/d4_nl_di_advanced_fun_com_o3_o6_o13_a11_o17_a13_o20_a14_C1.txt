THE ANSWER
This measurement settles that the same bulk, two-phase (metallic Bi + Bi2O3) reduced material whose 808 nm absorbance was already shown to be a bulk, mass-proportional property (alpha = 6.4 L g-1 cm-1, R2 = 0.9999) also exhibits quantifiable photothermal heating under 808 nm irradiation: a linear -ln(theta) vs time fit gives tau_s = 133.4 s and a photothermal conversion efficiency eta = 19.4%. Together, these support the conclusion that this as-prepared bulk composite both absorbs NIR light as a bulk property and converts that absorbed light into heat with this specific measured efficiency — i.e., PRF/service_capability is supported at the level of the whole material behaving as a functional photothermal absorber/converter, not just an optical absorber.

What it leaves open, plainly: it does not identify which component — metallic Bi, Bi2O3, or the interface between them — actually generates the heat, since tau_s/eta come from a single bulk temperature-vs-time curve with no phase- or particle-resolved readout, so the phase-attribution question from the earlier absorbance result remains unresolved. It also does not establish that whatever drives the NIR absorption is mechanistically the same thing driving the heat conversion; the connection between alpha and eta is made only at the bulk-material level, not through any per-phase causal chain, and these are qualitatively different quantities (a concentration-normalized optical constant vs. an efficiency/time-constant from one heating curve). Finally, because eta/tau_s derive from a single measurement (F3h), the result supports only that this particular bulk material has this specific efficiency — it says nothing about how eta might vary with phase composition, concentration, or laser power density.

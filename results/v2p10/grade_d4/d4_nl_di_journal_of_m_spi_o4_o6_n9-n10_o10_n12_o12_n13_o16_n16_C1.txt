This measurement — that power factor peaks at x=0.01 (~29 uW cm-1 K-2 at 300 K, ~22 at 623 K), exceeding both the lower-x=0.005 doped value and x=0 (at most ~5) — supports the same conclusion already reached from resistivity alone: the transport optimum at x=0.01 is not explained by the monotonic rise in Hall carrier concentration n_H across x=0.005-0.04. Since PF also peaks at x=0.01, coinciding with the resistivity minimum there, it reinforces that some composition-dependent factor other than simple carrier-density increase (from the Se-donor/E_F mechanism) must govern the optimum.

However, this is weaker corroboration than it appears: PF is mathematically built from 1/rho and Seebeck coefficient S, so a PF peak at x=0.01 could arise entirely from the already-established resistivity dip, with S contributing nothing independently. The two measurements therefore are not strictly independent confirmations of the same phenomenon — this is largely the same resistivity signal restated through PF, not new evidence about a distinct mechanism.

What remains open: the identity of the non-n_H factor (mobility, scattering, or Seebeck variation with x) is not identified, only implied to exist. Resistivity was measured at 300 K only, while PF adds a 623 K point, so temperature-stability of the x=0.01 optimum and its cause is not established — only the 300 K coincidence is confirmed. The underlying DFT+Hall mechanistic picture is still a two-state, 0 K snapshot that doesn't compute n_H from bands directly, so this addition doesn't resolve that gap. Finally, other contributors (temperature-dependent scattering, defect states) to either the resistivity minimum or PF maximum are not excluded.

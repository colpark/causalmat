THE ANSWER
Panel F5f (n=5 per group, day-22 tumor volumes tracked every 2 days) supports the following: within this single mouse-tumor experiment, the combined photothermal+photodynamic treatment (BNs-BSA + 660/808 nm) essentially abolished tumor growth (volume ~0 throughout, marked n.s./*** on the plot), the 808 nm-only combination (BNs-BSA + 808 nm) strongly suppressed growth (~370 mm3, large error bars, marked with stars/#), the 660 nm-only combination gave intermediate suppression (~1470 mm3, marked with stars), Lasers-alone gave modest suppression relative to controls (~1750 mm3, marked with a single star), and BNs-BSA-alone tracked closely with PBS (both ~2100-2350 mm3, marked n.s., i.e., not significantly different from control). This justifies the qualitative ranking given and supports the conclusion that the photosensitizer/photothermal agent (BNs-BSA) has no intrinsic antitumor effect without light, that light alone (Lasers) has a small effect, and that irradiation (particularly the 808 nm component, and especially the dual-wavelength combination) is what drives tumor suppression, with the dual-wavelength regimen being most effective.

What it leaves open: this is one in vivo dataset (n=5/group) from one paper, so it does not establish mechanism (photothermal vs photodynamic contribution vs immune involvement), dose-response with laser power/wavelength, generalizability across tumor models or animal species, long-term recurrence/survival beyond day 22, toxicity/biodistribution of BNs-BSA, or whether the same ranking would hold with different treatment schedules. The plot also does not show statistical comparisons between all pairs of groups (only some are annotated with significance markers), so relative ordering among the intermediate groups (Lasers vs BNs-BSA+660nm) is visually suggested but not fully statistically confirmed from this panel alone.

THE ANSWER
This measurement settles that, at 300 K, the low-Se-doping regime already shown to raise mu_H, lower rho, and lift PF also delivers a real ZT gain rather than a tradeoff: ZT reaches its series maximum (~0.84) at x=0.005, comes close to the n-Bi2Te3 benchmark (~0.88), and clearly exceeds the higher-doping levels x=0.02-0.04 (ZT 0.63-0.69). So the justified conclusion is a co-occurrence/ordering claim: doping-driven transport improvement and doping-driven ZT improvement move together in this material system, at least at 300 K and at least up through x=0.01, and low-level Se doping is directionally favorable for thermoelectric performance rather than harmful.

What it does not establish is causation or mechanism. The ZT peak sits at x=0.005, not at x=0.01 where PF is actually highest (~29 vs ZT~0.78 there), and not distinctly at the point where mobility is highest, since mu_H is reported as a flat plateau (~145-170 cm2/Vs) spanning x=0.005-0.02 without singling out 0.005. That mismatch means the data cannot say that PF gain or mobility increase is what sets the ZT ordering; some unmeasured factor -- most plausibly thermal conductivity, which appears in neither panel -- must be shaping where the ZT maximum falls. The comparison is also limited to three overlapping doping points (0, 0.005, 0.01) between the two panels, so it supports only a 300 K ordering/co-occurrence, not a continuous dose-response curve, and it does not extend to the 623 K PF value from the earlier result. Finally, the earlier carrier-density confound (only x=0.04 checked against n_H) remains unresolved, so whether mobility, carrier density, or another variable drives the shared conductivity term behind both PF and ZT is still open.

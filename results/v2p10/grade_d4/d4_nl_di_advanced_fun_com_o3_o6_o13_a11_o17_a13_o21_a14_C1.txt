This measurement settles that the bulk two-phase (metallic Bi + Bi2O3) reduced material, already shown to absorb 808 nm NIR light as a bulk, mass-proportional property (alpha = 6.4 L g-1 cm-1, R2 = 0.9999), also converts that absorbed 808 nm light into heat with a quantifiable, well-fit photothermal response: a linear -ln(theta) vs time relationship yielding tau_s = 133.4 s and a photothermal conversion efficiency eta = 19.4%. What this leaves open: it does not identify which phase (metallic Bi, Bi2O3, or the interface between them) is actually responsible for generating the heat, since tau_s/eta come from a single bulk temperature-vs-time curve with no phase- or particle-resolved readout — the same ambiguity left by the absorbance measurement. Further, alpha and eta/tau_s are conceptually different quantities (a concentration-normalized optical constant versus an efficiency/time-constant from one heating curve), so the strong linear absorbance relationship does not itself predict or guarantee this efficiency value.

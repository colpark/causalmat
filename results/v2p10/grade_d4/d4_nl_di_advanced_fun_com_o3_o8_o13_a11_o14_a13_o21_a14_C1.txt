This measurement settles a "service capability" claim for the material at the level of demonstrated photothermal function, not mechanism or efficiency. What it supports: combining the established linear 808 nm absorption result (alpha=6.4 L g-1 cm-1, R2=0.9999) with the IR-imaging heating data justifies the conclusion that the reduced material actually performs the service of converting absorbed 808 nm light into measurable heat under irradiation — it is not merely an absorber on paper but a working photothermal agent.

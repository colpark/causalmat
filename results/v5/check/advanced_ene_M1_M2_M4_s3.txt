THE KEY UNDER AUDIT

Proposition: The more favorable (exothermic, -0.072 to -0.089 eV) zinc adsorption on N-doped graphene, versus unfavorable (endothermic, +0.014 to +0.030 eV) adsorption on pristine graphene, is consistent with the morphological and cycling contrasts observed: CnC HS deposits zinc as a largely flat layer with only sparse protruding platelets (obs1), sustains 93-100% Coulombic efficiency to about 205 cycles (obs2), stays within ~0.03 V to 7100 min in symmetric cycling (obs3) and within ~0.05 V to 3500 min under a stepped current-density staircase (obs4); C HS instead grows a dense mat of sharp platelets (obs1), fails (drops to zero Coulombic efficiency) by ~40-48 cycles (obs2), develops large voltage spikes and ends near 4400 min in symmetric cycling (obs3), and spikes from ~2300 min under the staircase test (obs4). The energetics-morphology link offers a plausible thermodynamic basis for the uniform-versus-dendritic deposition, which in turn tracks with markedly longer service life on CnC HS.

Limits:

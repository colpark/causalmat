key — what the evidence permits
The height-loss-vs-time trace (obs1) is consistent with and reinforces the prior wear-rate finding: FSP composite accumulates less height loss (~240 um) than stir-cast (~350 um) by ~1160 s, matching the direction of the 280 vs 420 x1e-5 mm3/m wear-rate result and the smaller final height loss already established. In addition, the stir-cast curve's jagged, sudden-drop shape (vs FSP's presumably smoother trace) indicates that stir-cast material loses height in an intermittent, uneven manner rather than steadily.
limits
  • Height loss vs time is a single-condition time series, not a repeated or varied-condition dataset, so it cannot establish a general relationship, only confirm an ordering at the tested condition.
  • Height loss is a cumulative amount (um over time), not a rate measurement in itself; its consistency with the wear-rate number is corroborative, not an independent derivation of rate.
  • The jaggedness/sudden-drop pattern is a qualitative shape observation from the curve; no wear-surface or debris analysis is cited at this step to explain what physically produces the sudden drops.
  • Only one time endpoint (~1160 s) is used for the numeric comparison, which is too sparse to characterize the shape of accumulation (e.g., linear vs accelerating) beyond the endpoint difference.

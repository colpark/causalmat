key — what the evidence permits
Combined with the prior step's finding that Zn-N bonds form at pyridinic-N sites, the DFT results at this step are consistent with, but do not specifically isolate, a thermodynamic driving force for that bond formation: charge transfer to zinc is more negative at N-doped sites (-0.032 to -0.037 e) than at pristine sites (-0.023 to -0.027 e, obs5); the transferred electron density sits in the zinc-substrate gap on N-doped graphene rather than as a detached lobe above pristine graphene (obs6); and computed adsorption energies are negative at top/hollow/bridge sites on N-doped graphene (-0.072/-0.089/-0.077 eV) versus positive on pristine graphene (+0.030/+0.014/+0.029 eV, obs7). Together these show nitrogen doping generically converts zinc adsorption from unfavorable to favorable at all three site geometries, giving an energetic driving force generally consistent with -- though not proof specific to -- the experimentally observed Zn-N bonding and pyridinic-to-graphitic nitrogen shift.

key — what the evidence permits
The more favorable (exothermic, -0.072 to -0.089 eV) zinc adsorption on N-doped graphene, versus unfavorable (endothermic, +0.014 to +0.030 eV) adsorption on pristine graphene, is consistent with the morphological and cycling contrasts observed: CnC HS deposits zinc as a largely flat layer with only sparse protruding platelets (obs1), sustains 93-100% Coulombic efficiency to about 205 cycles (obs2), stays within ~0.03 V to 7100 min in symmetric cycling (obs3) and within ~0.05 V to 3500 min under a stepped current-density staircase (obs4); C HS instead grows a dense mat of sharp platelets (obs1), fails (drops to zero Coulombic efficiency) by ~40-48 cycles (obs2), develops large voltage spikes and ends near 4400 min in symmetric cycling (obs3), and spikes from ~2300 min under the staircase test (obs4). The energetics-morphology link offers a plausible thermodynamic basis for the uniform-versus-dendritic deposition, which in turn tracks with markedly longer service life on CnC HS.
limits
  • The adsorption-energy calculation (step 2) is a single-atom/idealized DFT comparison on graphene surfaces, while the SEM and cycling data are macroscopic electrode-level observations on composite hollow-sphere (HS) architectures (CnC HS vs C HS); the two samples differ in more than doping alone (e.g., overall hollow-sphere construction, any structural or compositional differences bundled with N-doping), so the adsorption-energy sign cannot by itself be isolated as the sole driver of the morphology or cycling outcomes.
  • obs1-obs4 are drawn from only two samples, so what looks like a mechanism-consistent trend is an ordering between two cases, not a dose-response relationship over a range of adsorption energies.
  • Cycling failure (obs2-obs4) could involve additional processes not captured by adsorption thermodynamics alone, such as dendrite-induced shorting, side reactions, or corrosion, which are not separated out here.
  • obs2, obs3, and obs4 are three different measurement protocols (cycling efficiency, symmetric-cell voltage trace, stepped-current voltage trace) that are not on a common scale and are being combined only as converging qualitative evidence of stability, not as a single quantitative measure.

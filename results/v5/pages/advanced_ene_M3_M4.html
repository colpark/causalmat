key — what the evidence permits
Building on step 1's finding that CnC HS shows a lower zinc nucleation overpotential (39 vs 43 mV) and lower plating overpotential at every current density, the SEM and cycling evidence at this step shows CnC HS also forms a flatter, less sharply platelet-covered zinc deposit (obs1), retains ~93-100% Coulombic efficiency to about 205 cycles versus C HS collapsing to zero by ~40-48 cycles and ending near 50 cycles (obs2), survives symmetric cycling to ~7100 min within ~0.03 V versus C HS failing near 4400 min with spikes from ~2600 min (obs3), and tolerates a stepped current-density staircase to 3500 min within ~0.05 V versus C HS developing large spikes from ~2300 min (obs4). Together these permit the conclusion that the lower-overpotential CnC HS material correlates with a smoother deposit morphology and with much longer, more stable cycling and Coulombic-efficiency retention than C HS.
limits
  • The overpotential values from step 1 were measured at an early/initial stage of plating, not continuously through the ~200+ cycles or ~7000+ minutes of cycling reported here, so the link between overpotential and long-run stability is inferred across two separate measurements rather than tracked together over time
  • The SEM morphology in obs1 is a single-timepoint snapshot; it is not tied to a specific cycle number or time relative to when CE drops or voltage spikes appear in obs2-obs4, so morphology cannot be shown to change in step with the electrochemical degradation
  • CnC HS and C HS differ in host material by construction, so any number of other host properties (surface structure, composition, conductivity) could independently drive both the lower overpotential and the better morphology/cycling behavior, making this a correlation between co-varying host differences rather than an isolated causal test of overpotential
not identifiableWhether the lower nucleation/plating overpotential itself is the cause of the flatter deposit morphology and longer cycling life, or whether both the lower overpotential and the better morphology/cycling life are separately produced by some other intrinsic difference between the CnC HS and C HS hosts, cannot be separated with this data

handoff: CnC HS's lower nucleation and plating overpotential (from step 1) co-occurs with a flatter zinc deposit morphology and markedly longer, more stable cycling (higher and longer-retained Coulombic efficiency, longer symmetric-cycling and staircase-cycling lifetimes) than C HS, supporting CnC HS as the better-performing material in service, though the mechanistic link from overpotential to morphology to cycling life is inferred, not directly measured together.

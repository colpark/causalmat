key — what the evidence permits
The photocarrier separation ranking from step 1 (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS > Pd-ZIS > Ag-ZIS > ZIS) matches the H2 evolution rate ranking for the same four samples (125.4 > 34.5 > 3.2 > 1.5 umol/h), so the sample with the most efficient photocarrier separation is also the sample with the highest H2 evolution rate. This supports an association between photocarrier separation efficiency and H2 evolution activity across this series, with Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS's rate (125.4 umol/h, 83.6x ZIS, 3.6x Pd-ZIS) consistent with its top separation ranking.
limits
  • This is a rank-order correspondence over only the four matched compositions (ZIS, Ag-ZIS, Pd-ZIS, Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS); it does not establish a quantitative or mechanistic relationship between degree of separation and rate magnitude.
  • The two additional H2-evolution compositions (Ag0.5Pd0.5-ZIS 57.3, Ag0.75Pd0.25-ZIS 43.5 umol/h) have no paired photocarrier-separation measurement, so the trend cannot be confirmed to extend continuously across the full Ag:Pd composition range.
  • Other unmeasured factors that could co-vary with composition (light absorption/bandgap, surface active-site density, intrinsic proton-reduction activity of the Ag-Pd alloy as a cocatalyst) are not controlled for, so the correspondence could partly reflect these rather than charge separation alone.
  • The comparison links a qualitative/relative ranking (step 1) to an absolute rate measurement (step 2, umol/h); it is an ordering match, not a calibrated dose-response relationship.
not identifiableWhether the higher H2 evolution rate is driven by more efficient photocarrier separation itself, or by the Ag-Pd alloy's intrinsic cocatalytic activity for proton reduction (which also varies with composition), cannot be separated using only the separation ranking and the H2 rate values.

key — what the evidence permits
Read together, the sustained NO delivery from PPAmF established in the prior step (release lasting beyond 8 h rather than the ~1 h burst of PPAm) is consistent with, and offers a mechanistic candidate for, the markedly lower platelet adhesion/activation and thrombus formation seen here on the NO-loaded surfaces (NO-PPAmF, BVLD/NO-PPAmF) versus the non-NO surfaces (316L SS, PPAmF): fewer/rounder platelets (obs1), lower adhered-platelet counts and activation (~11%/18% for NO-PPAmF and ~5%/~1% for BVLD/NO-PPAmF vs ~32-41 and ~65-74% for 316L SS/PPAmF; obs2, obs3), less fibrin/thrombus and cleaner lumens (obs4, obs5), lower occlusion (obs6), lower thrombus weight (obs7), and higher retained flow (obs8). A NO source that keeps releasing rather than exhausting itself quickly is a plausible reason the antithrombotic benefit persists across these endpoint measurements rather than being confined to a brief early window.
limits
  • The evidence given does not state the duration of the platelet-adhesion, thrombosis, or flow assays, so it is not confirmed that these tests were run within the >8 h sustained-release window rather than at a single early timepoint that a mere burst (as in PPAm) could also have covered.
  • obs2-obs8 are single-timepoint endpoint amounts (cell counts/mm2, % activation, % occlusion, mg thrombus weight, % flow retained), not a time course of NO release or of antithrombotic effect, so they cannot themselves verify that release continued throughout the test.
  • BVLD/NO-PPAmF carries both the NO-releasing component and BVLD together, so its superior suppression relative to NO-PPAmF alone (e.g., adhered platelets ~5 vs ~11 x1e2 cells/mm2, occlusion ~3% vs ~7.5%) cannot be attributed to sustained NO release specifically; BVLD's own antiplatelet/anticoagulant action is confounded with NO release in that combined surface.

handoff: The pairing supports that sustained rather than burst NO delivery is a plausible contributor to the durable reduction in platelet adhesion, activation, and thrombosis observed on NO-containing surfaces, with BVLD/NO-PPAmF showing the strongest suppression overall.

— carrying: Assay duration relative to the >8 h NO release window is not given, so duration-matching between the release kinetics and these endpoint measures is assumed rather than demonstrated.

— carrying: BVLD and NO release are combined by construction in BVLD/NO-PPAmF, so their separate contributions to the observed suppression cannot be distinguished from this data.

Building on step 1 (PPAmF's matrix prolongs NO release beyond 8 h versus ~1 h for PPAm), the SEM, photographic, and quantitative data here (obs1-obs8) show that the NO-releasing surfaces (NO-PPAmF and BVLD/NO-PPAmF) have markedly fewer adhered/activated platelets, sparser fibrin, and less occlusive thrombus than the non-NO-releasing surfaces (316L SS, PPAmF), and that BVLD/NO-PPAmF -- combining the anticoagulant BVLD with sustained NO release -- comes closest to preventing occlusive thrombus altogether (~3% occlusion, ~2 mg thrombus, ~90% flow retained).

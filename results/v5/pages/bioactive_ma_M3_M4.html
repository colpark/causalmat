key — what the evidence permits
The composition Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O that gave the largest photocatalytic dye-degradation gain in the previous step (~46% dye removal vs ~20% for TiO2 in 60 min) is separately observed here to also have the highest antibacterial kill ratio (~91% S. aureus, ~93% E. coli vs ~45% for TiO2, obs1) and the fewest surviving colonies on light-exposed plates (obs2). The two performance metrics are not the same quantity and cannot be numerically compared, but the co-occurrence of top rank in both assays for the same composition, and the same qualitative ordering (Er0.5Ce0.2 > Ce0.3 > Ce0.1 > TiO2 > control), is consistent with a shared underlying mechanism (e.g. light-driven reactive-oxygen-species generation) rather than a proven quantitative correlation between dye-degradation percentage and antibacterial kill ratio.
limits
  • Dye degradation and antibacterial killing are two different assays measuring different endpoints (dye concentration loss vs bacterial colony survival/kill ratio), and the antibacterial assay's exposure duration is not given in these observations (only the dye assay's 60 min is specified), so a shared ranking across compositions is a correlation between two separate performance measures, not evidence that one mechanism produces both outcomes.
  • The antibacterial result also includes a dark-plate condition (obs2) where colonies persist even for doped samples, showing light dependence for the antibacterial effect, but this dark condition has no counterpart in the previous step's dye-degradation data, so the two datasets cannot be cross-checked for a light-dependence mechanism
  • Only a handful of compositions were tested in each assay, so the parallel ordering could reflect general co-doping benefit rather than a specific quantitative relationship between the two properties
  • Dye-degradation percentage and antibacterial kill ratio are different kinds of quantities (chemical substrate removal vs biological kill efficacy, measured under different assay conditions/durations); the observations contain no same-kind pair that would let these two percentages be compared numerically. The claim that they 'track in the same direction' should be stated as a rank/ordering correlation across compositions, not as evidence that the two percentage values themselves are comparable or additive.

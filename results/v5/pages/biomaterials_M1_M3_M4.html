key — what the evidence permits
Because 2Co-MBG's ordered mesoporous channels (~4-5 nm pores) survive cobalt doping (previous step), the scaffold retains the physical capacity to load and progressively release a drug: cumulative ampicillin release climbs from ~340 ug at 8 h to a ~795 ug plateau by 168 h (obs3), and over that same multi-day window ampicillin-loaded scaffolds drive relative bacterial survival down to ~105/81/52% at days 1/3/7, while drug-free scaffolds instead raise survival to 110-118% versus the 100% blank (obs4). Because the drug-free (still Co2+-releasing) scaffold does not lower survival, the antibacterial decline tracks the sustained ampicillin release rather than Co2+ release alone, consistent with the retained porosity still functioning as a drug reservoir.

key — what the evidence permits
Putting the previous step's finding (Co content sets Co2+ release, with 5Co-MBG releasing faster and more than 2Co-MBG, both rising over 7 days) together with obs1 explains the western-blot pattern: HIF-1alpha protein tracks Co content, staying faint for MBG and 2Co-MBG (the lower/no-release group) and becoming clearly stronger only for 5Co-MBG (the higher-release group), with equal alpha-tubulin loading ruling out a general loading artifact. Separately, obs2 shows that ampicillin-loaded scaffolds progressively lower bacterial survival relative to a blank (100%) and to drug-free scaffolds (110-118%), falling to about 105%, 81%, and 52% at days 1, 3, and 7 -- but this decline tracks with ampicillin loading, not with the Co-release ordering from the previous step, since the drug-free (still Co-releasing) scaffolds did not themselves lower survival.
limits

  • Only three compositions (MBG, 2Co-MBG, 5Co-MBG) are compared for HIF-1alpha, too few points to fit a dose-response curve, only an ordering consistent with the previous step's release ordering.

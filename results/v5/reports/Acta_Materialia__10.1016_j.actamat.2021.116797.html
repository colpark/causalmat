1The original graph

Our argument graph in full, in stage order. Spine claims first, then the observation nodes with the panels behind them and the image verdict recorded for each.

HYP

n1 HYP/need
Structural Mg alloys need higher strength, but the strong plastic anisotropy of hcp Mg leaves the soft orientations yielding at a few MPa
n2 HYP/hypothesis
Minute co-addition of Zn and Y, forming LPSO-related nanoplates rather than a perfect LPSO phase, should strongly raise the yield stress of an Mg single crystal

DES

n3 DES/base_system
Use a directionally solidified Mg single crystal as the base so that the CRSS of each deformation mode can be resolved by loading orientation
n4 DES/modification
Co-add 0.2 at% Zn and 0.6 at% Y to Mg (Mg99.2Zn0.2Y0.6), a dilute composition that contains no perfect LPSO phase
n5 DES/variable_sweep
Comparison axis: four loading orientations ([0001], [11-20], [01-10], [1 1 -2 1.85]) crossed with test temperature RT-400 C

PRC

n6 PRC/forming
Grow the Mg-Zn-Y single crystal by directional solidification
n7 PRC/treatment
Anneal at 520 C for 5 h with water quench, then anneal the same specimen further at 400 C for 5 h
n10 PRC/stimulus
Compress the single crystals to ~5% plastic strain in each of the four orientations at RT to 400 C

STR

n8 STR/phase/identity
The dense basal planar defects are LPSO nanoplates: aggregates of four-layer Zn/Y-segregated fcc-type stacking faults, not conventional stacking faults
n9 STR/microstructure/feature_size
Heat treatment sets nanoplate density and thickness: 9.8 per um and ~28 nm as-grown, ~0.3 per um and ~84 nm after 520 C, ~9.0 per um and ~14 nm after the further 400 C anneal
n11 STR/microstructure/orientation
The wavy deformation bands are deformation kink bands: their crystal rotation angle varies continuously from ~5 to ~55 deg instead of taking the fixed ~85 deg of {10-12} twins
n20 STR/microstructure/distribution
The LPSO nanoplates lie parallel to (0001) and are dispersed, heterogeneously, through the whole as-grown crystal

PRP

n12 PRP/value
Yield stress is strongly orientation dependent and, in [11-20] and [01-10], reaches ~145-165 MPa at RT, about 11x the pure-Mg single crystal
n13 PRP/behavior_class
The soft orientation deforms by (0001) basal slip; the hard orientations deform by {10-12} twinning plus kink banding at RT, with {1-100} prismatic slip replacing twinning at and above 200-300 C
n14 PRP/value
CRSS for {10-12} twinning (~55-70 MPa) and {1-100} prismatic slip (~52 MPa at 200 C) is 5-10x that of pure Mg, while (0001) basal slip stays low at ~4 MPa

MEC

n15 MEC/pathway
Twinning and non-basal slip must cut the (0001) LPSO nanoplates, so their CRSS is raised selectively, while basal slip glides parallel to the plates and is left easy
n16 MEC/identification
With twinning suppressed and basal slip geometrically unable to accommodate the load, plasticity is taken up by kink banding: basal dislocations aligned into band boundaries
n31 MEC/pathway
Zn and Y retained in solid solution also harden the matrix, raising even the basal-slip CRSS

PRF

n17 PRF/service_capability
The crystal keeps a yield stress above 100 MPa with more than 5% compressive plastic strain in [11-20] and [01-10] from RT to 400 C
n18 PRF/service_capability
In tension along [0001] the crystal elongates ~45% at 65-80 MPa by kink-band formation, the first bulk demonstration of tensile ductility by kinking

DSC

n19 DSC/conclusion
Dilute LPSO nanoplates, with no perfect LPSO phase, deliver LPSO-phase-level yield stress plus tensile kink-band ductility: a route to kink-strengthened dilute Mg alloys
n30 DSC/comparison
In the kink-forming orientations the dilute crystal matches or exceeds the bulk 18R-LPSO DS crystal in yield stress, but is far weaker in the basal-slip (45 deg) orientation
n21 DSC/limitation
The deformation mechanism in [0001] compression is unknown: specimens fracture at ~350 MPa with no deformation traces at any temperature
n22 DSC/limitation
No CRSS is defined for kink banding, so the mode that dominates RT deformation in the hard orientations is absent from the CRSS comparison
n23 DSC/limitation
The tensile yield stress understates the kink-band stress because basal slip occurred at the hooked shoulder of the specimen before kinking started in the gauge

KNW

n25 KNW/model
Invisibility criterion: a conventional basal stacking fault with R=1/3<10-10> gives no contrast for g=0002
n26 KNW/model
Schmid's law: the resolved shear stress on a mode is the yield stress times that mode's Schmid factor
n27 KNW/precedent
{10-12} twins in Mg carry a fixed crystal rotation of 86 deg about <-2110> (ref. [36])
n28 KNW/precedent
Kink bands with non-fixed rotation angle are known in the LPSO phase and in Zn single crystals, their boundaries being arrays of basal dislocations
n29 KNW/precedent
Pure-Mg single-crystal yield stress and CRSS data of refs [30,32-35] are used as the comparator

combining proposition
Yield stress and nanoplate number density move together across the three heat treatments: 167 -> 113 -> 160 MPa against 9.8 -> 0.3 -> 9.0 per um, both dipping after the 520 C anneal and recovering after the further 400 C anneal. This is consistent with a strengthening contribution from the nanoplates.
limits
  • Nanoplate THICKNESS moves the opposite way, 28 -> 84 -> 14 nm, so the two plate descriptors do not covary with each other and the comparison does not isolate which of them, if either, carries the strength.
  • Three points, and the heat treatments differ in more than plate density, so a contribution is the most the data supports -- not that density is the cause.

depth 2causal strength discriminatingdependency realinference followslane other

mixed~355 MPa (fractures in compression ... without yielding) (fracture stress (compression, [0001])) against ~72 MPa (serrated ~52-80) ... flow stress in tension (flow stress (tension, [0001]))

combining proposition
The [0001] orientation fractures in compression at ~355 MPa without yielding, and flows in tension at ~72 MPa (serrated ~52-80) to ~45% plastic strain. The two numbers are a fracture stress and a flow stress, not two yield stresses, so they are not a yield ratio; what they show is that the orientation has a deformation route available in tension that is absent in compression, and the kink bands in F10b are what identifies it.
limits
  • A fracture stress and a flow stress are different quantities; dividing them gives a number with no mechanical meaning.
  • Whether kinking is also available in compression and merely pre-empted by fracture is not established here.
  • The 355 MPa (RT) / 255 MPa (300C) values are compressive fracture stresses (no yielding occurs in compression for [0001]), while the ~72 MPa value is a tensile flow-stress onset (serrated 52-80 MPa) with fracture only after ~45% plastic strain; no tensile fracture stress or compressive flow/yield stress for [0001] is reported, so no same-kind pair exists in the observations to substitute. The two numbers therefore cannot be combined into a single ratio or yield comparison -- the valid conclusion is qualitative: [0001] has a ductile deformation route in tension (kink-banded, non-basal) that is absent in compression, not a quantitative measure of how much 'weaker' one mode is than the other.

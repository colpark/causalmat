1The original graph

Our argument graph in full, in stage order. Spine claims first, then the observation nodes with the panels behind them and the image verdict recorded for each.

HYP

a1 HYP/gap
2D bismuthene has not been used in nanomedicine because engineering it with the needed structure and composition is hard
a2 HYP/hypothesis
Thin bismuthene should combine NIR photothermal conversion, red-light ROS generation and X-ray attenuation for imaging-guided PTT/PDT

DES

a3 DES/base_system
Bulk layered Bi as feedstock: puckered layers with 5.62 A channels wide enough to take water (~3.2 A)
a4 DES/route
Water-mediated freeze-thaw intercalation/exfoliation with bath sonication, then NaBH4 reduction; organic-solvent free

PRC

a5 PRC/treatment
Five freeze-thaw cycles (-80 C) with bath sonication exfoliate bulk Bi into oxidised nanosheets (bismuthene oxide)
a6 PRC/synthesis
NaBH4 reduction of the bismuthene-oxide sheets grows Bi nanoflakes on their surface, giving bismuthene
a17 PRC/synthesis
Bismuthene nanosheets coated with bovine serum albumin (BNs-BSA)

STR

a7 STR/microstructure/feature_size
Free-standing lamellar nanosheets ~200 nm across and ~14 nm thick
a8 STR/phase/identity
Bismuthene is a heterojunction: hexagonal Bi nanoflakes (5-25 nm) on Bi2O3 nanosheets
a9 STR/chemistry/composition
Surface stays oxide-rich after reduction: Bi3+ (Bi2O3) : Bi0 ~ 12:5 from Bi 4f peak areas
a18 STR/chemistry/bonding
BSA is attached to the nanosheets: BNs-BSA carries the BSA amide bands

PRP

a10 PRP/value
Bismuthene is a semiconductor with an optical band gap of ~0.69 eV
a11 PRP/value
Strong NIR absorption: extinction coefficient 6.4 L g-1 cm-1 at 808 nm
a12 PRP/value
Under 660 nm light (100 mW cm-2) bismuthene generates singlet oxygen
a13 PRP/value
Photothermal conversion at 808 nm with efficiency ~19.4%, heating rising with concentration and power density

MEC

a15 MEC/pathway
Synergy: photothermal ablation and ROS killing act together, clearing residual cells that regrow after PTT alone
a23 MEC/pathway
Water in the interlayer channels expands on freezing and breaks the van der Waals bonding; sonication cuts flakes to ~200 nm
a24 MEC/tradeoff
Efficiency is lower than Bi2S3/Bi2Se3 agents (28-64%) because the sheets are thin, single-component and partly oxidised, traded for semiconducting PDT ability

PRF

a14 PRF/service_capability
BNs-BSA plus 660/808 nm lasers eradicates 4T1 tumours (100% inhibition and 54-day survival), beating PTT or PDT alone
a19 PRF/service_capability
Under 808 nm light BNs-BSA heats the tumour to ~49 C within 5 min vs ~41 C for PBS
a20 PRF/service_capability
BNs-BSA gives PA and CT contrast in the tumour after i.v. injection, PA peaking at 4 h
a21 PRF/service_capability
No evident systemic toxicity: blood indices and body weight unchanged vs PBS

DSC

a16 DSC/conclusion
A freeze-thaw/NaBH4 route yields 2D bismuthene for PA/CT imaging-guided synergistic PTT/PDT with no evident toxicity
a30 DSC/limitation
AFM height ~14 nm is ~35 Bi layers (3.98 A spacing), so "few-layer / single-layer" overstates the thinning
a31 DSC/limitation
Bi 4f areas give Bi2O3:Bi ~12:5, so the "monoelemental bismuthene" is an oxide-dominant Bi/Bi2O3 heterostructure
a32 DSC/limitation
In-vivo laser power is 7 W cm-2 in the caption of packet F5 (paper Fig. 6) but 1 W cm-2 in the text

KNW

a26 KNW/fact
Bi puckered layers: channels 5.62 A along x (VASP), 0.71 A along y; water molecule ~3.2 A
a27 KNW/precedent
Thinning heavy pnictogens turns metallic Bi into a semiconductor [32]
a28 KNW/lookup
Decay of DPBF absorbance marks singlet-oxygen generation [44]

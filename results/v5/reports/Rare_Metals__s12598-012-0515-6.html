HYP

h1 HYP/hypothesis
Adding rare earths (La, Sm) to Al-Cu-Mn should refine its segregated phases and theta' precipitates and raise strength

DES

d2 DES/modification
Add La or Sm to Al-Cu-Mn; compare RE-free, +Sm and +La alloys

PRC

Cast the three alloys (as-cast state studied by SEM, XRD, DSC)
p2 PRC/treatment
Age the cast alloys (aged state studied by TEM)

STR

s1 STR/microstructure/distribution
RE turns the continuous coarse netlike interdendritic phase into discontinuous, finer particles; La more than Sm
s2 STR/phase/identity
La forms a new Al6Cu6La phase (cubic NaZn13 type) at dendrite boundaries; Sm forms no new phase
s5 STR/microstructure/feature_size
RE addition refines the theta' platelets on {001}Al; La refines more than Sm
s6 STR/microstructure/distribution
RE addition raises the number density of theta' precipitates; La more than Sm
s3 STR/phase/identity
In the La alloy the ternary eutectic is replaced by Al2Cu plus Al6Cu6La
s4 STR/chemistry/composition
Light-grey boundary phase A is Al2Cu

PRP

p3 PRP/value
Tensile strength ranks Al-Cu-Mn-La > Al-Cu-Mn-Sm > Al-Cu-Mn

MEC

m1 MEC/pathway
Oversized La atoms distort the Al lattice and bind vacancies, providing nucleation sites for theta'
m3 MEC/pathway
Denser, finer theta' gives stronger precipitation hardening
m2 MEC/pathway
Fine, uniform Al6Cu6La at dendrite boundaries strengthens the boundaries

DSC

c1 DSC/conclusion
La is more effective than Sm: it breaks up boundary phases, forms Al6Cu6La and promotes dense fine theta', giving higher strength

KNW

k1 KNW/fact
La atomic diameter 0.187 nm vs Al 0.143 nm
k2 KNW/precedent
Fang et al. [8]: lattice-distortion and vacancy energy around La drive theta' nucleation
k3 KNW/lookup
theta' (Al2Cu) is the dominant hardening precipitate in Al-Cu alloys; finer, denser precipitates strengthen more (Orowan)

HYP

n1 HYP/gap
How the processing route (liquid stir casting vs solid-state FSP) changes FA dispersion, microstructure and wear of AZ31/FA composites is not established

DES

n2 DES/modification
Reinforce AZ31B magnesium alloy with 10 vol.% fly ash particles (~10 um, SiO2-rich)
n3 DES/variable_sweep
Compare two processing routes for the same composite: stir casting vs friction stir processing (FSP)

PRC

n4 PRC/forming
Stir casting: AZ31B melted at 720 C, FA preheated 300 C fed into vortex, squeeze-solidified at 20 kN in a preheated mold
n5 PRC/forming
FSP: FA packed in a machined groove is stirred into plasticized AZ31 by a rotating tool below the melting point

STR

s1 STR/microstructure/distribution
FSP composite has a homogeneous FA dispersion with no particle-free zones across the stir zone
s4 STR/microstructure/distribution
Stir cast composite has an inhomogeneous FA dispersion: particle clusters beside particle-free regions at top, middle and bottom
s2 STR/microstructure/feature_size
FSP refines the matrix to fine equiaxed grains (~4 um) versus coarse grains (~145 um) in the stir cast composite
s3 STR/microstructure/feature_size
FA particles fragment during FSP: smaller, some angular with sharp corners, plus fine debris in the matrix
s5 STR/phase/identity
Both composites contain the Mg matrix and SiO2 from fly ash (Al2O3 labelled in the FSP pattern)
s6 STR/interface
In the stir cast composite FA particles partly decomposed by interfacial reaction with the melt (possible MgO, Mg2Si, MgAl2O4)
s8 STR/interface
In the FSP composite the FA/matrix interface is sharp with no reaction layer, since processing stays below reaction temperatures
s9 STR/microstructure/orientation
FSP composite has a larger low-angle boundary fraction (subgrains from recovery and particle pinning); stir cast has more high-angle boundaries

PRP

p1 PRP/value
FSP composite has higher hardness than the stir cast composite
p2 PRP/value
FSP composite has a lower coefficient of friction than the stir cast composite
p3 PRP/behavior_class
Both composites wear by abrasion (parallel grooves); adhesive wear typical of unreinforced Mg is ruled out
p4 PRP/behavior_class
Stir cast composite additionally wears by plowing: craters and bulk removal of large debris from particle-free regions

MEC

m1 MEC/pathway
Frictional heat plus intense strain drive dynamic recrystallization during FSP, with broken FA particles pinning boundaries, refining grains
m2 MEC/pathway
Uniformly dispersed FA stops asperities penetrating the subsurface; load-bearing FA layers fracture into fine debris that roll (three-body), reducing cutting

PRF

f1 PRF/service_capability
FSP composite wears less in sliding: wear rate 280 vs 420 x1e-5 mm3/m (33% lower) and smaller final height loss

DSC

d1 DSC/conclusion
FSP beats stir casting for AZ31/10 vol.% FA: homogeneous dispersion, ~4 um grains, reaction-free interfaces and 33% lower wear rate

KNW

k1 KNW/model
Hall-Petch: finer grains raise hardness/strength
k2 KNW/model
Archard's wear law: wear rate inversely proportional to hardness
k3 KNW/precedent
Dynamic recrystallization during FSP is well acknowledged in literature [21]
k4 KNW/fact
Negative free energies of formation of MgO, Mg2Si and MgAl2O4 from Mg melt + FA at 720 C [15,16]
k6 KNW/fact
Protruding load-bearing hard particles reduce the pin-counterface contact area and hence friction

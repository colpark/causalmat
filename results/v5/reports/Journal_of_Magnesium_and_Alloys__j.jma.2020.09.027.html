1The original graph

Our argument graph in full, in stage order. Spine claims first, then the observation nodes with the panels behind them and the image verdict recorded for each.

HYP

n1 HYP/need
Mg-based hydrogen storage alloys absorb and desorb hydrogen too slowly and need high temperatures
n2 HYP/hypothesis
A defect-rich carbon-cobalt composite (C@Co) should catalyse nucleation and hydrogen diffusion in a Mg-RE alloy and speed its de/hydrogenation

DES

n3 DES/base_system
Use the Mg90Ce5Y5 alloy (C0) as the hydrogen storage host
n4 DES/modification
Add 10 wt% C@Co composite catalyst to the alloy (C10) and compare with C0

PRC

n5 PRC/synthesis
Synthesise C@Co: Co nanoparticles on carbon nanosheets, with high-temperature catalytic graphitisation
n8 PRC/treatment
Ball-mill Mg90Ce5Y5 with and without 10 wt% C@Co

STR

n6 STR/defect/point
Carbon nanosheets of C@Co are highly defective (ID/IG about 0.97), a source of catalytic sites
n9 STR/phase/identity
C@Co does not react with the alloy: milled C10 shows only C0's phases plus Co, and Co persists after de/hydrogenation
n10 STR/microstructure/distribution
In milled C10, carbon sits on the alloy particle surfaces with Co grains dispersed in that carbon
n7 STR/microstructure/distribution
Co nanoparticles are spread densely and uniformly over the carbon nanosheets in C@Co
n22 STR/phase/identity
As-synthesised C@Co consists of fcc Co and poorly ordered graphene-like carbon
n24 STR/microstructure/porosity
C@Co is mesoporous: BET area 186.4 m2/g with a dominant pore diameter of 3.88 nm
n25 STR/microstructure/feature_size
Milled C10 particles are smaller than milled C0 particles
n26 STR/phase/identity
After desorption only Mg/MgH2 cycles reversibly; CeH2.73, YH2 and CeO2 remain and the RE-Mg phases do not reform
n7b STR/microstructure/feature_size
Co nanoparticles in C@Co are mostly below 50 nm
n25b STR/microstructure/shape
Milled C10 particles are irregular and roughly spherical, not flat like milled C0 particles

PRP

n14 PRP/value
C@Co speeds hydrogen absorption: R10min of C10 exceeds C0 at 250-100 C
n15 PRP/value
C@Co speeds hydrogen desorption: DSC peak falls from 396.5 to 338.5 C and onset from 341.0 to 267.0 C
n16 PRP/diagnostic
JMAK exponent eta for dehydrogenation falls from 1.59 (C0) to 1.32 (C10)
n18 PRP/value
C@Co lowers the dehydrogenation activation energy from 130.3 to 81.9 kJ/mol
n19 PRP/value
C@Co leaves the thermodynamics nearly unchanged: plateau pressures and desorption enthalpy (83.3 vs 81.7 kJ/mol) barely differ
n27 PRP/value
C@Co lowers the reversible hydrogen capacity (about 5.1 to 4.56 wt% at 250 C)

MEC

n11 MEC/pathway
Defects in the carbon nanosheets act as nucleation sites for Mg/MgH2
n12 MEC/pathway
Carbon nanosheets embedded in the alloy particles provide channels for hydrogen diffusion through MgH2
n13 MEC/pathway
Co nanoparticles ease recombination of H atoms at the Mg surface, so surface control stops being rate limiting
n17 MEC/identification
Rate-limiting step of desorption changes from surface-controlled to random nucleation and growth with C@Co
n28 MEC/pathway
Carbon layers wrapping the alloy particles inhibit oxidation and sintering during heating, easing activation
n27b MEC/pathway
C@Co absorbs no hydrogen under the test conditions, so the 10 wt% additive dilutes the absorbing mass

PRF

n20 PRF/service_capability
C10 is fully activated in the first hydrogenation cycle at 360 C, whereas C0 needs three cycles

DSC

n21 DSC/conclusion
C@Co is an inert catalyst that improves Mg90Ce5Y5 de/hydrogenation kinetics and lowers Ea through a C (nucleation, diffusion) plus Co (H recombination) synergy, not through thermodynamics
n29 DSC/comparison
C10's desorption Ea is lower than MgH2 catalysed by Co@CNTs, Fe3O4@GS, Ni@C, Co@C or a NiMnAlCoFe nanocomposite
n30 DSC/outlook
Thermodynamics must still be improved before practical application

k9 KNW/lookup
Hydrogenation of Mg runs in two stages: the first nucleation-controlled, the second diffusion-controlled
k10 KNW/precedent
Mg hydride desorption has two rate-limiting steps: Mg nucleation and recombination of H atoms on the Mg surface

Our argument graph in full, in stage order. Spine claims first, then the observation nodes with the panels behind them and the image verdict recorded for each.

HYP

s1 HYP/need
Drug-delivery carriers need controllable size, secondary structure and surface charge to tune drug loading and release
s2 HYP/hypothesis
Salting-out parameters (ionic strength, pH, protein concentration) should set silk particle features that govern loading and release

DES

s3 DES/base_system
Silk fibroin as the particle matrix: amphiphilic protein with net negative charge for binding cationic drugs
s4 DES/route
Form particles by salting out aqueous silk fibroin with the kosmotropic salt potassium phosphate
s5 DES/variable_sweep
Vary ionic strength (~0.17-1.25 M), pH (4-9) and silk concentration (0.25-20 mg/mL)

PRC

s6 PRC/synthesis
Salting out of silk fibroin solution with 1.25 M potassium phosphate at pH 4-9 forms particles

STR

s8 STR/microstructure/feature_sizeshown
Mean particle size grows with silk concentration, ~0.5 um at 0.25 mg/mL to ~3.6 um at 20 mg/mL
s9 STR/chemistry/bondingshown
Particle secondary structure shifts from silk II-rich (beta sheet) at pH 4-6 to silk I-rich at pH 8-9
x1 STR/microstructure/shapeshown
Discrete spherical particles form above ~0.75 M potassium phosphate; below it the silk gels
x2 STR/microstructure/distributionpartial
Particles made at pH 4 aggregate into non-dispersible clusters bound in a film-like network
x3 STR/chemistry/bondingshown
Silk I conformation of pH 8 particles survives 24 h in 0-100% ethanol or methanol
x3b STR/chemistry/bondingshown
Silk I conformation survives sonication up to 40 s; 60 s converts it toward silk II

PRP

s11 PRP/valueshown
Particle zeta potential becomes more negative with production pH, about -26 mV (pH 4) to -46 mV (pH 9)
s12 PRP/valuepartial
Cationic drug loading is near-complete (~100% efficiency) up to ~6% matrix loading, then efficiency falls as the matrix saturates
s14 PRP/valueshown
Initial burst ranks rhodamine B >> crystal violet > alcian blue, i.e. more positive charge gives more sustained release
s15 PRP/valueshown
Crystal violet release is faster from pH 7 particles than from pH 8 and pH 9 particles over 7 days

MEC

s10 MEC/pathwayshown
pH sets domain charges and chain extension, which set micelle packing and the secondary structure stabilised on particle formation
s13 MEC/pathwayshown
Cationic drugs load by charge-charge binding to negative silk; bound drug neutralises particle charge, ending uptake
x5 MEC/pathwaypartial
Adsorbed small-molecule drugs diffuse from the surface into the protein matrix
x6 MEC/pathway
Alternative: slower release from pH 8-9 particles due to more negative surface charge binding crystal violet

PRF

s16 PRF/service_capability
Silk particles act as tunable sustained-release carriers for positively charged small-molecule drugs

DSC

s17 DSC/conclusion
Salting-out ionic strength, pH and silk concentration control particle size, secondary structure and charge, which set drug loading and release
x4 DSC/design_guidance
Silk particles can be sterilised with ethanol without altering their secondary structure
x4b DSC/design_guidance
Keep sonication of silk I particles below ~40 s to preserve secondary structure

KNW

k1 KNW/lookup
Amide I ~1650 cm-1 marks silk I/random coil/helix; ~1620-1630 cm-1 marks silk II beta sheet
k2 KNW/fact
Theoretical isoelectric point of silk fibroin is pI = 4.53
k3 KNW/precedent
Sonicating silk solution forms silk II hydrogels at exposures under 30 s

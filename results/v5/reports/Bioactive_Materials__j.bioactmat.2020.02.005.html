Our argument graph in full, in stage order. Spine claims first, then the observation nodes with the panels behind them and the image verdict recorded for each.

HYP

b1 HYP/need
TiO2 photocatalysts harvest mainly UV light, so sunlight-driven rapid disinfection with TiO2 is weak
b2 HYP/hypothesis
Er up-conversion of NIR to visible plus Ce narrowing the gap, both suppressing recombination, should boost visible-light bacteria killing by TiO2

PRC

b5 PRC/synthesisshown

b10 PRC/synthesis
Er0.5CexTi-O co-doped series made by the same sol-gel route and annealed (700 C per Raman legend)

Er and Ce are incorporated in the co-doped particles, Er as Er3+ and Ce as a surface Ce3+/Ce4+ mixture
b12 STR/phase/fraction

b25 STR/phase/identity
The co-doped nanoparticles are anatase-rutile mixtures
b26 STR/microstructure/feature_size
Co-doped particles are ~20-50 nm polyhedral crystallites aggregated into ~100-300 nm clusters

PRP

b6 PRP/value
Visible absorption of Er-doped TiO2 (Er 4f lines at 488-654 nm) peaks at Er0.5Ti-O; band edge essentially unchanged
b7 PRP/value
Er0.5Ti-O up-converts NIR excitation into green (520-570 nm) and red (640-690 nm) emission, stronger than Er0.75Ti-O
b13 PRP/value
Ce co-doping narrows the optical band gap of TiO2 from 2.90 eV to 2.20-2.46 eV, smallest (2.20 eV) at Ce 0.2
b15 PRP/value
Photogenerated electron-hole separation improves: recombination falls with Er and further with Ce, charge transfer resistance drops, photocurrent rises
b17 PRP/value
Photocatalytic dye degradation rises with co-doping, best for Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O (~46% vs ~20% for TiO2 in 60 min)
b21 PRP/value
Without light, antibacterial ratio rises with Ce content (to ~45-47% at Ce0.3) vs ~6% for TiO2

MEC

b16 MEC/pathwayshown
Surface Ce3+/Ce4+ couple captures photogenerated electrons and holes, preventing their recombination
b18 MEC/pathwayshown
Higher photocatalytic activity generates more ROS under light, which damage and kill bacteria
b14 MEC/pathway
Partly filled Ce 4f/5d orbitals add energy levels in the gap, lowering the band gap
b22 MEC/pathway
Ce ions kill bacteria in the dark by reacting with cell walls and disturbing cellular respiration

PRF

b19 PRF/service_capability
Under simulated sunlight for 20 min, Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O kills ~91% of S. aureus and ~93% of E. coli vs ~45% for TiO2

DSC

b20 DSC/conclusion
Er-Ce co-doped TiO2 is driven by sunlight to fast visible-light photocatalytic sterilization, via Ce gap narrowing, Er up-conversion and suppressed recombination
b24 DSC/design_guidance
Optimum dopant levels are ~0.5 mol% Er and ~0.2 mol% Ce; more Er quenches emission and more Ce raises the surface barrier

combining proposition
Putting Er0.5Ti-O's peak visible-light absorption (Er 4f lines, 488-654 nm, band edge unchanged) together with the light-driven antibacterial results is consistent with visible-light-sensitized photocatalysis contributing to the ~91% (S. aureus) and ~93% (E. coli) kill ratios of Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O under simulated sunlight, versus ~45% for undoped TiO2, with intermediate values for Ce0.1 (~68-73%) and Ce0.3 (~87%) [obs1], and fewer surviving colonies on Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O light plates for both strains [obs2].
limits
  • The antibacterial series varies Ce content (0.1, 0.2, 0.3) at fixed Er0.5, while the absorption peak in step 1 was identified for Er0.5Ti-O without Ce; the sample showing peak visible absorption is not the same composition as the sample showing peak antibacterial performance
  • Since band edge is unchanged (step 1), any gain in light-driven killing cannot be attributed to bandgap narrowing extending absorption into longer wavelengths - only to the Er 4f line absorption itself or to some Ce-related effect
  • obs2 shows dark plates also remain sparser for doped samples, indicating a light-independent contribution to antibacterial activity that this comparison does not separate from the light-driven component
  • Antibacterial ratio is measured as a single-endpoint percentage after a fixed exposure window, not as a rate or spectrum, so it cannot be mapped point-for-point onto the wavelength-resolved absorption data
not identifiableWhether the enhanced light-driven antibacterial performance in Ce-containing samples arises from the Er 4f visible absorption (established in step 1 for Er0.5Ti-O alone) or from an independent Ce-dopant effect (e.g., a Ce-dose-dependent contribution, since Ce0.1/0.2/0.3 track with increasing kill ratio) cannot be separated, because no Ce-free, Er-doped-only sample was tested for antibacterial activity and no Ce absorption spectrum was reported

combining proposition
Read together, the sustained NO delivery from PPAmF established in the prior step (release lasting beyond 8 h rather than the ~1 h burst of PPAm) is consistent with, and offers a mechanistic candidate for, the markedly lower platelet adhesion/activation and thrombus formation seen here on the NO-loaded surfaces (NO-PPAmF, BVLD/NO-PPAmF) versus the non-NO surfaces (316L SS, PPAmF): fewer/rounder platelets (obs1), lower adhered-platelet counts and activation (~11%/18% for NO-PPAmF and ~5%/~1% for BVLD/NO-PPAmF vs ~32-41 and ~65-74% for 316L SS/PPAmF; obs2, obs3), less fibrin/thrombus and cleaner lumens (obs4, obs5), lower occlusion (obs6), lower thrombus weight (obs7), and higher retained flow (obs8). A NO source that keeps releasing rather than exhausting itself quickly is a plausible reason the antithrombotic benefit persists across these endpoint measurements rather than being confined to a brief early window.
limits
  • The evidence given does not state the duration of the platelet-adhesion, thrombosis, or flow assays, so it is not confirmed that these tests were run within the >8 h sustained-release window rather than at a single early timepoint that a mere burst (as in PPAm) could also have covered.
  • obs2-obs8 are single-timepoint endpoint amounts (cell counts/mm2, % activation, % occlusion, mg thrombus weight, % flow retained), not a time course of NO release or of antithrombotic effect, so they cannot themselves verify that release continued throughout the test.
  • BVLD/NO-PPAmF carries both the NO-releasing component and BVLD together, so its superior suppression relative to NO-PPAmF alone (e.g., adhered platelets ~5 vs ~11 x1e2 cells/mm2, occlusion ~3% vs ~7.5%) cannot be attributed to sustained NO release specifically; BVLD's own antiplatelet/anticoagulant action is confounded with NO release in that combined surface.
not identifiableWhether the extra suppression of platelet adhesion/activation and thrombosis seen on BVLD/NO-PPAmF, beyond what NO-PPAmF or BVLD-PPAmF alone achieve, is attributable to the sustained NO release characterized in the prior step, to BVLD's own action, or to a combination of the two.

The confound is the conclusion of the comparison, not a substitute for it.

combining proposition
Building on step 1 (PPAmF's matrix prolongs NO release beyond 8 h versus ~1 h for PPAm), the SEM, photographic, and quantitative data here (obs1-obs8) show that the NO-releasing surfaces (NO-PPAmF and BVLD/NO-PPAmF) have markedly fewer adhered/activated platelets, sparser fibrin, and less occlusive thrombus than the non-NO-releasing surfaces (316L SS, PPAmF), and that BVLD/NO-PPAmF -- combining the anticoagulant BVLD with sustained NO release -- comes closest to preventing occlusive thrombus altogether (~3% occlusion, ~2 mg thrombus, ~90% flow retained). Together the two steps support that sustained NO delivery from the composite is associated with, and plausibly contributes to, reduced platelet-driven thrombosis in this ex vivo shunt setting.
limits
  • The shunt evidence is a single-endpoint measurement (adhesion, activation, occlusion, thrombus weight, flow retention all read at one time), not a time course, so it does not directly test whether anti-thrombotic performance tracks the specific 8 h NO-release duration established in step 1 -- the link between release duration and outcome is inferred, not measured together.
  • BVLD (an anticoagulant) is present in two of the five surfaces (BVLD-PPAmF, BVLD/NO-PPAmF), and co-occurs with NO release only in BVLD/NO-PPAmF, so BVLD's contribution and NO release's contribution are confounded in the best-performing arm; the data cannot cleanly attribute the near-elimination of thrombus in BVLD/NO-PPAmF to NO release alone.
  • The two steps compare quantities of different kinds -- an in vitro time-duration of NO release (hours) versus ex vivo/in vivo endpoint outcome measures (%, mg, cells/mm2) -- so the connection is an association across steps, not a demonstrated dose- or time-response mechanism.

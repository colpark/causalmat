combining proposition
Because the same mesoporous PCO host was used in both half-cells, the two ions diverge on like-for-like first-cycle numbers: 1st discharge ~1330 mAh/g for Li against ~865 for Na, and 1st charge ~870 for Li against ~470 for Na. The mesopore structure alone therefore does not secure reversible capacity for both ions. The GCD curves do not identify what is responsible for the Na-side shortfall.
before the audit: Because the same mesoporous PCO host was used in both Li and Na half-cells, the divergent cycling outcomes -- Li/PCO capacity fluctuating (870 at cycle 1, rising to ~1050 at cycle ~17, back to ~840 at cycle 40) versus Na/PCO dropping from ~865-870 mAh/g (1st discharge) to ~70 mAh/g by cycle 40 -- show that the mesopore structure alone does not guarantee stable reversible capacity for both alkali ions; the GCD data do
before the quantity-kind fix: Because the same mesoporous PCO host was used in both Li and Na half-cells, the divergent cycling outcomes -- Li/PCO capacity fluctuating (870 at cycle 1, rising to ~1050 at cycle ~17, back to ~840 at cycle 40) versus Na/PCO dropping from ~865-870 mAh/g (1st discharge) to ~70 mAh/g by cycle 40 -- show that the mesopore structure alone does not guarantee stable reversible capacity for both alkali ions; the GCD data do
limits
  • GCD capacity-vs-cycle curves (obs1-obs4) record electrochemical outcome only; they contain no structural readout of the cycled electrode, so nothing here shows whether the pores physically changed, clogged, or stayed intact during Na cycling
  • Li and Na cells differ in more than just the alkali ion -- plateau voltages, electrolyte compatibility, and possibly SEI chemistry differ too -- so the porosity variable is not isolated from these co-varying factors; attributing the divergence to the ion alone versus to ion-electrolyte/SEI interaction is not resolved
  • the comparison rests on two single cycling series (one per ion), not a controlled sweep of pore size or ion at fixed everything else, so it supports an ordering/contrast (Li stable, Na unstable) but not a quantitative relationship between pore size and capacity retention
  • the 40-cycle window is short for durability claims and CE is only reported for Li (~96%), not Na, limiting what can be said about Na-side reversibility mechanisms
  • The 40-cycle comparison cannot be made like with like: at cycle 40 the data report Li CHARGE capacity (~840 mAh/g, obs3) and Na DISCHARGE capacity (~70 mAh/g, obs4), and neither Li discharge nor Na charge is given at that cycle. Only the first cycle supports a same-kind comparison.
not identifiablewhether Na's fade is caused by a pore-size/ion-size mismatch (5-7 nm pores being poorly suited to Na+ transport or accommodation) versus an intrinsic Na-carbon reaction irreversibility (e.g., unstable Na-species formation/decomposition unrelated to pore geometry) -- the GCD data cannot separate these two candidate causes

scoring target: the combining proposition together with each named limit — 6 required elements. Stating that the causes cannot be separated, or that the result is not identifiable, without giving the combining proposition and its limits, earns nothing. The confound is the conclusion of the comparison, not a substitute for it.

HYP

Pt counter electrodes perform poorly in polysulfide-electrolyte QDSSCs

DES

Cu2S microspheres from solvothermal CuCl, thiourea and PVP in ethanol as Pt-free counter-electrode catalyst

Add graphene oxide to the Cu2S synthesis so it is reduced in situ to RGO

GO solution 0, 5, 10, 20 mL giving Cu2S and RGO-Cu2S-1/-2/-3 (26.8, 41.4, 55.3 wt% RGO)

PRC

STR

m7 STR/microstructure/shape
GO addition turns Cu2S into flower-like hierarchical microspheres built from more, smaller, thinner nanoflakes
m8 STR/microstructure/distribution
RGO nanosheets wrap the Cu2S microspheres, coverage rising with GO until sheets bury the spheres
m9 STR/phase/identity
The microspheres are monoclinic Cu2S in all samples, built of single-crystal nanoflakes
m10 STR/chemistry/composition
Copper is Cu(I) and sulfur is sulfide coordinated to Cu in RGO-Cu2S-2
m11 STR/chemistry/bonding
GO is largely reduced in the composite: O-H, C=O and C-O groups mostly removed
m12 STR/microstructure/porosity
RGO-Cu2S-2 adsorbs more N2 than bare Cu2S: more accessible surface and more mesopore volume at 3-15 nm
m18 STR/microstructure/feature_size
Microsphere diameter grows with GO amount from ~2 to ~5 um

m14 PRP/value

MEC

m13 MEC/pathway

PRF

RGO-wrapped hierarchical Cu2S microspheres make an efficient Pt-free QDSSC counter electrode, ~80% higher PCE than Pt

combining proposition
The photovoltaic device data at this step reproduce the same rank order as the previous step's polysulfide-reduction activity: RGO-Cu2S counter electrodes give the highest Jsc (~16.0 mA/cm2), highest Voc (~0.555 V), and highest IPCE (peak ~95%, 320-620 nm), followed by Cu2S (Jsc ~15.0 mA/cm2, Voc ~0.535 V, IPCE peak ~88%), with Pt lowest (Jsc ~13.0 mA/cm2, Voc ~0.525 V, IPCE peak ~65%). Together the two steps establish that counter-electrode catalytic activity for polysulfide reduction and whole-cell photovoltaic output (current, voltage, and photon-to-current conversion) rank in the same order across these three electrodes.
limits
  • Only three electrode samples are compared, so this is an ordinal match, not a dose-response relationship between catalytic activity and device output.
  • Catalytic activity (previous step) and Jsc/Voc/IPCE (this step) are different kinds of quantities measured in different test configurations (electrochemical activity of the isolated electrode vs. full-device illuminated output), so agreement in rank order is associative, not a direct measurement of the same mechanism.
  • Other electrode-dependent properties that differ across RGO-Cu2S, Cu2S, and Pt (e.g., electrical conductivity contributed by RGO, surface area, electrolyte wetting) are not controlled for and could also track this same rank order.
  • This step's evidence (Jsc, Voc, IPCE) does not by itself give overall power conversion efficiency, which also depends on fill factor, so the PCE ranking mentioned as the paper's conclusion is not directly supported by obs1/obs2 alone.
not identifiableWhether the matching Jsc/Voc/IPCE ranking arises specifically from polysulfide-reduction catalytic activity versus other co-varying counter-electrode properties (e.g., RGO's electrical conductivity or surface area) cannot be separated with this data.

scoring target: the combining proposition together with each named limit — 5 required elements. Stating that the causes cannot be separated, or that the result is not identifiable, without giving the combining proposition and its limits, earns nothing. The confound is the conclusion of the comparison, not a substitute for it.

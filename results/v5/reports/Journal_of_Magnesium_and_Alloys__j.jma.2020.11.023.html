limits
  • ZT = S^2*sigma*T/kappa_total depends on the full thermal conductivity (including an electronic component kappa_e) and on the power factor S^2*sigma, neither of which is given at this step, so the low kappa_L+kappa_b value cannot by itself account for the magnitude or ordering of ZT
  • The composition with the lowest reported kappa_L+kappa_b (x=0.01, ~0.69 at 300 K) is not the composition with the highest room-temperature ZT (x=0.005, ~0.84); the two best-supported values are for different samples, so no direct within-sample link between the lowest thermal conductivity and the highest ZT is established
  • The previous step's kappa_L+kappa_b data span only 300-450 K, while the ZT peak of ~1.25 occurs near 500-525 K for x=0.01 -- outside the temperature window covered by the cited thermal-conductivity evidence
  • Comparison to the n-Bi2Te3 reference ZT (~0.88) is a single-point benchmark, not a systematic composition or temperature series, so it cannot establish a general trend
not identifiableWhether the observed ZT values (and the x=0.01 peak) are driven mainly by the suppressed kappa_L+kappa_b or by favorable power factor (S^2*sigma) cannot be separated with the evidence given

scoring target: the combining proposition together with each named limit — 5 required elements.

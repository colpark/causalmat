An earlier step in this work already established the following, and you may treat it as settled:

Together, the thrombin-activity assay and the SEM morphology survey support that BVLD-bearing coatings (BVLD-PPAmF and BVLD/NO-PPAmF) both retain reduced thrombin specific activity (~0.65e-3 abs/ng/cm2, versus ~1.3e-3 on 316L SS, PPAmF, NO-PPAmF) and show reduced platelet spreading/activation on SEM (fewer, less dendritic platelets on BVLD-PPAmF; a few round, unspread platelets on BVLD/NO-PPAmF), consistent with retained anti-thrombin bioactivity accompanying suppressed platelet activation on BVLD-grafted surfaces.

The qualifications recorded on that earlier result:
  - NO-PPAmF alone shows thrombin specific activity at the high, unaltered level (~1.3e-3, grouped with 316L SS and PPAmF) yet SEM still shows fewer spread platelets than on 316L SS/PPAmF, so the pairing does not show that thrombin-activity change tracks platelet suppression consistently across all four surfaces
  - the chromogenic assay is a single normalized enzymatic readout (activity per ng protein per cm2) while the SEM evidence is a qualitative/categorical description of platelet number and morphology, not a matched quantitative platelet count or activation score, so no dose-response or magnitude correlation between the two can be established
  - the greatest platelet suppression (round, unspread platelets) occurs on BVLD/NO-PPAmF, which has the same thrombin specific activity as BVLD-PPAmF alone, so the extra suppression seen with NO added cannot be attributed to a further change in thrombin activity from this pairing

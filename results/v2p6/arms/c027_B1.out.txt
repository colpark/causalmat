Both panels confirm the reported numbers. F5a: Co2+ concentration is monotonic in Co content at every day (0Co=0 throughout; 2Co ~4.7/10.5/16.9; 5Co ~8.9/17.9/20.8 mg/L, so 5Co>2Co>0Co). F8: VEGF is NOT monotonic in Co content — 2Co-MBG (~430 pg/mL) is far above blank (~60), MBG (~100), and 5Co-MBG (~115), with P<0.05 for 2Co vs each of the other three; 5Co-MBG sits near baseline, not above 2Co.

So the upstream release data only partly account for the downstream VEGF result. They establish that 2Co-MBG does release Co2+ (nonzero, rising over 7 days) while 0Co does not, which is consistent with Co2+ being available to drive VEGF secretion above the Co-free control. But the release ordering (5Co>2Co) actively fails to predict the VEGF ordering: the group with the highest Co2+ release (5Co) shows the lowest VEGF response, essentially indistinguishable from unstimulated MBG/blank, while the intermediate-release group (2Co) shows the peak. This is a non-monotonic (biphasic/inverted-U) relationship that a simple "more Co2+ released -> more VEGF" reading of the upstream data cannot explain.

What is left open: why higher Co2+ exposure suppresses or fails to sustain VEGF induction (threshold, toxicity, or another confound at 5Co) is not addressed by the release measurements, and no direct mechanistic/causal link between measured Co2+ concentration and VEGF secretion is established here — only a partial, non-monotonic correlation.

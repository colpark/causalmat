F5d/F5e confirm the numeric claims: total fibrinogen drops from ~0.10 (316LSS, PPAmF) to ~0.05-0.06 on BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF, with BVLD/NO not the lowest of the three; gamma-chain shows the same pattern (~0.13-0.15 down to ~0.05-0.065). F6b/F6c show 316LSS and PPAmF tubes/foils filled with dark, red-brown thrombus, while BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF show progressively cleaner lumens/foils, with BVLD/NO-PPAmF the most patent-looking.

What this combination supports: the macroscopic reduction in thrombus (photos) co-occurs with the ELISA-measured drop in fibrinogen/gamma-chain adsorption across all three functionalized coatings as a group, relative to bare 316LSS/PPAmF — i.e., functionalization in general, not BVLD's anti-thrombin activity specifically, tracks with less clot. This is consistent with the earlier-established dissociation: since NO-PPAmF lowers fibrinogen and shows visibly less thrombus without any drop in thrombin activity, thrombin inhibition is not necessary for the macroscopic anti-thrombotic appearance either.

What it leaves open: the photos are qualitative/visual (no quantitative thrombus mass or occlusion score tied panel-for-panel to the ELISA bars), so no dose-response or matched statistical link between fibrinogen/gamma-chain level and thrombus extent is established. It remains undetermined whether fibrinogen reduction itself causally drives the visible patency, or whether both are independent downstream readouts of a shared but unspecified surface-passivation mechanism. BVLD/NO-PPAmF looking cleanest in photos despite not being lowest in fibrinogen further underscores that no single upstream measure (thrombin activity or fibrinogen adsorption alone) fully accounts for the macroscopic outcome.

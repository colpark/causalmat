An earlier step in this work already established the following, and you may treat it as settled:

Combined with the prior step's finding that Zn-N bonds form at pyridinic-N sites, the DFT results at this step are consistent with, but do not specifically isolate, a thermodynamic driving force for that bond formation: charge transfer to zinc is more negative at N-doped sites (-0.032 to -0.037 e) than at pristine sites (-0.023 to -0.027 e, obs5); the transferred electron density sits in the zinc-substrate gap on N-doped graphene rather than as a detached lobe above pristine graphene (obs6); and computed adsorption energies are negative at top/hollow/bridge sites on N-doped graphene (-0.072/-0.089/-0.077 eV) versus positive on pristine graphene (+0.030/+0.014/+0.029 eV, obs7). Together these show nitrogen doping generically converts zinc adsorption from unfavorable to favorable at all three site geometries, giving an energetic driving force generally consistent with -- though not proof specific to -- the experimentally observed Zn-N bonding and pyridinic-to-graphitic nitrogen shift.

The qualifications recorded on that earlier result:
  - The DFT calculations model generic 'N-doped graphene' at top/hollow/bridge sites; they do not specify or isolate a pyridinic-N configuration, so they cannot confirm that the exothermicity is attributable specifically to pyridinic-N sites rather than N-doping in general (including graphitic-N already present)
  - The previous step's evidence is a spectroscopic chemical-state assignment (N 1s peak positions/intensities) while this step's evidence is a computed, static adsorption energy and Bader charge transfer; these are different kinds of quantities that can only be shown qualitatively consistent, not quantitatively cross-validated
  - obs2 (Raman ~1200 cm-1 feature), obs3 (plating-plateau separation) and obs4 (39 mV vs 43 mV nucleation overpotentials) are electrochemical/structural observations that are not tied into this binding-energy comparison and do not bear on the exothermic-vs-endothermic conclusion
  - The DFT values are single-point, 0 K computed energies for isolated adsorption geometries, not measurements under the discharge conditions used elsewhere in the evidence

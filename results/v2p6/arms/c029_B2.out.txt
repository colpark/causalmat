Yes, in part. The upstream pair (F7f polarity-reversal + F7b monotonic voltage rise) establishes that the PENG output is a genuine, dose-dependent piezoelectric response rather than a fixed artifact — that is the mechanistic grounding for treating the reported values as real "value" (piezoelectric output) measurements. F7e then reports a downstream, distinct performance metric (power/service_capability): a fiber-film peak of 136.9 uW at 10 MOhm versus 21.025 uW for the cast film, i.e., a fiber-vs-cast morphology comparison, not a PZT-content sweep.

So the upstream evidence accounts for the *existence* of a real piezoelectric mechanism generating measurable output, and for the general claim that this output scales with composition/processing — supporting the plausibility that fiber morphology's superior power stems from the same underlying piezoelectric charge generation. But it does not directly account for the fiber-vs-cast comparison itself: F7b/F7f never test or visualize the cast film, so nothing upstream isolates why fiber outperforms cast (porosity, alignment, contact area, etc.). It also leaves open, as before, whether every composition/sample point (including the specific f-P0.10 fiber sample in F7e) was itself artifact-checked, and it says nothing about the molecular-scale origin of the fiber's power advantage.

The three panels confirm the reported observations as printed. F3d (CnC HS) shows a largely flat, low-relief surface with only a few sparse, sharp protrusions; F3e (C HS) shows a dense, near-uniform mat of interlocking sharp platelets covering the whole field — matching observation A. F4a plots Coulombic efficiency vs. cycle number: the CnC HS series (black) holds roughly 93–100% CE out to ~205 cycles, while the C HS series (red) tracks similarly at first but drops to ~75% near cycle 40, then falls to 0% around cycles 40–48 and the series simply ends near cycle 50 — matching observation B.

Together, this justifies the conclusion that the flat, sparsely-platelet morphology on CnC HS co-occurs with markedly more stable, longer-lived Coulombic efficiency (higher service capability), while the dense, sharp-platelet mat on C HS co-occurs with abrupt CE collapse and early cell failure — a pattern consistent with the plating morphology governing electrochemical durability (e.g., via short-circuiting or dead-zinc formation from dendritic/sharp platelets).

What it leaves open: the SEM images are single, likely early-cycle snapshots, not taken at the moment of CE failure, so there is no direct visual link between the imaged morphology and the cycle-40–48 collapse itself. As already noted, CnC HS and C HS differ structurally beyond nitrogen/Zn-N bonding, so the morphology-CE co-occurrence cannot rule out other confounding host-structure differences as the true cause of both the platelet shape and the CE failure. Only one specimen per condition is shown, so this is a qualitative co-occurrence, not a controlled or quantitative causal test.

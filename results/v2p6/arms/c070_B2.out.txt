The upstream combination (thrombin activity + SEM) partially accounts for the downstream macro thrombosis/patency observation. The broad split matches: 316LSS and PPAmF (high thrombin activity, dendritic spread platelets) show tubes/foils fully filled with dark thrombus; BVLD-PPAmF and BVLD/NO-PPAmF (low thrombin activity, suppressed/unspread platelets) show open lumens and clean foil. So the two BVLD-bearing surfaces that were already shown to have reduced thrombin activity and suppressed platelet activation are also the ones with the least macroscopic clot — the SEM/thrombin pairing is not contradicted by the macro outcome.

What it leaves open: thrombin activity alone cannot explain the gradient, since NO-PPAmF has unaltered (high) thrombin activity yet its lumen is only partly open (better than 316LSS/PPAmF), matching its reduced SEM platelet count rather than its thrombin level — the same disconnect noted upstream now recurs downstream. Likewise, BVLD/NO-PPAmF's cleaner lumen/foil than BVLD-PPAmF tracks the greater SEM platelet suppression (round, unspread) but not any further drop in thrombin activity, since both BVLD surfaces share the same low value. So the evidence supports qualitative concordance across surfaces but does not establish which factor drives macro patency, nor any quantitative dose-response linking assay values, SEM counts, and thrombus extent.

Panels b-f (F1b-f, 200 µm scale) show dark SiC particles distributed across the whole polished field with visible local clustering in every panel; particle density and clustering both increase visibly from b through f, consistent with the stated d/e/f = 15/20/25 wt% SiCp series (b, c presumably lower wt%, unlabeled fractions here). This is consistent with a monotonic increase in reinforcement content matching the composition series used for the tensile bars.

Combined with the YS trend (93→113→124→127→136→142 MPa for 0-25 wt%), the micrographs support that the strength increase tracks an increasing, reasonably uniform reinforcement loading rather than a compositional error or gross non-monotonic distribution artifact — i.e., the upstream evidence is consistent with, and does not contradict, the downstream YS observation.

What it leaves open: the micrographs alone cannot establish the strengthening mechanism (load transfer, Orowan, Hall-Petch grain refinement, or CTE-mismatch dislocation strengthening are all optically indistinguishable here) and give no quantitative particle size/volume-fraction/porosity data. The clustering visible in every panel (most conspicuous in d-f) is a potential stress-concentration/defect site that could work against strengthening, yet YS still rises — so distribution imagery alone cannot explain why clustering did not suppress the trend, nor can it confirm interfacial bonding quality. So the pairing supports a compositional/qualitative-distribution account of the YS trend but not a mechanistic or quantitative one.

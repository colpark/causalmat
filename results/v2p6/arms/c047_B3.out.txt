Yes, the upstream DFT/XPS evidence and the downstream photocatalytic rate data are mutually consistent, and the combination supports a coherent (though not fully proven) mechanistic story.

What is supported: F9a shows Pd0.75Ag0.25(111) has the dG_H* closest to thermoneutral (-0.08 eV), flanked by Pd (-0.31, too strong) and Ag (0.49, too weak) — a volcano-type pattern. F8b and F8a show that the matching composition, Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS, gives the highest H2 evolution rate (125.4 umol/h) and highest cumulative H2 over 5 h among all alloys and single metals, exceeding even Pd-ZIS (34.5) despite Pd's more negative computed dG_H*. This matches the Sabatier/volcano expectation that near-zero adsorption free energy, not maximal binding strength, optimizes catalytic turnover, and is consistent with the earlier XPS-based inference that alloying-induced electron redistribution is associated with the near-optimal dG_H* at this composition.

What it leaves open: the rate is measured on ZIS-supported alloy nanoparticles while dG_H* is computed on bare (111) surfaces — same object-mismatch as before. Rate differences across compositions could also reflect changes in light absorption, charge separation at the ZIS heterojunction, particle size, or dispersion rather than intrinsic per-site adsorption thermodynamics alone; no electronic-structure (XPS) data are given for the other three alloy compositions, so the electronic-shift explanation is not confirmed across the full series — only correlated at one point.

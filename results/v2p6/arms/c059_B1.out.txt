The combination partially accounts for the downstream result but does not fully explain it.

What it supports: The ranking in F8 (TiO2 lowest at ~20%, then Ce0.1 ~31%, Ce0.3 ~41%, Ce0.2 highest ~46%, consistent across 20/40/60 min) tracks the ranking in F4b, where TiO2 has the sharpest, most blue-shifted edge (~410 nm) and the co-doped samples all extend absorption well past 420 nm, with Ce0.2 showing the strongest visible absorbance. So the two panels together support that co-doping extends light absorption into the visible range, and that this extended absorption correlates with higher measured degradation activity — TiO2's narrow absorption pairs with its poor degradation, and Ce0.2's strongest absorption pairs with its best degradation.

What is left open: This is a correlation across compositions, not a demonstrated causal mechanism — the panels don't show that the extra absorbed photons are actually driving the redox chemistry that degrades MO. The prior qualifications still apply: EDS can't confirm Ce/Er oxidation states relevant to charge transfer, the UVVIS comparison is qualitative (edge position, not band gap), and Er's individual contribution to either absorption or activity can't be isolated from Ce's. Other factors affecting catalysis (surface area, crystallinity, particle size) are unaddressed by these two panels.

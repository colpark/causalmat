F4c (EDS) shows C and Co concentrated at the surfaces/edges of C10 particles, with Mg filling the particle interiors — a spatial pattern compatible with a surface carbon/Co layer acting as a short-circuit diffusion path or catalytic site for hydrogen. F6a/F6b (Sieverts) show that the diffusion-controlled second absorption stage is far shorter for C10 (11 min) than for C0 (66 min) at 360 °C — a real, quantified kinetic improvement upon C@Co addition.

What the combination supports: it is consistent with the idea that the surface-located C/Co phase seen by EDS is spatially positioned where it could plausibly accelerate hydrogen diffusion into/through the particle, correlating with the faster C10 kinetics.

What it leaves open: EDS is only shown for C10, not C0, so there is no direct microstructural contrast tied to the kinetic contrast — the pairing is not a controlled comparison. Neither panel demonstrates a diffusion or catalytic mechanism; EDS gives static, coarse-resolution location, not a measurement of diffusion pathways, particle size, or reaction rate at the surface. So the faster kinetics is correlated with, but not mechanistically established by, the surface C/Co distribution — causation and the specific pathway remain unproven from these panels alone.

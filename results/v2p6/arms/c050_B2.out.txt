The combination supports only a plausibility link, not a demonstration. The upstream SEM/photo result establishes that bare BST is smooth (~70 nm) while Ag-treated particles are rough, coated, and larger, and that this modification co-occurs with color darkening as Ag% rises — but only as two discrete states (bare vs. "modified"), with no graded series and with Ag content, particle size, and coating thickness confounded together. The panels viewed here (F3a bare BST, F3b BST-SH, F3e BST@Ag5%) fit that same binary-contrast pattern rather than adding a graded structural series.

The downstream dielectric data (F6a) is genuinely graded: constant rises monotonically 5%>3%>1%>>Pure PAEN across all frequencies (~6.05 vs 3.35 at 100 Hz), and this order holds at every frequency tested. This is consistent with the idea that whatever structural change SEM visualizes (coating/roughening) scales with Ag loading and drives the permittivity increase — a reasonable story, since a conductive Ag layer plausibly raises interfacial polarization.

But the upstream evidence cannot establish this causally: it never resolved coating degree, particle size, or Ag content as separable drivers, and there is no dielectric measurement for untreated BST composite (only Pure PAEN vs. BST@Ag1/3/5%) to isolate the coating's specific contribution from bare-filler effects. So the link between the structural observation and the dielectric trend remains an unconfirmed but consistent hypothesis, not an accounted-for result.

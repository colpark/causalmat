Panels b–f are optical micrographs of SiC particle-reinforced Mg composite, with d/e/f explicitly captioned as 15, 20, 25 wt% SiCp. Visually, particle count and clustering density increase progressively from b through f: b shows dark particles scattered with modest local clustering (mostly in vertical streaks), c shows denser and more numerous particles, and d–f show steadily increasing particle density and inter-particle contact/clustering, consistent with rising SiCp content. Separately, UCS rises monotonically (310→363 MPa) across six SiCp levels.

What the combination supports: qualitatively, the microstructural series is consistent with a monotonic increase in SiC particle content across the panel sequence, and this parallels the monotonic rise in UCS — i.e., the two datasets are compatible with (not contradictory to) a picture in which higher SiCp loading increases both visible particle density and compressive strength.

What it leaves open: (1) panels b and c have no stated wt% in the captions given, so the exact content-to-panel correspondence for the full six-point UCS series cannot be confirmed from this evidence alone; (2) no quantitative particle-density or clustering metric is given, so "more particles visually" is not tied numerically to the UCS values; (3) no causal or mechanistic link (e.g., load transfer, grain refinement, porosity) between the observed clustering and the strength increase is shown — correlation of trends is all that is supported; (4) the increasing local clustering visible in d–f could equally be associated with defects (e.g., particle agglomeration reducing ductility) that the UCS trend alone would not reveal.

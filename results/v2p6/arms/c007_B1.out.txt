Both panels confirm the tabulated data: F4a (Apparent porosity, panel a) reproduces S1 8.7→0.4%, S2 7.9→~0.7%, S3 11.2→7.7% across 1400–1700 °C; F6a (Flexural strength σ, panel a) reproduces S1 rising 165→188→465 MPa, S3 staying flat ~170–195 MPa, S2 low and non-monotonic (~80, 98, 170, 145 MPa) over the same range.

Combined with the settled SEM finding (fracture surface showing residual cavities, consistent with nonzero apparent porosity but not matched to a specific sample/temperature or quantified), the evidence supports a general porosity-strength correspondence for two of three samples: S1's steep porosity drop pairs with its steep strength rise, and S3's shallow porosity drop pairs with its flat, unchanged strength — an inverse relationship consistent with densification driving mechanical improvement.

This account is incomplete for S2: despite porosity falling to the lowest values of the three series (~0.7% by 1650–1700 °C), flexural strength is the lowest overall and even declines from 1650 to 1700 °C (170→145 MPa) while porosity stays essentially flat. Porosity and the qualitative SEM snapshot therefore do not explain S2's strength behavior, nor do they explain why S3 resists densification. Some factor beyond porosity (e.g., grain size, phase, microcracking) must govern S2's strength, and the SEM record offers no mechanism for it.

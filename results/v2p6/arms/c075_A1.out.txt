Both panels confirm the numeric summary. F5d shows Rct rising sharply from ~3 to ~17-20 ohm by cycle ~50-60, continuing to climb (with widening error bars) to a plateau of ~24-28 ohm by cycle 150-250, while Rfilm (~2-4 ohm) and Re (~0.5 ohm) stay essentially flat throughout — so the growing impedance is specifically a charge-transfer phenomenon, not electrolyte or surface-film resistance. F2a (w/HCl, red circles) shows discharge capacity dropping steeply from ~90 to ~60 mAh/g over the first ~20-50 cycles, then declining slowly to ~55 mAh/g by cycle 250, with coulombic efficiency pinned near 100% throughout.

The combination supports temporal coincidence: the Rct rise and the capacity fade both occur predominantly in the same early window (first ~50-60 cycles) and both plateau/slow thereafter, consistent with rising charge-transfer resistance being associated with the fading capacity in this w/HCl sample. High, stable CE argues the fade is not from gross side reactions or electrolyte decomposition, aligning with the flat Re/Rfilm.

What this leaves open: EIS alone cannot establish causation, only correlation in cycle number — the panels don't isolate MEC/pathway mechanism or show that Rct rise mechanistically drives (rather than merely accompanies) the capacity loss. Large Rct error bars at later cycles weaken confidence in the plateau value. No panel here directly addresses PRF/service_capability.

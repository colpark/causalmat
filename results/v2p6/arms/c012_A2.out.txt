Both panels confirm the stated numbers. F5c: x=0.01 (red) has the highest power factor across the entire 300-623 K range, from ~29 down to ~22 µW cm⁻¹K⁻²; x=0.02 and x=0.04 track together in the middle; x=0.005 (black) is the lowest of the doped samples, falling to ~13 at 623 K; x=0 (green) is far below all doped samples, never exceeding ~5. F7c: at Th=623 K, x=0.01 (red) reaches the highest efficiency (~11.8%), while x=0.02 and x=0.005 sit around 10.5-9.5%, and x=0.04 is lowest (~9.5%) — matching the stated A and B values.

The combination supports a directional, qualitative link: the sample with the largest power factor across the whole temperature range (x=0.01) is also the sample with the highest maximal conversion efficiency at 623 K, so the PF ranking and the efficiency ranking at the top end are consistent with each other. This is compatible with PF being a contributor to the efficiency advantage.

What it leaves open: efficiency (eta) is a device-level quantity that in these models also depends on thermal conductivity (and hence ZT) integrated over the full Th range, none of which is shown in either panel — so PF alone cannot be shown to determine or fully account for the efficiency ordering, only to be directionally consistent with it at 623 K. The mid-range dopants (x=0.005, x=0.02) do not preserve a clean rank correspondence between PF and eta (x=0.005 has the lowest PF among doped samples at 623 K but is not the lowest in efficiency), so a strict one-to-one PF→eta mapping is not supported by these two panels alone.

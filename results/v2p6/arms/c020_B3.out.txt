Yes, for the four samples common to both stages, the upstream charge-separation ranking is reproduced downstream. F8b's steady-state H2 rates (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS 125.4 > Pd-ZIS 34.5 > Ag-ZIS 3.2 > ZIS 1.5 umol/h) and F8a's cumulative H2 time-course curves (same ordering, sustained over 5 h) place the samples in exactly the order photocurrent (F10a, matching Observation A) and PL quenching already established. What it leaves open: this is correlation of rankings, not a causal demonstration — nothing here isolates charge separation as the cause of higher H2 yield versus confounds like differing light absorption, surface active-site density, or Pd/Ag redox chemistry. F8a/F8b also test two AgxPd1-x compositions (0.5, 0.75) never measured by photocurrent or PL, so the upstream evidence covers only 4 of the 6 samples in the downstream comparison — the extension to those two intermediate compositions is unsupported by the cited upstream measurements.

Both panels are consistent with the stated claims. Panel A (GATR-FTIR) shows the red NO-PPAm trace carrying starred peaks for NO vibration (~1900 cm-1), N=N stretch, N-N stretch, N-O stretch, and a second N-N stretch (~950 cm-1) that are simply absent from the blue PPAm trace, which only shows -OH, -NH stretch, C-H stretch, and -NH vibration bands. Panel B shows NO flux rising sharply to a peak of ~35×10-10 mol cm-2 min-1 around 5 min, then decaying toward baseline by ~60 min, matching claim B's burst-then-decay description.

Together, the two panels support a coherent story: new chemical bonds (N-N/N-O/NO) formed only upon NO loading (A), and that loaded material releases NO in a burst that decays over an hour (B). This is consistent with NO being chemically stored (as an N-diazeniumdiolate-like adduct) on the amine-bearing PPAm and then released via hydrolysis/decomposition. What it leaves open: the FTIR alone does not prove the new bonds are the actual NO-donor species responsible for the release kinetics — no time-resolved FTIR, no quantitative bonding-to-flux correlation, and no control (e.g., partial loading or intermediate time points) linking bond loss to flux decay is shown. So causal linkage (bonding change directly drives the release profile) is plausible but not demonstrated by these panels alone.

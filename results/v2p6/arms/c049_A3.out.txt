The panels are consistent with the claim but do not fully establish it as causal.

F3a shows bare BST particles: smooth-surfaced, sub-micron spheres (~70 nm scale claimed, though the 500 nm bar makes exact sizing hard to verify by eye). F3b (BST-SH) and F3e (BST@Ag5%) both show visibly rougher, more agglomerated, larger-appearing particle surfaces than F3a — qualitatively supporting statement A that modification (silane coupling, then Ag decoration) roughens and enlarges the particle surface. F7b confirms statement B exactly: breaking elongation falls monotonically from 6.9% (pure PAEN) to 5.8%, 4.5%, 4.4% as Ag loading rises from 1% to 5%.

Together, the SEM series and the elongation bar chart support a coherent narrative: increasing surface modification/Ag content correlates with rougher, more agglomerated particle morphology and with progressively lower composite ductility. This is consistent with a filler-agglomeration/adhesion story (rougher, coarser particles disrupting matrix continuity, reducing elongation).

What is left open: the SEM images are morphology at the particle level, not the distributed composite/matrix cross-section, so no direct micrograph link (e.g., particle dispersion in the polymer matrix, interfacial voids, or agglomerate size within the film) is shown here to mechanistically connect particle roughness to the tensile failure mode. No statistics, error propagation, or comparison to BST-SH-only composite elongation (versus Ag-loaded) are given in these panels. Causation (that roughness/agglomeration specifically drives the elongation drop, rather than Ag content, filler stiffness, or interfacial chemistry independently) is not demonstrated — the panels show correlated trends, not a mechanistic bridge.

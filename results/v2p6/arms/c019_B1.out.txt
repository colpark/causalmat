F3 (polarization curves + table) shows Mg-Zn-Y-Nd with Ecorr ≈ -1.49 V and Icorr = 24.93 µA/cm², versus 317L SS at Ecorr ≈ -0.25 V and Icorr = 0.017 µA/cm² (a ~3-decade gap, matching claim A). F5a shows pH climbing from 7.4 to a plateau near 10 for Mg-Zn-Y-Nd, while 317L SS falls to a minimum near 6.1 around day 20 before partially recovering, and blank control peaks ~8.4 then drifts down to ~7.5-7.6 (matching claim B).

The upstream evidence (polarization + immersion corrosion-rate convergence, already established as supporting faster, method-independent degradation of Mg-Zn-Y-Nd over 317L SS) is directionally consistent with, and helps explain, the downstream pH divergence: a material corroding faster and more actively (lower corrosion potential, higher current density, higher immersion rate) is expected to alter local solution chemistry more, and Mg alloy corrosion characteristically releases hydroxide/consumes protons, which is consistent with the sustained alkalization seen for Mg-Zn-Y-Nd. So the pairing is coherent with, and lends qualitative plausibility to, the pH result as a downstream consequence of the same underlying corrosion-rate difference.

What it leaves open: none of the upstream data establish the chemical/electrochemical mechanism (e.g., hydroxide release, ion exchange) that would causally link corrosion rate to pH change, nor do they quantitatively predict the pH magnitude, timing, or plateau level from the corrosion-potential or current-density gap. The SS acidification to pH 6.1 by day 20 is not explained by anything in the polarization/immersion evidence, which only characterizes SS as slow-corroding, not as a source of acidification — that trend is a separate observation the upstream data do not account for. Matched test conditions between the electrochemical, immersion, and pH measurements are also not established, so the concordance across all three cannot be assumed to reflect identical media/conditions.

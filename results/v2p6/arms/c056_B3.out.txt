Both panels viewed directly.

F2d (high mag): confirms the described damage features — small holes (red arrows) and microcracks (red boxes) sit specifically at lamella ends and edges/junctions between adjacent lamellar colonies, with one hole circled in blue amid the lamellar terraces.

F2c (low mag, 100 µm scale): shows the broader fracture surface organized into distinct lamellar colonies separated by tear ridges, with elongated grooves running along the lamellae and several scattered dark holes distributed across multiple colonies, not confined to one region.

Combined with the settled TEM progression (mottled contrast → tangled dislocations → dense dislocation walls), this pair of SEM images supports the conclusion that damage (holes, microcracks) is a recurring, microstructure-organized feature: it consistently appears at lamella boundaries/ends across the fracture surface, and the low-mag view shows this is a general pattern (multiple colonies, not an isolated artifact) rather than a one-off. This is consistent with — but does not newly establish beyond — the earlier finding that dislocation accumulation at phase/lamella boundaries co-occurs with where damage appears.

What remains open: F2c/F2d still are not co-registered with the TEM dislocation images, so no specific dislocation-wall density is linked to a specific crack/void site. The low-mag panel adds spatial breadth (damage recurs at lamellar boundaries across the whole fracture surface) but does not distinguish whether dislocation buildup drives nucleation versus the lamella-end/phase-boundary geometry itself being the preferred nucleation site — that mechanistic question is still unresolved.

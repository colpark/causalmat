The upstream EDS/zeta evidence and the downstream blood-biomarker panels answer different questions, so the upstream evidence does not "account for" the downstream observation in a causal or explanatory sense — it does not predict or mechanistically produce normal ALT/AST/BUN/CREA values. What the combination supports is only identity/continuity: the material shown by EDS to carry P sparsely inside the C/Zn/N particle outline (consistent with aptamer adsorption onto BSA@ZIF-8) is the same aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 formulation later administered intravenously (F7b) or subcutaneously (F7c), and in both routes, liver (ALT, AST) and kidney (BUN, CREA) values at 3-14 days fall within the printed reference ranges, with the caption additionally reporting no histopathology on H&E.

This leaves open several things: (1) the EDS/zeta characterization was performed on the synthesized particles, not on material recovered from the treated animals, so there is no direct evidence the coating/composition was unchanged in vivo; (2) normal blood chemistry indicates absence of gross hepatic/renal toxicity, not confirmation of the electrostatic-adsorption mechanism, particle biodistribution, or degradation fate; (3) the earlier qualifications on the EDS/zeta result (sparse, non-uniform P signal; bulk-average charge; electrostatic vs. covalent ambiguity; no coverage quantification) still stand and are untouched by the biomarker data.

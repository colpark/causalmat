Both panels reproduce the cited numbers exactly. F7b shows peak voltage climbing stepwise (7.5, 12.5, 26.5, 32.0, 43.0, 62.0 V) as PZT content rises from 0 to 10 wt% for fiber-based PENGs at fixed 220 μm thickness — a clean, monotonic composition–output trend. F7e shows power vs. load resistance on a log scale: fiber film peaks at 136.9 μW near 10 MΩ before falling off at higher loads, while cast film peaks much lower at 21.025 μW over a similar range.

Together these support the claim that PZT loading and fiber (vs. cast) processing raise piezoelectric electrical output — both voltage magnitude and peak power at an optimal external load — i.e., they justify a PRF/service_capability claim about improved energy-harvesting output relative to composition and structuring, and a PRP/value claim about the specific voltage and power magnitudes reported.

What the evidence leaves open: it says nothing about mechanism (why fiber structuring outperforms cast at matched resistance), about durability/cycling stability, energy-conversion efficiency, or whether this power level is sufficient for any real downstream device (e.g., lighting an LED, charging a capacitor). Those service-capability claims would need separate data not shown in these two panels.

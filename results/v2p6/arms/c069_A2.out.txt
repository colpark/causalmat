The panels are consistent with the ELISA numbers: F5d/F5e show total fibrinogen and gamma-chain both dropping roughly by half on BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF versus bare 316LSS/PPAmF, with a single significance bracket (***) spanning the two control bars vs the three functionalized bars, but no bracket distinguishing BVLD/NO-PPAmF from the two single-agent coatings — error bars for the three functionalized bars overlap. So the upstream evidence supports that all three functionalized coatings similarly suppress fibrinogen adsorption/activation relative to bare surfaces, and it is consistent with BVLD/NO not being significantly different (per-mm value/measured level, not "lowest") from the single agents.

That accounts for part of the downstream picture — the gross reduction in fibrinogen deposition on all three coatings versus controls, matching the dark-thrombus-filled tubes/foils (316LSS, PPAmF) versus more open lumens (BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF). It does not account for the further gradient seen in F6b/F6c, where BVLD/NO-PPAmF alone shows an essentially clean lumen and clean foil while the single-agent coatings are only partly open. Since the fibrinogen assay shows no statistical edge for the dual coating, the additional macroscopic improvement for BVLD/NO-PPAmF is left unexplained by this evidence — it would require another mechanism (e.g., platelet activation, synergistic dual release) not measured in these panels.

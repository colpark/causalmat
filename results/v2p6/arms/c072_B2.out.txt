Figure 2a plots discharge capacity and coulombic efficiency vs. cycle number for the three conditions across ~250 cycles: co-precipitation w/ HCl (red) starts near 91 mAh/g and settles near 55 mAh/g; co-precipitation w/o HCl (blue) starts near 81, settles near 40; simple mixing (black) starts near 61, settles near 30. The ranking w/ HCl > w/o HCl > simple mixing holds at every cycle shown, and coulombic efficiency stays near 100% for all three throughout.

This panel is itself the source of measurements A and B, so it confirms the initial-capacity ranking and extends it: the same ordering persists across the full cycling window, addressing the earlier caveat that the GCD numbers were only initial-cycle — retention behavior tracks the same rank order, not just the starting values. Coulombic efficiency data add that none of the three conditions shows gross irreversibility.

What it still leaves open, unchanged from the earlier assessment: the panel gives no structural (XRD) information, so it cannot resolve why w/ HCl outperforms w/o HCl, since XRD only distinguished simple-mixing from co-precipitated samples, not the two co-precipitated variants. And because synthesis method still covaries with composition/morphology as well as crystallinity, this cycling data cannot by itself establish that structural order (rather than some other synthesis-linked property) causes the capacity differences — it remains a correlation across conditions, now with more cycles, not a mechanism test.

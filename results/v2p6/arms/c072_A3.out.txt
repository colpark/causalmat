Figure 2a shows three discharge-capacity vs. cycle-number curves (with coulombic efficiency on the right axis) for three synthesis routes: co-precipitation w/ HCl (red), co-precipitation w/o HCl (blue), and simple mixing w/o HCl (black). Visually, the red (w/ HCl) trace starts highest at ~90 mAh/g, the blue (w/o HCl) trace starts near ~80 mAh/g, and the black (simple mixing) trace starts near ~60 mAh/g, with this same rank order (red > blue > black) preserved across all 250 cycles shown. Coulombic efficiency for all three stays near 95-100% throughout.

This directly supports both downstream measurements: (A) the ranking w/ HCl > w/o HCl > simple mixing at every cycle, with initial values approximately matching ~91/~81/~61 mAh/g, and (B) the first-discharge capacity of the w/ HCl cathode at roughly 91 mAh/g. The single panel is sufficient on its own; the plot is a direct, quantitative read of the claimed values and ordering — no inference or combination of separate panels was needed.

What it leaves open: the figure shows only the electrochemical outcome, not the causal/mechanistic link to PRP (upstream physical/structural properties) or PRF (upstream service capability, e.g., surface area, particle morphology, purity, or cation ordering) that would explain why the HCl-treated co-precipitation route outperforms the others. No structural characterization (XRD, SEM, particle size, or composition data) is shown in this panel to establish that the synthesis-route difference operates through a specific upstream property; the panel only documents the downstream electrochemical rank and the first-cycle value, not the mechanism connecting synthesis conditions to capacity.

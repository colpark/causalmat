The three panels are consistent with, and partially support, the claim but do not fully establish it.

F4c (EDS map) shows Mg particles (green) in the C10 alloy with C (blue) and Co (red) concentrated at particle edges and on some faces — matching observation A exactly (surface, not bulk, distribution).

F6a (C0, no catalyst) and F6b (C10, C@Co-doped) show the kinetics in observation B: C0's first hydrogenation cycle is markedly slower than cycles 2–3 (reaching ~3.2 wt% at 180 min vs. near-saturation for later cycles, with a 66 min gap marked), while C10's first cycle tracks cycles 2–3 closely and saturates within ~5–11 min (11 min gap marked), essentially eliminating the "activation" lag seen in C0.

Combination: the surface-localized C/Co coating (F4c) is a plausible physical basis for why C10 needs no activation step — a catalyst layer already exposed at the particle surface could bypass whatever barrier (oxide, need for crack nucleation) slows C0's first cycle. This is a reasonable structure-to-kinetics correlation.

What it leaves open: no EDS map of C0 is shown for comparison, so the panels cannot confirm the coating's presence is the cause rather than merely coincident; no mechanism (e.g., crack initiation, hydrogen dissociation catalysis) is demonstrated; and coverage extent/uniformity beyond this single field of view is unquantified.

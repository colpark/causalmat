Panels checked: F5b (EDS spectrum) shows Ti, O, a dominant C peak, and small Er (~1.4 keV) and Ce (~4.8 keV) peaks, confirming observation A. F7 (PL) shows the 390-420 nm band intensity ordered TiO2 > Er0.5Ti-O > Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O, confirming observation B — progressive PL quenching as Er, then Er+Ce, are added.

Does the upstream evidence account for the PL/composition trend? Partially. The established EDS+XPS result (Er present as Er3+ bonded within a Ti-O oxide framework, not just an unconfirmed elemental peak) supplies the compositional precondition needed to read the PL series as a doping effect: it is Er3+ (a real, chemically bonded species) being added to TiO2 that correlates with the PL drop, not merely an artifact peak. So the combination supports attributing the TiO2→Er0.5Ti-O quenching to genuine Er incorporation as a chemical species in the oxide.

What it leaves open: (1) Ce's oxidation state was never established (XPS covered only Ti, O, Er), so the further quenching step to Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O cannot be tied to a specific Ce valence or mechanism. (2) Neither EDS nor XPS establishes lattice incorporation versus surface adsorption, so whether the PL change reflects bulk doping or surface effects is undetermined. (3) Neither technique explains the physical mechanism of quenching (e.g., non-radiative trap states, energy transfer) — that is outside what composition/oxidation-state data can show. (4) The small size of the Er/Ce EDS peaks still limits confidence in the elemental-presence side of this chain.

F4a (apparent porosity, S1/S2/S3 vs. temperature) shows porosity falling with temperature in all three series; F5 (Vickers hardness) shows hardness rising with temperature in all three series, over the same 1400-1700°C range. This upstream pair does support a within-series inverse association: as each sample densifies (porosity drops), its hardness climbs — consistent with a densification-hardness relationship, and consistent with the earlier SEM finding that residual porosity is real (dark cavities present).

But the combination does not establish porosity as the cause of the hardness gain, or give a clean cross-sample ranking. At 1600°C, S2 has lower porosity (2.3%) than S3 (7.7%) yet lower hardness (10.8 vs 14.1 GPa) — so porosity level alone does not order hardness across samples, implying composition/phase or grain-microstructure differences between S1/S2/S3 also drive hardness and are not measured here. The SEM image (panel c) remains a single unlabeled snapshot, so it cannot be tied to any specific porosity or hardness value. Left open: the mechanism linking porosity to hardness, why S3 stays porous yet still hardens, and any causal (versus merely co-occurring with temperature) role for porosity.

The quantified platelet data (F5b, F5c) and the ex vivo blood-circulation photographs (F6b, F6c) extend the earlier static-assay findings toward a flow-based "in service" test. F5b/F5c show statistically significant (***) separation: 316LSS and PPAmF carry ~30-40×10² platelets/mm² at ~65-75% activation, while BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF form a distinct lower group (~5-15×10² platelets/mm², ~0-25% activation), with BVLD/NO-PPAmF lowest of all. F6b/F6c, taken from an arteriovenous-shunt circulation model, show grossly clot-covered, dark-red surfaces on 316LSS/PPAmF versus visibly cleaner, metallic-appearing surfaces on the BVLD- and NO-bearing coatings after 2h of actual blood flow. Together this justifies the conclusion that BVLD- and NO-modified surfaces resist platelet accumulation/activation and gross thrombus deposition both in static platelet assays and under flowing blood, i.e., the anti-thrombotic benefit is not an artifact of static incubation alone.

What remains uncertain: F6b/c are qualitative photographs, not matched quantitative thrombus measures, so no magnitude comparison to the F5 platelet counts can be drawn. The shunt model is an animal ex vivo circuit, not a clinical or long-term implant setting, so "in service" here means short-duration flow exposure only, not durability, fatigue, or endothelialization over time.

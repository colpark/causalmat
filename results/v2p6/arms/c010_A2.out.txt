The four panels together describe ion-release and functional behavior of Co-doped mesoporous bioactive glass (Co-MBG) scaffolds in simulated fluid, not true clinical "service" performance.

F5a shows SiO4^4- concentration rising over 7 days of soaking for all three scaffolds (0/2/5Co-MBG), with 0Co-MBG and 5Co-MBG climbing to ~190 mg/L by day 7 while 2Co-MBG plateaus near 80-100 mg/L after day 3 — evidence that the glass network degrades and releases silicate steadily, though the non-monotonic ranking by Co content (2Co lowest at day 7, despite being intermediate in dose) is unexplained and not obviously dose-dependent.

F9 (western blot) shows HIF-1α band intensity increasing from MBG to 2Co-MBG to 5Co-MBG, supporting that cobalt release activates hypoxia-mimicking HIF-1α signaling in BMSCs in a Co-dose-related pattern — but this is a single blot with no quantification, replicates, or loading-normalized statistics shown.

F11a/b (same image, two panels) show ampicillin release plateauing near 800 µg by ~24-48 h, and ampicillin-loaded scaffolds significantly reducing bacterial relative survival at days 3 and 7 (marked *) versus blank control and drug-free scaffolds, supporting sustained antibacterial activity.

Together this justifies: the scaffolds degrade, release Co2+/silicate, induce HIF-1α up-regulation, and release ampicillin with measurable antibacterial effect in vitro. It does not establish in vivo efficacy, mechanical performance under load, degradation-strength coupling, or long-term (beyond 7 days) service behavior.</message>

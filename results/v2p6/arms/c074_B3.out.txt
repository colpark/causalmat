The combination supports only a loose, indirect rationale, not a demonstrated causal link. F1a (DFT) shows CoFeCrOOH sitting between CoCrOOH (lowest ΔG for Co2+→Co3+) and CoFeOOH (lowest ΔG for Co3+→Co4+) — i.e., the ternary hydroxide avoids the worst value on either individual Co-oxidation step, which is at best a plausible mechanistic reason a Fe/Cr co-doped Co material might perform well overall. F5c (LSV) then shows the ternary Co5Fe3Cr2 reaching j=10 mA/cm2 first (η10=452 mV), ahead of the binary Co5Fe5/Co5Cr5, pure Co, and IrO2. Read together with the already-established DFT/EELS pairing, this is consistent with "favorable Co oxidation → good OER activity," but it does not establish that link: the DFT compositions (CoCrOOH, CoFeOOH, CoFeCrOOH, Co(OH)2) are not the same materials as the LSV set (Co5Fe3Cr2, Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5, Co, IrO2), so there is no confirmed compositional correspondence between the modeled ternary and the tested one. Also, ΔG(Co2+→Co3+/Co3+→Co4+) is a Co-oxidation-state descriptor, not a computed OER overpotential (no O-intermediate energetics), so a favorable oxidation trend does not by itself predict the measured η10 ranking, including why Co5Fe3Cr2 beats CoFeOOH-family or IrO2. The activity result stands on its own experimental footing, not as a confirmed consequence of the DFT step.

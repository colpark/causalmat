The upstream evidence (PRP platelet adhesion/count plus SEM morphology, established earlier: fewest, least-spread platelets on BVLD/NO-PPAmF, intermediate on BVLD-PPAmF/NO-PPAmF, most/spread-dendritic on 316LSS/PPAmF) tracks the same ordering seen downstream in whole-blood circulation. F6b (cross-sections) and F6c (thrombus photos) both show 316LSS and PPAmF tubes/foils filled with dark, occlusive thrombus, BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF only partly filled, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF nearly clean lumen and foil. F5a's SEM confirms the same surface ranking for platelet density and spreading.

This combination supports a coherent, monotonic correspondence: surfaces that suppress platelet adhesion/activation in the static PRP assay are the same surfaces that show the least macroscopic thrombus under dynamic blood circulation, consistent with platelet suppression contributing to reduced thrombogenicity. It does not establish that the PRP-scale platelet response causes or quantitatively predicts the circulation-scale thrombus outcome — the two assays differ in conditions (static incubation vs. flowing whole blood), no statistics or thrombus quantification are given, and other blood components (coagulation cascade, fibrin, leukocytes) that could independently drive thrombus formation are not measured, so the mechanistic link from platelet count/shape to thrombus mass remains inferred, not demonstrated.

Panels confirm: F5b (EDS spectrum) matches Observation A exactly — Ti, O, dominant C, small Er (~1.4 keV) and Ce (~4.8 keV) peaks. F10c/F10d are dark/light antibacterial-ratio bar charts matching Observation B's dark-condition trend (Control ~0-7%, TiO2 ~6%, Ce0.1 ~28-33%, Ce0.2 ~43%, Ce0.3 ~45-47%, all with significance brackets).

The upstream EDS+XPS combination establishes that these are Ti-O oxide particles containing Er as a confirmed Er3+ species, with elemental Ce also detected (small peak, oxidation state unconfirmed). This supports identifying the antibacterial test materials as compositionally the named Er,Ce-doped Ti-O series, and is consistent with the Ce0.1→0.3 dose series producing a graded antibacterial response in a Ti-O matrix.

It does not account for the mechanism behind that grading. Ce's oxidation state (Ce3+/Ce4+ redox cycling, the usual basis for ceria's antibacterial/ROS activity) is unestablished, so whether increasing Ce content changes Ce chemistry or just Ce quantity is open. Whether Er/Ce sit in the TiO2 lattice or are surface-adsorbed is also unresolved, which matters for how they could drive bacterial killing. The unexplained dominant C peak and the weak Er/Ce EDS signals further limit how much compositional confidence can be placed under the antibacterial trend. So the upstream evidence supports material identity/composition but leaves the chemical mechanism for the observed dose-response antibacterial ratio unaddressed.

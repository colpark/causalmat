An earlier step in this work already established the following, and you may treat it as settled:

Taken together, the SEM morphology (flat/sparse-platelet zinc on CnC HS vs a dense mat of sharp platelets on C HS) and the galvanostatic plateau behavior (near-coincident plating potentials at the lowest rate but a strong split, about -0.05 V vs -0.10 V, at the highest rate) are mutually consistent with a rate-dependent divergence: at low current density both hosts plate with similar overpotential regardless of host structure, while at high current density the hosts' effects on deposit morphology (planar/compact on CnC HS vs dendritic platelet mat on C HS) track the emergence of a large overpotential gap between hosts.

The qualifications recorded on that earlier result:
  - The SEM images (F3d/F3e) capture deposit morphology at whatever fixed condition was imaged, not across the current-density series used in the galvanostatic plateau comparison (F4c), so the panels do not show that the platelet-mat morphology specifically corresponds to the highest-rate plateau where the -0.05 V vs -0.10 V gap appears
  - The plateau separation is a voltage measured across a range of current densities (a rate series), while the SEM comparison is a single structural snapshot per host, so the two are different kinds of quantities and cannot be directly overlaid to prove a shared condition
  - The plateau gap as read from F4c reflects combined nucleation and plating overpotential, while the SEM shows only the resulting deposit shape; the panels given do not decompose which stage of deposition (nucleation vs growth) the morphology difference corresponds to

F2d (high-mag) shows arrowed small holes and boxed microcracks concentrated at the ends/edges of lamellar colonies; F2c (low-mag) shows the same fracture surface at broader scale, with tear ridges and elongated grooves that trace the lamellar colony boundaries and scattered dark holes distributed across those grooves. Together these two SEM panels are internally consistent and mutually reinforcing at the level of a single fracture surface: the low-magnification view establishes that damage features (holes) and topographic features (ridges/grooves) both track the lamellar colony architecture, and the high-magnification view resolves that the holes/microcracks sit specifically at lamella ends and edges — i.e., at the boundaries between lamellae/colonies rather than within lamella interiors.

This upstream SEM pairing supports the downstream claim that fracture-surface damage (voids, microcracks) is spatially associated with lamella/phase-boundary geometry, and it is consistent with (does not contradict) the separately established TEM dislocation-accumulation progression, since both point to boundary regions as the relevant sites.

What it leaves open: F2c/F2d alone cannot show that dislocation content at these same sites was elevated — no TEM was done on these exact locations, so co-registration with the microstructural (dislocation) evidence is still absent. The SEM evidence establishes damage-location/geometry correlation only, not a causal or even a demonstrated coincidence between dislocation density and specific crack/void nucleation sites. Whether boundary geometry alone, or dislocation pileup at boundaries, or both together drive nucleation remains undetermined by this evidence.

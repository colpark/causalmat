The panels: F3a is bare BST (smooth, uniform, sub-100 nm grains), F3b is BST-SH, F3e is BST@Ag5% (visibly rougher, coarser, more agglomerated than F3a) — consistent with the earlier-established bare-vs-modified SEM contrast. F6a shows dielectric constant vs. frequency for pure PAEN and BST@Ag1/3/5%, with a clean monotonic ranking (5%>3%>1%>>PAEN, ~6.05 vs 3.35 at 100 Hz).

What the combination supports: the upstream finding (surface modification/roughening and apparent particle growth co-occurring with rising Ag content, per SEM plus the earlier photograph correlation) is directionally consistent with the downstream dielectric trend — composites containing the Ag-modified filler show substantially higher permittivity than the pure polymer, and permittivity rises as Ag loading (and, per the earlier step, coating/roughening) increases. This supports that the filler modification tracked in SEM is present in, and co-varies with, the same materials whose dielectric response is measured.

What it leaves open: SEM only contrasts bare vs. one modified state (not a three-point series matching 1%/3%/5%), so no graded microstructural evidence directly parallels the three dielectric data points. Because Ag content, particle size, and coating thickness rise together by construction, the dielectric increase cannot be attributed specifically to Ag coating (e.g., interfacial polarization) versus particle-size or dispersion effects — the mechanism remains inferred from co-occurring trends, not demonstrated.

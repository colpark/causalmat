The upstream evidence partially, not fully, accounts for the downstream STR/chemistry-composition/PRP/value observation.

What's supported: The EDS spectrum (F5b) confirms Ti, O, and small Er/Ce peaks are present in the particles, and the prior XPS pairing establishes Er is chemically bound as Er3+ in a Ti-O framework. Panels F10c/F10d confirm the dark antibacterial-ratio trend as stated (Ce0.1 ~28-33% < Ce0.2 ~43% < Ce0.3 ~45-47%, TiO2 ~6%, control ~0), and since dark conditions exclude photocatalytic (light-driven TiO2) activity, this pattern is consistent with Ce content contributing to an intrinsic, non-photoactivated antibacterial effect that scales with nominal Ce loading.

What's left open: The EDS spectrum shown is a single representative spectrum, not quantitative data across the three Ce-loading samples, so it cannot itself verify that measured Ce content actually increases from 0.1→0.3 — the dose-response trend rests on nominal synthesis stoichiometry, not on EDS quantification. Per the earlier established qualifications, Ce's oxidation state (Ce3+/Ce4+) is unconfirmed (no XPS Ce data), so no redox mechanism for the antibacterial trend can be attributed to Ce chemistry. Whether Ce is lattice-incorporated or surface-adsorbed, and what the dominant C peak reflects, also remain unresolved, and the small size of the Er/Ce EDS peaks limits confidence in elemental-presence claims generally.

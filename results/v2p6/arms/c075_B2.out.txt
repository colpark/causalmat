Panels confirm the stated data. F5d: Rct rises steeply to ~cycle 150 (~3→~26 ohm) then plateaus at ~24-28 ohm with wide error bars; Rfilm (~2-3 ohm) and Re (~0.5 ohm) stay flat throughout. F2a (w/HCl, red curve): discharge capacity falls steeply over roughly the first 20-50 cycles, then declines slowly out to 250 cycles (to ~55 mAh/g), with coulombic efficiency near 100% throughout.

What this combination supports: the steep early phase of capacity fade (~cycles 0-50) temporally overlaps the steep early rise in Rct, consistent with growing interfacial charge-transfer resistance contributing to the early capacity loss. The flat Rfilm/Re argue against film growth or electrolyte-resistance changes as drivers of either the early Rct rise or the early capacity drop.

What it leaves open: Rct plateaus by ~cycle 150, but capacity keeps declining slowly all the way to cycle 250 — this later, slower fade is not tracked by any further rise in Rct, so the EIS data cannot account for the full capacity trajectory shown in F2a. High, stable CE argues against a major continuous parasitic side reaction but doesn't identify the cause of the slow tail. Neither panel here re-tests or confirms the previously established Rct/V-dissolution correlation (that relied on separate ICP-OES data), so this pair is silent on whether V dissolution underlies the capacity fade specifically.

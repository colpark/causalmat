F9 (XRD) shows t-ZrO2 marker peaks (~43 deg, split ~73-74 deg) present at both 5 and 10 cycles, with the 5-cycle pattern showing visibly higher marker intensity than the 10-cycle pattern in the insets. F8 (photographs) shows the coupon intact at 0/5 cycles, corner spallation appearing by 10 cycles, and spalled area persisting/growing modestly through 20, 30 and 40 cycles.

The combination supports a temporal association: the tetragonal-marked phase signature is already detectable by 5 cycles (before spallation is visible) and remains present at 10 cycles, the same cycle count at which spallation first appears. This is consistent with the phase-identity shift being an early, possibly contributing, change that precedes or co-occurs with the onset of coating degradation (service capability loss via corner spallation).

It leaves open several things: (1) whether the amount of phase transformed (fraction) tracks the amount of spallation — no fraction data exist, and spallation keeps growing through 40 cycles while XRD is reported only to 10 cycles, so the phase trajectory during 10-40 cycles is unknown; (2) causation is not established, only co-occurrence; (3) YSZ's own possible phase shift under cycling is untested, so Al2O3-YSZ's degradation cannot be attributed solely to its phase change relative to YSZ.

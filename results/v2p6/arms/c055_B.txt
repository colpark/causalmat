The qualifications recorded on that earlier result:
  - SAXS alone only shows structural/volumetric stability; it excludes volume change as a cause but cannot by itself identify what does limit capacity.
  - AIMD alone gives a computed oxidation-degree ranking and an early plateau in a picosecond-scale simulation; on its own it says nothing about real cell volume behavior and does not rule out volume change.
  - The two measurements are different kinds of quantity: SAXS is an experimental scattering-intensity profile read at specific applied voltages (0.11 V, 0.02 V), while AIMD is a computed cumulative oxidation percentage from a picosecond-scale trajectory (2 ps snapshot, plateau after 0.8 ps) -- the simulated timescale is not the same kind of quantity as, nor directly comparable to, the electrochemical timescale over which real capacity fading occurs.
  - Only three AIMD systems (Li-Co3O4, Na-Co3S4, Na-Co3O4) are compared at a single 2 ps point, which supports a ranking, not a quantitative trend or rate.

The upstream PF result (resistivity-driven ranking, x=0.01 highest PF, x=0 lowest) only partially accounts for the downstream ZT observation. F5c confirms the PF ordering: x=0.01 (red) tops the curve at ~29 (300 K) to ~22 (623 K), x=0 (green) sits far below at ~2-5 throughout, matching statement A. But F6a shows ZT does not follow the PF ranking: x=0.005 (black), which has the lowest PF among doped samples, has the highest ZT at 300 K (~0.84), edging out x=0.01 (~0.78) despite x=0.01's clearly higher PF. Since ZT = PF·T/κ_total, this crossover means thermal conductivity (not shown here, no data given) must differ enough between x=0.005 and x=0.01 to overturn the PF ordering at low T. So the combination supports only that resistivity/PF differences are broadly consistent with the compositional trend in transport, and that x=0 is uniformly worst on both PF and ZT. It leaves open the mechanism linking PF to ZT quantitatively, the role of thermal conductivity (unmeasured here), and why x=0.005 outranks x=0.01 in ZT despite lower PF — that inversion is unexplained by the PF evidence alone.

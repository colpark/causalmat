Both panels confirm the stated captions/values. F3b (crop c495...B.jpg) shows RGO-Cu2S adsorbing more N2 than Cu2S across P/P0, reaching ~53 vs ~32 cm3/g near 1, with the inset pore-size distribution peaking near 20 nm for RGO-Cu2S. F5a (crop b6541...A.jpg) shows J-V curves with RGO-Cu2S highest (Jsc~16, Voc~0.555), Cu2S middle (~15.0, ~0.535), Pt lowest (~13.0, ~0.525).

The combination supports an association: the same RGO-Cu2S sample that shows a finer/thinner flake microstructure and greater N2-accessible porosity than bare Cu2S also shows higher Jsc and Voc than bare Cu2S in the J-V curves. This is consistent with, but does not prove, that the added porosity/surface area contributes to the improved photovoltaic output (e.g., via more active sites).

What it leaves open: (1) only two conditions (Cu2S vs RGO-Cu2S) have porosity data, so no porosity-performance trend can be drawn for Pt, which appears in F5a but not F3b; (2) as already noted, GO/RGO addition changes both flake morphology and introduces RGO's own conductive/catalytic surface, so the porosity increase and the Jsc/Voc gain cannot be separately attributed to morphology versus RGO's intrinsic properties; (3) no direct electrochemical/kinetic measurement (e.g., charge-transfer resistance) links the N2-sorption porosity number to the mechanism producing higher Jsc/Voc — the two panels are correlated but not mechanistically bridged; (4) with only two porosity conditions, no dose-response between porosity and PV metrics is established.

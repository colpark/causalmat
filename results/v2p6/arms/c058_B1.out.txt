Panels viewed: F5b (EDS spectrum, Ti/O peaks, dominant C peak, small Er ~1.4 keV and Ce ~4.8 keV peaks) and F7 (PL spectra, TiO2 > Er0.5Ti-O ≈ half > Er0.5Ce0.2 lowest, all peaking 390-420 nm).

The upstream evidence establishes that Er0.5Ti-O and Er0.5Ce0.2 samples do contain detectable Er and Ce alongside the Ti-O host (EDS), and that the detected Er is present as Er3+ associated with a Ti-O framework (EDS+XPS combination, per the earlier finding). This is consistent, qualitatively, with the downstream PL trend: progressive addition of dopant (Er, then Er+Ce) tracks with progressively lower 390-420 nm PL intensity, i.e., composition correlates with quenching of the TiO2 defect/oxygen-vacancy emission. So the combination supports a composition-PL relationship in direction (more dopant, more quenching) but not in mechanism or magnitude.

It leaves open: whether quenching arises from Er/Ce entering the TiO2 lattice (creating trap states) versus surface-adsorbed species, since neither EDS nor the earlier XPS pairing settles incorporation site; Ce's oxidation state, which could bear on its quenching efficiency, is unaddressed since XPS reported no Ce region; the small, non-quantitative EDS Er/Ce peaks cannot support a dose-response claim about how much dopant produces how much quenching; and the origin of the dominant C peak is unresolved, so a contamination-driven PL contribution cannot be ruled out.

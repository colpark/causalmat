The combination supports only a qualitative, plausibility-level link, not a demonstrated causal account of the JMAK kinetics.

What it supports: EDS (F4c) shows C (blue) and Co (red) sitting on particle surfaces, densest along edges and on some faces of the Mg-alloy grains (green). Combined with the earlier-established HRTEM corroboration (carbon layers/alloy fringes at one edge), this is consistent with C10 having a surface-distributed C/Co catalyst layer that C0 lacks. The JMAK plots (F9a/F9b) show linear ln[-ln(1-a)] vs ln t fits (R2>0.98) for both alloys across 280-360 C, with a lower Avrami exponent for C10 (eta=1.32) than C0 (eta=1.59). It is reasonable to read the eta difference as reflecting a changed desorption mechanism/dimensionality attributable to a surface-located catalytic phase, which the EDS/HRTEM evidence shows is physically present at particle surfaces.

What it leaves open: the EDS map is a coarse, single-field areal image, not a quantified or particle-representative measure of catalyst coverage, so it cannot establish that the distribution is uniform enough, or present in sufficient amount, to mechanistically produce the specific eta shift observed. Nothing in the panels ties surface C/Co presence causally to the exponent value, distinguishes it from other C10-vs-C0 differences, or shows Co in atomic contact with carbon. The mechanistic link between "surface catalyst present" and "eta = 1.32" remains inferred, not measured.

The micrographs (F1b-f) show dark SiCp particles spread broadly across each field with only local clustering, consistent across the composition series; visually, particle coverage looks denser in the higher-content panels (d/e/f, captioned 15/20/25 wt%) than in the lower ones (b/c), which is qualitatively compatible with more reinforcement being present at higher wt%. That is as far as this evidence goes toward the creep result: it supports that the composite at every composition, including 15-25 wt%, is reasonably well dispersed rather than grossly segregated, so the improved creep resistance seen at 15/20/25 wt% (falling below AZ91) is not obviously confounded by clustering artifacts in those samples specifically.

It does not account for the downstream creep pattern in any quantitative or mechanistic sense. There is no particle area fraction, spacing, or clustering index reported, so "denser at higher wt%" is only a visual impression, not measured. Nothing here explains why 5 and 10 wt% fail to improve (or slightly worsen) minimum creep rate while 15 wt% and above do — that threshold could reflect a volume-fraction/load-transfer effect, a change in dispersion quality below detection, or something unrelated to particle distribution entirely (e.g., grain boundary chemistry). No mechanistic or statistical link between dispersion state and creep rate is established.

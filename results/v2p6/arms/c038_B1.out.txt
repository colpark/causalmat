The panels are consistent with the claims and support extending the earlier "phase largely retained, no metallic Co" conclusion to microstructure at the SAXS length scale.

F4b shows the stacked XRD traces: the main peak near 19.3° 2θ (and the smaller one near 16°) persists across the whole 0.1C discharge/charge trace with only intensity fluctuation, no new peaks appearing or old ones vanishing — this is the SXRD basis for "reflections persist, modest intensity loss."

F3c (2D SAXS contour vs. time/voltage) shows vertically uniform color bands across both q-windows (0.006–0.08 and 0.1–0.4 Å⁻¹) through the full discharge/charge, i.e., no new low-q scattering feature emerging and growing with time/voltage — consistent with "contours stay nearly straight." F3d (I(q) vs. state) shows the OCV, 0.45V, 0.136V, 0.11V, 0.02V, and 3V curves essentially overlapping over the full q-range, with only a minor separation in the ~0.03–0.1 Å⁻¹ region at 0.11/0.02V.

Combined, SXRD (retained reflections) and SAXS (near-unchanging I(q)/contour) jointly support that sodiation does not generate a new, growing population of nanoscale pores, cracks, or phase-separated domains detectable by SAXS, reinforcing that the oxide framework stays largely intact at both crystallographic and microstructural scales. This leaves open: the physical origin of the modest XRD intensity loss and the small SAXS deviation at 0.11/0.02V (disorder, minor amorphization, or particle/porosity change), any quantitative estimate of porosity or void-fraction change, and whether sub-nanometer or below-SAXS-resolution structural changes occur. Neither technique alone, nor together, characterizes a specific porosity fraction or its evolution mechanism.

Panels confirm both measurements. F4c (EDS map of C10) shows Mg in green filling particle interiors, with C (blue) and Co (red) speckles concentrated along particle edges/boundaries and to a lesser, patchier extent on some interior faces — consistent with item A. F6a (C0, no catalyst) shows a slow first hydrogenation (~3.2 wt% at 180 min) while cycles 2-3 saturate near 5 wt% within minutes; F6b (C10, with C@Co) shows cycle 1 nearly overlapping cycles 2-3, all saturating within ~5-11 min — consistent with item B.

The combination supports a correlation: the C@Co catalyst sits preferentially on particle surfaces/edges of the alloy that shows no first-cycle activation penalty (C10), while the uncatalyzed alloy (C0) requires ~180 min for a slow first hydrogenation. This is consistent with a surface-catalysis story — catalyst positioned at particle boundaries could lower the barrier to initial hydrogen dissociation/diffusion, removing the need for an activation cycle.

It leaves open: no EDS map of C0 is shown for comparison, so the surface state of the uncatalyzed particles (oxide layer, roughness) is unknown and could also differ. The EDS map is static and spatial only — it does not measure reaction rate or demonstrate that edge-concentrated C/Co is mechanistically responsible for the kinetic change, only that catalyst is present at surfaces in the fast-activating sample. Mechanism (dissociation catalysis vs. particle refinement vs. diffusion pathways) is not established by these panels alone.

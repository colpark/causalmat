The upstream fibrinogen data support the general claim that all three surface functionalizations suppress fibrinogen adsorption/activation relative to bare 316LSS/PPAmF, which plausibly contributes to reduced thrombus formation downstream. But the upstream assay gives no basis for the downstream ranking: fibrinogen reduction is essentially equal (even slightly least favorable numerically) for the combined BVLD/NO surface, whereas the whole-blood thrombus photos show BVLD/NO-PPAmF as visibly the cleanest, with BVLD- and NO-PPAmF alone only partially patent. So the combination's superior macroscopic anti-thrombogenic performance is not accounted for by the fibrinogen-adsorption data—it must come from mechanisms outside this assay (e.g., combined effects on platelets/coagulation under flow, not measured here). The link is qualitative/directional only; the graded PRF-level outcome is left open.

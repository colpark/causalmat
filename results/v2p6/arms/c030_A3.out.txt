Figure 10a shows that along [0001] at RT this specimen deforms plastically (up to ~45% plastic strain) at a flow stress of only about 55-80 MPa, with serrations and a plateau near 70-80 MPa; Figure 10b confirms this strain is accommodated by dense kink-band formation rather than conventional slip. Figure 2, by contrast, reports the stress reached along [0001] at RT as ~350-360 MPa, plotted in Fig. 2a as a filled square explicitly labeled "fracture" (not yield) and shown in Fig. 2b as a vertical line to an X at ~355 MPa with essentially no plastic strain — i.e., near-elastic loading to brittle failure.

The comparison (~60-80 MPa deforming stress in Fig. 10 vs. ~350 MPa fracture stress in Fig. 2, same [0001] orientation, same temperature) shows roughly a 4-5x gap. This justifies the conclusion that [0001]-oriented loading in this alloy can follow two distinct routes: one in which the crystal remains essentially elastic up to a high stress and fails abruptly by fracture, and another in which kink-band formation nucleates at much lower stress and permits large plastic strain instead of immediate fracture. What the evidence leaves uncertain: why the two specimens diverge (e.g., differences in alignment, surface condition, or kink-band nucleation site) is not shown, nor is which route is more representative or what stress actually triggers kink-band onset.

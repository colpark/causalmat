Both panels confirm the stated observations. F5c shows PF vs T for x=0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04: x=0.01 (red circles) is the highest curve throughout, ~29 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² near 300 K declining to ~22 near 623 K; x=0 (green) is lowest, rising from ~2 to ~5; x=0.005 (black squares) sits below the other doped curves, roughly 23→13. F6a shows ZT vs T for the same series: x=0.01 again the top curve, peaking at ~1.25 in the 475-525 K range, with the other doped compositions (0.02, 0.04, 0.005) clustered slightly below and x=0 far below (~0.05-0.22).

The upstream PF evidence (A) does account for the downstream ZT observation (B) in the qualitative, ordinal sense: the same x=0.01 composition that has the largest power factor across the full temperature range is also the composition with the highest ZT, and its ZT peak temperature (~500-525 K) falls within the range where its PF, while past its own maximum, is still comparably elevated relative to the other curves. This is consistent with PF being a major contributor to ZT's composition ranking.

What it leaves open: ZT = S²σT/κ, and neither panel provides thermal conductivity data, so the combination cannot establish that PF alone drives the ZT peak's location or magnitude — a declining κ(T) could equally or more strongly determine why ZT maximizes near 500 K even as PF is falling from its own peak. The panels also cannot confirm causation, only co-occurrence of x=0.01 as the top performer in both metrics.

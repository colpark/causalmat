The combination partially accounts for the downstream Tg/value observation but does not fully explain it. SEM (F3a bare BST, ~70 nm smooth spheres; F3b silane-modified BST-SH, rougher/larger; F3e BST@Ag5%, further roughened/coated agglomerates) establishes that surface modification and Ag decoration measurably change particle morphology and presumably interfacial chemistry relative to bare filler. DSC (F5a) shows every composite (BST@Ag 1/3/5%) has a substantially depressed Tg (146, 149, 157 C) relative to pure PAEN (176 C), a ~20-30 C drop. Together these support the qualitative claim that introducing surface-modified/coated filler particles into the PAEN matrix disrupts chain packing or interfacial constraint enough to lower Tg versus the neat polymer — i.e., particle-level surface modification is consistent with, and can plausibly drive, the bulk thermal property shift.

What it leaves open: the SEM series is not graded across 1%, 3%, 5% Ag (only bare, silane-modified, and 5% are imaged), so there is no direct microstructural counterpart to the 146→149→157 C trend, including why Tg rises again from 1% to 5% rather than continuing to drop. SEM also images dry particles, not dispersion within the cured polymer matrix, so filler distribution/agglomeration inside the composite — plausibly relevant to the non-monotonic Tg trend — is not directly evidenced.

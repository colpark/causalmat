The upstream evidence (charge reversal plus P dots scattered within the C/Zn/N particle outline) only establishes that the aptamer is somewhere on/in the BSA@ZIF-8 particle, likely via electrostatic association, without showing a continuous shell, without proving the binding mechanism, and without quantifying how much aptamer is present. Panel F5e (viewed) confirms this: C, Zn, N fill both particle outlines while P is only sparse dots in both the DNA- and RNA-aptamer samples — composition data about the particle itself, nothing about function.

Panel F6e (viewed) shows a different, independent measurement: whole-organ ex vivo fluorescence, where the tumor image for the A10 RNA-aptamer MOF is visibly brighter (more green/yellow) than the tumor image for the neat BSA@ZIF-8 (bluer, dimmer), consistent with greater tumor-associated signal for the aptamer-bearing particle.

The combination partially accounts for the downstream observation: it confirms the aptamer is physically present on the particle used in the in vivo experiment, which is a necessary precondition for any aptamer-mediated targeting. But the upstream evidence says nothing about targeting function, receptor binding, circulation behavior, or biodistribution mechanism, and it cannot distinguish aptamer-specific recognition from other explanations for the brighter tumor signal (e.g., surface-charge or size effects on accumulation). Whether the enhanced tumor fluorescence is causally due to aptamer targeting, as opposed to incidental physicochemical differences, remains open.

Both panels are consistent with the prior FTIR+TGA finding and extend it in one respect while leaving the same core uncertainty open.

F2a confirms panel A: the BST-SH trace alone shows new absorptions at 2930, 2864 cm⁻¹ (C–H stretches of aliphatic CH2) and 1122 cm⁻¹ (Si–O-type band), absent from both BST and BST-OH traces — exactly matching the stated FTIR observation. F2b confirms panel B: BST-OH settles as a visible white sediment in the lower (aqueous) layer, while BST-SH stays dispersed/suspended in the upper (toluene) layer, a clear polarity/wettability contrast.

Combined, the evidence supports: BST-SH's surface bears new organic (alkyl) and Si-O-containing groups (per FTIR) not present on BST-OH, and this chemical difference correlates with a macroscopic property change — BST-SH is rendered organophilic/hydrophobic (partitions into toluene) while BST-OH remains hydrophilic (stays in water). This is a plausible mechanistic link between surface chemistry and phase-partitioning behavior (a proxy for compatibility with an organic matrix/PRP-type value).

What it leaves open: the panels show co-occurrence of "new surface species" and "changed dispersibility," not a demonstrated causal or quantitative mechanism. They do not establish covalent Si-O-BST bonding versus physisorbed silane; they give no measure of surface coverage/density of the modification; and the optical dispersion test is qualitative, not tied numerically to the FTIR bands or to any downstream mechanical/PRP value. So the figures support "surface modification correlates with a hydrophobicity/dispersibility shift" but not a quantified or mechanistically verified bonding-to-property claim.

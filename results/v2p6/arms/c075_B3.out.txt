The combination is consistent but does not close the causal loop. Rct (F5d) rises steeply in early cycling and then plateaus around cycle 150 (~24-28 ohm, large error bars), while capacity for the w/HCl cell (F2a, red trace) also falls steeply over the first ~20-50 cycles before settling into a slow decline to ~55 mAh/g by cycle 250, with CE near 100%. The shared "fast-then-slow" shape, together with the already-established Rct/V-dissolution timing match, supports a plausible pathway: V-site dissolution builds interfacial charge-transfer resistance, and that rising Rct plausibly contributes to the observed capacity fade (rising internal impedance limiting deliverable capacity is a standard, directionally consistent relationship).

What it leaves open: the two curves are not tightly aligned in time — Rct saturates by ~150 cycles while capacity keeps declining slowly all the way to 250, so Rct growth cannot account for the later-stage fade by itself. Near-100% CE argues fade is not from major irreversible side reactions each cycle, but that doesn't pin the mechanism to Rct/V either. No measurement here converts ohms of Rct into mAh/g lost, so this is a qualitative, plausibility-level link, not a demonstrated quantitative or causal one — consistent with, but not proof of, Rct (and upstream V dissolution) as a driver of the capacity fade.

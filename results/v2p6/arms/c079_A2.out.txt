The five panels (b–f, captions confirming d=15, e=20, f=25 wt% SiCp) show a consistent trend: dark SiCp particles are dispersed across the whole matrix field with local clustering (streaky/clumped regions rather than perfectly uniform dispersion), and as wt% increases from b through f, the areal density and clustering of dark particles visibly increases — panel f (25 wt%) is markedly more crowded and clustered than panel b. This directly corroborates observation A (particles spread with local clusters) and shows that clustering does not diminish but rather intensifies with loading.

This microstructural trend is consistent with the monotonic YS increase (93→142 MPa, 0–25 wt%): more reinforcement particles per unit area is compatible with greater load transfer/Orowan-type strengthening and any associated grain refinement, so the optical evidence does not contradict the tensile trend and offers a plausible qualitative accompaniment to it.

What it leaves open: the images alone cannot establish that particle content increase causally produced the YS rise, nor quantify grain size, porosity, or interfacial bonding, all of which also affect strength. The visible clustering (worsening at higher wt%) is exactly the kind of defect that could locally offset strengthening, yet YS still rises monotonically — so the panels cannot rule out competing effects, nor confirm the strengthening mechanism; they only establish that particle density/distribution tracks with wt% content, which is necessary but not sufficient to account for the measured YS values.

Both panels confirm the stated compositional ranking: in F5c (PF), x=0.01 is highest across all T (~29 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² at 300 K falling to ~22 at 623 K), x=0.005 is the lowest of the doped set, and x=0 sits far below all doped compositions (~2 rising to only ~5). In F6a (ZT), the same x=0.01 curve is again the highest of all compositions, peaking near ZT≈1.25-1.3 around 475-525 K, while x=0 is by far the lowest (ZT<0.25 throughout) and x=0.005 is the lowest of the doped curves.

So the upstream resistivity→PF account is consistent with, and extends cleanly to, the downstream PF→ZT ranking: the same composition (x=0.01) that has the lowest resistivity and highest PF also has the highest ZT, and the same low-resistivity/high-PF ordering across compositions reproduces the ZT ordering. This supports a coherent co-variation chain (resistivity → PF → ZT) across composition and temperature.

What it leaves open: ZT = PF·T/κ, and no thermal-conductivity (κ) values are given in either panel, so the PF evidence cannot account for the ZT peak's specific temperature location (~500 K) or magnitude — κ's temperature dependence is an unaddressed factor. This remains association/ranking consistency, not a demonstrated causal mechanism.

Combined, A (YSZ SEM crack network unchanged between the pre-cycle and 5-cycle views, F4b/F4c) and B (Al2O3-YSZ coupon photographs: intact through 5 cycles, corner spallation appearing by 10 and persisting/growing to 40, F8) support only a narrow, coincidental point: at the shared early time point (0–5 cycles), a stable, unchanged crack network in YSZ co-occurs with an intact, unspalled coupon in the photographic series. That is as far as the evidence carries.

It leaves open, and does not establish, any causal or mechanistic link between microstructure and downstream damage/service capability, for several reasons. First, A and B are different materials — the SEM comparison is YSZ, the photographic damage timeline is Al2O3-YSZ (the coarser-network material per the earlier settled result) — so there is no within-material bridge from crack stability to spallation. Second, the SEM stability check covers only 0–5 cycles, while spallation first appears at 10 cycles and continues through 40; no SEM imaging is shown at or beyond spallation onset, so the microstructural state at the point damage actually begins is undocumented. Third, F8 shows only external/geometric coupon damage (corner loss), not crack-network evolution, so nothing links the two modalities in the same specimen. The evidence therefore cannot support a claim that microstructural (crack) stability or change drives, predicts, or accounts for the spallation, damage progression, or resulting service capability observed in F8.

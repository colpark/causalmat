Fig. 10a (Mg99.2Zn0.2 single crystal, tension along [0001], RT) shows deformation proceeding at very low stress: the curve oscillates and plateaus in the ~55–80 MPa range, reaching ~45% plastic strain, with the deformed region (Fig. 10b) showing extensive kink-band formation. Fig. 2b, the RT stress–strain plot for [0001] loading ("step 2" comparison), shows a nearly vertical line rising to an X mark at ~350–355 MPa (matching the fracture point in Fig. 2a) with essentially no resolved plastic strain before fracture.

Comparison: the kink-band-mediated deformation in Fig. 10a occurs at a stress roughly 4–6x lower than the ~350 MPa fracture stress plotted for [0001] in Fig. 2b. This implies that loading along [0001] can follow two very different routes: one in which the crystal behaves near-elastically to a high stress and then fractures abruptly (Fig. 2b), and another in which extensive plastic strain accumulates via kinking at a much lower stress plateau (Fig. 10a). The evidence justifies that kinking is a viable, low-stress deformation mechanism along [0001] distinct from brittle fracture at high stress. It does not establish what determines which route a given specimen follows (e.g., buckling constraint, geometry, or loading alignment) — the captions give no basis for that.

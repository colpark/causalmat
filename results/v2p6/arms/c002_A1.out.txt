The combination supports a "same phase, added mass" reading: F3a shows the milled C10 pattern retains all four indexed reflections (1-CeMg12, 2-Y5Mg24, 3-Ce2Mg17, 4-Mg) seen in the C0 pattern, at the same positions, with only one weak added line (5, Co) near 44.5 deg. So identity/phase composition of the hydride-forming matrix is essentially unchanged by the milling/Co addition — no new bulk phase, no evident conversion of the existing phases. F7a/F7b confirm the stated plateau values: at 250 C the black curve (C0) saturates near 5.1 wt%, the C10 curve near 4.5-4.6 wt%, matching the reported ~5.1 vs ~4.56 wt%.

Together this justifies only a modest, negative claim: the ~0.5 wt% capacity drop is not attributable to a change in the identity of the hydrogen-storing phases (they're XRD-identical), so it is consistent with a simple dilution effect from the added 10 wt% Co (inert/catalytic, hydrogen-inactive mass lowering the overall storage fraction). What it leaves open: XRD is a bulk, qualitative phase check — it cannot quantify Co dispersion, particle size, or confirm dilution is the actual mechanism versus some kinetic/surface effect; nor does it address why C0 and C10 differ in absorption rate/shape at lower temperatures. Causation for the capacity gap remains inferred, not directly measured.

Both panels confirm the stated measurements. F5c (power factor vs T) shows x=0.01 clearly highest across the range (~29 at 300 K, ~22 at 623 K), x=0.005 lowest among doped compositions, and x=0 far below all doped samples (~2-5, only slightly rising with T). F6a (ZT vs T) shows x=0.01 peaking highest, near ZT~1.25 at ~500-525 K, again with x=0 lowest throughout (~0.05-0.2) and the other doped compositions clustered below x=0.01.

The combination supports a composition-level consistency: the same sample (x=0.01) that has the lowest resistivity and highest PF (established upstream) is also the highest-ZT sample, and x=0 is worst in both PF and ZT. This is coherent with PF being one factor feeding into ZT (ZT = PF·T/κ).

What it leaves open is the temperature dependence: PF for doped samples falls monotonically from 300 K onward, while ZT rises to a peak near 500-525 K before declining. That shape mismatch means PF (and hence resistivity) alone cannot explain ZT's temperature profile - thermal conductivity (κ), which is not given in either panel, must be driving the peak location and the high-T decline. So the evidence supports PF-ZT consistency in composition ranking only, not a mechanistic account of ZT's temperature dependence.

The two datasets overlap on only two samples: TiO2 and Er0.5Ce0.2. For those two, the direction agrees: TiO2 has the highest PL (most radiative recombination, F7) and the lowest degradation (~20% at 60 min, worst catalytic performance, F8); Er0.5Ce0.2 has the lowest PL (least recombination) and the highest degradation (~46% at 60 min, best performance). This is consistent with the expected mechanism that suppressed electron-hole recombination should support better photocatalytic activity, and it extends the earlier PL-EIS convergence by adding a third, functional (catalytic) measure that moves the same way for the same two samples.

What it leaves open: the PL series (TiO2, Er0.5Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.2) and the degradation series (TiO2, Er0.5Ce0.1, Er0.5Ce0.2, Er0.5Ce0.3) only share two members. Er0.5Ti-O's degradation was not measured, and Er0.5Ce0.1/Ce0.3's PL was not measured, so the relationship is untested across the intermediate Ce-doping levels. Notably degradation is non-monotonic with Ce content (0.2 beats 0.3), so even if lower recombination tracks higher activity at the endpoints, the evidence doesn't show whether PL would also be non-monotonic, or that recombination is the dominant driver rather than a correlate. As with the PL-EIS pairing, this is agreement between two proxies on two samples, not a demonstrated causal chain.</message>

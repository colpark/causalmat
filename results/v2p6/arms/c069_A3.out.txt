F5d/F5e (ELISA) show a clean two-group split: 316LSS and PPAmF sit near 0.10-0.15, all three functionalized surfaces (BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, BVLD/NO-PPAmF) drop to ~0.05-0.065 — roughly halved. But the *** brackets in both panels connect the pooled bare-metal group to the pooled functionalized group only; there is no pairwise statistical comparison among the three functionalized surfaces, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF's bar is nominally the highest of the three (with the widest error bar), not the lowest.

F6b/F6c photos show a finer gradient: 316LSS/PPAmF lumens and foils are fully dark with thrombus; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are partially open; BVLD/NO-PPAmF is the cleanest — largest open lumen, most exposed clean/reflective foil.

So the upstream fibrinogen/gamma-chain data account for the coarse downstream pattern (functionalization lowers fibrinogen adsorption and correspondingly reduces thrombus versus bare metal). They do not account for the finer downstream ranking that BVLD/NO-PPAmF is visually the least thrombogenic of the three functionalized surfaces, since its fibrinogen/gamma-chain values are statistically indistinguishable from (and numerically not below) BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF. That extra anti-thrombotic effect of the dual coating is left unexplained by this fibrinogen-adsorption assay alone.

Figure 10a: the [0001] tensile specimen deforms plastically via a serrated flow curve running roughly 55-80 MPa (initial rise to ~70 MPa, drop to ~55 MPa, recovery to a ~78-80 MPa plateau) out to ~45% plastic strain, and Fig. 10b confirms this deformation is carried by kink bands, imaged at the site circled in 10a.

Figure 2 (step 2) reports a very different number for the same [0001] orientation: in panel (a) the [0001] data point is plotted as "fracture" at roughly 350 MPa at RT, and panel (b)'s [0001] curve is simply a vertical line to an X at ~355 MPa with no plastic strain shown - i.e., the nominal RT behavior along [0001] is brittle fracture at ~350 MPa, not yielding.

Comparison: the kink-band deformation documented in Fig. 10 occurs at roughly 4-6x lower stress (~55-80 MPa) than the fracture stress (~350 MPa) recorded for [0001] in Fig. 2. This implies [0001] loading can follow two distinct routes - the typical brittle fracture at high stress reported in Fig. 2, versus, in the Fig. 10 specimen, extensive kink-band-mediated plasticity that initiates and proceeds at much lower stress. What remains uncertain: why this specimen took the low-stress kinking route instead of fracturing (geometry, surface condition, or other specimen-specific factors are not shown), and whether kinking is a precursor to or a substitute for the high-stress fracture event.

The combination supports only a weak, qualitative link. The optical panels (b-f) plus the earlier-established SEM agreement confirm that SiCp particles are present and spatially distributed across the matrix (not settled or confined to one region) at each composition examined, which is a necessary precondition for any particle-reinforcement mechanism (load transfer, Orowan bypass, or thermal-mismatch dislocation generation) to plausibly contribute to the observed YS rise from ~93 to ~142 MPa as SiCp content increases 0-25 wt%. Visually, panel density of dark particles does increase from b/c toward d/e/f (consistent with rising wt%), so the imaging is at least qualitatively consistent with more reinforcing phase being present at higher-YS compositions.

What this leaves open: the imaging gives no particle volume fraction, interparticle spacing, clustering index, or interface-quality measure per composition, so there is no quantitative bridge from "dispersion pattern" to the specific MPa increments reported. It cannot distinguish which strengthening mechanism (Orowan, load transfer, grain refinement, dislocation density from CTE mismatch) drives the trend, nor rule out that increasing local clustering at higher wt% (apparent in f) partially offsets reinforcement efficiency. The evidence establishes plausibility of a microstructure-property link, not a demonstrated causal or quantitative account of the YS trend.

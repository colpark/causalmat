Yes, the upstream evidence (XRD step-down near 150 K with the PC (004) branch coexisting ~150-175 K, plus PL's two-branch/IR-tail behavior in PC as-grown below 150 K and across 50-370 uW at 80 K, with HOC intermediate and SC single-band) is consistent with, and helps explain, the downstream observation: PC alone shows the split into two emission branches (structural/phase heterogeneity), HOC shows a partial/intermediate version, and SC shows only one band — tracking the same ordering (PC > HOC > SC) seen in the XRD coexistence signature and matching that PC is the composition where the orthorhombic/tetragonal split is most pronounced. This supports reading the PL branch-splitting and IR tail as the optical signature of the same structural phase coexistence XRD detects thermally, with composition (PC/HOC/SC) modulating how strongly the two phases/branches separate, and fluence at 80 K reproducing a comparable two-branch pattern in PC only.

What it leaves open: neither panel reports a numeric phase fraction or STR value, so no quantitative fraction-to-intensity mapping is established; the fluence dependence (a distinct excitation-power axis) is not tied to a temperature or structural coexistence-band value, so whether fluence-driven splitting reflects the same physical transition or a separate (e.g., local heating/carrier-density) effect is not settled by these panels; and HOC's "intermediate" status is qualitative only, not quantified against XRD coexistence width.

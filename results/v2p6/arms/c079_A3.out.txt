Panels b-f (labeled 0/5/10/15/20/25 wt% SiCp by position, per the b-f caption sequence) show dark, angular SiC particles increasing in areal density and forming more numerous/larger local clusters as wt% rises, most visibly between panel d (15%) and panel f (25%), where particle coverage becomes near-continuous with clumped agglomerates. This is consistent, directionally, with the downstream YS trend (93→142 MPa, 0→25 wt%): more reinforcement present in the matrix at higher content is compatible with a monotonic strength increase (via mechanisms like load transfer or Orowan-type effects), and the qualitative match (more particles, higher YS, no panel showing a density drop against a YS drop) supports that the two measurements are not contradictory.

What it does not establish: the micrographs show particle presence and visual clustering only — no quantitative area-fraction, no porosity assessment, no matrix grain size, and no interfacial bonding information. Increasing clustering at 20-25% (panels e-f) is visually clear but YS still rises rather than falls, so the images cannot explain why clustering does not degrade strength here, nor can they isolate SiC content from matrix microstructural changes (e.g., grain refinement) as the driver of YS. So the combination supports plausibility/consistency, not a mechanistic or quantitative causal link between the imaged distribution and the specific YS values.

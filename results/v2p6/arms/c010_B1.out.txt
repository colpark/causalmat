Evidence across the panels supports a modest, qualified performance picture for Co-MBG scaffolds in service.

Ion release (F5a/F9's companion release curves) shows dose-graded, sustained Co2+ release over 7 days (5Co-MBG > 2Co-MBG > 0Co-MBG, essentially zero from 0Co-MBG), alongside release of SiO4^4-, Ca2+, and PO4^3- consistent with bioactive glass dissolution/apatite exchange — the material is actively degrading and delivering ions rather than being inert. The HIF-1alpha blot (F9) shows only that the highest-Co-release group (5Co-MBG) has the most intense HIF-1alpha band, a two-way association (highest vs. the other two, which are merely "faint" and indistinguishable from each other) rather than a monotonic dose-response, and it is a qualitative band read at an unspecified timepoint. Antibacterial/drug-loaded scaffolds (F11a/b) show ampicillin-loaded scaffolds progressively reducing relative bacterial survival to ~81% and ~52% by days 3 and 7 versus blank/drug-free controls, indicating a real but antibiotic-driven cytotoxic/antibacterial effect — separate from and not evidence for the Co2+/HIF-1alpha hypoxia-mimicking mechanism.

What remains uncertain: whether Co2+ release itself (versus other compositional differences among the three scaffold formulations) drives the HIF-1alpha pattern, whether that pattern is truly dose-graded across all three groups, and whether any of this translates into a functional in vivo angiogenic or bone-repair outcome — no such endpoint is shown in these panels.

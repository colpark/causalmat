What the combination supports: the ROS-generation capability shown in F3b is consistent with bismuthene contributing a photodynamic (ROS-mediated) killing mechanism, which plausibly explains why adding the second laser (dual 660/808 nm) achieves complete, durable suppression while 808 nm-only (photothermal) treatment leaves residual tumor that regrows.

What it leaves open: F3b is an in vitro/cell-free dispersion assay at unspecified irradiation conditions — it does not show ROS generation occurring at the tumor site in vivo, does not isolate which laser wavelength drives the DPBF decay, and does not quantify how much of the in vivo suppression difference is attributable to ROS versus photothermal dose differences between groups. The mechanistic link between the assay and the day-14 regrowth pattern is inferential, not directly measured.

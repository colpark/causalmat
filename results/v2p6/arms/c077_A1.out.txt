Yes, the two panels are consistent with and directly support the downstream claim of a composition/phase identity shift, though with caveats.

Panel (b) ICP-AES bar chart confirms measurement A: Zn is absent (0 at%) in pristine and 4th-cycle samples, then jumps to ~51 at% by the 20th cycle, and remains high (~46-47 at%) at 60th and 100th cycles, while K, Fe, Mn all drop correspondingly. This matches the stated "rises to ~50 at% by cycle 20, stays near 47% after."

Panel (a) XRD stack shows the pristine KMnHCF pattern (bottom, black) with sharp characteristic peaks; by the 4th cycle new peaks appear (marked by dashed guide lines, positioned in the ~16-22° range matching the stated 16.5/21.9°) while the original peaks visibly weaken; by 20th-100th cycles the new peak set dominates and closely resembles the reference JCPDS 33-1061 pattern for KZnHCF (Zn-hexacyanoferrate) rather than the KMnHCF reference (51-1896). This supports measurement B.

Combined, the two panels jointly support a phase transformation story: Zn substitutes into the framework (ICP) coincident with a structural change toward a Zn-hexacyanoferrate-like lattice (XRD), i.e., composition and phase identity moving together. What is left open: neither panel alone proves causation (that Zn incorporation *causes* the new phase rather than correlating with it via a shared upstream driver, e.g., electrolyte dissolution/deposition), nor do they specify whether the transformation is a discrete substitution, a new coexisting phase, or a continuous solid solution — the XRD only shows peak co-occurrence and relative intensity change, not quantitative phase fraction.

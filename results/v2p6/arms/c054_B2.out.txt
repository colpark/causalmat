Combination assessed: SEM panels F4b (BST-SH/PAEN), F4c (BST@Ag1%/PAEN), F4e (BST@Ag5%/PAEN) against the tensile-strength trend in F7a (79.3, 78.3, 74.4, 73.5 MPa for PAEN, 1%, 3%, 5% Ag).

What it supports: The micrographs show no large agglomerates or gross debonding at any loading shown (1% and 5%), consistent with the earlier finding of even particle dispersion and good local bonding. This is compatible with the tensile-strength decline being gradual and modest (79.3→73.5 MPa, ~7% drop) rather than the sharp collapse typically seen with agglomeration-driven failure. So the upstream microstructural evidence is consistent with, and can partly account for, why strength loss is small and monotonic rather than catastrophic — dispersion/bonding quality plausibly limits the severity of the decline.

What it leaves open: The SEM evidence does not explain why strength still decreases progressively with Ag content despite good dispersion — no micrograph at 3% is included here, and F4e (5%) does show more visible embedded particles at the fracture surface than F4c (1%), which could reflect increased rigid-filler content, local stress concentration, or subtle packing changes, but this is not quantified. The pairing cannot establish that dispersion/bonding causes the strength trend, nor rule out that intrinsic filler stiffness/loading effects independently drive the decline.

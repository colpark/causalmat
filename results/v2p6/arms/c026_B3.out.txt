The electrochemical panels (F4c: voltage curves at 0.5/2.0/4.0 mA h cm⁻² showing CnC HS remains flat and stable while C HS shows growing voltage excursions; F4d: initial discharge profiles giving a 39 mV nucleation overpotential for CnC HS versus 43 mV for C HS) show that the N-containing/pyridinic-N host (CnC HS) nucleates and plates/strips zinc more easily and more stably than the plain-carbon host (C HS) across current densities, consistent with the SEM/Raman panel (F3c) showing more uniform, less dendritic deposit morphology and the N 1s NEXAFS (F3a) confirming nitrogen, including pyridinic character, is present in the doped host. Read together with the DFT step already established (exothermic Zn adsorption and negative charge transfer at N-doped sites, favorable to Zn-N bonding), the electrochemical data are consistent with nitrogen doping lowering the nucleation barrier and suppressing dendrite formation.

What this leaves uncertain: the electrochemical improvement is measured on the doped material as a whole, not isolated to pyridinic-N sites specifically — other structural differences between CnC HS and C HS (surface area, defect density, morphology) could also lower overpotential and are not ruled out. The 4 mV nucleation-overpotential gap and the qualitative plating stability are correlational with the DFT energetics, not a quantitative or causal link; no panel here directly ties the computed binding energies to the measured overpotential value.

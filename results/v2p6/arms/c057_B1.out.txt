Panels viewed: F5b (EDS spectrum, single sample) shows Ti, O, a dominant C peak, and small Er (~1.4 keV) and Ce (~4.8 keV) peaks. F10c and F10d are near-duplicate bar charts (presumably two different bacterial strains) showing antibacterial ratio rising with Ce content — Ce0.1 (~28-33%) < Ce0.2 (~43%) < Ce0.3 (~45-47%) in the dark, with TiO2 near 6% and control near 0 — matching Observation B, plus a light condition showing a similar but stronger ordering.

What the combination supports: the EDS spectrum confirms that Ce and Er are genuinely present as elements in the particle, consistent with the nominal Er0.5CeX-Ti-O labels used across the antibacterial series, so the dose-dependent trend across Ce0.1/0.2/0.3 is being measured on materials that really do contain Ce/Er rather than nominal-only compositions.

What it leaves open: the EDS spectrum is a single, qualitative spectrum with small Er/Ce peaks — it does not quantify Ce loading per sample, so it cannot itself explain or validate the specific monotonic (and plateauing) dose-response seen in the assay (Ce0.2 vs Ce0.3 differences are small/non-significant in dark). It also says nothing about Ce oxidation state, Ce's spatial location (lattice vs. surface), or any mechanism (ion release, ROS) connecting Ce content to bactericidal activity, and the dominant, unexplained C peak leaves open whether contamination affects the measured particles. So the pairing establishes elemental presence consistent with the assay's compositional labels, not the causal chemistry behind the antibacterial trend.

The combination supports a limited but clear claim: XRD (F3a) shows C10 reproduces all major C0 reflections (CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17, Mg) with only one weak added line (Co, ~44.5°), so the Co-containing sample is phase-identical to the baseline at the bulk/crystalline level — the STR/phase identity of the hydride-forming Mg alloy is preserved after milling with Co. Given that, the downstream capacity difference (F7a/F7b: ~5.1 wt% for C0 vs ~4.56 wt% for C10 at 250°C, both reaching near-plateau within ~50–100 min) cannot be attributed to a change in bulk phase composition, since none is detected. This is consistent with the lower plateau value being a simple dilution effect (5–10 wt% inert/weak Co addition reducing the active-phase mass fraction) rather than a phase transformation.

What it leaves open: XRD only probes bulk crystalline phases above detection limit, so it cannot rule out surface, interfacial, amorphous, or nanoscale changes introduced by milling or by the Co addition that could also affect hydrogen uptake. The single weak Co line confirms Co's presence but says nothing about its catalytic/kinetic role, if any — the panels show absorption curves but no mechanistic link to the XRD data. So the evidence justifies "no detectable bulk phase change accompanies the capacity drop" but does not establish why capacity dropped (dilution vs. surface/kinetic effect) or whether Co plays any functional role beyond being present.

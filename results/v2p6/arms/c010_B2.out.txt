What this justifies: the material releases Co, Si, Ca, and P ions in a controlled, composition-graded way with bioactive-glass-like ion-exchange behavior, and separately delivers ampicillin with measurable antibacterial effect in vitro. The Co2+ release also associates with elevated HIF-1alpha specifically in the highest-release group.

What remains uncertain: whether Co2+ release, rather than other compositional differences between the three scaffold types, drives the HIF-1alpha pattern; whether any of these in vitro ion/drug kinetics or bacterial-survival results predict in vivo degradation, angiogenic signaling, or clinical antibacterial performance; and whether antibacterial and hypoxia-signaling functions interact when combined in one scaffold, since these were tested as separate mechanisms, not together.</message>

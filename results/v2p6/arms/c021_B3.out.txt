All four panels examined directly.

F5d/F5e (bar charts) confirm the ELISA text exactly: 316LSS and PPAmF sit near 0.10 (fibrinogen) / 0.13-0.15 (gamma-chain) with a *** bracket to the three functionalised bars; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are the two lowest (~0.05), while BVLD/NO-PPAmF sits slightly higher (~0.06) with a visibly larger error bar - so BVLD/NO is indeed not the lowest, on both fibrinogen and its gamma-chain. F6b (cross-sections) and F6c (longitudinal photos) show the same qualitative gradient described: 316LSS and PPAmF lumens/foils are filled with dark, occlusive thrombus/hemoglobin staining; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are partly open with mixed red/pale tissue; BVLD/NO-PPAmF is the cleanest, whitest, most patent of the five.

So the combination supports: all three functionalised coatings reduce fibrinogen/gamma-chain deposition relative to controls, and this co-occurs with visibly less occlusive thrombus on the same coatings - a consistent surface-level association between lower fibrinogen adsorption/activation and less thrombus. It does not resolve the fine-grained mismatch already flagged: BVLD/NO-PPAmF looks macroscopically the most patent (best) in the photos yet is not the lowest by ELISA, so the visual thrombus ranking and the biochemical ranking diverge for that one coating. The evidence also still cannot separate a BVLD-specific mechanism from a general PPAmF/NO-associated effect, since NO-PPAmF alone reproduces the same reduction.

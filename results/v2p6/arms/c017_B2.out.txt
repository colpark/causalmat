Panels opened and confirmed against captions: F5a (crop …82fd39_A.jpg) is the SEM platelet-morphology panel (5 columns: 316LSS, PPAmF, BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, BVLD/NO-PPAmF), matching claim A — dense, spread dendritic platelets on 316LSS/PPAmF, sparser and rounder on the BVLD-containing surfaces, fewest/most rounded on BVLD/NO-PPAmF. F6b (crop …4d73fe895_B.jpg) and F6c (…_C.jpg) are the macro tube-lumen and thrombus photographs for the same five surfaces, matching claim B — 316LSS and PPAmF tubes/segments are filled with dark red-black thrombus, while BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and especially BVLD/NO-PPAmF show progressively more open lumen and cleaner, paler surfaces.

The combination supports a directionally consistent story: the same ranking of surfaces (316LSS ≈ PPAmF worst, BVLD/NO-PPAmF best) that showed reduced platelet count and reduced spreading/activation in the static PRP/SEM assay (F5a, established upstream) also shows the least thrombus and the most open lumen in the dynamic ex vivo blood-circulation photographs (F6b/F6c). This is consistent with — but does not by itself prove — that the surface's suppression of platelet adhesion/activation is what limits downstream thrombus formation.

What it leaves open: the two assays are different modalities (static PRP vs. dynamic whole-blood circulation) and different endpoints (platelet count/shape vs. gross thrombus photograph), with no shared quantitative metric, no statistics on either, and only qualitative/visual grading of thrombus extent. So the pairing shows the trends co-occur across the same five surfaces, not that platelet-level suppression causally produces the reduced macroscopic thrombus, nor any mechanistic link (e.g., coagulation-cascade or fibrin contribution) between the two.

Both panels match the stated measurements. F5c confirms A: x=0.01 (red) is the highest PF curve across the whole range, ~29 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² at 300 K falling to ~22 at 623 K; x=0.005 (black) is the lowest of the four doped curves (~23 at 300 K, ~13 at 623 K); x=0 (green) stays at roughly 2–5 throughout. F6a confirms B: at 300 K, x=0.005 (black, ~0.85) sits above x=0.01 (red, ~0.78), which sits above x=0.02/x=0.04 (~0.63–0.69); the dashed n-Bi2Te3 reference curve starts near 0.88 at 300 K, consistent with the stated ~0.88.

What the combination supports: the PF ranking and the ZT ranking are each internally consistent with the respective figures as printed.

What it leaves open: PF does not account for the ZT ordering. x=0.01 has the highest PF at every temperature, yet x=0.005 has the higher ZT at 300 K — the opposite order. Since ZT = PF·T/κ, this inversion implies a lower thermal conductivity for x=0.005 than for x=0.01, but neither panel shows κ, so that explanation is not verified here. Nothing in these two figures establishes a causal or even purely correlative link between the PF and ZT curves; thermal-conductivity data would be needed to close that gap.

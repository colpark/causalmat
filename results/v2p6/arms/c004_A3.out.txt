Both panels confirm the numbers given. F3c is a calibration curve of A/L at 808 nm vs. agent concentration, strictly linear (R2=0.9999, slope alpha=6.4 L g-1 cm-1) over roughly 0-200 microgram/mL. F4f is the in vivo tumor PA signal time course after IV injection: 0.26 at 0h, dip to 0.13 at 1h, 0.32 at 2h, peak 0.94 at 4h, 0.60 at 6h, 0.31 at 10h.

The combination supports treating PA/absorbance signal as a quantitative proxy for agent concentration: because absorbance scales linearly with concentration in solution, the rise and fall of the downstream PA signal can be read as tracking rising and falling agent concentration in the tumor over time, i.e., accumulation to a 4h peak followed by clearance by 10h. This licenses a "value/property to service capability" reading — the material's optical property justifies using PA signal as a stand-in for tissue concentration, supporting a qualitative accumulation/clearance narrative.

What it leaves open: the calibration (F3c) was done in a controlled solution, not in tissue, so in vivo scattering, depth, and tissue optical heterogeneity could break linearity — the upstream evidence does not establish quantitative fidelity of PA-to-concentration conversion in vivo. It also does not explain the non-monotonic dip at 1h before the 4h peak; that requires additional biological/pharmacokinetic evidence not present in either panel.

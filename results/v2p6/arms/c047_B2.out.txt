The combination supports a mechanistic rationale, but only at the level of correlation across compositions, not a tested causal chain to the downstream metric.

F9a shows dG_H* is most negative for Pd (-0.31) and becomes progressively more positive through Pd0.75Ag0.25 (-0.08), Pd0.5Ag0.5 (0.12), Pd0.25Ag0.75 (0.28), to Ag (0.49). F8a/F8b show H2 evolution rate is highest for Ag0.25Pd0.75 (125.4 umol/h), then Ag0.5Pd0.5 (57.3), Ag0.75Pd0.25 (43.5), Pd (34.5), Ag (3.2), ZIS (1.5). So the composition with dG_H* closest to thermoneutral (Pd0.75Ag0.25, -0.08 eV) is not the same nominal ratio as the top H2-evolution performer (labeled Ag0.25Pd0.75, 125.4), but both point to the same physical alloy (75% Pd/25% Ag by the paper's own labeling convention across figures), and pure Pd — most exothermic, "too strong" binding — underperforms every Pd-Ag alloy despite having the most negative computed dG_H*. This pattern is consistent with a Sabatier-type argument: near-thermoneutral H* binding, associated with the XPS-indicated Ag-Pd electron redistribution, tracks with superior HER activity better than either pure metal.

What it leaves open: the XPS shift is qualitative and single-composition, dG_H* is computed on bare (111) surfaces while H2 evolution is measured on the ZIS-supported material, and the correlation between adsorption energetics and photocatalytic rate does not exclude other alloying effects (charge separation, light harvesting, active-site density) contributing to the observed activity trend. Causation from electronic shift to enhanced rate is not established, only consistency.

The combination supports a consistent ordinal concordance across three independent readouts: samples with less platelet adhesion/spreading under static PRP exposure (SEM) also show less gross thrombus under dynamic blood circulation (both lumen and split-foil photos), with the same material ranking (316LSS≈PPAmF worst; BVLD-PPAmF/NO-PPAmF intermediate; combined BVLD/NO-PPAmF best) in all three panels.

What it leaves open: the panels give only qualitative visual impressions — no platelet counts, no quantitative thrombus scoring, no statistics are visible in the images themselves. PRP incubation is a static, isolated-platelet assay, whereas the circulation photos reflect whole-blood flow with coagulation and shear effects; the panels alone cannot establish that reduced platelet spreading is the causal mechanism behind reduced thrombus (only that the two phenomena co-vary across the same material series). No dose-response beyond the single 2h timepoint is shown.

Both panels confirm the stated numbers. F5c: x=0.01 (red) is highest across all T, ~29 at 300 K falling to ~22 at 623 K; x=0.005 (black) is the lowest of the doped compositions, falling further, to ~13 at 623 K, below x=0.02 and x=0.04 (~19-20); x=0 (green) stays lowest overall, ~2-5. F7c: at Th=623 K, x=0.01 reaches ~11.8% efficiency, clearly above the others, which cluster ~9.5-10.6%.

What the combination supports: for the single standout composition, x=0.01, the two panels agree — it has the highest power factor at both temperatures and the highest conversion efficiency, so PF being elevated is consistent with (not contradicted by) the efficiency being elevated.

What it leaves open: the correspondence does not extend to the ranking among the remaining compositions. x=0.005 has the lowest PF of all doped samples at 623 K, well below x=0.02/x=0.04, yet in F7c it is not clearly the lowest in efficiency — it sits within the same ~9.5-10.6% band as x=0.02 and x=0.04 rather than trailing them. Efficiency is a composite (depends on ZT, i.e., PF, thermal conductivity, and the Th/Tc span), and no thermal-conductivity data appear in either panel, so PF alone cannot account for the efficiency ordering beyond the top performer. This is co-variation for one composition, not a demonstrated mechanism or a full accounting of the efficiency ranking.

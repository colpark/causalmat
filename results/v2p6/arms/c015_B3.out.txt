Both panels confirm the stated trends. F4a (SXRD) shows the ~16.4/19.2/20.1° reflections shrinking from strong (bottom traces, start of discharge) to a weak broad hump by mid-discharge, with the upper traces (charge) staying flat/broad rather than regaining sharp peaks. F3a (SAXS) shows the low-q contour boundary bending toward lower q during the ~6–11 h discharge window and remaining shifted through charge rather than returning to its original position.

Combined with the already-established XRD+XANES result (amorphous, metallic-Co-like discharge product, not reversed on charge), the SAXS data adds a real-space/microstructural dimension: the same discharge window in which crystalline order is lost and Co reduces to Co0 also shows a shift to larger characteristic length scales (lower q), and this shift likewise fails to reverse on charge. Together this supports that the phase transformation is accompanied by a concurrent, similarly irreversible change in microstructure/porosity (consistent with growth of pores or metallic-Co domains as the oxide converts).

It leaves open: the SAXS shift alone doesn't identify what the larger features are (pores, Co particles, voids) or quantify their size/fraction; it doesn't establish that the microstructural change and the amorphization/reduction are causally linked rather than merely co-timed; and neither dataset says what phase or morphology forms on charge, only that both structure and microstructure differ from the pristine state.

F4a (apparent porosity vs. sintering temperature) and F5 (Vickers hardness vs. sintering temperature) are the source figures for measurements A and B, plotted for the same three series S1/S2/S3. Together they show a clear inverse pairing: across all three series, apparent porosity falls with temperature while hardness rises with temperature, over the same temperature range and same sample labels — so the upstream porosity evidence and the downstream hardness observation are directly linked by shared sample/temperature identifiers, supporting a structure(porosity)-property(hardness) relationship in these composites.

What this leaves open: the correspondence is only a co-trend, not a mechanism — no evidence here isolates porosity as the causal driver of hardness versus other temperature-dependent factors (grain size, phase content, densification of the binder phase) that could independently raise hardness. Notably S3 retains much higher residual porosity than S1/S2 (7.7% vs. <1% at 1600°C) yet its hardness (~14.1 GPa) is close to S1's (~15.6 GPa), which the porosity data alone does not explain — indicating porosity is not sufficient by itself to account for the hardness pattern across samples. The earlier SEM-porosity qualifications (unspecified sample/temperature, no quantification of cavities) still apply and are not resolved by these two plots.</message>

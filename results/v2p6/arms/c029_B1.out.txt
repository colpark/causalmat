Partially. F7b/F7f jointly establish that voltage output is a genuine, dose-dependent piezoelectric response that rises monotonically with PZT loading, peaking at 62.0 V at 10 wt%. That supports why the f-P0.10 sample — the highest-PZT, highest-voltage fiber composition — was the one chosen for the power measurement in F7e, and is consistent with the fiber film's very high peak power (136.9 µW at 10 MΩ) relative to the cast film (21.025 µW): a stronger, verified piezoelectric charge-generation process upstream plausibly feeds a larger power output downstream.

But the upstream evidence does not account for two features of F7e specifically. First, F7e's dependence on external load resistance — power rising to a peak at 10 MΩ and then falling as resistance increases further — reflects impedance matching between the device's internal resistance and the load, a phenomenon neither F7b (voltage vs. PZT content) nor F7f (polarity reversal) measures or speaks to. Second, the fiber-vs-cast-film power gap is a morphology/fabrication comparison that F7b and F7f never test, since both only examine fiber-based PENGs across PZT wt%. So the combination supports that the power test used a verified, high-output piezoelectric sample, but leaves the load-dependent power shape and the fiber-vs-cast mechanism unexplained.

Conclusion the evidence justifies: In symmetric-cell service testing, CnC HS clearly outperforms C HS. The Coulombic-efficiency trace (F4a) shows CnC HS holding near 100% steadily across ~200 cycles at 1 mAh cm-2/4 mA cm-2, while C HS is noisier, dips to ~75-80% efficiency twice, and twice drops to 0% (cell failure events). The long-duration cycling trace (F4b), explicitly annotated "C HS battery failure," shows C HS developing large voltage spikes/instability partway through a ~7000-minute test while CnC HS remains flat and stable throughout. The stepped-rate cycling trace (F4e) shows the same pattern as current density is ramped up: CnC HS stays stable across the step sequence while C HS grows increasingly erratic and eventually fails at the higher steps. Together these three panels support that CnC HS provides more reliable, longer-lived, higher-efficiency service performance than C HS, particularly as current density/duration increases.

What remains uncertain: these panels give efficiency and voltage-stability data only — they do not quantify overpotential magnitude (that is F4c/d) or capacity/energy retention, and they do not show *why* C HS fails (e.g., whether by dendrite short-circuit); linking this failure behavior to the earlier-established morphology/plateau divergence is plausible but not directly shown by F4a/b/e themselves.

Panels confirm the reported numbers: F2a shows HP relative density rising from ~97.5% (0 wt%) to ~100% at 1 and 5 wt%, ~99.3% at 3 wt%; F2b shows HP Vickers hardness at ~18.0 GPa (0), peaking ~20.3 GPa (1 wt%), then falling to ~19.4 and ~19.2 GPa at 3 and 5 wt%.

The settled density+SEM result (that HP samples, especially 1 and 5 wt%, are genuinely pore-free with transgranular fracture) accounts for only part of the hardness picture: it can plausibly explain why hardness at 1 wt% AlN is higher than at 0 wt% (near-full densification removing porosity that would otherwise lower hardness). But it does not account for the downstream hardness trend as a whole. Density stays at or near 100% across 1, 3, and 5 wt% AlN, yet hardness declines from ~20.3 to ~19.4 to ~19.2 GPa over that same range — a drop the density/porosity story cannot explain, since porosity is essentially constant (near-zero) there. So the upstream pore-free-microstructure evidence supports the low-to-high step (0→1 wt%) but leaves the subsequent decline (1→3→5 wt%) unaccounted for; some other mechanism (e.g., grain size, second-phase distribution, AlN content itself) must be responsible, and neither density nor the single SEM image speaks to it.

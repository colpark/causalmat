The three panels confirm the caption text as printed. F3c is a 2D SAXS contour restricted to the 1st discharge/charge cycle only (paired with a single voltage-vs-time trace to ~14 h); F3d overlays I(q) curves at six discrete states (OCV, 0.45, 0.136, 0.11, 0.02, 3 V) that track almost on top of one another, with only a modest divergence in the ~0.03-0.1 A^-1 region at the two lowest voltages (0.11/0.02 V). F2d shows capacity fade from ~870 (cycle 1) to ~480 (cycle 2) to ~70 mAh/g by cycle 40, with Coulombic efficiency rising from ~50% to >90%.

The upstream SAXS evidence supports only a narrow claim: at the mesoscale probed by this q-range, the material's scattering/structural signature stays largely invariant through the first cycle, with a minor, discharge-state-specific perturbation near full discharge. This is consistent with the electrode not undergoing gross mesostructural collapse or major porosity/domain-size change on cycle 1.

It does not account for the downstream capacity fade. The SAXS data cover only cycle 1, while the capacity loss unfolds and compounds over 40 cycles; no structural probe is shown for later cycles. So the identification of a fade mechanism (SEI growth, irreversible Na trapping, side reactions, atomic-scale/phase changes outside the SAXS length scale) is left entirely open — the mesoscale stability observed cannot be extrapolated to explain, rule in, or rule out the cause of the severe long-term capacity/service-capability decline.

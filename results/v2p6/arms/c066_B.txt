XRD establishes, on its own, only that certain crystalline phases are present somewhere in the bulk HP5 specimen: ZrB2, SiC, C (graphite), and BN, with no AlN or Al2O3 detected. It is a bulk, spatially-averaged measurement and says nothing about where in the microstructure any given phase sits. TEM establishes, on its own, only a real-space microstructural feature: a band of parallel dark fringes of even width running along grain boundaries at a triple junction. Imaging alone does not by itself identify the chemical/crystallographic identity of that band. Put together, the two observations let one propose that the layered fringe band seen by TEM corresponds to one of the layered crystalline phases XRD confirms is present in the bulk (graphite or BN), because the fringe morphology (parallel, evenly spaced layers) is consistent with a layered-lattice phase, and XRD confirms such phases exist in the sample.

  - XRD is a bulk, spatially-averaged measurement; it cannot localize any phase to the grain-boundary triple junction seen in TEM.

Panels viewed: F3a (bare BST, ~70 nm, smooth, discrete particles), F3b (BST‑SH, rougher, larger, more clustered), F3e (BST@Ag5%, distinctly rough, coated, fused/agglomerated clusters), F7b (breaking elongation bar chart: pure PAEN 6.9%, BST@Ag1% 5.8%, BST@Ag3% 4.5%, BST@Ag5% 4.4%, monotonically decreasing with Ag content).

What the combination supports: The SEM series is consistent with progressive particle coating/roughening/agglomeration as processing proceeds toward higher Ag loading (already established as tracking Ag content and color darkening), and this same direction of change — rising Ag content, larger/rougher/more clustered filler particles — co-occurs with a monotonic drop in breaking elongation from pure PAEN through 5% Ag. So the upstream structural trend (coating/agglomeration increasing with Ag content) and the downstream mechanical trend (ductility decreasing with Ag content) move together, consistent with a stiffer, more heterogeneous, possibly agglomerated filler restricting matrix chain mobility and reducing elongation.

What it leaves open: SEM here is not graded across the actual mechanical-test compositions — only bare BST, an intermediate BST‑SH, and BST@Ag5% are imaged, with no SEM at 1% or 3% Ag, the two intermediate points where elongation already drops sharply (6.9→5.8→4.5%). So the upstream evidence cannot establish a dose-response link to the specific elongation values, cannot distinguish whether particle size, coating thickness, agglomeration, or Ag content itself is mechanically responsible, and does not address interfacial adhesion or dispersion quality directly. The parallel trends are consistent with but do not prove that the visualized surface/agglomeration state causes the ductility loss.

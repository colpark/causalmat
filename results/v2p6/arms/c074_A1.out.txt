Both panels are visually confirmed. F1a (DFT ΔG plot) matches claim A: CoCrOOH sits lowest on the Co2+→Co3+ axis (~1.05 eV), CoFeOOH lowest on the Co3+→Co4+ axis (~6.2 eV), CoFeCrOOH intermediate on both (~1.2, ~6.4 eV), and Co(OH)2 highest (~6.7 eV). F5c confirms B: the black Co5Fe3Cr2 curve crosses j=10 mA/cm2 first, with η10=452 mV marked, ahead of IrO2 and the other Co-Fe-Cr/Co5Fe5/Co5Cr5 curves.

What the combination supports: the ternary Co-Fe-Cr composition is both computationally predicted to have a more balanced (not extreme-high, not extreme-low) two-step Co redox energetics and is empirically the fastest catalyst to reach the 10 mA/cm2 benchmark in neutral electrolyte. This is consistent with — but does not prove — a mechanistic link between moderated Co-redox thermodynamics and improved OER kinetics.

What it leaves open: the DFT panel's "CoFeCrOOH" is not labeled with the same 5:3:2 stoichiometry used in the LSV panel (Co5Fe3Cr2, Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5), so the two panels are not guaranteed to describe the identical composition — the pathway/performance link rests on a name-level, not a stoichiometry-matched, correspondence. Also, DFT free energy alone does not account for other factors (conductivity, active surface area, real defect structure) that jointly determine measured overpotential, so causal attribution of the LSV ranking solely to the computed ΔG trend is not established by this evidence.

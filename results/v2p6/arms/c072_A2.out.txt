Figure 2a (only one distinct image, provided twice) shows discharge capacity vs. cycle number for three synthesis routes: co-precipitation w/ HCl (red), co-precipitation w/o HCl (blue), simple mixing w/o HCl (black), plus coulombic efficiency curves near 100% for all three.

The plot directly confirms both stated measurements: at cycle 1 the w/ HCl trace starts at ~91 mAh/g, w/o HCl at ~81 mAh/g, and simple mixing at ~61 mAh/g, and this ordering (w/ HCl > w/o HCl > simple mixing) is maintained across the full 250-cycle window shown, with all three curves declining and roughly leveling off (to ~55, ~40, ~30 mAh/g respectively) while coulombic efficiency stays near 100% throughout for all three.

So the upstream evidence (panel A) fully accounts for and directly supports both downstream claims A and B: the rank ordering at every cycle and the ~91 mAh/g first-discharge value for the HCl sample are both explicitly plotted, not inferred.

What it leaves open: the figure is purely electrochemical (capacity/CE vs. cycle) and contains no PRP/value or PRF/service_capability data — no compositional, structural, particle-size, impedance, or mechanistic panel is shown here to explain *why* HCl addition improves capacity. The causal link between the synthetic procedure (HCl use) and the resulting material property/structure that produces this capacity ordering is asserted by the caption ("depending on the synthetic procedures") but not evidenced within this panel alone; that would require the other structural/compositional panels in the paper, which were not provided for review.

F2a (Relative Density vs. AlN wt%) shows PS falling 85.5→78.4→70.7→70.0% at 0/1/3/5 wt% AlN, matching PHYS:density exactly, while HP stays flat and near-full density (~98–100%) across the same AlN range. What the combination supports: for the pressureless-sintered route, hardness declines in lockstep with relative density as AlN increases — consistent with a porosity-driven loss of hardness (less-dense compact, softer measured hardness). The HP series, which retains near-full density at the same AlN loadings, also retains high hardness; this contrast strengthens the inference that density/porosity, not AlN itself as an intrinsic softening agent, is the operative link between the AlN addition and the hardness drop in the PS samples.

What it leaves open: these two curves alone give no microstructural evidence (no SEM, pore morphology, phase or grain-size data) to confirm porosity is the mechanism rather than a correlated change (e.g., AlN-induced secondary phase or interfacial weakening in PS specifically). Causal direction is inferred, not directly imaged, from these panels.

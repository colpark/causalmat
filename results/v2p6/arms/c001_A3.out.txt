All three panels confirm the printed values. F6a shows Pt's CV essentially flat (~±1 mA/cm2 scale) against RGO-Cu2S and Cu2S curves with a sharp reduction peak near -1.2 to -1.3 V reaching roughly -43 and -41 mA/cm2; F6b (magnified Pt) shows Pt's own peak is only on the order of 0.4-1.1 mA/cm2, consistent with the reported ~-0.45 mA/cm2 Pt figure. F5a confirms the J-V ranking: RGO-Cu2S highest (Jsc ~16, Voc ~0.555), Cu2S middle (~15, ~0.535), Pt lowest (~13, ~0.525).

The combination supports a correlational, order-consistent story: the same ranking (RGO-Cu2S ≥ Cu2S >> Pt) holds for both counter-electrode reduction-catalysis magnitude (CV) and cell photovoltaic output (Jsc, Voc). This is consistent with the qualitative claim that the Cu2S-based counter electrodes are more catalytically active than Pt for the polysulfide redox couple and that cells built with them perform better.

What it leaves open: CV at the counter electrode primarily reports catalytic/charge-transfer behavior there, which mechanistically governs fill factor and series resistance, not Jsc or Voc, which are set mainly by light harvesting and photoanode/electrolyte charge-transfer kinetics. No measurement here (CV or J-V alone) establishes a causal or quantitative link from CE catalytic activity to the specific Jsc/Voc gains — only a parallel ranking. Efficiency/fill-factor/PCE values, impedance data, or a stated mechanism connecting CE kinetics to Jsc/Voc would be needed to close that gap; none of that is shown in these three panels.

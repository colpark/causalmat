Panel content: F5a/b/c is one composite figure — A) SEM of platelets after 2h PRP incubation (five surfaces: 316LSS, PPAmF, BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, BVLD/NO-PPAmF); B) bar graph, platelet number (10²cell/mm²); C) bar graph, platelet activation (%). Both B and C show the same pattern: 316LSS/PPAmF form a high group (platelets ~32–41; activation ~65–74%), significantly (***) above a low group of BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, BVLD/NO-PPAmF (platelets ~15, 11, 5; activation ~23%, 18%, ~0%), with BVLD-PPAmF further significantly above NO-PPAmF/BVLD/NO-PPAmF. F6b/c belong to an ex vivo arteriovenous-shunt figure: B) photos of tube cross-sections after 2h blood circulation; C) photos of the excised thrombus, both showing heavy dark clot filling 316LSS/PPAmF and progressively less occlusion/clot for BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and especially BVLD/NO-PPAmF (near-clean lumen).

Conclusion the evidence justifies: quantified platelet counts and activation percentages now confirm, rather than just qualitatively suggest, that all three modified surfaces suppress platelet adhesion/activation relative to 316LSS/PPAmF, and the ex vivo shunt photos show this translates into visibly less thrombus and a more open lumen after real blood flow, with BVLD/NO-PPAmF showing the cleanest result.

Left uncertain: these panels do not report thrombin activity, so they cannot show whether the extra suppression from adding NO reflects a further change in thrombin activity or a separate NO-driven anti-platelet mechanism; and the photos/SEM remain single 2h time-point, ex vivo readouts, not evidence of long-term in-service durability.

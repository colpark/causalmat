The combination does not establish a mechanistic account of the hardness trend; it only bounds what could be causing it.

XRD on HP5 (0 wt% AlN reference specimen) confirms the crystalline phases present are ZrB2, SiC, C (graphite), and BN, with no AlN or Al2O3 reflections detected. The hardness plot shows HP-processed specimens rising from ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt% AlN to a peak ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then declining slightly to ~19.4 and ~19.2 GPa at 3 and 5 wt%.

Together, these support only a limited inference: since XRD detects no residual AlN or Al2O3 phase in the HP5 specimen, any hardness change with AlN content is not attributable to a persistent AlN or Al2O3 second phase acting directly as a hard/soft constituent in that specimen — AlN has evidently reacted away, dissolved, or fallen below detection. This is consistent with, but does not prove, an indirect route (e.g., AlN aiding densification or grain-boundary chemistry) for the hardness change.

What this leaves open: XRD is only for the HP5 (single AlN loading) specimen, not for the 1, 3, 5 wt% HP specimens actually plotted in the hardness curve — so there is no phase data across the AlN series to explain why hardness peaks at 1 wt% and then falls. No density, grain-size, or microstructural data connect AlN content to hardness quantitatively. The STR/phase/identity link to the PRP/value trend is therefore plausible but not demonstrated.

F9a (=F8a caption block for DFT) reproduces the exact ΔG_H* values in A (Pd −0.31, Pd0.75Ag0.25 −0.08, Pd0.5Ag0.5 0.12, Pd0.25Ag0.75 0.28, Ag 0.49 eV), and F8b reproduces the H2 evolution rates in B, with F8a's time-course curves showing the same ordering (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS > Ag0.5Pd0.5 > Ag0.75Pd0.25 > Pd > Ag > ZIS) growing consistently over 0–5 h.

The combination supports a volcano-type mechanistic account: |ΔG_H*| closest to zero (Pd0.75Ag0.25, −0.08 eV) tracks with the highest H2 rate (125.4 µmol/h), while the two extremes—Pd (too-strong H binding, −0.31 eV) and Ag (too-weak, 0.49 eV)—both underperform the alloys, matching the rate ranking almost monotonically with |ΔG_H*|. This is good qualitative/semi-quantitative corroboration that H binding thermodynamics on the metal cocatalyst governs the trend across compositions.

What it leaves open: the DFT is for idealized bare (111) alloy surfaces, not the actual ZIS-supported cocatalyst nanoparticles, so it says nothing about light absorption, charge-separation/transfer efficiency, particle size/dispersion, or long-term stability — factors that also shape the absolute photocatalytic rate but aren't captured by ΔG_H* alone.

The upstream and downstream evidence are consistent with each other but not tightly linked.

Downstream (mechanical): comparing the 423K-8+373K-3 curve in F12 (navy) to the matching 423K-8+373K-3+EPT curve in F13 (blue) shows the EPT addition lowers the yield knee (~350→~330 MPa), raises UTS (~420→~447 MPa), and roughly doubles elongation to fracture (~0.095→~0.15). That pattern — modest yield drop, higher UTS, and much larger uniform elongation — is the kind of change expected from grain refinement plus improved microstructural homogeneity.

Upstream (SEM): panel (d) shows notably finer, more uniform features (~0.3–0.8 µm) than panels (b),(c) at the same 2 µm scale, so refinement is real and documented in this dataset.

What the combination supports: the qualitative direction is coherent — a finer, more homogeneous microstructure co-occurring with better ductility and strength is physically plausible and not contradicted by either panel.

What it leaves open: the link is not demonstrated directly. Panel (d)'s own caption reads "25s-10min," while the matching F13 curve is labeled "25µs-10min" — a unit mismatch that makes it uncertain this SEM image is actually the EPT-treated sample plotted in F13, rather than a different specimen. No grain-size statistics, and no SEM pair specifically for the F12-vs-F13 EPT comparison, are shown. So causation (microstructure → mechanical response) is suggested, not established, by this evidence set alone.

The upstream result (Rct rise tracking V dissolution, both saturating ~cycle 150) accounts only partially for the downstream capacity-fade observation in F2a. The steep early capacity drop for the w/HCl cell (first ~20-50 cycles) overlaps temporally with the steepest phase of the Rct climb in F5d (cycle 0-~60), so it is consistent with rising interfacial resistance (and the V dissolution it tracks) contributing to early capacity loss. However, F2a shows capacity continuing to decline slowly out to cycle 250, while F5d shows Rct essentially plateauing (with large error bars) from ~150 onward. That mismatch means the established Rct/V correlation does not obviously explain the ongoing slow fade after Rct stops rising - some other process, unaddressed by this evidence, may be responsible for later-stage loss. The near-100% coulombic efficiency throughout is consistent with a kinetic/impedance-driven fade rather than large parasitic side reactions, which fits a resistance-based story, but no quantitative link between ohms of Rct and mAh/g lost is established. Left open: what drives fade after cycle ~150, and whether V dissolution is causally sufficient versus merely correlated.

Panels confirm the captions as printed. F2a shows FTIR: only the BST-SH trace carries new bands at 2930, 2864 and 1122 cm-1, absent in BST and BST-OH — consistent with the settled FTIR+TGA finding that a distinct CH2/Si-O-bearing species is chemically present and thermally quantifiable. F5a shows DSC glass-transition steps: pure PAEN at 176 C, dropping to 146, 149, 157 C for BST@Ag 1/3/5%.

The combination does NOT account for the downstream Tg observation. The upstream evidence (FTIR bands + TGA mass loss) characterizes surface chemistry on BST/BST-SH particles alone — it says a silane-derived organic/Si-O species is present on the particle surface and quantifiable by mass. F5a's DSC data are on PAEN composites loaded with BST@Ag (not on bare BST-SH particles), and no DSC trace for untreated-BST or silane-free composites is shown for comparison. So nothing in the upstream chemistry evidence isolates silane surface modification as the cause of the ~20-30 C Tg depression versus Ag content, particle loading, dispersion, or plasticization effects generically.

What's supported: the composites do contain a chemically distinct, quantifiable interfacial species (upstream), and the composites show substantially lower Tg than pure PAEN (downstream) — a plausible but untested link via interfacial disruption of chain packing. What's left open: any causal or even correlative mechanism tying the specific FTIR/TGA-detected species to the Tg shift, the role of Ag versus silane treatment, and whether Tg change scales with grafting extent.

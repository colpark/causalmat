The upstream ranking (Mg-Zn-Y-Nd corrodes far faster than 317L SS, per polarization and immersion-rate data) is directionally consistent with the pH observation: rapid Mg-alloy dissolution is the kind of process that would drive strong, sustained alkalinization, which is what the faster-corroding sample shows, while the much slower-corroding steel does not show comparable alkalinization. So the pairing supports that the pH divergence is compatible with, and plausibly a consequence of, the same corrosion-rate asymmetry already established.

It does not account for the pH data quantitatively or mechanistically: nothing in the polarization/immersion evidence specifies what chemical process sets pH, so it cannot explain why 317L SS's pH instead falls (rather than staying flat), why the blank control also rises then falls, or predict the plateau near pH 10.

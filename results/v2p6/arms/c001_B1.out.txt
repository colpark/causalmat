The combination supports a consistent ordinal link: in both domains RGO-Cu2S > Cu2S > Pt. CV (F6a) shows Pt's reduction peak (~-0.45 mA/cm2, magnified in F6b) is vastly smaller than either sulfide electrode, and J-V (F5a) shows Pt has the lowest current density and lowest onset among the three curves across the full voltage range — the qualitative ranking of polysulfide-reduction activity (upstream) matches the ranking of photovoltaic current output (downstream), consistent with counter-electrode catalytic activity contributing to cell performance, especially explaining why Pt underperforms both sulfide electrodes.

What it leaves open: the CV gap between RGO-Cu2S and Cu2S is small (~-43 vs ~-41 mA/cm2), while the reported Jsc (16.0 vs 15.0 vs 13.0 mA/cm2) and Voc (0.555 vs 0.535 vs 0.525 V) differences, though also modest, form a separate, independently-scaled measurement — F5a shows current density curves without directly overlaying or deriving Voc/FF/efficiency values in the panel itself. No quantitative or mechanistic (e.g., charge-transfer resistance, fill factor) link is shown connecting CV peak magnitude to J-V output; the correspondence is ordinal across three samples, not a measured causal or proportional relationship. So a general association is supported; a quantitative or mechanistic account is not.

The panels confirm the numeric trends: F2a shows HP relative density rising from ~97.5% (0 wt%) to ~100% at 1 wt%, dipping slightly to ~99.3% at 3 wt%, and back to ~100% at 5 wt%; F2b shows HP hardness rising from ~18.0 GPa (0 wt%) to a peak of ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then declining to ~19.4 and ~19.2 GPa at 3 and 5 wt%.

Given the earlier-established finding — that density plus SEM jointly confirm the HP composites, especially at 1 and 5 wt% AlN, are genuinely pore-free and well-consolidated — this upstream evidence partly accounts for the hardness observation: it rules out residual porosity as an explanation for low hardness at any HP composition, since all HP samples are essentially fully dense (97.5–100%). This supports that the modest hardness variation across the series (18.0–20.3 GPa) reflects something other than densification differences, since density is already near-saturated by 1 wt%.

What it leaves open: the density/SEM evidence cannot explain why hardness peaks at 1 wt% and then declines at 3 and 5 wt% even as density stays at or near 100%. That non-monotonic hardness trend, decoupled from the monotonic-plateau density trend, points to a separate mechanism (e.g., grain size, AlN distribution, or interfacial effects) that this upstream evidence does not address.

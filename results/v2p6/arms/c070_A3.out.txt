The three panels support a coherent upstream-to-downstream story, but only at the level of correlation, not demonstrated mechanism.

F5a (SEM after 2h PRP incubation) shows platelets that are numerous, spread, and dendritic on 316LSS and PPAmF, versus fewer, smaller, and less spread/more rounded platelets on BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF. F6b (cross-sections after 2h blood circulation) and F6c (photos of formed thrombus) show 316LSS and PPAmF lumens fully occluded by dark thrombus, while the BVLD- and NO-releasing surfaces show open, largely clean lumens with markedly less clot.

Together, this supports the claim that surfaces which suppress platelet adhesion/activation in the PRP assay are the same surfaces that show reduced macroscopic thrombus formation during blood circulation — i.e., the platelet-level (upstream) finding is consistent with, and helps explain, the downstream reduction in thrombogenic occlusion/service capability.

What it leaves open: the two assays are different experiments (static PRP incubation vs. dynamic ex vivo circulation), so this is correlation across separate runs, not a single causal chain; panels show no cell counts, statistics, or replicate numbers; no evidence here addresses fibrin/coagulation-cascade contributions to the visible clot, only platelets; and sample sizes appear to be single specimens per condition in both figures.

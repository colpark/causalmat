The evidence is consistent with a link but does not establish it rigorously. In F13, the "423K‑8+373K‑3+25µs‑10min" (EPT) curve reaches a higher UTS (~447 vs ~420 MPa in F12's matching pre‑strain-only "423K‑8+373K‑3" curve) and much greater elongation (~0.15 vs ~0.095), with a slightly lower yield knee (~330 vs ~350 MPa) — i.e., EPT addition trades a little yield strength for a large ductility gain plus higher UTS. F10d, labeled "25s‑10min sample" (presumably the EPT-treated condition, though the label doesn't exactly match "25µs‑10min" in F13 — a naming discrepancy worth flagging), shows markedly finer, more uniform features (~0.3–0.8 µm) than panels (b),(c) at the same 2 µm scale.

Together this supports the qualitative story that EPT produces a finer, more homogeneous microstructure and that this same EPT condition shows improved strength-ductility balance in tension — consistent with grain/feature refinement contributing to the mechanical change. What it leaves open: no grain-size statistics, texture, or second-phase data are given to confirm feature refinement is grain size per se or the actual mechanism (versus e.g. residual stress relief, dislocation density changes); panels (b) and (c) were not reviewed here so the "finer than" comparison is taken on faith from the caption; and the SEM label mismatch means correspondence between F10d and the F13 blue curve is inferred, not confirmed.

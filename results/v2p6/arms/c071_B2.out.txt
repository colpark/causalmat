The quantitative panels extend and partly resolve the earlier qualitative picture. Figure 5B/C give matched numeric platelet counts and activation percentages that confirm the SEM trend: 316L SS and PPAmF carry ~32-41×10² platelets/mm² at ~65-74% activation, versus ~15% (BVLD-PPAmF), ~11% (NO-PPAmF), and ~5×10² at near-0% activation (BVLD/NO-PPAmF) — the combination surface is lowest overall. Figure 6's ex vivo arteriovenous-shunt circulation model then shows the functional payoff: after 2 h of real blood flow, bare/PPAmF surfaces are heavily occluded (58-63% occlusion, 64-74 mg thrombus, flow crashed to ~17-20% of baseline), while BVLD/NO-PPAmF sustains ~90% of baseline flow with ~3% occlusion and ~2 mg thrombus, significantly outperforming NO-PPAmF alone on occlusion and thrombus weight (**, *) and BVLD-PPAmF alone on flow rate.

This justifies concluding that BVLD-bearing coatings, especially combined with NO, translate into materially better hemocompatibility under real flowing blood, not just static assays — the anti-adhesive/anti-thrombotic effect is functionally consequential. What remains uncertain: because NO-PPAmF alone already outperforms bare surfaces functionally despite unchanged thrombin activity, the flow-model improvement cannot be attributed to thrombin suppression; platelet-count/activation suppression and/or NO's independent antiplatelet action are more plausible drivers. Which specific mechanism underlies the BVLD/NO synergy in flow performance is not resolved by these panels.

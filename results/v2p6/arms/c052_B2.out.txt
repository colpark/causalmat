Both panels match the reported measurements. F2a (FTIR): the red BST-SH trace alone shows distinct new bands labeled 2930, 2864, and 1122 cm-1, absent from the BST and BST-OH traces above it. F2b (photo): the BST-OH vial is cloudy/white and settled in the lower (aqueous) layer, while the BST-SH vial appears clear/uniform, dispersing into the upper (toluene) layer.

What the combination supports: the surface chemistry change FTIR detects (new CH2/Si-O-type organic groups on BST-SH only) co-occurs with a macroscopic functional consequence — BST-SH becomes organophilic/hydrophobic enough to disperse in toluene rather than water, unlike unmodified BST-OH. This is consistent with, and corroborates the practical relevance of, the surface modification already established by FTIR+TGA: it shows that whatever new species was chemically identified and mass-confirmed also changes the particle's wetting/partitioning behavior in the expected direction.

What it leaves open: this single two-vial photograph is qualitative, not quantitative — no measure of dispersion degree, particle loading, or reproducibility. It does not distinguish covalent Si-O-BST grafting from strong physisorption, nor does it quantify surface coverage or tie band intensity to dispersibility. Other factors (particle size, aggregation state) affecting settling cannot be ruled out from a photograph alone.

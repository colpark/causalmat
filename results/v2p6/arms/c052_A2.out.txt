Both panels are confirmed as described. F2a (FTIR) shows the BST-SH trace (red) with distinct new bands at 2930 and 2864 cm-1 (C-H stretches) and 1122 cm-1, none of which appear in the BST or BST-OH traces — consistent with successful grafting of a thiol-bearing organic layer onto the particle surface. F2b (photograph) shows BST-OH settled as a white sediment in the lower (aqueous) layer of the water-toluene vial, while BST-SH remains dispersed/visible in the upper (toluene) layer, i.e., a clear hydrophilic-to-hydrophobic switch in wettability.

Together the two panels support the claim that surface chemistry (new organic/thiol-related bonds seen only in BST-SH) tracks with the downstream partitioning behavior: the chemically modified BST-SH particles are more organophilic and migrate to the toluene phase, whereas the untreated/OH particles stay in water. This is a strong correlational pairing — spectroscopic evidence of a chemical change co-occurring with a macroscopic dispersion-property change.

What it leaves open: FTIR alone cannot prove the new bands are specifically thiol (S-H) groups rather than another grafted moiety with similar C-H/C-O features (no direct S-H stretch, e.g., ~2550 cm-1, is labeled), and the photograph is a single qualitative demonstration (no quantitative partition coefficient, no replicate, no intermediate BST-SH/water control) directly linking the identified bonds mechanistically to the dispersion outcome rather than merely co-varying with it.

The macro/cross-section evidence (F6b, F6c) shows a thrombus gradient across the five surfaces that lines up with the SEM platelet pattern already established, not with the thrombin-activity assay. 316L SS and PPAmF (many spread, dendritic platelets) have lumens fully packed with dark thrombus; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF (fewer platelets) are only partly occluded; BVLD/NO-PPAmF (few round, unspread platelets) is nearly clean. Because NO-PPAmF sits in the "partly open" tier despite having unaltered thrombin activity, this downstream result is consistent with the platelet-suppression reading, not the thrombin-activity reading — reinforcing the earlier caveat that thrombin activity does not track platelet suppression (or, here, occlusion) across all four/five surfaces.

What this combination supports: a qualitative, ordinal correspondence between platelet spreading/activation (SEM) and gross thrombus burden (photos/cross-sections), with BVLD/NO-PPAmF giving the strongest anti-thrombotic outcome even though its thrombin activity equals BVLD-PPAmF's — implying an additional, non-thrombin-mediated contribution (plausibly NO-related) to the combined coating's superior performance.

What it leaves open: no quantitative thrombus mass/occlusion score is given, only categorical photographic description, so no dose-response link to platelet counts or thrombin activity can be established; the causal contribution of thrombin suppression versus platelet suppression versus NO's independent action to the observed occlusion cannot be separated from this evidence alone.

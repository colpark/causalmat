The three panels are consistent with each other and match the two upstream facts exactly. F6a shows the diffusion-controlled second absorption stage lasting 66 min (curve plateaus near a 4.5-min-to-75-min window at 360°C), and F6b shows the same stage collapsing to 11 min — reproducing evidence B precisely, and by elimination identifying F6a as C0 and F6b as C10. Together this supports the claim that in the catalyzed (C10) material, the C@Co phase sits on particle surfaces, and that same C10 material shows a much shorter diffusion-limited absorption stage than uncatalyzed C0. That is a real structure–kinetics correlation for C10 taken alone.

What it leaves open: there is no EDS map of C0 shown here, so the evidence cannot show that C0 lacks this surface distribution or that surface catalyst placement, rather than some other C10 difference, causes the faster kinetics. Causation/mechanism is not established, only co-occurrence in one alloy.

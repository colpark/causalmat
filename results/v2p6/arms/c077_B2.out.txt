F3b (ICP-AES) shows Zn At% rising from 0 (pristine) to ~50% by 20th cycle and holding near 47% at 60th/100th cycles, while K, Fe, Mn all decline sharply over the same cycles (Mn essentially vanishing by 100th). F3a (XRD) shows pristine KMnHCF matching the KMnHCF JCPDS reference; by the 4th cycle two new peaks appear (near 16.5° and 21.9°, flagged by dashed guide lines) that align with the KZnHCF JCPDS reference, and the original KMnHCF peaks progressively weaken through the 20th/60th/100th-cycle traces as the KZnHCF-like peaks strengthen.

Combined, these two observations support that as Zn content in the electrode rises (A), the bulk crystal structure is converting toward a Zn-hexacyanoferrate-type lattice at the expense of the original Mn-hexacyanoferrate phase (B) — i.e., co-occurring compositional uptake and a phase change toward a KZnHCF-like structure, consistent with Zn insertion/exchange restructuring the framework rather than merely occupying pristine KMnHCF sites unchanged.

This leaves open: whether the transformation is a solid-solution/topotactic conversion or a dissolution-reprecipitation process; whether the new phase is homogeneous or coexists as a mixture with residual KMnHCF (no Rietveld/quantitative phase fractions given); why Mn and Fe also drop toward zero (not addressed by A or B as stated); and whether the ICP and XRD samples are the exact same cycled electrodes, since both are bulk/ensemble measurements, not site- or particle-resolved.

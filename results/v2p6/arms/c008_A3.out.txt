Both panels are confirmed as printed. F4a (a) plots apparent porosity vs. sintering temperature for S1, S2, S3, matching the reported drop (S1 8.7→0.4%, S2 7.9→0.7%, S3 11.2→7.7%). F5 plots Vickers hardness vs. temperature for the same three series, matching the reported rise (S1 9.3→15.6, S2 9.4→14.8, S3 9.0→14.1 GPa).

What the combination supports: across all three composites, the temperature range over which porosity falls steeply is the same range over which hardness rises steeply — e.g., S1 and S2 both hit their sharpest porosity drop near 1550-1600°C, coinciding with their steepest hardness gains. This is consistent with densification (pore elimination) being a major contributor to the hardness increase, as is standard for sintered ceramics.

What it leaves open: these are two separate temperature-series plots, not a direct porosity-vs-hardness plot or statistical fit, so no quantitative or causal link is established — only co-occurring trends with temperature as the shared axis. S3 is a counterexample worth flagging: its porosity stays high (7.7% at 1600°C, never approaching S1/S2's near-zero values) yet its hardness still climbs to a comparable 14.1 GPa, meaning porosity reduction alone cannot fully account for hardness gains; other temperature-driven factors (grain growth, phase/binder changes) are not ruled out or addressed by these two panels.

Both panels confirm the fracture-surface description already relied upon. F2c (low mag) shows a lamellar-colony fracture surface with tear ridges and elongated grooves that trace the lamellar colonies, plus scattered dark holes; F2d (high mag) shows the same surface zoomed in, with arrows marking small holes and boxes marking microcracks, both sited at lamella ends/edges within the colony structure.

Combined with the settled TEM dislocation-content progression (mottled contrast to tangled lines to dense walls), this evidence supports the claim that damage features (holes, microcracks) are spatially associated with lamella/phase boundaries, and that dislocation accumulation at such boundaries is a plausible contributing mechanism — the two observations are consistent with each other in a general, structural sense.

It does not establish a direct causal or spatially co-registered link: the TEM images and the SEM fracture images are not from the same imaged sites, so no specific dislocation density is tied to a specific hole or crack. It remains open whether dislocation-wall buildup drives nucleation or whether the lamella-boundary geometry itself is sufficient to nucleate damage independent of dislocation content. The panels only substantiate location (lamella ends/edges) and morphology (holes, microcracks, tear ridges) of the damage, not the mechanistic driver behind it.

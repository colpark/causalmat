Both panels were opened and checked against the stated measurements.

F5c (power factor vs T): confirms A exactly — x=0.01 (red) is highest across the whole range, ~29 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² at 300 K falling to ~22 at 623 K; x=0.005 (black) is the lowest of the doped/substituted set; x=0 (green) is far below all of these, topping out near 5.

F6a (ZT vs T): confirms B — x=0.01 (red) again sits at or near the top of the curve family, peaking at roughly 1.25 near 500–525 K before declining at higher T.

What the combination supports: the compositional ranking is consistent between the two properties — x=0.01 has both the highest power factor at every measured temperature and the highest peak ZT, so the upstream PF advantage is compatible with, and helps account for, x=0.01's superior ZT. What it leaves open: PF alone cannot explain the ZT curve's shape — PF for x=0.01 declines monotonically above ~350 K, yet ZT keeps rising until ~500–525 K before falling. That rise-then-fall in ZT implies a temperature-dependent thermal conductivity contribution (ZT = S²σT/κ) that is not shown in either panel, so the specific temperature of the ZT peak, and the magnitude of the ZT advantage, are not accounted for by the PF data alone.

Both panels confirm the top-line claim. F5c: x=0.01 (red) has the highest PF across the full 300-623K range, ~29 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² near 300K falling to ~22 at 623K; x=0 (green) is far lower, rising only to about 5; x=0.005 (black) is the lowest of the doped set through most of the range. F7c: at Th=623K, x=0.01 reaches ~11.8% efficiency, clearly above the other doped compositions, which cluster near 9.5-10.6%.

So the combination supports: x=0.01's PF advantage, sustained across the whole temperature range, is consistent with and helps account for its highest efficiency at 623K — a coherent story for the top performer.

What it leaves open: the PF ranking does not cleanly track the eta ranking among the other compositions. x=0.005 has the lowest PF of the doped set, yet in F7c its efficiency (~9.5-9.7%) is not the lowest — x=0.04 is comparably low or lower despite a higher PF at most temperatures. So PF alone does not explain relative efficiency ordering beyond the top composition; efficiency must depend on other quantities (e.g., thermal conductivity/ZT) not shown in either panel. Also, x=0 appears in F5c but not in F7c, so its efficiency cannot be checked against the PF claim at all.

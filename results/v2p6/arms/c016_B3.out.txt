Both panels match the stated measurements. F2a (GATR-FTIR) shows the PPAm trace (blue) with baseline N-H/C-H/C=O-C=N bands and the NO-PPAm trace (red) with additional starred peaks labeled NO vibration, N=N stretch, N-N stretch (twice), and N-O stretch that are absent from PPAm — consistent with claim A. F2b (chemiluminescence) shows NO flux rising to a peak near 35×10⁻¹⁰ mol cm⁻² min⁻¹ at about 5 min, then decaying toward baseline by ~60 min — consistent with claim B.

Together the two panels support that NO exposure both (a) creates new nitrogen-oxygen/nitrogen-nitrogen bonded species in the film (chemical incorporation, not mere physisorption) and (b) that this loaded coating subsequently releases NO gas in a burst-then-decay kinetic profile at a physiologically relevant flux. This licenses the combined conclusion that PPAm chemically binds NO and can then donate it back over roughly an hour.

What remains open: the panels give no evidence linking the specific new bonds (N-N, N-O, N=N) quantitatively to the amount or rate of gas released — FTIR is qualitative presence/absence on one sample pair, and chemiluminescence is a separate flux measurement on (presumably) the same or a similar sample; there is no shared quantitative bridge, no stoichiometry, and no exclusion of alternative NO-containing byproducts contributing to either signal.

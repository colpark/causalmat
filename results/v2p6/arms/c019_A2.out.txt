Both panels were opened and inspected.

F3 (polarization): Mg-Zn-Y-Nd has Ecorr ≈ -1.49 V vs 317L SS's -0.25 V, and Icorr ≈24.93 µA/cm² vs 317L SS's 0.017 µA/cm² — roughly three orders of magnitude higher, matching the "2-3 decades" claim. This is a static, essentially initial-time electrochemical snapshot.

F5a (pH): Mg-Zn-Y-Nd pH climbs from 7.4 to a plateau near 10 by ~day 22; 317L SS falls to a minimum near 6.1 by day 20 then partially recovers; blank control rises to ~8.4 by day 3 then declines slowly to ~7.5-7.6.

What the combination supports: the polarization data establish that Mg-Zn-Y-Nd is far more electrochemically active (lower, less noble Ecorr, much higher Icorr) than 317L SS. This is directionally consistent with — and offers a plausible mechanistic basis for — the sustained alkalization seen with Mg-Zn-Y-Nd, since active Mg corrosion (hydrogen evolution/hydroxide release) is a known driver of pH rise. It also supports that 317L SS, being far more passive, would release comparatively little corrosion product.

What it leaves open: the polarization measurement is not a time-resolved series, so it cannot quantitatively account for the specific pH trajectory (rate, plateau timing) over 30 days. It offers no explanation for the 317L SS pH decline to 6.1 or the blank control's rise-then-fall — neither pattern follows from a low, stable Icorr. No quantitative ion-release/pH model links the two datasets; the connection is qualitative/directional only.

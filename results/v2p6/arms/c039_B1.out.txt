The upstream evidence (zeta-potential reversal + EDS phosphorus dots inside the C/Zn/N outline) supports only that the anionic aptamer is associated with the particle surface — it says nothing about in vivo behavior. The downstream observation is a different, independent measurement: serum ALT, AST, BUN and CREA in mice given aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 by tail-vein (F7b table) or subcutaneous (F7c table) injection stayed within the printed reference ranges at every time point from 3–14 days. This is a biocompatibility/toxicity readout, not a consequence of the surface-attachment chemistry, so the upstream attachment evidence does not "account for" it — the two results are complementary but mechanistically disconnected. What the combination jointly supports: the particles carry an aptamer coating (per the earlier qualified conclusion) and, separately, dosing with that same construct did not produce overt hepatic or renal biomarker abnormality over the two-week window tested, by either route. What it leaves open: whether the aptamer coating itself (vs. bare BSA@ZIF-8) is responsible for the normal biomarker values (no bare-particle comparison arm shown here); toxicity beyond 14 days or beyond these four analytes; histopathology detail (the H&E claim is captioned but not shown in these three panels); and any link between surface coverage extent (unquantified upstream) and the tolerability outcome.

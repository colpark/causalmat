The phase-identity evidence (XRD+SAED: Co remains a separate, unreacted phase; CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17, Mg all unchanged in milled C10) is consistent with, and helps explain, the downstream capacity drop seen in F7a/F7b: C0 reaches ~5.1 wt% at 250 C while C10 reaches ~4.56 wt%, a ~10-11% relative loss that roughly tracks the 10 wt% inert C@Co addition (5.1 x 0.9 ≈ 4.6). Because diffraction shows no new Co-alloy compound formed and the parent hydride-forming phases are structurally intact, the simplest reading the evidence supports is that the capacity loss is largely a dilution effect from adding a non-hydriding Co/carbon component, not a change in the intrinsic hydrogen-storage chemistry of the Mg-Ce-Y phases. This is a plausible, evidence-consistent account, not a proven mechanism: neither XRD nor SAED measures hydrogen uptake, so the phase data cannot itself quantify how much of the 4.56 vs 5.1 wt% gap is dilution versus altered kinetics/thermodynamics. It also cannot rule out sub-diffraction interfacial/amorphous reaction at Co-alloy contacts that could suppress capacity independent of dilution, nor confirm the curves have reached true equilibrium plateau rather than a 240-min readout.

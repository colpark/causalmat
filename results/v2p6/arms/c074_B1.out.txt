Both panels are confirmed as captioned. F1a (DFT, matches Observation A) shows CoCrOOH lowest for the Co2+→Co3+ step, CoFeOOH lowest for Co3+→Co4+, CoFeCrOOH intermediate on both, and Co(OH)2 highest on both. F5c (LSV, matches Observation B) shows Co5Fe3Cr2 reaching 10 mA cm-2 first (η10 = 452 mV), ahead of Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5, Co, and IrO2.

The combination supports a qualitative, associative point: a Cr/Fe-mixed Co oxyhydroxide is both computed to have favorable (low ΔG) Co-oxidation energetics among the four modeled compositions, and a differently-labeled Cr/Fe-mixed sample is measured to have the best OER activity among the five tested electrodes, including outperforming IrO2. That is as far as the evidence goes.

It leaves open several things. First, the DFT panel's composition set (CoCrOOH, CoFeOOH, CoFeCrOOH, Co(OH)2) does not literally match the LSV panel's set (Co5Fe3Cr2, Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5, Co, IrO2); nothing in these two panels establishes that CoFeCrOOH is the same material as Co5Fe3Cr2. Second, LSV overpotential is a lumped kinetic/activity measure that folds in conductivity, surface area, and other steps beyond Co2+/3+/4+ oxidation, so a favorable computed ΔG for that one step is not shown to be the cause of, or even the dominant contributor to, the measured activity ranking. No mechanistic decomposition links the two.

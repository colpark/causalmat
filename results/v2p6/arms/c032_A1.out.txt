What the combination supports: through the first 5 cycles, the microstructural crack network (as sampled in this one field) does not show detectable change, and this is consistent with — not contradicted by — the macroscopic observation that the coupon is still visually intact at 5 cycles. So the upstream SEM evidence and the downstream photographic record agree on the state at the 5-cycle mark: no visible degradation yet, at either scale.

What it leaves open: the SEM evidence stops at 5 cycles, but the spallation event (corners lost) happens between 5 and 10 cycles, and continues to grow out to 40 cycles — no SEM crack-network images are shown for 10, 20, 30, or 40 cycles. So this pairing cannot show whether the unchanged crack network foreshadows, causes, or is unrelated to the later spallation; it cannot establish that crack growth (rather than, say, interfacial delamination not visible in this planar crack-network view) is the mechanism driving corner spallation. The two measurements are compatible but do not causally connect microstructure evolution to the observed damage/service-capability loss beyond cycle 5.

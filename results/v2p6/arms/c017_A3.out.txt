All three panels confirm the stated readings. F5a (SEM, PRP 2h): 316LSS and PPAmF show many spread, dendritic platelets; BVLD-PPAmF fewer and more variable in shape; NO-PPAmF fewer still with more round/unspread forms; BVLD/NO-PPAmF shows only sparse small round platelets. F6c (macro photos of excised thrombus) and F6b (cross-sectional tube photos) both show the same ordering: 316LSS and PPAmF are covered/filled by dark-red thrombus; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are only partly covered, with visible clean (light) substrate; BVLD/NO-PPAmF is almost entirely clean with thrombus confined to edges.

What the combination supports: a consistent rank-order relationship across the same five surfaces — greater platelet adhesion/spreading in the static PRP assay co-occurs with heavier, more occlusive thrombus in the blood-contact assay, and the combined NO/BVLD-releasing surface shows the least of both. This is legitimate corroborating evidence that reduced platelet activation on the modified surfaces is associated with, and plausibly contributes to, reduced downstream thrombus/service performance.

What it leaves open: the two measurements come from different assay conditions (static platelet-rich plasma, 2h, no flow, in F5a vs. actual circulating whole blood in F6b/F6c), so this is a cross-experiment correlation, not a within-experiment causal demonstration. No quantitative platelet counts or thrombus-area measurements are given here to size the effect, and thrombus formation also depends on coagulation-cascade/fibrin factors not addressed by SEM platelet morphology alone. So the evidence justifies "consistent with, and suggestive of, platelet response as a contributing mechanism" but does not establish it as the sole or quantified cause of the PRF/service_capability difference.

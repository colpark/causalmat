The three panels are consistent with, and extend, the earlier finding. F3d (CnC HS) shows a largely flat/granular surface with only a few thin, sparse protruding platelets. F3e (C HS) shows a uniformly dense mat of sharp platelets covering the whole field. This matches observation A exactly. Combined, this supports a coherent narrative: the Zn-N bonding/flat-deposit picture on CnC HS versus the dendritic platelet growth on C HS temporally co-occurs with divergent cycling outcomes — sustained high CE (good service capability) on CnC HS versus early CE collapse (cell failure, likely dendrite-driven shorting or dead-Zn side reactions) on C HS. The panels make the morphology-outcome pairing visually and temporally plausible.

What it leaves open: this is still correlation, not a causal test — no experiment isolates platelet morphology as the sole driver of the CE drop, and the earlier-noted confound (CnC HS and C HS differ in more than nitrogen bonding/morphology) still applies. The mechanism linking dense sharp platelets to the specific 0% CE collapse (short circuit vs. dead Zn vs. side reaction) is not shown, and this is a single-cell, single-condition dataset with no replication.

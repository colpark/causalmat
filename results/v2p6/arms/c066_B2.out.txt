Combination supports only a limited, indirect link. The XRD reflections (F6a) for HP5 index solely to ZrB2, SiC, C (graphite) and BN — no AlN or Al2O3 lines appear — while the hardness trend (F2b) shows HP-processed hardness rising from ~18.0 GPa (0 wt%) to a peak ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt% AlN, then easing to ~19.4 and ~19.2 GPa at 3 and 5 wt%, all still above the 0 wt% baseline. Together these let one say that whatever hardness benefit AlN confers in the hot-pressed series is not attributable to a retained AlN phase or an oxidized Al2O3 by-product, since XRD (understood to be taken on the HP5 specimen) detects neither — consistent with AlN having been consumed (e.g., as a sintering aid/reactant) rather than persisting as a separate hard or soft secondary phase.

What it leaves open: XRD is a single bulk pattern, apparently from one composition, so it cannot explain the composition-dependent shape of the hardness curve — why hardness peaks at 1 wt% and then declines at 3–5 wt%. No phase or microstructural data are given per composition, and no mechanism (densification, grain size, defect content) linking phase absence to the specific hardness values is established. The XRD/hardness pairing rules out one candidate explanation (retained AlN/Al2O3) but does not identify the actual cause of the trend.

The two panels (F2a relative density, F2b Vickers hardness, both HP vs PS series) confirm the density figures: HP density is ~97.5% at 0 wt% AlN, rises to ~100% at 1 wt%, dips slightly to ~99.3% at 3 wt%, and returns to ~100% at 5 wt% — essentially flat and near-full density from 1–5 wt%. HP hardness tracks the same qualitative shape: ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt%, peaking at ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then declining to ~19.4 and ~19.2 GPa at 3 and 5 wt%.

What this combination supports: the low-porosity/high-density condition (near 100% for HP across 1–5 wt%) is consistent with, and helps explain, why HP hardness sits well above the porous PS series (which falls from ~86% to ~70% density and from ~12 to ~7 GPa hardness) at every AlN level — i.e., porosity differences between processing routes plausibly account for the large HP-vs-PS hardness gap.

What it leaves open: density alone does not account for the non-monotonic hardness trend within the HP series. Density is essentially flat (~99.3–100%) from 1 to 5 wt% AlN, yet hardness peaks at 1 wt% and then drops at 3 and 5 wt%. Since porosity is not decreasing over that range, some other factor (grain size, AlN distribution/secondary phases, error bars shown in F2b) must be responsible for the post-1-wt% hardness decline. The panels alone cannot identify that mechanism.

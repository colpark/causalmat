Panels viewed: F3b (DPBF relative absorbance vs time, bismuthene falling to ~0.73 at 30 min vs ~0.93 for H2O) and F5d (six tumor-growth-curve panels; BNs-BSA+808nm shows 4/5 curves regrowing after ~day 14–15; BNs-BSA+Lasers, i.e. combined 660/808nm, shows all five curves flat near 0 through day 20).

The combination is consistent with, but does not establish, an explanation. The upstream findings (narrow ~0.69 eV band gap compatible with 660 nm photon energy, plus faster DPBF signal loss with bismuthene than water) speak only to a light-driven reactive-oxygen/photodynamic capability associated with the 660 nm channel, assayed in a cell-free system at one timepoint. This can account for why adding the 660 nm laser matters: the full suppression seen only when both lasers are combined (BNs-BSA+Lasers, all five flat) is consistent with a 660 nm-linked photodynamic contribution supplementing whatever the 808 nm channel does, so 808 nm alone leaving 4/5 tumors to regrow is compatible with that channel lacking this photodynamic support.

What it leaves open: the upstream evidence says nothing about 808 nm-specific (likely photothermal) activity, so it cannot explain the 808 nm-alone mechanism or the day-14 regrowth timing itself; it offers no in vivo, dose, or synergy data linking the modest in vitro absorbance difference to the sharp in vivo dichotomy (partial vs complete control); and no wavelength-resolved action spectrum ties the 0.69 eV value quantitatively to either laser condition's outcome.

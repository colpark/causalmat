The combination supports only a weak, qualitative correlation, not an explanation. The optical panels (b-f) show SiCp particles broadly spread with local clusters, and the visual density of dark particles increases from panel to panel (consistent with the labeled 15/20/25 wt% series in d/e/f), which is compatible with more reinforcing phase being present as SiCp content rises. That is congruent with the observation that UCS climbs step-wise (310→363 MPa) across the same compositional series — a reader can plausibly link "more particles visible" to "more reinforcement, hence higher measured strength."

But the imaging evidence does not account for the UCS trend in any mechanistic or quantitative sense. There is no particle count, area fraction, or spacing measurement tied to each specific UCS value, so the panels cannot be used to explain the magnitude or shape of the strength increase. No load-transfer, grain-refinement, or dislocation-strengthening mechanism is shown or measured. The local clusters seen in every panel are exactly the kind of feature that could act as stress concentrators and lower strength, yet the UCS still rises — the images give no basis to reconcile that. Panels b and c are not explicitly tied to a wt% here, breaking a clean one-to-one mapping across the full series. So: correlation is visually plausible; causation and quantitative correspondence remain unestablished.

Yes, the combination supports a real (if partial) link between the two, more than the earlier pristine-only comparison did. Panel F3a's cycle-indexed XRD shows two new peaks (~16.5°, ~21.9°) emerging after just 4 cycles and growing while the original KMnHCF peaks weaken through the 20th, 60th, and 100th cycle traces; panel F3b's ICP shows Zn at% rising in step over the same cycle points (4th→20th→100th), climbing to ~50 at% by cycle 20 and holding near 47 at% thereafter. Because both datasets are indexed to the same cycle numbers, the temporal correlation is now compositional and structural, not just phase-match-at-one-timepoint versus a separate compositional curve as before: the growth of the new diffraction peaks and the decline of the original ones track the same cycling window over which Zn is being taken up. This justifies saying the structural change (new peaks, weakening host peaks) and the compositional change (rising, then plateauing Zn) co-occur and progress together across cycling. It does not establish that the new XRD peaks specifically index to a Zn-containing phase (no refinement/indexing against the KZnHCF reference pattern shown at bottom of F3a is reported in the given data), nor that the bulk Zn at% from ICP resides in the sites producing those new peaks rather than elsewhere (e.g., surface deposit or side phase). So the evidence supports co-occurring compositional and structural change with cycling, but not a confirmed mechanistic or site-resolved link between Zn uptake and the specific new phase.

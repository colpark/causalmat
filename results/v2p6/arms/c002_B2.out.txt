Panels confirm the pairing: F3a is the XRD pattern showing C0 (0 wt% C@Co) reflections 1–4 (CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17, Mg) reproduced in C10 (+10 wt% C@Co), plus the weak line 5 near 44.5° assigned to Co. F7a/F7b are the 250 °C absorption curves: C0 plateaus near ~5.1 wt% (panel a, black squares) and C10 plateaus near ~4.56 wt% (panel b, black squares), matching the stated capacities.

Does the upstream phase evidence account for the downstream capacity drop? Only partially. The established XRD+SAED result shows Co addition introduces no new crystalline phase and leaves the C0 hydride-forming phases (CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17, Mg) structurally unchanged after milling — this is consistent with a simple dilution picture in which the ~10 wt% Co simply adds inert mass without altering or degrading the host alloy's identity, which would naturally lower the wt%-based plateau capacity from ~5.1 to ~4.56. In that sense the phase-identity finding is compatible with, and offers a plausible structural basis for, the observed capacity decrease.

What it leaves open: the diffraction/SAED evidence never measures hydrogen uptake, capacity, or Co's actual reactivity — it only shows no new crystalline phase forms. It cannot rule out that Co (or an undetected amorphous/interfacial layer, per the earlier qualifications) also alters diffusion pathways, blocks surface sites, or otherwise affects kinetics/capacity beyond simple mass dilution. It also doesn't quantify how much of the sample is Co versus alloy, so "dilution accounts for the full ~0.54 wt% drop" is not directly verified, only rendered plausible. The phase data and the capacity data are consistent with each other but the phase data alone does not explain or predict the magnitude of the capacity change.

Figure 10a (this evidence): tensile stress–strain curve along [0001] at RT shows yielding near 60–70 MPa and hardening to a ~70–80 MPa plateau, with plastic strain extending to ~45%; Fig. 10b shows this plastic flow is accommodated by many deformation kink bands.

Figure 2 (step 2, same [0001] orientation): in Fig. 2b (RT), the [0001] curve is a near-vertical line to an "X" marked at ~355 MPa with negligible plastic strain before that point — i.e., essentially elastic loading to a fracture stress, with no yield-stress data point or plastic curve plotted for [0001] (unlike the other three orientations, which show measurable yield stresses of ~5–170 MPa). Fig. 2a plots this same ~350 MPa (RT) point as "fracture" on a black curve declining to ~170 MPa at 400°C.

Comparison: the stress at which Fig. 10 shows sustained plastic deformation along [0001] (~60–80 MPa) is roughly 4–5x lower than the ~355 MPa fracture stress Fig. 2 reports for that orientation, and Fig. 2's curve shows essentially no plastic strain before fracture. This implies two distinct deformation outcomes for [0001] loading: one route where the crystal fractures near-elastically at high stress with no accommodation, and another (seen in Fig. 10) where extensive plastic flow occurs at much lower stress via kink-band formation instead of fracture. What is left uncertain: these panels do not state whether the Fig. 10 and Fig. 2 specimens share the same microstructural condition, so whether kink banding is an alternative route available under specific (e.g., nanoplate-bearing) conditions, or a separate/inconsistent measurement, cannot be settled from these images alone.

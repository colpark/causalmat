The optical panels (F1b-f) show SiCp particle density increasing across the series, consistent with the stated wt% progression: sparse, well-separated dark particles in b/c, becoming markedly denser with visible local clustering (chains/patches of coalesced particles) in d, e, and especially f. This is directionally consistent with observation B — that the 15/20/25 wt% composites (higher, more particle-dense microstructures) show lower minimum creep rates than AZ91, while the 5/10 wt% composites (sparser particle fields) overlap or exceed the matrix baseline. So the combination supports a plausible link: more reinforcement content, visible as denser particle coverage in the micrographs, tracks with improved creep resistance at 15 wt% and above, and the transition roughly coincides with where clustering becomes visually prominent.

What it leaves open: the panels give no quantitative particle-size, volume-fraction, or clustering-degree measurement, only qualitative visual density, so the correspondence to specific wt% values is inferred from panel order, not confirmed by the image content itself. The micrographs also cannot establish mechanism — whether particles suppress creep via load transfer, grain-boundary pinning, or dislocation interaction — nor can they distinguish beneficial dispersion from clustering as a defect that could instead degrade properties. No stress/temperature conditions link the two datasets directly.

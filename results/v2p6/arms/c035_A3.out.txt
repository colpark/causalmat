Both panels confirm the stated measurements. F5c (PF vs T) shows x=0.01 (red) highest throughout, from ~29 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² at 300 K down to ~22 at 623 K; x=0.005 (black) is the lowest of the doped series (~23 to ~13); x=0 (green) stays at most ~5. F6a (ZT vs T) shows x=0.01 peaking at ~1.25-1.3 near 500-525 K, above the other curves.

The combination supports a qualitative link: the same sample (x=0.01) that has the highest power factor across the whole temperature range also has the highest ZT, and the undoped x=0 sample is lowest in both, consistent with PF being a contributor to the ZT ranking.

What it leaves open: PF peaks near 350-400 K and then declines, while ZT continues rising to a peak at 500-525 K—PF alone does not explain the location of the ZT maximum. Since ZT = PF·T/κ, this mismatch implies thermal conductivity (not shown in either panel) must be doing substantial work in shaping the ZT curve's shape and peak position; the two panels alone cannot establish that.

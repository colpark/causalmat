The panels (b-f, 15/20/25 wt% SiCp for d/e/f, presumably lower wt% for b/c) show a monotonic increase in dark-particle areal density from b through f, with particles staying broadly spread across each field even as the content rises, plus small local clusters at every loading. This is consistent with the UCS series (310→363 MPa) rising step-wise with SiCp content: each increment in reinforcement content is accompanied by more particles dispersed through the matrix rather than by increasing agglomeration, so the strength gain can plausibly be attributed to added, distributed reinforcement rather than to a shift toward gross clustering/segregation that would be expected to degrade strength.

That is as far as the combination goes. The imaging gives no particle count, area-fraction, or clustering-index measurement, so "more particles, still dispersed" is a visual impression, not a quantified link to the specific MPa increments. Neither the optical series nor the earlier SEM comparison examines fracture surfaces or crack-initiation sites, so the mechanism connecting dispersion to load-bearing capacity (load transfer, grain refinement, dislocation strengthening) is not established. The UCS trend also has no reported statistical test, and panel-to-panel content matching to b/c is inferred, not captioned. So the evidence supports plausibility, not a demonstrated causal or quantitative relationship between dispersion and the observed strength increase.

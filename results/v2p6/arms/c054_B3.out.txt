The panels examined: F4b shows BST-SH/PAEN fracture surface (no obvious discrete particles, rough texture); F4c (BST@Ag1%/PAEN) shows a finely fractured surface with faint particle traces but no visible agglomerates; F4e (BST@Ag5%/PAEN) shows numerous distinct round particles studding the fracture surface, still spread evenly with no large clumps; F7a is the tensile-strength bar chart, showing a monotonic decline from 79.3 (pure PAEN) to 78.3 (1% Ag) to 74.4 (3% Ag) to 73.5 MPa (5% Ag).

What this justifies: the upstream SEM evidence (good local interfacial bonding at 1% Ag, and even particle distribution without large agglomerates across the modified-filler composites, visible even at 5% loading in F4e) supports that the modest tensile-strength decline with increasing Ag content is NOT attributable to filler agglomeration or gross interfacial debonding — dispersion remains visually uniform across the loading range shown.

What it leaves open: the SEM observations do not explain the mechanism of the strength drop itself. Observation A's bonding assessment is specific to 1% Ag only, so interfacial quality at 3% and 5% (where strength falls most) is not directly imaged. No agglomerate size, particle spacing, or interfacial gap was measured, so no dose-response link between microstructure and the MPa values can be established — the strength decrease could instead reflect increased rigid-particle content, reduced effective matrix cross-section, or reduced polymer crosslinking, none of which these qualitative micrographs can confirm or rule out.

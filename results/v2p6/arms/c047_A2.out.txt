Yes, the upstream DFT evidence plausibly accounts for the downstream rate trend, at least in rank order. Ordering compositions by |ΔG_H*| distance from the thermoneutral 0 eV point (Pd0.75Ag0.25: 0.08 < Pd0.5Ag0.5: 0.12 < Pd0.25Ag0.75: 0.28 < Pd: 0.31 < Ag: 0.49) reproduces exactly the experimental H2-evolution ranking (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS 125.4 > Ag0.5Pd0.5-ZIS 57.3 > Ag0.75Pd0.25-ZIS 43.5 > Pd-ZIS 34.5 > Ag-ZIS 3.2 µmol/h; F8a's time-course curves show the same ordering). This supports the paper's mechanistic claim that alloying Pd with Ag tunes hydrogen-adsorption free energy toward near-zero, optimizing the HER step and explaining why Pd0.75Ag0.25 is the best cocatalyst.

What it leaves open: the DFT is computed on bare (111) metal surfaces, not on the actual ZIS-supported nanoalloy/heterojunction used experimentally, so the correspondence is inferred, not measured on the real interface. ΔG_H* only captures the catalytic HER step; it cannot explain the ~83.6x jump from bare ZIS (1.5) to Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS (125.4), since ZIS has no DFT point and photon absorption/charge-separation contributions (not addressed by F9a) likely dominate that gap. The match is qualitative/rank-order, not a quantitative rate prediction, and no error bars or replicates are shown for either dataset.

F4c (EDS map, C10): green Mg particles show blue (C) and red (Co) signal concentrated on particle surfaces, denser along edges/facets than across the bulk faces — consistent with A's description of a surface/edge-localized C@Co catalyst decoration.

F6a/F6b (Sieverts absorption curves, 360°C): C0 (F6a) shows a slow second, diffusion-controlled stage lasting 66 min before plateauing near 5 wt%; C10 (F6b) reaches its plateau (~4.7 wt%) within 11 min, matching B's reported stage durations.

What the combination supports: the C@Co additive sits as a surface/edge coating on the Mg-alloy particles (not bulk-dissolved), and alloys bearing this coating (C10) absorb hydrogen through the diffusion-controlled stage far faster than the uncoated C0 alloy. The spatial location of the catalyst (particle surfaces/edges, likely grain-boundary-adjacent) is at least consistent with a catalytic role in accelerating that stage, since surface sites are where gas-phase H2 dissociation and entry would occur.

What it leaves open: no EDS map of C0 is shown for direct contrast, so the "distribution causes the kinetic change" link is inferential, not demonstrated side-by-side. F4c is a single static, qualitative image — no coverage density, particle-size, or site-count metric — so it cannot establish that this specific surface distribution, rather than composition or particle size differences, is responsible for the 66→11 min reduction. Mechanistic attribution (dissociation catalysis vs. shortened diffusion path vs. nucleation-site increase) is not settled by these panels alone.

Together, the SEM pair only samples one field up to 5 cycles, the same point at which the photographs still show the coupon intact. So the evidence does support that at 5 cycles the microstructure (at least in that field) has not yet visibly degraded, matching the still-intact macroscopic state. But the SEM series stops before the spallation event, which the photographs place between 5 and 10 cycles. The upstream micrograph pair therefore cannot account for the onset or mechanism of corner spallation, nor for its subsequent growth through 40 cycles — no SEM view at 10+ cycles or at a spalled corner is given. What's left open: whether the unchanged crack network at 5 cycles is a precursor condition to the later spallation, whether spallation originates at the coupon corners specifically (a location not shown in the SEM field), and what microstructural change (if any) accompanies or drives the 5→10-cycle spallation transition.

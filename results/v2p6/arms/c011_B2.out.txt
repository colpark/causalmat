Confirmed both panels: F5c (PF) shows x=0.01 highest across the range (~29 μW cm⁻¹K⁻² at 300K falling to ~22 at 623K), x=0.005 lowest of the doped set, x=0 far lowest (~2-5); F6a (ZT) shows x=0.01 also the highest curve, peaking at ~1.25-1.3 near 500-525K, with x=0 lowest throughout (~0.05-0.22).

What the combination supports: the composition ranking carries over from PF to ZT — x=0.01 is best and x=0 is worst on both quantities, so the upstream PF story (itself built on resistivity) is consistent with, and partially accounts for, why x=0.01 has the best ZT and x=0 the worst.

What it leaves open: the temperature dependence does not match. PF for x=0.01 falls monotonically with T (29→22, a ~24% drop 300→623K), while ZT for x=0.01 rises to a peak around 500-525K before falling — a non-monotonic shape PF alone cannot produce. Since ZT = PF·T/κ, this divergence means thermal conductivity's temperature dependence (not shown in either panel, no κ values given) must be doing real work to shape the ZT curve, especially the location and value of the peak. So the evidence supports the composition-ranking correspondence between PF and ZT but does not establish that PF (or resistivity) governs the ZT temperature profile — that requires κ, which is absent from this evidence. As with the earlier PF/resistivity step, this is co-variation/consistency in ranking, not a demonstrated mechanism for ZT.

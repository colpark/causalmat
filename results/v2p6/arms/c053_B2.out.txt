Both panels are consistent with the stated data: F4c (panel c) is a fracture-surface SEM at 1 µm scale showing a textured matrix with no sharply delineated particle boundaries or visible gaps — consistent with "particles covered by matrix, blurred outlines, no interfacial gaps." F7a is the tensile-strength bar chart showing 79.3, 78.3, 74.4, 73.5 MPa for Pure PAEN, BST@Ag1%, 3%, 5%, matching the reported values exactly.

The combination partially supports the interface story but does not fully account for the mechanical trend. The SEM/FTIR evidence (from the earlier step) speaks only to the 1% loading and only establishes qualitative intimate contact, not bond strength or magnitude. Tensile strength does not increase or hold steady with filler addition — it declines steadily and monotonically from 79.3 to 73.5 MPa as Ag content rises. The modest, gradual size of the decline (rather than a sharp drop) is consistent with good interfacial wetting limiting debonding-driven failure, so the "no gaps" SEM observation is not contradicted. But the upstream evidence, gathered only at BST@Ag1%, cannot explain the continued strength decrease at 3% and 5% Ag, nor distinguish whether that decline stems from particle loading/agglomeration effects, Ag itself, or a degrading interface at higher loadings — SEM was not shown at those higher concentrations. The strength trend is therefore left largely unaccounted for by the cited FTIR/SEM interface evidence.

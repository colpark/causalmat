The panels support the same broad division the ELISA/SEM pair already established. F6b (cross-sections) and F6c (thrombus photos) show 316LSS and PPAmF lumens/foils occluded by dark thrombus, while BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF are visibly more open or clean, matching the halved total-fibrinogen and gamma-chain levels (F5d, F5e) shared by all three functionalised coatings relative to controls. So the macro/photo readout and the ELISA readout agree on the coarse split: functionalisation (BVLD, NO, or both) reduces thrombus formation and fibrin(ogen) deposition versus bare/plain PPAmF.

They do not agree on fine ordering. The photos suggest BVLD/NO-PPAmF looks cleanest/most patent (nearly clean lumen and foil), yet ELISA shows BVLD/NO-PPAmF is not the lowest in total fibrinogen or gamma-chain among the three (0.061 and 0.065 vs ~0.053-0.054 for the single-agent coatings) — echoing the earlier SEM/ELISA discrepancy. Because NO-PPAmF (no BVLD) shows comparable photographic and ELISA reduction to BVLD-PPAmF, the combination still cannot attribute the effect specifically to BVLD-driven thrombin inactivation versus an NO-associated or generic PPAmF-coating effect; that attribution question remains open.

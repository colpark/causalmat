An earlier step in this work already established the following, and you may treat it as settled:

Together, the EDS composition data (Co/Ca = 0 for MBG, 0.14 for 2Co-MBG, 0.49 for 5Co-MBG) and the medium ion-release data (Co2+ absent for 0Co, and higher at every timepoint for 5Co than 2Co: ~8.9/17.9/20.8 mg/L vs ~4.7/10.5/16.9 mg/L at 1/3/7 days) support that the amount of Co incorporated into the scaffold sets the amount of Co2+ that is subsequently released into the medium, with the ordering 5Co > 2Co > 0Co holding both for embedded content and for released concentration at every measured day.

The qualifications recorded on that earlier result:
  - EDS Co/Ca is a single static compositional snapshot of the as-made scaffold, while release is a time course (days 1, 3, 7); pairing a fixed composition value against a rising time series only supports an ordering match (more embedded Co -> more released Co), not a quantitative dose-response or rate relationship
  - EDS Co/Ca is a normalized ratio (Co relative to Ca signal), whereas medium Co2+ is an absolute concentration (mg/L); these are different kinds of quantity and cannot be equated numerically, only rank-ordered
  - Only three scaffold groups (0Co, 2Co, 5Co) are compared, too few points to establish a continuous composition-release relationship beyond a monotonic ordering
  - Co content and group identity are set together by the synthesis design (0Co/2Co/5Co are defined by how much Co was added), so the match in ordering is partly a construction check rather than an independent test of a release mechanism

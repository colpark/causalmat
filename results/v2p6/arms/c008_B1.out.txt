Both panels were opened. F4a (porosity vs. temperature) shows S1: 8.7→0.4%, S2: 7.9→0.7%, S3: 11.2→7.7% over 1400–1700°C. F5 (hardness vs. temperature) shows S1: 9.3→15.6, S2: 9.4→14.8, S3: 9.0→14.1 GPa, all rising monotonically.

What the combination supports: for S1 and S2, large porosity drops (to <1%) co-occur with the largest hardness gains, consistent with densification driving property improvement — a plausible structure→property (STR→PRP) link. Together with the earlier-settled SEM result, this builds a coherent qualitative picture: sintering temperature reduces porosity, and reduced porosity accompanies higher hardness.

What it leaves open: the correspondence is not proportional. S3 only drops from 11.2% to 7.7% porosity (a modest ~3.5-point change) yet its hardness still climbs by nearly the same magnitude as S1/S2 (9.0→14.1 GPa). This mismatch means porosity reduction alone does not account for the hardness rise — some other mechanism (grain bonding, phase change, composition difference across S1/S2/S3) must also be contributing, and the data here cannot identify it. The two curves are separate measurements on the same temperature axis, not a paired/statistical test, so only correlation, not causation or mechanism, is established. No numeric tie to the earlier SEM snapshot is possible either, per the standing qualifications.

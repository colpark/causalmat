The three panels are consistent with each other and support a qualitative link between platelet adhesion morphology and downstream thrombus outcome. F5a (SEM, static PRP incubation) shows numerous spread, dendritic (activated) platelets on 316LSS and PPAmF, fewer and rounder/less-spread platelets on BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF, and very few on BVLD/NO-PPAmF. F6b (tube cross-sections) and F6c (photos of extracted foils) after 2h blood circulation show the same rank order: 316LSS and PPAmF lumens/foils are fully dark with thrombus, BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are partly occluded, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF is largely clean with little clot. So the combination supports the claim that surfaces promoting platelet spreading/activation in vitro (PRP assay) also promote greater thrombus burden under dynamic blood flow (service-relevant outcome).

What it leaves open: F5a is a static PRP assay while F6b/F6c reflect whole-blood flow through a tube circuit — different fluid, different shear, different exposure geometry — so the images establish correlation across the same five surface conditions, not a demonstrated causal/mechanistic pathway from platelet spreading to thrombus mass. No platelet counts, spreading quantification, or thrombus mass/area measurements are given, only qualitative images, and no statistics or replicate numbers are shown in these crops.

The two measurements are logically connected but do not together explain the hardness trend.

XRD (A) establishes only that HP5's bulk crystalline phase list is ZrB2, SiC, C (graphite), and BN, with no AlN or Al2O3 peaks detected — a bulk, spatially-averaged result that says nothing about mechanical response or about where any un-reacted/residual AlN-derived material might reside (e.g., dissolved in solid solution, present as amorphous/sub-detection-limit phase at grain boundaries, or absent entirely).

Hardness (B, HP curve) shows a non-monotonic trend: ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt% AlN, jumping to ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then easing to ~19.4 and ~19.2 GPa at 3 and 5 wt%.

What the combination supports: since XRD detects no crystalline AlN phase in the sintered HP5 body, the hardness changes with nominal AlN content cannot be attributed to a distinct hard AlN second phase acting as reinforcement — AlN is not present as such a phase (at least not above XRD detection limits). That is the one thing the pairing rules out.

What it leaves open: XRD gives no mechanism for the actual hardness trend. It does not show densification, grain size, porosity, or any solid-solution/grain-boundary effect that AlN addition might have produced instead. It also cannot confirm whether AlN reacted, volatilized, or remains as an undetected minor/amorphous phase. So the STR/phase identity (XRD) and PRP/value (hardness) observations are consistent but not causally joined — no shared measurement ties the phase data to the specific hardness values.

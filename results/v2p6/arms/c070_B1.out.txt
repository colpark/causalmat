Panels viewed: F6b (cross-sectional tube photos), F5a (SEM platelet images), F6c (thrombus strip photos), all across 316LSS, PPAmF, BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, BVLD/NO-PPAmF.

The downstream photographic evidence (F6b, F6c) shows a ranking of thrombus burden: 316LSS and PPAmF are fully dark/occluded (heaviest thrombus); BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are partly open with thrombus mainly at the ends; BVLD/NO-PPAmF is nearly clean lumen/foil with the least thrombus. This ordering tracks the SEM platelet-suppression ordering (many spread dendritic platelets on 316LSS/PPAmF; fewer on BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF; fewest, roundest, most unspread on BVLD/NO-PPAmF) closely across all five surfaces, including NO-PPAmF and BVLD/NO-PPAmF, where the thrombin assay does not track cleanly (per the earlier qualifications). So the combination supports that platelet-morphology suppression, more than the chromogenic thrombin-activity readout, is the correlate that carries through to reduced macroscopic thrombus — the upstream SEM evidence plausibly accounts for the downstream pattern, while thrombin activity alone would not (it groups NO-PPAmF with the heavy-thrombus surfaces and cannot explain why BVLD/NO-PPAmF outperforms BVLD-PPAmF).

Left open: no quantitative platelet count or thrombus mass was matched against thrombin activity, so no dose-response can be claimed; the photographs are single-timepoint, qualitative, unreplicated-looking documentation, not statistics; and the mechanism behind BVLD/NO-PPAmF's extra thrombus reduction beyond BVLD-PPAmF (same thrombin activity, less thrombus) remains unexplained by either upstream assay.

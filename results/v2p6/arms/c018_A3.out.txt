Conclusion the evidence justifies: Across both a static in vitro platelet-adhesion assay (Fig. 5, PRP incubation 2 h) and a dynamic ex vivo blood-circulation test (Fig. 6, arteriovenous shunt, 2 h), the combined BVLD/NO-PPAmF surface consistently outperformed bare 316LSS and PPAmF alone. It showed the fewest and least-activated adherent platelets and the least fibrinogen/fibrinogen-γ-chain deposition (Fig. 5B–E, ***), and in the shunt test it produced the least occlusion, lowest thrombus weight, and highest sustained blood flow (~90% of initial vs ~20% for 316LSS/PPAmF; Fig. 6E–G, ***). Cross-sections and photographs (Fig. 6B–C) visually confirm minimal thrombus/occlusion on BVLD/NO-PPAmF versus heavily occluded, thrombus-filled lumens for 316LSS/PPAmF. This supports that the combined topographical (BVLD) and NO-releasing (PPAmF) modification meaningfully improves hemocompatibility relative to unmodified stainless steel.

What it leaves uncertain: the single BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF arms are intermediate between the unmodified controls and the combined surface, but the panels bracket significance only at the group level, so the relative individual contribution of BVLD versus NO release isn't statistically resolved. Test duration is limited to 2 h, the animal/species for the shunt model isn't stated in these panels, and no long-term in vivo durability or clotting-cascade mechanism data are shown, so persistence beyond this short window and cellular activation mechanism remain unaddressed.

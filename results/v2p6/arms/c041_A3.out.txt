F5e confirms the aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 particles' chemical identity: C, Zn, and N (inherited from the ZIF-8 framework) fill the particle outline in both the DNA and RNA aptamer samples, while P — attributable to the aptamer's phosphate backbone — appears only as sparse dots within that same outline, consistent with a low but real aptamer loading co-located on/in the framework. F6b shows a downstream functional readout: aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 produces brighter (green, higher on the 0–2500 scale) ex vivo lymph-node fluorescence than the non-aptamer BSA@ZIF-8 control (cyan) in both inguinal and axillary nodes, for both subcutaneous and footpad injection. Together these support that the material actually injected and imaged was verified to carry the aptamer, and that aptamer-bearing particles are associated with greater lymph-node accumulation than the neat/BSA control — i.e., structural identity plus a correlated in vivo outcome. What the combination does not establish is mechanism: the EDS map is static, dry-particle elemental data and cannot show that the same sparse P/aptamer signal persists in vivo or is what drives node accumulation (versus size, charge, or EPR-type effects). There is also no quantification linking P density (aptamer loading) to fluorescence intensity, and the fluorescence images are representative single examples with the color scale saturating information, so reproducibility/statistics across animals are not shown here. In short: identity of the aptamer-functionalized particle and a qualitative advantage in node targeting are both supported; a causal, dose-dependent link between the aptamer loading seen in F5e and the enhanced accumulation seen in F6b is not.

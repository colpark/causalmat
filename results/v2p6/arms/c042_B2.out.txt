F5d (zeta potential) shows a clean sign flip: BSA@ZIF-8 sits near +25 mV, while the DNA- and RNA-aptamer samples both drop to roughly -16 and -14.5 mV. F5e (EDS maps) shows C, Zn, and N densely filling the particle outline for both aptamer samples, consistent with the underlying ZIF-8/BSA particle, while P appears only as scattered, sparse dots within that same outline rather than as a filled field.

Together this justifies: a net-anionic, phosphorus-bearing species (the aptamer) is present in/on the particle whose surface charge has reversed from cationic to anionic — consistent with electrostatic adsorption of the polyanionic aptamer backbone onto the cationic BSA@ZIF-8 surface.

What it leaves open, visible directly in the panels: zeta potential is one bulk value per sample and cannot localize where the charge/species sits; the P channel's sparse-dot pattern (versus the dense C/Zn/N fill) argues against a continuous surface-spanning phosphorus layer; the images cannot distinguish electrostatic adsorption from partial covalent/coordination binding; and no coverage density or bound-aptamer quantity is given, so extent of surface coverage remains undetermined.

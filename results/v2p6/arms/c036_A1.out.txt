Both panels confirm the reported values and their ranking. F5c (power factor) shows the x=0.01 curve (red) sitting above every other composition across the full 300-623 K range, from ~29 down to ~22 µW cm⁻¹K⁻²; x=0.005 (black) tracks lowest among the Se-doped set (x=0.005, 0.02, 0.04 cluster together, with x=0.005 at the bottom of that cluster at high T); x=0 (green) is far below all doped samples, ~2-5. F7c (efficiency) shows the same x=0.01 red curve as the clear top performer at Th=623 K (~11.8%), while x=0.005, 0.02, 0.04 cluster at ~9.5-10.6%.

The combination supports the conclusion that the composition with the highest power factor (x=0.01) is also the composition with the highest maximal conversion efficiency, i.e., the PF ranking and the efficiency ranking are consistent with each other across the doping series. It does not establish that the PF advantage is what causes the efficiency advantage: efficiency in these models also depends on thermal conductivity, which is not shown in either panel, so a causal (PRP→PRF) link is only suggested, not demonstrated. It also leaves open why x=0.005 is second-lowest in PF but not correspondingly lowest in efficiency (it's mid-pack there), which the PF data alone cannot explain.

Evidence justifies a clear service-performance conclusion: nitrogen-doped carbon host (CnC HS) suppresses zinc dendrite growth relative to plain carbon host (C HS), extending usable cycle life. Panels d/e show SEM of the two Zn-plated surfaces (both 10 µm): CnC HS (d) has a more open, platelet-like deposit; C HS (e) shows a denser, filamentous/mossy morphology consistent with non-uniform, tip-driven growth. This matches the mechanism schematic (f vs g): zincophilic (Zn–N) sites promote spacious nucleation and lateral growth into a homogeneous Zn layer, while their absence gives dense nucleation, tip growth, and dendrite formation.

Electrochemically (Fig. 4), CnC HS holds Coulombic efficiency near 100% for ~200 cycles, whereas C HS collapses to near-0% CE after ~40–50 cycles (a). In symmetric-cell cycling, C HS develops voltage spikes and outright "battery failure" by ~2700–4300 min, while CnC HS runs stably past 7000 min (b); the same failure pattern recurs at multiple current densities (e), with C HS failing near 2500–3000 min.

What remains uncertain: these are half-cell/symmetric-cell data only, not full-cell energy density, rate capability at higher currents, or long-term performance beyond the tested windows; mechanism is inferred from morphology/schematic, not directly imaged in situ.</message>

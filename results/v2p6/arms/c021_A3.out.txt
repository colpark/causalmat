F5d/F5e (ELISA bars): total fibrinogen and gamma-chain both drop roughly by half on BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF and BVLD/NO-PPAmF relative to 316LSS/PPAmF (single *** bracket separates the group of three from the group of two), matching claim A. Within the three functionalized coatings the bars overlap in error range — BVLD/NO-PPAmF is not the lowest; its mean (~0.06) is if anything marginally higher than BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF alone.

F6b/F6c (macro photos): 316LSS and PPAmF lumens/foils are dark and fully thrombus-filled; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are partly open with residual clot; BVLD/NO-PPAmF is the cleanest — lumen largely clear, foil largely unstained. This matches claim B, and here the combination coating does look best.

Combined support: the ELISA data justify the conclusion that functionalization (any of the three) suppresses fibrinogen adsorption/activation to roughly the same reduced level, and this is consistent in direction with the macro observation that functionalized coatings resist thrombus better than bare 316LSS/PPAmF. What it leaves open: fibrinogen level alone does not explain why BVLD/NO-PPAmF is macroscopically the cleanest, since it is not the lowest (or even tied-lowest) on fibrinogen/gamma-chain. Some additional mechanism (e.g., synergy between BVLD's anticoagulant action and NO release) beyond fibrinogen adsorption/activation must account for the combination's superior macro patency — this is not demonstrated by the ELISA data shown.

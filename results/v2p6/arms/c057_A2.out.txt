Partial support, with a key gap.

F5b (EDS) shows a Ti/O matrix with a dominant C peak and small Er (~1.4 keV) and Ce (~4.8 keV) peaks — consistent with successful incorporation of trace Er and Ce into the Ti-O material, matching claim A. This confirms the doped elements are present in the material used, so it is compatible with the downstream antibacterial claim that Ce-containing samples (Er0.5Ce0.1/0.2/0.3Ti-O) outperform undoped TiO2 and control.

F10c and F10d (near-identical bar charts, likely two bacterial strains) both show the dark-condition trend claimed in B: Ce0.1 (~28-33%) < Ce0.2 (~42-43%) < Ce0.3 (~45-47%), with TiO2 ~6% and control ~0%, all significance-marked. This is directly what the assay claim describes.

What this leaves open: the EDS spectrum appears to be a single, unlabeled sample spectrum — it does not report Ce content quantitatively, nor does it show comparative spectra across the three Ce0.1/0.2/0.3 formulations. So it confirms Ce/Er are present in the doped material generally, but it cannot itself account for *why* antibacterial activity scales monotonically with nominal Ce loading — that dose-response is an assay-level observation, not something the single EDS trace demonstrates or explains mechanistically.

The panels are consistent with the claimed link, but the evidence only supports correlation, not mechanism.

F3b (DLS) shows the microfluidic mode as a narrow peak near 300-400 nm and the hydrothermal mode as a broader peak near ~1000 nm — matching the stated particle-size result. F4f, F4g, F4h show, for siRNA, DOX, and BSA respectively, that microfluidic-route particles have significantly higher (starred) encapsulation efficiency than hydrothermal-route particles in every cargo, with the size-encapsulation ordering matching across all three loads.

Combined, this justifies: the smaller, more uniform microfluidic particles are consistently associated with higher and more reproducible (starred, tighter replicate spread) cargo loading across three chemically distinct cargos, while the larger, more polydisperse hydrothermal particles show consistently lower and more variable encapsulation. That the pattern holds for three different cargo types strengthens the association beyond a single-cargo coincidence.

What it leaves open: these are four separate panels reporting size and encapsulation as parallel outcomes of the two synthesis routes — none of them tests size or distribution width as a causal driver of encapsulation (no intermediate measurement, e.g., surface area, porosity, or loading kinetics, links the two). Confounding by other synthesis-route differences (defect density, crystallinity, pore accessibility) is not excluded by this evidence alone.

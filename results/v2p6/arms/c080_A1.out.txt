Findings from the five panels (F1b–e, optical; F5a, SEM):

F1b–e show a light matrix with dark, irregular particles scattered across the whole field in every panel, with visible local aggregates/streaks (b, c, e) and a markedly denser, more clustered population in d. This directly supports observation A: distribution is broadly field-wide with real local clustering, and panel-to-panel comparison (d vs. b/c/e) suggests particle density/clustering increases with whatever variable the panels are stepping through (captions "15 and," "20 and" imply a wt%/vol% series, though the exact labels are cut off).

F5a (SEM, x150) shows a fibrous, tortuous surface with bright ridge-like tearing features interspersed with smoother, flatter facet-like patches — consistent with observation B's "rough fracture, tear ridges around flat facets," i.e., a mixed ductile-tear/brittle-facet fracture morphology.

What this combination supports: the optical series justifies a STR/microstructure/distribution claim of widespread particles with local clustering that varies across samples, and F5a justifies a PRP/behavior_class claim of mixed fracture character (ductile tearing plus flatter facet regions) rather than pure ductile dimple rupture.

What it leaves open: only one SEM panel (a) was provided, so "in both samples" cannot be confirmed from the images alone — a second SEM panel is needed. Particle identity/composition (SiC vs. intermetallic) is not shown, so no causal link between clustering and the fracture facets can be drawn from these images; that would need EDS or fractography at higher magnification correlating facets to particle locations. Quantitative clustering statistics and the exact sample labels for d/e are also absent.

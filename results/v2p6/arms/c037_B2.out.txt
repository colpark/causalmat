Both panels match their captions: F5c (e97b8...C.jpg) is the power-factor vs T plot with x=0.01 highest (~29→22 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² from 300→623 K), x=0.005 lower, x=0 flat and low (~2–5) — consistent with observation A. F6a (83b69...A.jpg) is ZT vs T with x=0 lowest throughout (~0.05–0.2) and doped compositions clustered higher, peaking mid-range T; at 300 K the doped curves run roughly 0.6–0.85, consistent with observation B, alongside the n-Bi2Te3 and literature reference curves noted in the caption.

The upstream resistivity–PF covariation accounts for the coarse contrast in both downstream panels: x=0 (highest resistivity) is the clear outlier with both the lowest PF and the lowest ZT, while all doped compositions (lower resistivity) sit well above it in both PF and ZT — supporting that whatever suppresses PF in the undoped sample also suppresses ZT there. It does not account for the finer ranking: PF is highest for x=0.01, but ZT at 300 K is highest for x=0.005, with x=0.01 not topping the ZT ranking. Since ZT = PF·T/κ, this divergence implicates thermal conductivity (unmeasured in the cited evidence) as a factor the PF/resistivity pairing cannot explain. So the combination supports only that resistivity differences broadly track the doped-vs-undoped PF and ZT contrast, not the detailed composition ordering of ZT, and it remains an association, not a demonstrated causal chain.

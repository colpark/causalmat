The three panels are consistent with the numeric claims: F10a reproduces the photocurrent ordering (Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS > Pd-ZIS > Ag-ZIS > ZIS, ~2.1/1.5/0.9/0.4 uA/cm2) and F8b reproduces the H2 rates (125.4/34.5/3.2/1.5 umol/h for the same four samples), while F8a extends the same rank order over time to two additional alloy compositions (Ag0.5Pd0.5, Ag0.75Pd0.25) that sit between Pd-ZIS and Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS.

This combination supports treating the photocurrent/PL-based ordering as consistent with, and predictive of, the catalytic output ordering: the sample identified by two independent charge-separation proxies as best also delivers the highest steady H2 evolution rate and cumulative yield, and the worst-ranked sample (ZIS) is also the worst producer. That triple convergence (electrochemical, optical, catalytic) strengthens confidence that photocarrier separation efficiency and photocatalytic activity track together across this sample set.

It leaves open: (1) no causal mechanism is established — better charge separation is correlated with, not proven to cause, higher H2 evolution; (2) absolute quantum efficiency or utilization of separated charges for the actual reduction step is not measured; (3) why the specific 0.25 Ag / 0.75 Pd ratio is optimal remains unexplained; the evidence only shows the same rank order recurs across techniques, not why.

The upstream SEM evidence (Observations A/B: even particle spread, no large agglomerates, blurred/gap-free interfaces for modified fillers) is compatible with the downstream mechanical trend but does not explain it. Panels F4b (BST-SH), F4c (BST@Ag1%), and F4e (BST@Ag5%) all show embedded particles without visible large agglomerates or macroscopic voids even as Ag loading rises to 5%, so the combination supports that the monotonic tensile-strength decline (F7a: 79.3→78.3→74.4→73.5 MPa, Pure PAEN through BST@Ag5%) is not caused by gross agglomeration or dispersion failure at higher filler content — dispersion still looks uniform at the highest loading imaged.

What it leaves open: the SEM observations are qualitative (no measured gap width, particle size, or adhesion strength), so they cannot account for the mechanism actually driving the ~7% strength loss — e.g., rigid-particle stress concentration, reduced matrix chain mobility, or partial debonding under load that would not be visible as "agglomeration" in unstressed micrographs. Sample correspondence is also incomplete: the imaged panels are BST-SH, 1%, and 5% Ag, while the tensile series is Pure PAEN, 1%, 3%, and 5% Ag — the 3% composite's dispersion is not shown, and BST-SH is not tensile-tested separately, so the dispersion-strength pairing is not fully sample-matched across the series.

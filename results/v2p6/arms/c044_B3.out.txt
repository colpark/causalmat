Combination supports: F2a and F2b show the same qualitative pattern for PS across all four AlN levels (0,1,3,5 wt%) — density falls 85.5→78.4→70.7→70.0% while hardness falls in parallel, 12.0→9.1→7.1→6.8 GPa — with HP essentially flat and high in both panels (~97-100% density, ~18-20 GPa). This parallel PS decline, paired with the settled SEM result (PS0 has more/larger pores and fewer/smaller necks than HP0), makes it plausible that porosity is at least part of why PS hardness is also depressed relative to HP: at the one point where microstructure was actually imaged (0 wt%), the density gap that SEM explains coincides with a hardness gap of similar character (PS well below HP). That is as far as the evidence goes.

What it leaves open: SEM was never taken at 1, 3, or 5 wt% AlN, so the parallel density/hardness decline across the PS series is only a correlation between two indirect measures (density, hardness), not a confirmed pore/neck trend. Nothing here shows porosity, rather than AlN's own mechanical effect, grain growth, or reaction products, is what drives the hardness drop from 1-5 wt%. The pairing also never directly measures a porosity-hardness relationship — only density-porosity (at 0 wt%) and density-hardness (all points) separately. So the SEM finding grounds the endpoint density/porosity story, but the hardness trend's cause across the AlN series remains inferred, not verified.

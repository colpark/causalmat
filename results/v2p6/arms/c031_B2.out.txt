The two datasets are consistent with each other but do not establish a direct causal link.

What is supported: F13 shows that adding EPT (blue, "423K-8+373K-3+25µs-10min") to the pre-strained baseline shifts the curve relative to F12's non-EPT analogue ("423K-8+373K-3"): UTS rises (~420→~447 MPa) and strain-to-failure nearly doubles (~0.095→~0.15), with a slightly lower yield knee (~350→~330 MPa). F10d's SEM shows the finest, most uniform feature size (~0.3-0.8 µm) of the three microstructure panels, consistent in general terms with a microstructure that would favor improved ductility. So the combination is compatible with a "finer/more uniform structure → more ductile, tougher curve" narrative.

What it leaves open: (1) F10d's caption reads "25s-10min," not "25µs-10min," and omits the "423K-8+373K-3" prefix used in F13's legend — so it is not confirmed that the SEM and the blue tensile curve are the same specimen. (2) No sample-matched, paired grain-size/mechanical-property dataset exists here; the two figures are never linked quantitatively. (3) The yield stress slightly decreasing while UTS and ductility rise is not the pattern classic Hall-Petch grain refinement predicts (which would raise yield strength), so a grain-size-only explanation is incomplete; texture or dislocation-density changes may also be involved, and the images/curves alone cannot adjudicate this.

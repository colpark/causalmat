F4c is an SEM fracture-surface image of the BST@Ag1% film: a continuous, wave-like fractured matrix with no discrete particle boundaries or gap features visible — consistent with the "blurred outlines, no interfacial gaps" description. F7a is a bar chart: tensile strength falls monotonically from 79.3 MPa (pure PAEN) to 78.3 (1% Ag) to 74.4 (3%) to 73.5 (5%).

What the combination supports: at the 1% Ag loading — the only composition for which SEM interfacial evidence exists — tensile strength is nearly unchanged from neat PAEN (79.3→78.3 MPa, ~1% drop). This is consistent with (not proof of) the SEM/FTIR inference that good filler-matrix adhesion at that loading preserves load transfer and largely avoids strength loss.

What it leaves open: the SEM/FTIR evidence was collected only for BST@Ag1%, yet tensile strength continues to decline further at 3% and 5% Ag (down to 73.5 MPa, a ~7% drop from baseline). Nothing in the given upstream evidence characterizes interfacial quality at those higher loadings, so the combination cannot explain that continued decline — it could reflect degraded bonding, particle agglomeration, increased defect density, or intrinsic stress concentration from higher filler content, none of which the SEM/FTIR data distinguish. The link remains correlational and confined to a single, low-loading data point.

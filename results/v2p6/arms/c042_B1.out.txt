Both panels match the reported data. F5d (zeta potential) shows BSA@ZIF-8 at ~+25 mV flipping to ~-16 mV (DNA aptamer) and ~-14.5 mV (RNA aptamer). F5e (EDS maps) shows C, Zn, and N densely filling the particle outline in both aptamer samples, while P appears only as sparse, scattered dots within that outline, not as a filled/continuous signal.

This combination supports the conclusion that a net-anionic, phosphorus-bearing species (the aptamer) is associated with the particle: the charge reversal shows the surface now carries anionic character, and the co-located P signal (however sparse) shows phosphorus is present within the same particle boundary in both cases, consistent with the polyanionic aptamer backbone attaching to the cationic BSA@ZIF-8 surface.

What it leaves open: zeta potential is one bulk-average number per sample, so it cannot localize the anionic species or confirm a coating geometry. The sparse, non-filling P dots argue against a continuous, surface-spanning phosphorus layer — the data do not establish a uniform shell. Neither measurement can distinguish electrostatic adsorption from partial covalent/coordination binding, and no quantitative aptamer loading or surface coverage is given.

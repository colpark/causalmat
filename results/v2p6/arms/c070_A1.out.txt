All three panels confirm the described observations. F5a (SEM, PRP incubation, 2h) shows numerous spread, dendritic platelets on 316LSS and PPAmF, markedly fewer and more rounded/less-spread platelets on BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF. F6b (cross-sections) and F6c (thrombus photographs) show 316LSS and PPAmF lumens/tubes filled with dark/red thrombus, while BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are partly open, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF is nearly clean with a bright, largely unoccluded lumen.

The combination supports a consistent, monotonic correspondence across the same five-substrate series: substrates that induce more platelet spreading/activation in static PRP incubation are the same substrates that develop more macroscopic thrombus in the (implied) blood-circulation/loop test, and the combined-release surface (BVLD/NO-PPAmF) shows both the least platelet spreading and the least thrombus. This is consistent with platelet activation contributing to thrombus formation.

What it leaves open: the two assays are different experiments (static PRP adhesion vs. dynamic blood circulation), so this is correlation across matched substrate rankings, not a demonstrated causal or quantitative link — no co-registered sample, no statistics tying platelet count/morphology to thrombus mass or area. It also does not rule out NO/BVLD acting on thrombus via coagulation or other pathways independent of platelet adhesion.

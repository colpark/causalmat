An earlier step in this work already established the following, and you may treat it as settled:

Small-angle XRD (n8) showing a single low-angle reflection in all three patterns (~1.37 deg MBG, ~1.47 deg 2Co, ~1.35 deg 5Co) confirms an ordered mesoporous channel structure persists in the cobalt-doped glasses, and the release assay (n15) shows ampicillin is released gradually and cumulatively (~340 ug at 8 h, ~670 at 24 h, ~780 at 72 h, ~795 ug at 168 h, plateauing). Together the two observations are consistent with an ordered mesoporous channel structure being present alongside a gradual, plateauing release profile -- i.e., the material that retains long-range pore ordering is the same material that shows sustained rather than burst release.

The qualifications recorded on that earlier result:
  - The XRD reflection is reported across three compositions (MBG, 2Co, 5Co) but the release curve corresponds to a single material (2Co-MBG per the stated conclusion), so there is no matched comparison showing that samples with stronger/weaker or shifted low-angle reflections (1.37 vs 1.47 vs 1.35 deg) release drug faster or slower -- no dose-response between degree of structural order and release kinetics is established.
  - The two measurements are different kinds of quantity: a static structural signature (peak position/presence, a single snapshot, non-kinetic) versus a cumulative mass-released-over-time curve (ug, 8-168 h window); pairing a structural presence/absence result with a kinetic cumulative curve cannot by itself demonstrate that the pores are the transport pathway responsible for the release rate or the plateau.
  - Only one low-angle peak and four release time points are given; this is too sparse to support a rate law or diffusion model linking pore geometry to release kinetics, only that both an ordered structure and a plateauing release exist in the same/related material.

Combining F9 (XRD) and F8 (photographs): the XRD patterns at both 5 and 10 cycles retain the same t-ZrO2 marker peaks (~43°, split ~73-74°) matching the tetragonal reference sticks, with no evidence in either pattern of the additional peaks that would signal monoclinic ZrO2. The insets do show the t-ZrO2 peak intensities somewhat lower at 10 cycles than at 5 cycles, a change that coincides with the photographic transition from an intact coupon at 5 cycles to spalled corners at 10 cycles — consistent with, but not proof of, some phase/microstructural change accompanying spallation onset.

This upstream evidence only partially accounts for the downstream observation. It supports the claim that spallation onset (5→10 cycles) is not obviously driven by a gross tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation, since t-ZrO2 remains the identified phase at both sampled points, while leaving open whether the modest intensity/fraction shift between 5 and 10 cycles reflects a real, mechanistically relevant phase or crystallinity change versus measurement noise. Critically, XRD was only collected at 5 and 10 cycles, but the photographs show spalled corners persisting and growing through 40 cycles — a window with no XRD coverage at all. So the phase data cannot explain, confirm, or rule out any role of phase change in the continued spallation growth from 10 to 40 cycles; that link is untested.

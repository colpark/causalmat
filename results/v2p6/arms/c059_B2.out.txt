The combination supports a correlational link: across the same co-doped series (TiO2 < Ce0.1 < Ce0.3 < Er0.5Ce0.2), the compositions with more red-shifted, extended UVVIS absorption edges (F4b) also show higher photocatalytic MO degradation at 20/40/60 min (F8), and TiO2 is both the least red-shifted and the least active. This is consistent with enhanced visible-light absorption from co-doping contributing to the observed increase in degradation activity, i.e., an association between the optical property and the catalytic performance that tracks composition in the same direction.

What it leaves open: neither panel demonstrates a causal or mechanistic link between light absorption and degradation rate — no action spectrum, quantum yield, or dark-control data is shown to confirm the degradation is driven by the extended absorption rather than by surface area, crystallinity, defect density, or other compositional changes accompanying co-doping. The panels also do not resolve which dopant (Er or Ce) or which oxidation state drives either effect, since Er and Ce are always co-present, and Ce0.2 (most red-shifted) is not the top degrader at every timepoint reported for the ranking (Er0.5Ce0.2 is highest overall), so the ordering between the two properties is not exact — the association is directional/qualitative, not a matched quantitative correspondence.

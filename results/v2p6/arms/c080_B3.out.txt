The upstream evidence (optical + SEM dispersion imaging) establishes only that SiCp particles are broadly, near-uniformly spread with occasional local clusters in the polished/imaged cross-sections — a spatial-distribution observation. The downstream observation (F5a) is a fracture-surface topography observation from a different specimen state (post-fracture) and different imaging geometry, showing tear ridges and facets that speak to fracture mode/ductility, not to particle location per se.

What the combination supports: it is plausible that the same particles shown to cluster locally in the dispersion images are the particles or interfaces responsible for the facet-like regions seen on the fracture surface (e.g., particle cleavage or particle/matrix decohesion producing flatter facets, with matrix tearing producing the bright ridges between them). This is a reasonable, physically consistent story linking distribution to fracture behavior.

What is left open: no co-location, fractography-to-microstructure correlation, or particle identification on the fracture surface itself is shown, so the upstream dispersion evidence does not directly demonstrate that clusters caused the specific facets/ridges in F5a — that link is inferred, not measured. The fracture-surface evidence also comes from a single field per sample, so its own representativeness is unverified.

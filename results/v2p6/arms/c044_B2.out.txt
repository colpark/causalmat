F2a and F2b both plot HP (red) vs PS (blue) against AlN wt% (0,1,3,5). F2a confirms the density numbers exactly (PS: ~85.5, 78.4, 70.7, 70.0%; HP flat ~98-100%). F2b shows PS Vickers hardness falling in the same pattern (~12.0, 9.1, 7.1, 6.8 GPa) while HP stays flat (~18-20 GPa).

What this supports: the hardness decline in PS tracks the density (porosity) decline point-for-point, and both diverge from HP the same way. This parallelism is consistent with the settled finding that PS's lower relative density reflects real pore/neck microstructure (verified by SEM at 0 wt%) — i.e., it is consistent with porosity being the mechanism suppressing PS hardness, since porosity is a well-known hardness-reducing factor and the two property curves co-vary.

What it leaves open: this is a correlation between two independently plotted property curves (PHYS density and MECH hardness), not new microstructural evidence. SEM was reported only at 0 wt% AlN (PS0 vs HP0), so no image confirms that the hardness drop at 1, 3, 5 wt% is driven by the same pore/neck changes rather than by AlN content itself (composition, phase, or grain-size effects) or other unverified microstructural shifts. The combination therefore supports an inference — porosity plausibly explains the hardness trend — but does not establish it causally or microstructurally beyond the single verified 0 wt% endpoint.

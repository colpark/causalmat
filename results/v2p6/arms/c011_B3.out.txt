Both panels were opened and match the stated observations. F5c (PF): x=0.01 is highest throughout (~29 μW cm⁻¹K⁻² at 300 K falling to ~22 at 623 K), x=0.005 is the lowest of the doped set, and x=0 sits far below all doped compositions (~2 rising to ~5). F6a (ZT): x=0.01 is again the top curve, peaking at ~1.25 near 500-525 K, with x=0 far below (~0.05-0.22) throughout.

What the combination supports: the composition ranking is consistent between PF and ZT — x=0.01 is highest and x=0 is lowest in both quantities, and this is the same ranking already tied to resistivity in the earlier step. So the resistivity→PF chain established upstream is compatible with, and helps account for, which composition has the best ZT: since ZT = PF·T/κ, a composition with higher PF at a given T has a built-in advantage toward higher ZT, and that ordering holds here.

What it leaves open: the temperature dependence does not line up. For x=0.01, PF is essentially flat/declining from ~300-400 K onward and drops monotonically to its lowest value by 623 K, whereas ZT keeps rising to a peak around 500-525 K before declining. That mismatch means PF's trajectory alone cannot explain where ZT peaks — thermal conductivity (κ), which is not shown in either panel or in the earlier resistivity/PF step, must be governing the location of the ZT maximum. Seebeck coefficient values are also still absent. So the evidence supports consistency in composition ranking between PF and ZT but does not establish that PF (or the resistivity behind it) drives ZT's temperature dependence, and it does not demonstrate mechanism/causation.

Yes, the two upstream measurements jointly support the downstream chemistry/phase claim, within limits.

F3b (ICP-AES) shows Zn at 0 at% in the pristine electrode, rising through cycling (~22% at 4th, ~51% at 20th) and plateauing near 46-47% at 60th/100th — matching claim A's "zero to ~50 at% by cycle 20, staying near 47 at%." However, F3b also shows K, Fe, and Mn are not eliminated: they persist and vary alongside Zn, so the composition is a mixed K/Fe/Mn/Zn system, not pure Zn substitution.

F3a (XRD) shows the pristine KMnHCF pattern, then patterns at 4th/20th/60th/100th cycles with two dashed reference lines (~16.5°, ~21.9°) where new peaks emerge and grow while the original KMnHCF peaks visibly weaken from the 4th cycle onward — consistent with claim B. Reference patterns below (JCPDS 33-1061 KZnHCF, 51-1896 KMnHCF) suggest the new peaks track a Zn-hexacyanoferrate-type phase, aligning with the compositional Zn rise.

Together, the two panels support that increasing Zn incorporation coincides with, and plausibly drives, formation of a new Zn-containing phase replacing the original KMnHCF structure. What they leave open: the exact peak indexing/stoichiometry of the new phase, whether it is single-phase or a mixture, the transformation mechanism (ion exchange vs. dissolution-reprecipitation), and the role of retained K/Fe/Mn in that new phase.

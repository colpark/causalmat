Partially — the polarization data account for the pH observation only in direction, not in detail.

What's supported: The polarization curves (F3) put Mg-Zn-Y-Nd at a far more negative corrosion potential (-1.49 V vs -0.25 V) with current density ~3 orders of magnitude higher (24.93 vs 0.017 µA/cm²) than 317L SS, indicating Mg-Zn-Y-Nd is corroding actively while 317L SS is comparatively passive. This is mechanistically compatible with the pH curve (F5a): Mg-Zn-Y-Nd's pH climbs steadily from 7.4 to ~10 by day 22 and plateaus, consistent with an alkalizing corrosion process (Mg dissolution/hydroxide and H2 evolution), while 317L SS shows no comparable rise. So the electrochemical result and the direction of the pH shift for the Mg alloy are consistent.

What's left open: The polarization scan is a single instantaneous measurement and gives no chemical mechanism (no evidence of Mg(OH)2 formation or H2 evolution as such), so it cannot predict the specific pH trajectory, magnitude, or day-22 plateau. It also does not explain why 317L SS's pH falls below baseline to 6.1, or why the metal-free blank control rises then declines — those patterns imply media/buffering effects the electrochemical data don't address. So the combination supports a qualitative, mechanistically plausible link, not a quantitative or complete explanation of the pH curve.

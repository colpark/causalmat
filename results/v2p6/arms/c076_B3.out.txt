F3b confirms the DPBF numbers as reported: bismuthene's normalized absorbance falls to ~0.73 by 30 min while water stays near ~0.93, a clear divergence attributable to light-driven quenching only in the bismuthene sample.

F5d shows six individual-curve panels. The "BNs-BSA+Lasers" (combined 660/808 nm) panel: all five tumor curves stay flat near 0 through day ~20, i.e., full, durable suppression. The "BNs-BSA+808 nm" panel: tumors are suppressed initially, but four of five curves rise sharply after roughly day 14–15 (one stays low), matching the described "regrowth" observation.

The upstream evidence (narrow ~0.69 eV band gap plus the 660 nm-relevant DPBF decay) supports that bismuthene is capable of light-driven singlet-oxygen-type activity, and this is consistent with the complete, durable tumor suppression seen when both lasers (660+808 nm) are applied together. It does not, however, account for the partial/late failure specific to 808 nm alone. The DPBF assay was run under conditions tied to the 660 nm photon energy comparison, not 808 nm; there is no wavelength-dependent action spectrum linking 808 nm illumination to singlet-oxygen yield. So the combination leaves open why four of five 808 nm-only tumors regrow — whether 808 nm relies on a different (e.g., photothermal, dose-limited, or lower-efficiency photodynamic) mechanism than 660 nm is not addressed by this evidence.

So the upstream Arrhenius result (lower Ea for C10) is mechanistically consistent with the downstream kinetic observation (C10 desorbs far faster than C0 at every temperature tested) — a lower activation-energy barrier qualitatively predicts faster kinetics, and the panels confirm that pattern holds. What the panels do not establish is a quantitative derivation: they don't show that the plotted rate constants k (used for the Arrhenius fit) were extracted from these specific curves, nor do they prove the absolute desorption times follow strictly from Ea alone (pre-exponential factor, diffusion path, or particle-size effects could also contribute). So the combination supports qualitative consistency between reduced Ea and faster kinetics for C10, but leaves the precise quantitative link between the Arrhenius fit and the raw time values open.

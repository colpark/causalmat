Panels confirmed as described: F5c (PF vs T) shows x=0.01 (red) on top across 300-623 K, falling from ~29 to ~22; x=0.005, 0.02, 0.04 cluster together at lower PF, with x=0.005 clearly the lowest of the doped set at high T (~13 at 623 K, vs ~19-20 for x=0.02/0.04); x=0 stays lowest overall (~2-5). F7c (eta vs Th) shows x=0.01 clearly highest at Th=623 K (~11.8%), with x=0.005, 0.02, 0.04 clustered together (~9.5-10.6%), largely overlapping and not sharply ordered by composition.

The combination supports one thing directly: x=0.01's advantage in power factor across the whole temperature range is consistent with its being the composition with the highest conversion efficiency at 623 K — the two panels agree on which single sample is "best." That is a defensible, if limited, directional link between the upstream PF result and the downstream efficiency observation.

It leaves open more than it settles. First, efficiency depends on the full figure of merit (which needs thermal conductivity, not shown in either panel), so PF cannot be claimed as the cause of the efficiency ranking, only as consistent with it for the top performer. Second, the ranking among the other four samples does not track: x=0.005 has distinctly the lowest PF of the doped set at high T, yet its efficiency is not correspondingly lowest — it overlaps with x=0.02 and x=0.04. So PF differences do not account for efficiency differences among the non-best compositions, indicating thermal conductivity or other factors must also govern eta there.

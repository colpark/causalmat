The two measurement sets are consistent with, and jointly support, a limited claim: the aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 particles retain their expected composition (C, Zn, N filling the particle outline, P present only as sparse dots — confirming the aptamer/ZIF-8/BSA structure already established) and that IV (3–14 days) and SC dosing did not push liver (ALT, AST) or kidney (BUN, CREA) function markers outside the printed normal reference ranges in either route. Together these support "the material's composition is confirmed and short-term systemic organ-function biomarkers remain normal after administration" — a reasonable basis for saying the formulation is not causing detectable hepatic or renal dysfunction over this window.

What this leaves open: the biomarker panels (F7b, F7c) report blood chemistry, not tissue morphology — they cannot by themselves establish "no noticeable histopathology changes" (that claim requires the H&E images, which are not among the panels reviewed here); blood chemistry and histology are correlated but distinct endpoints. No statistics, group sizes, or control comparisons are shown, so "within reference range" is a qualitative read against broad normal bands, not a formal non-inferiority test. The EDS map (F5e) speaks only to particle composition/structure, not biodistribution or clearance, and does not itself bear on organ toxicity. So the evidence supports compositional confirmation plus normal short-term organ-function chemistry, but does not independently establish the histopathology qualification.

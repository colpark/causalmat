The four panels support a coherent upstream-to-downstream story only in part. F3a (bare BST) shows smooth, small, discrete ~70 nm spheres; F3b (BST-SH) and F3e (BST@Ag5%) show progressively rougher, larger, more agglomerated coated particles — consistent with claim A and with coating/roughening increasing as modification (and Ag loading) proceeds. F5a (DSC) shows every BST-containing sample (146, 149, 157 C) below pure PAEN (176 C), and among the modified samples Tg rises monotonically with Ag content (146→149→157 C), moving in the same direction as the SEM-visualized increase in coating/roughness/size from F3b to F3e.

This justifies saying the particle-level surface modification that intensifies with Ag loading co-occurs with, and trends in the same direction as, the Tg increase among BST@Ag samples. It does not justify a mechanistic or quantitative link: SEM here again only samples discrete states (bare, SH, Ag5%), not a graded series matching each DSC point, so continuous scaling can't be shown. The uniform Tg depression of all modified samples relative to pure PAEN is not addressed by the SEM contrast at all (SH sample already looks coated/larger, yet its Tg is lowest). And because Ag content, particle size, and coating thickness co-vary by construction, the panels cannot separate which factor drives the Tg shift.

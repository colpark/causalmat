Panels F4a, F4b, and F4e show cell-level performance rather than only morphology/plateau data. In F4a (Coulombic efficiency at 1 mAh cm⁻², 4 mA cm⁻²), CnC HS maintains CE near 100% for ~200 cycles, while C HS is more erratic, dips toward 75%, and drops to 0% around cycles 40–50 — a signature of short-circuit/cell failure. F4b (symmetric-cell cycling over ~7000 min) shows CnC HS's voltage trace stays flat and periodic throughout, whereas C HS develops large voltage spikes around 2700–4300 min explicitly annotated "C HS battery failure." F4e (cycling across stepped current densities) shows the same pattern: CnC HS tracks the current-density steps smoothly, while C HS again shows growing voltage instability/spikes as current density increases.

Together these justify the conclusion that in service, CnC HS supports substantially longer, more stable, higher-efficiency Zn plating/stripping than C HS, including avoidance of the short-circuit failure C HS exhibits — consistent with (but not proof of a mechanistic link to) the earlier-established morphology and plateau-gap story. What remains uncertain: these panels don't establish that the specific dendritic/platelet morphology (F3d/e) or the -0.05 V/-0.10 V plateau gap (F4c) is the direct cause of the C HS failure event seen here; that causal chain is plausible but not demonstrated by these panels alone.

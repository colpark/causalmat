F5a: Co2+ release rises with dose and time. At every timepoint 5Co releases more Co2+ than 2Co (day1: 8.9 vs 4.7; day3: 17.9 vs 10.5; day7: 20.8 vs 16.9 mg/L); 0Co stays at zero.

F8: VEGF is far higher for 2Co-MBG (~430 pg/mL) than for blank (~60), plain MBG (~100), or 5Co-MBG (~115), with P<0.05 brackets for 2Co vs each. 5Co-MBG's VEGF sits close to the blank/MBG baseline, with no significance bracket shown between 5Co and blank/MBG.

Does the upstream evidence account for the downstream VEGF result? Only partially, and not in the simple direction one might expect. If more Co2+ release straightforwardly drove more VEGF, 5Co (which releases more Co at every timepoint) should out-produce 2Co, but it does not — VEGF at 5Co falls back near baseline. So the combination supports a non-monotonic (inverted-U-type) relationship between Co dose/release and VEGF stimulation, consistent with a stimulatory window at lower Co exposure (2Co) that is lost or reversed at higher exposure (5Co), rather than a linear dose-response.

Left open: why the 5Co response collapses (no cytotoxicity/viability data in these two panels to test a toxicity explanation), the exact Co2+ concentration at which the switch occurs, whether VEGF was measured at a soak time matched to the ion-release curve, and any direct causal (vs. correlational) test linking ion release to VEGF secretion.

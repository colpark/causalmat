F4c is an SEM fracture-surface micrograph of BST@Ag1%/PAEN showing embedded particles with matrix coating them and blurred/indistinct particle-matrix boundaries — consistent with the stated observation that particles are covered by matrix with no visible interfacial gaps. F7a is the tensile-strength bar chart: 79.3, 78.3, 74.4, 73.5 MPa for pure PAEN, BST@Ag1%, BST@Ag3%, BST@Ag5%, matching the reported values exactly.

Together, the upstream SEM evidence (good particle-matrix adhesion, no debonding gaps at 1% loading) is consistent with the small, modest drop in tensile strength from pure PAEN to BST@Ag1% (79.3→78.3 MPa, within/near the error bars) — the near-absence of interfacial voids plausibly explains why strength is barely affected at this low loading rather than dropping sharply as would be expected from poor bonding or debonding. This supports a qualitative link between "good interfacial contact" and "retained strength" at 1% Ag.

What it leaves open: the SEM image is from a single micrograph at one loading (1%) and does not show BST@Ag3% or BST@Ag5%, so the continued strength decline at higher loadings (down to 73.5 MPa) is not evidenced by any interface micrograph here — it could reflect increasing agglomeration, filler content effects, or other mechanisms not shown in F4c. The SEM is also qualitative (no quantitative interfacial adhesion metric, no statistical comparison across loadings), so causality (good interface → retained strength) is plausible but not directly demonstrated; the modest 1%-to-1% strength difference could also arise from stiffness/composition effects unrelated to interfacial bonding.

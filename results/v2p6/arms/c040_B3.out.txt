The combination partially, not fully, accounts for the downstream observation. The EDS map (plus the already-settled zeta-potential reversal) establishes that a phosphorus-bearing aptamer is present on/near the cationic particle surface, which is a necessary precondition for aptamer-mediated tumor recognition and could plausibly explain why the aptamer-bearing particle accumulates more in tumor tissue than the neat/non-targeted MOF. But the EDS data only show sparse, non-uniform P dots — not a quantified surface density or a demonstration that the aptamer is correctly folded/oriented and functionally capable of target binding. The fluorescence comparison itself is a single qualitative snapshot (one time point, two conditions, no replicate counts, quantification, or statistics), so it does not by itself prove the aptamer, rather than differences in particle size, FITC labeling, or nonspecific EPR accumulation, caused the brighter tumor signal. So: consistent with an aptamer-driven tumor-targeting effect, but causal attribution, functional-activity confirmation, and quantitative accumulation levels remain open.

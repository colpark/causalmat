Partial account only. The upstream combination (EDS: Ti, O, dominant C, small Er/Ce peaks; XPS: Ti 2p, O1s, Er 4d confirming Er3+ in a Ti-O oxide) establishes that the three PL samples are chemically distinct — undoped TiO2, Er-doped Ti-O, and Er+Ce co-doped Ti-O — with Er present as a confirmed Er3+ species tied to the Ti-O framework. That is consistent with, and gives compositional grounding to, the graded PL trend in F7 (TiO2 highest, Er0.5Ti-O roughly half, Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O lowest): different dopant loadings correspond to different luminescence intensities.

What it does not establish: (1) Ce's oxidation state is unknown (no Ce XPS data), so nothing here explains why adding Ce further quenches PL beyond Er alone — the chemistry driving that specific step is unaddressed; (2) whether Er/Ce sit in the TiO2 lattice or are surface-adsorbed is unresolved, which matters because PL quenching mechanisms (recombination centers, energy transfer, surface trapping) differ for bulk-incorporated vs surface dopants; (3) the Er/Ce EDS peaks are small, so the evidence weakly supports dopant "presence" but says nothing about dopant concentration correlating quantitatively with the ~50% and further PL intensity drops; (4) no mechanistic link (e.g., defect states, energy transfer pathway) between confirmed Er3+ and reduced PL is shown — only co-occurrence of composition and intensity trend across three samples.

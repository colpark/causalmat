The combination does not account for the elongation decline. F3a-b-e are SEM images of dry BST powder at three modification stages (bare, BST-SH, BST@Ag5%), showing progressive roughening/coarsening consistent with heavier surface coating. F7b is a bar chart of tensile breaking elongation across the composite series PAEN, BST@Ag1%, 3%, 5%, showing a decline (6.9 to 5.8 to 4.5 to 4.4%), with the 3%→5% step inside overlapping error bars.

What the pairing supports: both trends move together with increasing Ag-related modification — particles look progressively more coated/roughened, and composites get less ductile — so it is consistent with a co-occurrence between particle surface modification and reduced elongation.

What it leaves open, and it is substantial: the SEM panels are of loose powder, not composite fracture surfaces or matrix cross-sections, so they show nothing about particle dispersion/distribution within the PAEN matrix, interfacial bonding, or agglomeration — the actual mechanistic route to embrittlement. The SEM series (bare, BST-SH, Ag5%) also does not match the tensile series (PAEN, Ag1%, Ag3%, Ag5%) composition-for-composition, so no direct correlation can be drawn across the full set. No causal mechanism for the elongation drop is established by this evidence.

F4b/F4c show near-identical SEM crack networks (sparse thin cracks, same dark porosity spots) at 0 and 5 cycles — visually consistent with the "unchanged" reading already established for YSZ. F8 shows a separate, later-arriving process: a coating intact at 0/5 cycles, corner spallation appearing by 10 cycles, and that spalled region persisting and growing slightly through 40 cycles — and its caption identifies this as the Al2O3-YSZ coating, not YSZ.

So the combination is limited by a material and window mismatch rather than continuity. The upstream SEM comparison (A) speaks only to YSZ and only spans 0-5 cycles; the downstream spallation record (B) speaks to Al2O3-YSZ and spans 0-40 cycles, with the damage-defining transition (5→10 cycles) falling entirely outside the interval SEM was imaged. Together the evidence supports only a narrow, separate claim: YSZ's crack network was stable over the short window where its low, stable misorientation was also measured, and independently, Al2O3-YSZ (the higher-misorientation, more compositionally complex material) is the one that visibly spalls starting at 10 cycles.

It does not show that the upstream microstructural stability in YSZ accounts for, predicts, or is mechanistically linked to the downstream spallation — no SEM crack data exists for Al2O3-YSZ, no macroscopic spallation photos exist for YSZ alone, and the 5-10 cycle transition where damage begins is unobserved at the SEM scale for either material.

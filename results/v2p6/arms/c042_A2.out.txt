What the combination supports: Panel E (F5e) shows C, Zn, N densely filling the particle outline for both the DNA- and RNA-aptamer samples, with P appearing only as sparse discrete dots in the same outline — matching measurement A exactly. Panel D (F5d) shows zeta potential at roughly +25 mV for BSA@ZIF-8, dropping to about -16 mV (DNA aptamer) and -14.5 mV (RNA aptamer) — matching measurement B (caption text gives slightly different values, +23.5/-15.7/-13.8, but these fall within plausible bar-height reading tolerance, not a real conflict). Together, the sparse-but-present P signal (phosphate backbone marker) colocalized with the particle, plus the shift from positive to negative surface charge upon aptamer conjugation, is consistent with nucleic-acid aptamer successfully loading onto/coating the BSA@ZIF-8 surface and imparting negative charge.

What it leaves open: EDS mapping here is qualitative dot density, not quantitative atomic percentage, so it cannot establish how much aptamer is needed to produce a ~40 mV charge swing, nor tie a measured P density to a measured zeta value. No bare BSA@ZIF-8 P-channel control map is shown for comparison. Zeta potential is a bulk suspension average while EDS is single-particle imaging, so the two measurements are not on the same particles. The evidence is consistent with, but does not mechanistically demonstrate, that the mapped P (aptamer) is the direct cause of the charge reversal.

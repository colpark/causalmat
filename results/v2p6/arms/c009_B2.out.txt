Yes, the upstream evidence accounts for the downstream desorption-time observation, but not independently. The Ea values in F10 are extracted directly from the same temperature-dependent desorption-rate data shown in F8a/F8b (the rate constants k used for the Arrhenius fit come from these curves). So C10's much lower Ea (81.9 vs 130.3 kJ/mol) and C0/C10's disparate desorption times (150 vs 11 min at 300 °C; <40 vs ~450 min comparing 280 °C to 300 °C) are two views of one kinetic dataset, not two separate confirmations - faster desorption at a given temperature is mathematically what a lower activation energy predicts, so the panels are internally consistent rather than cross-validating.

What this combination supports: C10's faster hydrogen desorption is quantitatively consistent with its lower measured Ea, and both track the TEM-established Co dispersion. What it leaves open (per the earlier qualifications, still applicable): whether the Co dispersion causes the lower Ea/faster kinetics, since TEM was only imaged for C10, only two Ea points exist, and the TEM/Arrhenius link remains inferred rather than measured on a shared axis.

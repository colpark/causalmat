Panels confirm: F2a plots PS relative density falling 85.5→78.4→70.7→70.0% across 0/1/3/5 wt% AlN while HP stays flat ~98-100%; F2b plots PS Vickers hardness falling ~12.0→9.1→7.1→6.8 GPa over the same series while HP stays flat ~18-20 GPa. Both curves are measured on the same PS specimen series at all four AlN levels, and both trace closely parallel monotonic declines against the same x-axis, with HP as a stable, non-declining comparator in both.

What this justifies: within the PS series, the density drop and the hardness drop co-vary tightly across the full 0-5 wt% AlN range (not just at one endpoint), consistent with reduced relative density (i.e., greater porosity) accompanying reduced hardness as AlN increases. Combined with the earlier PS0-vs-HP0 SEM finding (lower density = more/larger pores, fewer/smaller necks), this pairing gives a plausible physical story: increasing porosity with AlN plausibly degrades hardness.

What it leaves open: this is a correlation between two macroscopic property curves, not a mechanistic or imaging-based demonstration that porosity specifically drives the hardness loss at 1, 3, or 5 wt%. No SEM/microstructure evidence exists at those compositions, so alternative or additional contributors (AlN reaction phases, grain-size changes, neck geometry beyond what porosity alone implies) cannot be ruled out. The porosity-hardness causal link therefore remains inferred, not directly observed, above 0 wt%.

The two datasets converge for the pair they share. PL (F7) ranks radiative recombination TiO2 (highest, worst) > Er0.5Ti-O (~half) > Er0.5Ce0.2 (lowest, best). The degradation assay (F8) ranks photocatalytic activity Er0.5Ce0.2 (~46%, best) > Ce0.3 (~41%) > Ce0.1 (~31%) > TiO2 (~20%, worst), consistently across 20/40/60 min. For the two samples common to both measurements, TiO2 and Er0.5Ce0.2, the orderings are inverse-consistent: lowest recombination (Er0.5Ce0.2) pairs with highest degradation, and highest recombination (TiO2) pairs with lowest degradation. This is the same kind of cross-method agreement already noted for PL/EIS, now extended to a functional (activity) endpoint rather than another spectroscopic/electrochemical proxy, and it is directionally sensible (less recombination, more carriers available to do photocatalytic work).

What it leaves open: the degradation assay includes Ce0.1 and Ce0.3, which have no PL measurement reported, so the assay's four-way ranking is not matched against a four-way PL ranking, only a two-way one (TiO2 vs Er0.5Ce0.2). Er0.5Ti-O, which does have a PL value, was not run in the degradation assay, so its position in that ordering is untested. The combination shows consistency between recombination rank and activity rank for the two shared samples; it does not establish that recombination is the (sole or dominant) cause of the degradation differences, since surface area, adsorption, and Ce redox chemistry could also vary across samples and were not isolated.

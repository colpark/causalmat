Figure 2a confirms measurements A and B directly: three GCD curves ranked co-precipitation w/ HCl (red, ~91 mAh/g first cycle) > co-precipitation w/o HCl (blue, ~80 mAh/g) > simple mixing (black, ~61 mAh/g), consistent with the cited values, and this ranking is preserved across all ~250 cycles shown (final values roughly ~55, ~40, ~30 mAh/g respectively). Coulombic efficiency for all three conditions stays near 95-100% throughout cycling.

So the combination (earlier XRD/GCD correspondence plus this full cycling panel) supports: the same three-way ordering established at cycle 1 (structural-order proxy via XRD tracking with initial capacity) also holds as a stable ranking over extended cycling, and it is not an artifact of a single early measurement — the w/HCl sample remains highest-capacity and the simple-mixing sample lowest at every cycle, with all three maintaining coulombic efficiency near unity.

What it leaves open: this is still observational covariation, not a mechanism test — it does not show that crystallinity/structural order (rather than composition, morphology, or another synthesis-linked variable) causes the retention behavior. It also does not resolve the earlier-noted gap: XRD as reported cannot distinguish w/HCl from w/o HCl, so the capacity difference between those two conditions specifically remains unexplained by the XRD evidence, only by the GCD ranking itself. Relative fade rates (percent capacity retained) are not stated and would require further calculation.

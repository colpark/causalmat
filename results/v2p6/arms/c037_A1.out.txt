Both panels were opened and check out against the stated measurements. F5c (PF) confirms x=0.01 (red) is highest across 300–623 K (~29→~22 µW cm⁻¹K⁻²), x=0.005 (black) is the lowest doped curve (~23→~13), and x=0 (green) sits far below (~2–5). F6a (ZT) confirms x=0.005 (black) is highest at 300 K (~0.84–0.85), x=0.01 (red) next (~0.78), x=0.02/0.04 lower (~0.63–0.69), with the dashed n-Bi2Te3 reference near ~0.88 at 300 K.

The combination supports that x=0.01 has the best power factor over the whole temperature range, and at high temperature (above ~450–500 K) this also corresponds to the highest ZT among the doped samples — consistent with PF driving ZT there. But it does NOT account for the low-temperature (300–400 K) ZT ranking: x=0.005, despite having a lower PF than x=0.01 throughout, shows the highest ZT near room temperature. Since ZT = PF·T/κ, that crossover implies a lower thermal conductivity for x=0.005 that isn't shown in either panel. So the PF evidence explains part of the ZT trend but leaves the low-T ZT lead of x=0.005 — and thus the full PRP→PRF link — open; thermal-conductivity data would be needed to close it.

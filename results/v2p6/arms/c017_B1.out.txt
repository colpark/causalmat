Yes — the upstream evidence and the downstream photographs move together, but the correspondence is descriptive, not causal or quantified.

The already-established upstream finding (SEM shape + BIO platelet counts) ranks the five surfaces from most to least platelet adhesion/activation: 316L SS and PPAmF (high counts, many spread dendritic platelets) > BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF (intermediate counts, fewer spread platelets) > BVLD/NO-PPAmF (lowest count, mostly round unspread platelets). F6b (cross-sections after 2h blood circulation) and F6c (excised thrombus photos) show the identical ordering downstream: 316L SS and PPAmF tubes/segments are filled with dark thrombus; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are partly occluded; BVLD/NO-PPAmF is nearly clean, with visible metallic/foil surface. This parallel ranking across three independent readouts (SEM morphology, platelet counts, gross thrombus extent) supports the conclusion that surfaces suppressing platelet adhesion and spreading also show markedly less macroscopic thrombus formation under flow — i.e., the platelet-level suppression is consistent with, and plausibly contributes to, the reduced thrombosis observed downstream.

What it leaves open: no statistics, thrombus mass/area quantification, or error bars accompany F6b/F6c, so the stepwise ordering is visual/descriptive only, as with the upstream pair. Thrombus formation in whole blood also depends on coagulation-cascade and fibrin components not measured by platelet SEM/counts, so this evidence cannot isolate platelet suppression as the mechanism — it only shows platelet-level and thrombus-level readouts co-vary across the same five surfaces, without establishing which drives which or ruling out other contributors.

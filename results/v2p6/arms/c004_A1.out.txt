Both panels confirmed as described. F3c (upstream, panel A) shows PA/absorbance at 808 nm rising linearly with concentration (R²=0.9999, α=6.4 L g⁻¹cm⁻¹), an in-solution calibration. F4f (downstream, panel B) shows tumor PA signal over time after IV injection: 0.26→0.13(1h)→0.32(2h)→0.94(4h, peak)→0.60(6h)→0.31(10h).

The calibration in F3c establishes that PA signal is a linear, quantitative readout of agent concentration under controlled solution conditions — this is what licenses treating the magnitude of the in vivo PA curve in F4f as a proxy for relative agent concentration in the tumor over time, rather than as an uninterpretable instrument signal. So the upstream evidence does support reading F4f's shape (dip, then rise to a 4 h peak, then decline by 10 h) as reflecting a real accumulation-then-clearance kinetic profile, not signal artifact — that much the combination justifies.

What it leaves open: the calibration was performed in a simple solution, not in tissue, so it does not establish that the same linear A/L-to-concentration relationship holds unchanged in the optically complex tumor environment (scattering, blood, depth). It also gives no mechanistic account of why the in vivo signal dips at 1 h before peaking at 4 h and falling by 10 h — that pharmacokinetic/biodistribution behavior is not addressed by an in-vitro calibration curve.

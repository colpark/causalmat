The four panels are consistent with the two upstream/downstream claims but only loosely link them.

F3a (bare BST) shows small, fairly uniform, smooth-looking particles; F3b (BST-SH) and F3e (BST@Ag5%) show visibly rougher, more agglomerated, coarser particle surfaces — this is compatible with claim A (smooth ~70 nm bare particles vs. rough, coated, larger modified particles), though no size measurements or scale-bar-based quantification are shown in these crops to confirm the ~70 nm figure. F6a plots dielectric constant vs. frequency for pure PAEN and BST@Ag at 1/3/5%, showing the ranking 5%>3%>1%>>PAEN across the full frequency range, consistent with claim B (and the ~6.05 vs ~3.35 gap at 100 Hz is visually plausible from the curves).

Together, the SEM series and the dielectric plot support the qualitative story that Ag-driven surface modification of BST particles accompanies a monotonic rise in composite dielectric constant with Ag loading. What is left open: these SEM images are of bare filler particles, not composite cross-sections, so they don't show filler dispersion, interfacial quality, or coating composition inside the polymer — meaning no direct mechanistic (as opposed to correlational) link between the observed morphology change and the dielectric increase is established here.

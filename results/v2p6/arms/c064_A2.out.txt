Findings from the three panels plus caption text:

- F4c (EDS map, captioned "EDS mapping of the C10 alloy"): shows Mg (green) particle bodies with C (blue) and Co (red) signal concentrated on particle surfaces, edges, and some faces — consistent with statement A, and specific to the C10 (C@Co-doped) alloy.
- F6a (captioned "...Mg90Ce5Y5+10 wt.% C@Co"): shows a hydrogenation curve set — but per the stated readings, this panel is actually the C0 (undoped) case: 1st cycle rises slowly to ~3.2 wt% at 180 min, while 2nd/3rd cycles saturate near 5.2 wt% within minutes (66 min gap marked). This matches statement B's C0 description.
- F6b (captioned "alloys," same figure): shows 1st, 2nd, 3rd cycles all saturating together within ~11 min around 4.6-5.5 wt% — no lagging first cycle. This matches statement B's C10 description.

What the combination supports: the EDS map (F4c) demonstrates that in the catalyst-doped (C10) material, C and Co are physically present as a surface/edge coating on Mg particles rather than being absorbed into the bulk. Paired with the kinetics (F6a vs F6b), doped C10 shows no first-cycle activation lag (1st cycle already matches cycles 2-3), while undoped C0 requires ~2 cycles before reaching the same fast saturation rate. Together this is consistent with the C@Co surface layer acting as a pre-existing catalytic/diffusion pathway that removes the need for an in-situ "activation" step (e.g., oxide-layer cracking) before fast hydrogen uptake.

What it leaves open: the panels do not include an EDS map of C0 for direct comparison, so the absence of surface C/Co on the undoped alloy is inferred from formulation, not shown. The link between surface decoration and kinetics is correlational — the panels do not isolate catalyst placement as the causal mechanism versus other differences between the two compositions (e.g., particle size, oxide state, milling history). Coverage extent/uniformity of C@Co is also only qualitatively visible, not quantified.

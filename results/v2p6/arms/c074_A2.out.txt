F1a plots computed ΔG for the two elementary Co-oxidation steps: CoCrOOH is lowest for Co2+→Co3+ (~1.05 eV), CoFeOOH is lowest for Co3+→Co4+ (~6.2 eV), CoFeCrOOH sits intermediate on both axes, and Co(OH)2 is highest for the Co3+→Co4+ step (~6.68 eV). F5c shows neutral-electrolyte LSV curves where the black Co5Fe3Cr2 curve rises earliest, crossing 10 mA cm-2 at η10 = 452 mV, ahead of IrO2 and the binary/unary controls.

Together these support a plausible mechanistic story: the ternary (oxy)hydroxide, being moderate rather than extreme on either individual Co-redox step, avoids the high barrier that penalizes Co(OH)2 and the imbalance that limits the binaries, consistent with the ternary composition also being the best-performing catalyst experimentally. That is what the combination justifies — a qualitative correlation between "balanced" computed thermodynamics and superior measured activity.

It leaves open several things: the DFT composition (CoFeCrOOH, presumably 1:1:1-type model) is not the same stoichiometry as the best experimental catalyst (Co5Fe3Cr2); no calculation is shown at that specific ratio, so the quantitative link is inferential, not direct. The DFT also covers only two thermodynamic steps, not full OER kinetics/PRF, and no computation was done for IrO2, so the DFT-to-LSV comparison for that control is unaddressed.

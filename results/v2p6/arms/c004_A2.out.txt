Both panels are confirmed as printed. F3c (crop C) shows A/L at 808 nm rising linearly with concentration (5 points, 0–200 µg/mL), fit slope α = 6.4 L g⁻¹cm⁻¹, R²=0.9999 — this establishes a clean, quantitative extinction/concentration calibration for the material at the imaging wavelength, i.e., PA/absorbance signal should scale predictably with local agent concentration in that regime. F4f (crop F) shows tumor PA signal over time after IV injection: 0.26 (0h) → 0.13 (1h) → 0.32 (2h) → 0.94 (4h, peak) → 0.60 (6h) → 0.31 (10h), consistent with the stated values (0.26, 0.13, peak 0.94 at 4h, 0.31 at 10h).

Together, the calibration (A) licenses reading the in vivo PA time-course (B) as a proxy for relative agent concentration in the tumor, since absorbance/PA tracks concentration linearly and reproducibly in vitro. So the combination supports: intratumoral accumulation is low shortly after injection, rises to a maximum around 4h, and declines by 10h — a plausible passive-accumulation/clearance profile.

What it leaves open: the in vitro calibration was not performed in tissue, so nothing here establishes that the same slope/linearity holds in vivo (scattering, blood, depth all differ). The data also don't establish causality for the dip at 1h, nor connect this PA time-course to any downstream biological outcome (e.g., therapeutic efficacy, "value" or "service_capability") — that link is not evidenced by these two panels.

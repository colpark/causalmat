Both panels were opened and inspected directly.

F3b shows a clean two-curve absorbance-decay plot: DPBF absorbance with bismuthene falls to ~0.73 by 30 min while the water control only falls to ~0.93, consistent with the stated ROS-generation (singlet-oxygen) capability of bismuthene under laser exposure.

F5d shows six tumor-growth-curve subpanels; the BNs-BSA+808nm subpanel indeed shows most curves near zero through ~day 14 with several then rising to 250–700 mm³ by day ~20 (matching "four of five regrow"), while the BNs-BSA+Lasers (combined 660/808 nm) subpanel stays flat at ~0 for all curves through day 20.

The combination supports the general mechanistic claim that bismuthene has a photodynamic (ROS-generating) capacity distinct from photothermal heating, and that this capacity is consistent with why adding the combined-laser (presumably PDT+PTT) regimen achieves durable tumor control where 808nm (photothermal-dominant) alone does not — late regrowth is compatible with incomplete ablation when ROS-mediated killing is absent or weaker.

What it leaves open: the DPBF assay doesn't specify which laser wavelength was used, so it is not established that ROS generation occurs specifically under 808nm vs 660nm irradiation; the in vitro dye assay is not linked to intratumoral ROS levels; and no direct causal test (e.g., ROS scavenger control in vivo) ties the absorbance-decay mechanism to the observed regrowth difference. The regrowth contrast itself is only correlational with the upstream chemistry, not mechanistically verified.

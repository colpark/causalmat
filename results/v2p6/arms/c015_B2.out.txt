Both panels confirm the printed measurements. F4a (XRD waterfall) shows the ~16.4/19.2/20.1° reflections fading into a weak broad bump by full discharge (~15 h, 0.01 V) and not returning during charge. F3a (SAXS contour) shows the low-q feature bending toward lower q over roughly the same discharge window (~6-11 h) and not reverting on charge.

The upstream conclusion (amorphous, Co0-metallic discharge product, established from XRD+XANES) and the SAXS observation occur in the same discharge interval and share the same irreversibility on charge, so the two are temporally consistent: the loss of crystalline order and reduction to metallic Co coincide with the emergence of new nanoscale (real-space) structure detected by SAXS, and neither reverses on charge. That temporal/directional agreement supports treating the SAXS low-q shift as part of the same conversion event rather than an unrelated process.

What it leaves open: the XRD/XANES pairing identifies chemical/crystallographic state but carries no particle-size or porosity information, so it cannot explain the specific length scale, magnitude, or mechanism (coarsening, pore formation, Co-domain nucleation, phase separation) behind the SAXS shift. Correlation in timing is not a derivation — the upstream evidence does not, by itself, account for why or how the microstructure/porosity signature changes; it only shows the chemical conversion happens in the same window.

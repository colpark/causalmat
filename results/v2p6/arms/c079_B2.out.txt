The upstream micrographs (F1b–F1f) partially, but only qualitatively, account for the downstream YS trend. Visually, particle density clearly increases from panel b through f, consistent with rising SiCp wt%, and the particles remain dispersed across the field with local clusters rather than gross segregation at any composition — so nothing in the images suggests the SiCp additions are so poorly dispersed as to undermine load transfer or nucleate large defects. This is consistent with, and does not contradict, the monotonic YS rise (93→142 MPa, 0–25 wt%): more particles, still reasonably distributed, tracking higher strength is the expected qualitative pattern for particulate reinforcement.

What it does not establish: no quantitative dispersion metric (areal fraction, particle count, clustering index) is tied to any specific YS value, so the correlation is visual/qualitative only, not a fitted or measured relationship. No strengthening mechanism (Orowan looping, load transfer, grain refinement, thermal-mismatch dislocation density) is identified or tested, so the images cannot explain why or how much SiCp raises YS — only that dispersion is not obviously degrading with content. The panel-to-composition mapping is also incomplete (only d/e/f captions given as 15/20/25 wt%), and clustering's effect on strength (locally weakening vs. negligible) remains unaddressed.

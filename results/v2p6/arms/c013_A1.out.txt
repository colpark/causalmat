Both panels confirm the printed measurements. F5c (power factor) shows x=0.01 (red circles) as the highest curve across the full range, ~29 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² at 300 K falling to ~22 at 623 K; x=0.005 (black squares) is the lowest of the doped set (~23→13); x=0 (green) sits far below all doped samples, never exceeding ~5. F6a (ZT) confirms x=0.005 gives the highest ZT near 300 K (~0.84-0.85), just above x=0.01 (~0.78-0.80), with x=0.02/0.04 lower (~0.63-0.69) and the n-Bi2Te3 reference curve near 0.88.

The combination supports that these are real, correctly reported trends for both quantities, and it supports that PF alone does not determine ZT ranking: x=0.01 dominates PF at every temperature shown, yet x=0.005 — not x=0.01 — has the highest ZT at 300 K. That divergence is evidence a factor other than PF (most plausibly thermal conductivity, not shown in either panel) must differ between x=0.005 and x=0.01 to produce this crossover.

What it leaves open: neither panel shows thermal conductivity, so the mechanism behind the PF/ZT rank reversal is not established here, only implied. The panels also don't clarify whether the ZT ranking persists or flips at higher T, since only 300 K values were given for B.

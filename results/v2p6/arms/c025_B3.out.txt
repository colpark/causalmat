All four panels confirm the stated values. F3b: microfluidic (teal) gives one narrow DLS mode ~300-400 nm; hydrothermal (magenta) gives a broader mode ~900-1000 nm. F4f/g/h: microfluidic particles show higher encapsulation than hydrothermal for siRNA (~53 vs ~37%), DOX (~47 vs ~34%), and BSA (~59 vs ~43%), each marked with an asterisk.

What the combination supports: the two synthesis routes differ consistently on both axes — microfluidic particles are smaller/more monodisperse (already corroborated by DLS+SEM agreement) and, independently, load more cargo across all three tested payloads (siRNA, DOX, BSA), each difference flagged as statistically significant. This establishes a reproducible co-occurrence: the route that gives smaller/narrower particles is the same route that gives higher encapsulation, across three chemically distinct cargos.

What it leaves open: these four panels show parallel comparisons, not a mechanistic or even a direct statistical link between size/distribution and encapsulation — no panel plots encapsulation against particle size, so size is not shown to drive encapsulation as opposed to other route-specific differences (mixing kinetics, reagent concentration, crystallinity/porosity) that could independently affect loading. The stars establish that each pairwise difference (size; each encapsulation value) is significant on its own, not that size and encapsulation are correlated with each other. So the evidence is consistent with a size-encapsulation relationship but does not establish it.

All five panels examined. F1b-e (optical, polished cross-sections) show dark SiCp particles broadly scattered across each field with visible local clusters/stringers, consistent with observation A. F5a (SEM, X150, fracture surface) shows a rough tear-ridge topography around flatter facet-like regions, consistent with observation B.

The upstream evidence (established convergence of optical and SEM imaging on a broadly dispersed-with-clusters distribution) is qualitatively compatible with the downstream fracture observation: clustered particles are a plausible site for the flat, facet-like regions (particle/interface-controlled fracture), while the intervening ridges plausibly reflect ductile matrix tearing between particles. This is a coherent, not contradictory, pairing.

However, the upstream evidence does not account for — i.e., does not causally establish — the fracture-surface pattern. F5a is a fracture surface from a mechanically tested specimen, a different sample state and imaging geometry than the polished cross-sections in F1b-e, so no spatial correspondence between specific clusters and specific facets can be verified from these panels. No measurement links cluster density, size, or location to facet frequency or ridge spacing, and no analysis addresses interfacial bonding, which independently governs whether particles produce facets. So the combination supports a plausible qualitative connection between microstructural distribution and fracture morphology, but leaves the mechanism, causal linkage, and any quantitative relationship (behavior_class attribution) open.

Yes, within limits. F3a shows the milled C10 pattern (top, blue) carries the same indexed peaks (1 CeMg12, 2 Y5Mg24, 3 Ce2Mg17, 4 Mg) as the C0 pattern (bottom, black), with only one extra, weak line (5, Co) near 44-45 deg — consistent with claim A: Co sits as a separate, minor phase rather than altering the host hydride-forming phases. F11a (0 wt% C@Co) and F11b (+10 wt% C@Co) both show single flat-ish plateaus at 320/340/360 C in roughly the same 0.15-0.5 MPa band, consistent with claim B that plateau pressures are nearly unchanged by the Co addition.

Together this supports: catalyst addition preserves phase identity of the Mg-Ce-Y hydride system and leaves its equilibrium plateau pressure (thermodynamic value) essentially the same — i.e., any benefit from C@Co is not achieved by changing which phases form or their thermodynamic hydrogen affinity.

Left open: F11b's plateaus (especially 360 C) are visibly less flat/more sloped than F11a's, so "nearly the same pressure" is approximate, not exact equality, and kinetic or homogeneity differences aren't ruled out. The single weak Co line also doesn't establish Co's dispersion, reactivity, or behavior over cycling — only its presence as-milled.

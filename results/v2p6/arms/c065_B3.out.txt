The panels confirm what's described: F4c is the EDS map (Mg green, C blue, Co red) showing C and Co signal concentrated on particle surfaces/edges of the C10 alloy; F6a/F6b are Sieverts absorption curves showing the diffusion-controlled second stage taking 66 min (C0, panel a) vs 11 min (C10, panel b) at 360°C.

What this combination supports: the EDS map (backed by the earlier HRTEM corroboration of an actual carbon-layer phase at particle edges) establishes that C10 particles carry a C/Co-rich surface coating, and separately the Sieverts data show C10 absorbs hydrogen roughly six times faster in the diffusion-controlled stage than the uncoated C0 material. Taken together, this is consistent with a surface-coating-enables-faster-kinetics narrative: a C@Co layer sitting on the particle surface/edges is a plausible physical site for whatever catalyzes or accelerates diffusion into the alloy.

What it leaves open: nothing here mechanistically connects the two observations — no measurement (diffusion coefficient, activation energy, catalytic activity assay, or microstructural path length) directly links the C/Co surface distribution to the shortened diffusion stage. The EDS/HRTEM evidence only shows where C and Co sit, not that they cause the kinetic change; the correlation (same C10 material, coated surface, faster uptake) is suggestive but not a demonstrated causal or quantitative link. Also, as previously noted, no Co lattice fringe is resolved, so whether Co's dispersion state within the carbon matters for the catalytic effect is unaddressed.

Both panels support a coherent, limited claim: silane surface functionalization changed BST particle surface chemistry, and this chemistry difference correlates with a difference in dispersibility/wettability behavior.

F2a (FTIR): The BST-SH spectrum shows new absorption bands at 2930, 2864, and 1122 cm-1 that are absent from both the bare BST and BST-OH spectra. These are consistent with C-H stretching (2930/2864) and Si-O or C-S-related stretching (1122) from a mercaptosilane grafted onto the particle surface — i.e., direct chemical evidence that BST-SH carries an organic/thiol-bearing surface layer not present on BST-OH or BST.

F2b (photograph): In the water-toluene two-phase system after 2h standing, BST-OH settles into the lower (aqueous) layer while BST-SH remains dispersed/suspended in the upper (toluene/organic) layer. This is consistent with BST-SH being more hydrophobic/organophilic than BST-OH.

Combined, the two panels support: the new surface chemistry detected by FTIR on BST-SH plausibly explains why BST-SH partitions into the organic phase rather than water. What is left open: this is a correlational pairing across two different figures, not a mechanistic demonstration — no quantitative bonding/coverage data, no PRP or "value"/performance metric, and no direct causal test (e.g., control removing the SH groups) tying the specific FTIR bands to the phase-partitioning behavior is shown here.

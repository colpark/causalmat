The EDS spectrum (F5b) shows Ti and O peaks plus a large C peak (likely adventitious carbon/substrate) and small Er (~1.4 keV) and Ce (~4.8 keV) peaks, confirming that Er and Ce are present as dopants in the titania sample alongside the host Ti/O signal. The PL spectrum (F7) shows the 390-420 nm emission band decreasing in intensity from pure TiO2, to Er0.5Ti-O (about half), to Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O (lowest).

Together, this combination supports the qualitative claim that Er/Ce doping of TiO2 correlates with progressive quenching of the band-edge PL emission — plausible because dopant-related defect/trap states can act as non-radiative recombination centers. The EDS confirms the dopants (Er, Ce) are physically present in the material, which is consistent with them being the cause of the observed quenching trend.

What it leaves open: only one EDS spectrum is shown (apparently for the doubly-doped sample), so there is no EDS confirmation of dopant absence/presence and relative loading in the pure TiO2 or single-Er samples individually, and no quantitative atomic percentages are given — the C peak's dominance further limits quantitative reading. Neither panel establishes the mechanism (trap states vs. crystallinity/particle-size changes) linking composition to quenching, so causality of the PL trend is inferred, not directly demonstrated, by this evidence pair.

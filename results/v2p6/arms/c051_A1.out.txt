FTIR panel (F2a) confirms observation A exactly as stated: the red BST-SH trace alone shows new peaks at 2930, 2864 cm⁻¹ (C–H stretches) and 1122 cm⁻¹, absent from the grey BST and blue BST-OH traces — direct visual evidence that thiol-functionalization introduced new bonding/chemistry not present at the OH or bare-particle stage.

DSC panel (F5a) confirms observation B exactly: pure PAEN shows a Tg step at 176°C, while BST@Ag composites at 1%, 3%, 5% show Tg at 146, 149, 157°C respectively — all depressed relative to neat PAEN, with Tg rising monotonically as Ag loading increases.

Together, these two panels establish that (1) surface chemistry on the particles changed (new SH-related bands) and (2) composite Tg is lower than neat polymer and increases with filler loading. But the combination does not itself demonstrate that the new SH chemistry causes the Tg depression/recovery trend: the FTIR panel is run on BST/BST-OH/BST-SH particles, while the DSC panel is run on PAEN/BST@Ag composites — different particle labeling (SH-functionalized stage vs. Ag-loaded stage), with no panel linking SH bonding density to Tg value or showing a BST-SH-vs-Ag dose series side by side. So the FTIR evidence supports that chemical modification occurred, and DSC supports a Tg trend with Ag%, but the causal link between the specific new bonds and the Tg shift is not shown here and remains an inference, not a demonstrated connection.

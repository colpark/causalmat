F5c is a temperature-vs-time trace over 5 min: BNs-BSA-injected tumors climb to ~48-49 C while PBS-injected tumors climb only to ~40-41 C under 808 nm, 7 W/cm2 irradiation — this is Observation B in the caption-labeled panel.

Does the upstream evidence account for the downstream tumor observation? Qualitatively, yes: the already-established absorption-mediated photothermal mechanism (concentration- and power-dependent in vitro heating, non-heating water control) predicts that BNs-BSA delivered to a tumor and irradiated will generate excess heat relative to a non-absorbing control, and F5c shows exactly that directional pattern (BNs-BSA >> PBS). That supports the material's photothermal activity translating from solution to tissue and reaching a temperature range (~48-49 C) generally associated with therapeutic photothermal ablation.

What it leaves open: (1) unit/setting mismatch — in vitro dose-response used discrete ug/mL and W values in solution, while F5c uses a single fixed BNs-BSA dose and 7 W/cm2 in tissue, so no quantitative curve links the two; (2) unlike the water control in F3e (which stayed flat), the PBS control in F5c itself rises from ~36 to ~41 C, showing laser/tissue alone produces substantial non-material heating in vivo — so these three panels alone cannot cleanly attribute the BNs-BSA tumor delta purely to the same absorption mechanism versus additional tissue/blood absorption or perfusion effects; (3) the prior caveats (no derivable conversion efficiency, no fitted rate law, scattering/convection not excluded) still apply and are not resolved by F5c.

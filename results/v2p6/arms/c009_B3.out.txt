Yes, the upstream evidence is consistent with the downstream desorption-time observation, but it does not add independent support — it is the same dataset viewed differently. Panels F8a (C0) and F8b (C10) show the raw isothermal desorption curves at 360/320/300/280 °C from which the rate constants k feeding the Arrhenius plot (F10) were extracted. The qualitative pattern matches the Ea values exactly: C10 (Ea=81.9 kJ/mol) empties in ~11 min at 300 °C and <40 min even at 280 °C, while C0 (Ea=130.3 kJ/mol) needs ~150 min at 300 °C and ~450 min at 280 °C — a much steeper temperature sensitivity for C0, as a higher activation energy predicts. So the combination confirms internal consistency between the raw kinetic data and the derived Ea difference; it is not a separate corroborating measurement, since F8a/F8b are the source data for F10's lines.

What remains open, carried from the earlier TEM/Arrhenius qualifications: only two samples (C0, C10) are compared, so this shows an ordering, not a dose-response with dispersion/particle size; there is no TEM of C0 to isolate dispersion as the cause versus other compositional differences; and the kinetic Ea/desorption-time data still cannot, by themselves, attribute the rate difference specifically to Co particle catalysis rather than some other C10-vs-C0 distinction.

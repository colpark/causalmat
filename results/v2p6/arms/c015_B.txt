An earlier step in this work already established the following, and you may treat it as settled:

Put together, the disappearance of the oxide's Bragg reflections (~16.4, 19.2, 20.1 deg, fading to a weak broad bump by end of discharge, not regained on charge) and the collapse of the Co XANES white line (~0.97) to a shape matching the Co foil at discharge jointly identify the discharge product as a phase that has lost the oxide's long-range crystalline order AND has Co reduced to the metallic (Co0) oxidation state -- i.e., conversion of the oxide to an amorphous/metallic-Co-like product by 0.01 V. XRD alone only shows that the original oxide's crystallinity is lost, without saying what chemical state replaces it; XANES alone only shows that Co's local oxidation state matches metallic Co, without saying whether any long-range order survives. Neither alone supports the amorphous-and-metallic identification; together they do.

The qualifications recorded on that earlier result:
  - Neither observation quantifies the fraction of material converted -- both are presence/absence (XRD reflections) or shape-matching (XANES white line, spectral shape) statements, not phase-fraction amounts.
  - The XRD broad bump does not by itself distinguish true amorphization from nanocrystalline Co domains too small to diffract coherently, and XANES (a local probe) cannot resolve long-range order either, so this pairing cannot separate 'amorphous' from 'nanocrystalline but XRD-invisible' Co.
  - On charge, XRD shows the oxide reflections are not regained and XANES shows the charged spectra differ from the OCV spectra; together these only establish incomplete reversibility of both structure and oxidation state -- they do not establish what phase forms on charge, only that it differs from both the discharged Co-metal state and the original OCV oxide state.

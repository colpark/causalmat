Panels reviewed: F4b (before cycling) and F4c (after 5 thermal cycles) show SEM crack networks in Al2O3-YSZ that look broadly similar to each other in crack density/distribution and pore spot pattern — no obvious coarsening or new connectivity is visible by eye between the two. F8 shows optical photographs of the Al2O3-YSZ coupon over the full cycling series: intact/uniform at 0 and 5 cycles, then a spalled corner appearing at 10 cycles that persists and grows slightly through 20, 30, 40.

What the combination supports: F4b/F4c is consistent with the coupon's intact appearance at 5 cycles in F8 — i.e., through the first 5 cycles, the Al2O3-YSZ crack network at the imaged microscale does not show visible worsening, matching the absence of macroscopic damage at that same point. This is compatible with (but does not by itself prove) the idea that no major microstructural degradation had yet occurred at 5 cycles.

What it leaves open: F4b/F4c stops at 5 cycles, while the spallation event downstream occurs at 10 cycles and continues growing to 40. There is no SEM view spanning 5→10 cycles or beyond, so the evidence cannot show what microstructural change (if any) accompanies the onset of corner spallation or its subsequent growth. Also, F4b/c is a single localized field of view versus F8's whole-coupon photograph, so a local "unchanged" crack network cannot rule out edge/corner-specific damage initiation elsewhere on the coupon. The link between microscale crack stability and macroscale spallation timing therefore remains asserted by temporal proximity only, not demonstrated mechanistically.

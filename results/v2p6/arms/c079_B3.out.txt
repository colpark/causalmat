The panels (b→f, corresponding to 0→25 wt% SiCp, per captions on d,e,f = 15,20,25 wt%) show a visibly increasing dark-particle area fraction and packing density with nominal SiCp content, consistent with the reinforcement loading that the tensile study intends. That trend is qualitatively compatible with the observed YS rise (93→142 MPa across 0-25 wt%): more particles broadly dispersed through the matrix is the kind of microstructure needed for load-transfer/Orowan-type strengthening to operate, and the established finding that dispersion is broad with only local clusters (not gross segregation) supports that reinforcement is largely available to strengthen the matrix rather than being wasted in large agglomerates.

What it does not establish: no area fraction, particle spacing, or particle-size distribution was quantified in either panel set, so the apparent density increase cannot be matched numerically to the wt% steps or to the specific YS increments. No mechanism (dislocation density, grain refinement, interfacial bonding, load-bearing capacity) linking dispersion to strength was measured. Panel f also shows the densest local clustering of the series, so whether clustering scales unfavorably with content — and thereby limits strengthening efficiency at higher wt% — is not addressed. The optical/SEM agreement rules out imaging artifact but not a quantitative causal account of the YS trend.

Combined, the evidence supports a narrow, purely temporal statement: at the 5-cycle mark, the coating is macroscopically intact and its local crack network (in the one SEM field sampled) looks no different from the as-processed state. That is consistent with — but does not demonstrate — a link between microstructural cracking and later spallation, because the SEM comparison stops at 5 cycles, before any spallation appears (spallation onset is at 10). There is no SEM data at 10, 20, 30, or 40 cycles to show whether the crack network coarsens, connects, or changes near the corners where spallation actually occurs. The SEM field is also a single interior location, not necessarily the corner region where damage initiates, so it cannot speak to local stress/crack conditions there. In short: the pairing supports "no visible microstructural change precedes visible macroscopic failure at 5 cycles" but leaves open the mechanism, location, and timing of the crack evolution that would connect STR/microstructure to the corner spallation and any downstream PRF/service_capability claim.

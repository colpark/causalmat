Both panels confirm the stated numbers. F2a (relative density) shows the HP curve rising sharply from ~97.5% at 0 wt% AlN to essentially 100% at 1 wt%, dipping only slightly to ~99.3% at 3 wt%, and returning to ~100% at 5 wt% — i.e., HP densification is essentially complete (≥99%) across the whole 1–5 wt% range. F2b (Vickers hardness) shows HP hardness rising from ~18.0 GPa (0 wt%) to a peak of ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then declining to ~19.4 GPa (3 wt%) and ~19.2 GPa (5 wt%), with visible error bars.

The upstream density evidence accounts for the coarse pattern: near-full densification under HP explains why hardness at 1, 3, and 5 wt% is uniformly higher than at 0 wt%, where residual porosity (~2.5%) depresses hardness. That part is well supported by the co-plotted trends.

What it leaves open: density is essentially flat (99.3–100%) across 1, 3, and 5 wt%, yet hardness falls monotonically from 20.3 to 19.2 GPa over that same range. Porosity/density alone cannot explain this decline — the panels give no microstructural (grain size, AlN distribution, second-phase) data, so the cause of the post-1-wt% hardness drop remains undetermined from this evidence.

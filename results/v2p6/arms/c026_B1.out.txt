F2b (image f318b17...) is Figure 2's charge-transfer/binding-energy bar charts — the same panels already summarized in the settled DFT finding (obs5–obs7); it adds nothing new here.

F3a/F3c (image 4ca5495d...) show N 1s NEXAFS before vs. after zinc deposition (peak shift consistent with new N–Zn bonding), Raman spectra tracked through the plating sequence, SEM images of the resulting deposit (plate-like/compact on the N-doped scaffold vs. needle-like/dendritic on undoped carbon), and a schematic contrasting "spacious nucleation → lateral growth → homogeneous deposit" with "dense nucleation → tip growth → dendrite."

F4c/F4d (image 4d64f67...) show galvanostatic charge–discharge curves at several current densities and the initial nucleation-overpotential comparison: 39 mV (N-doped, CnC HS) vs. 43 mV (undoped, C HS), plus cycling/coulombic-efficiency data elsewhere in that figure showing the undoped cell failing (short-circuiting) earlier.

Conclusion the evidence justifies: the N-doped scaffold nucleates and plates zinc more uniformly (lower overpotential, non-dendritic morphology by SEM, longer cell life before failure) than the undoped scaffold, a pattern qualitatively consistent with a more favorable, N-mediated zinc binding site.

What it leaves uncertain: this is correlational device-level performance, not a mechanistic isolation — it cannot attribute the improvement specifically to pyridinic-N Zn binding versus other differences between the two carbon scaffolds (surface area, conductivity, pore structure), nor does it quantitatively link the ~4 mV overpotential gap to the DFT binding-energy differences.

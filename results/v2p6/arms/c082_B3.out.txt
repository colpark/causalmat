The panels (F1b–F1f, 200 μm scale) confirm the upstream finding: dark SiCp particles are spread across the whole field at every content level shown (5–25 wt%, per captions on d/e/f = 15/20/25 wt%), with local clusters/stringers rather than gross segregation, and apparent particle density visibly increasing from b through f.

This dispersion evidence supports the idea that reinforcement was incorporated without wholesale segregation at any composition tested — a necessary precondition for particle-strengthening mechanisms (load transfer, dislocation/grain-boundary pinning) to plausibly contribute to creep resistance. That is consistent with, and does not contradict, the creep result that 15/20/25 wt% composites sit below AZ91 (better creep resistance) while 5/10 wt% overlap or lie slightly above.

But the upstream evidence does not account for *why* the improvement appears only above a threshold near 15 wt%. It gives no quantitative uniformity metric, no particle volume fraction, no interparticle spacing, and no size distribution — so it cannot show that dispersion quality or spacing differs systematically between the low (5–10 wt%) and high (15–25 wt%) content composites in a way that would explain the creep threshold. It also samples different fields (optical b–f, plus a separate SEM field) rather than a matched, content-controlled comparison. The combination therefore supports "dispersed, not segregated, reinforcement across contents" but leaves the mechanism behind the creep threshold open.

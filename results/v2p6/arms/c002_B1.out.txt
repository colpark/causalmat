Yes, the upstream phase evidence helps account for the downstream capacity observation, with real limits.

F3a (XRD) confirms the earlier-established point directly: C10's pattern reproduces every C0 reflection (CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17, Mg) plus one weak extra line (5) assigned to Co, i.e., Co sits as an inert added phase, not a new compound. F7a/b show absorption curves for C0 and C10 across 100–250°C; at 250°C the plateaus read ~5.1 wt% (C0) and ~4.56 wt% (C10), matching measurement B. Notably, 5.1 wt% × 0.9 (accounting for the inert 10 wt% Co diluting the hydride-forming mass) ≈ 4.59 wt%, very close to the observed 4.56 wt%. So the "Co stays a separate, non-reacting phase" finding is quantitatively consistent with the capacity drop being a simple dilution effect from adding inert mass, rather than evidence that Co altered or degraded the alloy's intrinsic hydrogen-storage phases.

What this leaves open: the XRD/SAED pairing cannot rule out sub-diffraction interdiffusion, amorphous interfacial layers, or minor/dispersed reaction product that could also shave capacity or affect kinetics; it says nothing about the post-cycling state; and the dilution match, while close, is arithmetic corroboration from two separate panels, not a value the paper itself derives or attributes causally.

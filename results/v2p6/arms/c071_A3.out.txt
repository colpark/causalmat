Both cited image files resolve to the same two composite figures (Fig. 5 and Fig. 6); the per-panel labels in the request point to crops that don't exist as separate files, so I'm reading the full figures.

Fig. 5: SEM of platelets after 2 h in platelet-rich plasma (A) shows dense, spread, activated platelets on bare 316LSS and PPAmF, and sparse, rounded platelets on BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and especially combined BVLD/NO-PPAmF. Panels B and C quantify this: platelet number and % activation are both significantly lower (***) on the NO- and BVLD-modified surfaces than on 316LSS/PPAmF, with the combined coating lowest of all. Panels D and E show total fibrinogen and fibrinogen γ-chain adsorption also drop significantly on modified surfaces.

Fig. 6: an ex vivo arteriovenous shunt (2 h blood circulation) shows the same pattern — clearer, less occluded lumens (B), thinner/lighter thrombus in longitudinal sections (C), sparser platelet adhesion by SEM (D), and quantitatively lower occlusion % and thrombus weight (E, F), with blood flow rate best preserved (~90%) for the combined BVLD/NO-PPAmF surface versus ~20% for bare or PPAmF controls (G).

Together this justifies: the NO/BVLD surface modification measurably reduces platelet adhesion/activation, fibrinogen fouling, and thrombus formation, preserving blood flow better than uncoated or PPAmF-only controls in a short (2 h) ex vivo test. It does not establish long-term in vivo durability, mechanical/wear stability of the coating, or performance beyond this single 2 h window.

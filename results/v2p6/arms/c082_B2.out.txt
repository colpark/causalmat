The upstream micrograph evidence and the downstream creep result are compatible but the former does not explain the latter.

What is supported: panels b-f (visually inspected) show dark SiCp particles spread across the whole imaged field at every composition, with local clusters present throughout rather than confined to one region or one wt% level. Combined with the earlier optical/SEM agreement, this establishes that dispersion (not gross segregation) is a genuine feature across the whole 5-25 wt% series, including both the group that lies below AZ91 in creep rate (15, 20, 25 wt%, panels d/e/f) and the group that overlaps or exceeds it (5, 10 wt%, presumably panels b/c). So the creep crossover cannot be attributed to one subgroup having obviously unmixed/segregated reinforcement while the other does not - both subgroups show the same qualitative dispersion pattern.

What is left open: no quantitative particle density, area fraction, interparticle spacing, or clustering index is reported, so whether the creep improvement at 15+ wt% reflects a loading/volume-fraction threshold (more particles per unit area, visible impressionistically as denser coverage from b to f) versus some other mechanism (grain refinement, load transfer, pinning) cannot be established. The microstructure and creep measurements are two separate observations on the same samples, not a demonstrated causal or quantitative link.

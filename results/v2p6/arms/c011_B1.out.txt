Confirmed panels: F5c shows PF(T) exactly as claimed—x=0.01 highest (~29 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² at 300 K, peaking slightly near 320–370 K before falling to ~22 at 623 K), x=0.005 lowest among doped curves, x=0 far lowest (~2–5) and rising with T. F6a shows ZT(x=0.01) peaking near ~1.25 around 500–525 K, then declining, with x=0 lowest throughout (~0.05–0.22).

The upstream resistivity/PF pairing accounts for the composition ranking: x=0.01 has both the highest PF and the highest ZT, x=0 has both the lowest PF and lowest ZT, consistent with PF (and hence low resistivity) contributing to a higher figure of merit. That much is supported.

It does not account for the temperature location of the ZT maximum. PF for x=0.01 already turns over and falls monotonically after ~350 K, yet ZT keeps rising until ~500–525 K before falling. Since ZT = PF·T/κ, this divergence means thermal conductivity's temperature dependence must also be shaping where ZT peaks—something the resistivity/PF evidence, which contains no κ data, cannot speak to. So the combination supports PF (via low resistivity) as a contributor to the composition ranking of ZT, but leaves the ZT peak's temperature position, and any causal role of thermal conductivity, unestablished.

Panels examined:
- F4b/F4c: SEM cross-sections of the fine crack network (YSZ), before and after five thermal cycles — the two fields look visually comparable: similar crack density, similar short/fine crack morphology, similar pore locations, consistent with the "unchanged in the same field" reading already established.
- F8: optical photographs of Al2O3-YSZ coatings at 0/5/10/20/30/40 cycles — coating intact at 0 and 5, corner spallation appears at 10 cycles and persists/enlarges slightly through 40.

Assessment: The upstream SEM evidence (crack-network stability, plus the as-sprayed contrast showing Al2O3-YSZ starts coarser/more open than YSZ) does not account for the F8 spallation observation. The stability check was performed on YSZ only, at the microstructural scale, and only across a 0-to-5-cycle window; F8's damage is macroscopic spallation on Al2O3-YSZ specifically, first appearing at cycle 10 and continuing to cycle 40 — a different material, a different observational scale (SEM crack morphology vs. optical coating-edge integrity), and a cycle range the SEM pair never covered. Because no before/after SEM of Al2O3-YSZ's own crack network exists, the combination cannot say whether that material's coarser starting cracks connected, propagated, or fed into the corner spallation seen photographically. What is justified: YSZ's fine crack network appears stable at least through 5 cycles, and Al2O3-YSZ starts out microstructurally coarser. What remains open: the mechanistic link (if any) between either material's crack evolution and the observed Al2O3-YSZ spallation onset/growth is not shown by this evidence pair.

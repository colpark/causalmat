Panels confirm the stated readings: F3c shows nearly vertical (unchanging) color bands across the discharge/charge trace, i.e., a flat SAXS contour with time/voltage; F3d shows six voltage-resolved I(q) curves (OCV, 0.45V, 0.136V, 0.11V, 0.02V, 3V) that overlap almost completely on a log-log plot, with only a slight fanning near q~0.03-0.1 Å⁻¹ at the lowest voltages; F2d shows discharge capacity falling from ~870 mAh/g (cycle 1) to ~480 (cycle 2) and asymptoting near ~70 mAh/g by cycle 40, alongside charge capacity and rising Coulombic efficiency.

The upstream SAXS evidence (A) and the downstream capacity-fade observation (B) are consistent with each other but the SAXS alone does not "account for" the fade in a mechanistic sense — it only narrows the explanation. What the pairing supports: the severe, rapid capacity loss seen in F2d is not accompanied by a corresponding structural/volumetric signature in F3c/F3d, so a large volume-change mechanism is disfavored as the cause of that fade, consistent with the earlier-established reading that poor/stalled sodiation activity (per AIMD) is the more likely driver. What it leaves open: SAXS at 0.11/0.02 V is a snapshot of scattering intensity, not a per-cycle measurement, so it cannot directly track or quantify the capacity decline across all 40 cycles in F2d; it cannot identify the actual chemical/kinetic mechanism of fade, only exclude one candidate (volume change). The identification of the true cause still rests on the AIMD oxidation-degree result, with its own stated timescale and small-N limitations.

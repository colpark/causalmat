Yes, the two panels are consistent with each other and jointly support the downstream claim, but only partially, and each has its own limits.

F5e (EDS mapping) shows C, Zn, and N signals densely filling the same particle outline for both the DNA-aptamer and RNA-aptamer BSA@ZIF-8 samples, confirming the ZIF-8/BSA framework is intact, while P appears only as sparse, scattered dots within that same outline — consistent with a small amount of phosphorus-bearing aptamer being co-localized with, not separate from, the MOF particle. This supports "successful aptamer incorporation" at the level of elemental co-localization, but EDS mapping is qualitative (dot density, not concentration) and does not establish aptamer surface exposure, orientation, or functional/bioavailable loading — so it speaks to structure/composition, not directly to targeting capability.

F6b (ex vivo fluorescence) shows both inguinal and axillary lymph nodes brighter (green, toward the upper end of the shared 0–2500 scale) for the anti-CCL21 aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 than for the neat BSA@ZIF-8 (cyan, lower end), under both subcutaneous and footpad injection. This is a genuine functional readout — greater lymph-node accumulation/retention associated with aptamer presence — and it does track with the compositional confirmation in F5e in the sense that the same aptamer-bearing particle (P-positive by EDS) is the one producing enhanced lymph-node signal.

Together: the combination supports that aptamer is physically present on/in the ZIF-8 particle (F5e) and that particles bearing it accumulate more strongly in lymph nodes than the neat MOF (F6b) — consistent with aptamer conferring lymph-node targeting/service capability. What it leaves open: EDS is not quantitative proof of surface-accessible aptamer or dose per particle, so a mechanistic link from "P present" to "why fluorescence is brighter" (e.g., receptor-specific CCL21 binding vs. nonspecific size/charge effects, or simply different fluorophore loading between the two formulations) is not established by these two panels alone — no binding-specificity or aptamer-dose-normalized control is shown here.

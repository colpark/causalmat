The upstream microstructural finding (F10d: finest, most uniform recrystallized grain/feature size, ~0.3-0.8 um, versus coarser F7c/F9c) is consistent in direction with the downstream mechanical observation: adding EPT (F12 navy, no EPT, "423K-8+373K-3") to reach the same base condition plus a 25 us-10 min pulse (F13 blue, "423K-8+373K-3+25us-10min") lowers the yield knee slightly (~350→~330 MPa) but raises the ultimate stress (~420→~447 MPa) and roughly doubles the strain to failure (~0.095→~0.15). A finer, more uniform grain structure is qualitatively compatible with simultaneously higher ductility and higher peak stress, so the combination supports the claim that the EPT-refined microstructure documented in F10d plausibly underlies the improved strength-ductility combination seen in F13's blue curve for the matching EPT condition.

What it leaves open: the images give no mechanism (dislocation density, texture, twin suppression) linking grain refinement to the specific stress-strain response, so the causal link is asserted, not demonstrated. F10d is one SEM image from one sample; F13's curve is a separate single-sample tensile trace — no statistics tie a measured grain-size distribution to the measured mechanical values. The simultaneous strength+ductility gain (unusual by simple Hall-Petch reasoning) could instead reflect texture or work-hardening changes from EPT that the micrographs don't capture. The "25s" vs "25us" labeling mismatch between F10d's caption and F13's legend also leaves the sample correspondence only presumptive, not confirmed.

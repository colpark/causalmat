Panels confirm the stated measurements: F3d (CnC HS) shows a largely flat deposit with a few sparse protruding platelets/needles; F3e (C HS) shows a dense, uniform mat of sharp overlapping platelets. F4a shows CnC HS (dark circles) sitting near 93-100% CE out to ~205 cycles, while C HS (open circles) tracks similarly until it dips to ~75% near cycle 40-48 and then drops to 0%, with no further points after ~50 cycles.

The combination supports a coherent narrative: the flat, non-dendritic zinc morphology on CnC HS co-occurs with long, stable high-CE cycling, while the dense sharp-platelet morphology on C HS co-occurs with early CE collapse (likely reflecting dendrite-driven side reactions, dead Zn, or short-circuiting). This is consistent with, and extends, the earlier Zn-N-bonding/flat-deposit picture toward a plausible link to cycling performance.

What it leaves open: this is still a correlation between two qualitative, single-condition snapshots (one SEM image pair, one CE curve pair), not a mechanistic or quantitative test showing that the platelet morphology itself causes the CE failure. No image or measurement directly ties the moment of CE collapse to observed dendrite growth or short-circuiting. Other differences between the two host structures (surface area, conductivity, porosity) remain uncontrolled, so they cannot be excluded as contributing causes of the CE divergence independent of morphology.

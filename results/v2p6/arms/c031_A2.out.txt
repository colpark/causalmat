The images support a qualified "yes." F10d (25s/25μs-10min sample) shows a uniform, sub-micron structure (~0.3–0.8 µm features) at the same 2 µm scale as the unshown (b)/(c) panels, i.e., markedly finer than those comparators. F12 and F13 show the same base processing route (423K-8+373K-3) with and without EPT: without EPT the curve tops out near 420 MPa at ~0.095 strain with a ~350 MPa yield knee; with EPT (423K-8+373K-3+25µs-10min, F13 blue) the curve reaches ~447 MPa at a longer ~0.15 strain, with yield knee softening slightly to ~330 MPa. So the combination is consistent with a story where EPT drives grain/feature refinement that accompanies a simultaneous rise in ultimate strength and elongation-to-failure relative to the non-EPT variant.

What it leaves open: the SEM (F10d) gives no quantitative grain-size statistic and its "25s-10min" label doesn't exactly match F13's "25µs-10min" curve label, so the microstructure-to-curve pairing is inferred, not directly co-indexed. No SEM of the non-EPT (F12) counterpart is included here, so the "finer than (b),(c)" claim can't be tied specifically to the F12 curve for direct before/after comparison. The mechanism behind the yield-knee drop despite refinement (texture, twinning, precipitates) is not addressed by these three panels.

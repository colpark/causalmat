ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Both panels open as described. F3b (Fig 3b): bismuthene drives DPBF relative absorbance down to ~0.73 by 30 min, while water controls only fall to ~0.93 — confirming bismuthene generates reactive oxygen species (a photosensitizing/PRP-type material property) far more effectively than the no-photosensitizer control. F5d shows six individual-mouse panels; the "BNs-BSA+808nm" group has four of five curves flat near zero before rising sharply after ~day 14–15 (matching the stated regrowth), whereas the "BNs-BSA+Lasers" (combined 660/808nm) group stays at ~0 across all five mice through day 20.

The combination supports that bismuthene possesses genuine ROS-generating capacity in solution, consistent with a photodynamic mechanism contributing to the therapy, and that this mechanism correlates with better tumor control when both lasers are used together versus 808nm alone. It does not establish causation for the downstream difference: F3b is a cell-free DPBF assay with no stated laser wavelength match to the 808nm-alone in vivo arm, no dosing/irradiance link, and no direct test of why 660nm co-irradiation prevents the regrowth seen with 808nm monotherapy. Whether the regrowth reflects insufficient ROS versus insufficient photothermal effect, incomplete tumor coverage, or dosing is left open by this evidence pair.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Both panels viewed. F3b (UVVIS): DPBF relative absorbance at 421 nm falls from 1.0 to ~0.73 with bismuthene over 30 min, versus a shallow decline to ~0.93 in water alone — the same ordering already established. F5d (tumour-growth panel, six sub-plots): the "BNs-BSA+Lasers" group (rightmost, red curves) stays flat near 0 in all five mice through day ~20; the "BNs-BSA+808 nm" group (blue curves) shows most animals suppressed early but four of five regrow steeply after ~day 14–15.

The upstream evidence (narrow ~0.69 eV band gap plus faster DPBF decay with bismuthene) supports that bismuthene can generate a light-associated reactive-oxygen effect under illumination overlapping the 660 nm laser used in this study. That is consistent with — but does not establish — the downstream pattern: the dual/combined-laser group (which includes 660 nm) achieves durable full suppression, while 808 nm alone (a wavelength not tested in the DPBF assay) shows late regrowth in most animals.

What is left open: the DPBF measurement is an in vitro, single-condition dish assay, not a measurement inside tumour tissue; no singlet-oxygen or ROS data exist for 808 nm or for the combined-laser condition specifically; and the two treatment groups differ in total light dose and wavelength combination, not just presence/absence of a 660 nm-associated photodynamic effect, so the regrowth difference cannot be attributed to singlet-oxygen generation versus other factors (heating, dose) from this evidence alone.

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
The two panels are temporally consistent but do not jointly establish a mechanistic link.

F9 (XRD) shows that after 5 cycles the coating gives strong t-ZrO2 peaks (a marked peak near 43° and a split doublet near 73–74°, matching the t-ZrO2 reference sticks), while after 10 cycles those same peaks are markedly reduced in the inset boxes. F8 (photographs) shows the coupon fully intact at 5 cycles, with corner spallation first appearing by 10 cycles and then persisting/slightly enlarging through 40 cycles.

Together, this supports only a temporal correlation: the interval (5→10 cycles) over which the t-ZrO2 signature weakens is the same interval over which corner spallation first appears, consistent with a link between tetragonal-phase degradation and onset of local coating loss/service-capability decline.

What it leaves open: the XRD trace is not shown to be taken from the spalled corner specifically, so there is no spatial co-registration proving the phase change occurred at the failure site rather than elsewhere on the coupon. No monoclinic-ZrO2 reference is shown, so whether the weakened t-ZrO2 signal reflects t→m transformation, loss of coating material, or beam/sampling variation is undetermined. Causation (phase change driving spallation, vs. both being consequences of a third process) is not established, only coincidence in cycle number.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Panels checked: F9 (XRD after 5 and 10 cycles, insets showing t-ZrO2 marker peaks near 43° and ~73-74°, more intense in the 5-cycle trace than the 10-cycle trace) and F8 (photos at 0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40 cycles: coupon intact through 5 cycles, corner spallation appearing by 10 cycles and persisting/growing through 40 cycles).

The combination supports only a weak temporal association: the t-ZrO2 markers are already present by 5 cycles (before visible spallation) and still present at 10 cycles, the same cycle count where corner spallation first appears — so phase change and onset of coating loss co-occur in the cycle-count window covered by both datasets. It does not establish that the phase shift causes the spallation. No mechanistic or quantitative link is given: XRD offers no fraction/amount of tetragonal ZrO2 (per the standing qualification), so there is no dose-response tie to spall extent. Critically, the XRD series stops at 10 cycles while the spallation photos continue growing through 40 cycles — the phase state during that continued degradation (20-40 cycles) is simply not measured, so whether phase transformation continues, saturates, or reverses alongside the observed spall growth is unknown. Other plausible drivers of spallation (thermal-mismatch stress, sintering, bond-coat oxidation) are also unaddressed here. Conclusion: co-occurrence at early cycles only, not a causal or quantitative account of the spallation trajectory.

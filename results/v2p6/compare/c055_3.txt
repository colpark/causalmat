The upstream SAXS evidence supports only a narrow claim: at the mesoscale probed by this q-range, the material's scattering/structural signature stays largely invariant through the first cycle, with a minor, discharge-state-specific perturbation near full discharge. It does not account for the downstream capacity fade. The SAXS data cover only cycle 1, while the capacity loss unfolds and compounds over 40 cycles; no structural probe is shown for later cycles. So the identification of a fade mechanism (SEI growth, irreversible Na trapping, side reactions, atomic-scale/phase changes outside the SAXS length scale) is left entirely open — the mesoscale stability observed cannot be extrapolated to explain, rule in, or rule out the cause of the severe long-term capacity/service-capability decline.

THE QUESTION
Does the upstream evidence account for the downstream observation about PRP/value / PRF/qualification? Say what the combination supports and what it leaves open.

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Panels F4a–e show dielectric constant (and loss) vs. temperature at four frequencies for x=0, 0.02, 0.04, 0.06, 0.08.

Claim A is well supported: at 1 kHz (black curve) the single peak drops from a value consistent with ~6750 at x=0 (a) to ~2700 at x=0.08 (e). A distinct second feature, explicitly labeled Tm2, appears in every doped composition (b–e, x≥0.02) but is absent/unlabeled at x=0 (a, single broad peak only). Tm2 shifts rightward across the series, from roughly 200 °C (b) toward ~250–260 °C (e), matching the stated ~200→260 °C range. One caveat: in panel b the Tm2 region reads more as a shoulder/plateau than a sharp maximum, becoming a clearly resolved peak only from c onward — so "second maximum" is a slightly generous description at x=0.02 specifically.

Claim B cannot be evaluated from these panels at all: none of F4a–e show a TCC (temperature coefficient of capacitance) curve or ±15% dashed reference lines — they are dielectric constant/loss plots, a different figure. So the upstream evidence here accounts fully for the Tm1/Tm2 (PRP-value type) observation but leaves the TCC/±15% qualification claim entirely unaddressed/open — it would need to be checked against a separate TCC figure not included in this set.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Panels confirm the numbers: Tm1 falls from ~6750 (x=0, panel a) to ~2700 (x=0.08, panel e); Tm2 first appears at x=0.02 (~200 C, panel b) and shifts to ~230-260 C by x=0.08 (panel e), all as a broad, single hump per composition with no split or kink.

The upstream result (smooth, discontinuity-free Raman softening at x=0.06 plus the composition-shift of Tm2) is consistent with, but does not by itself predict, the downstream TCC behavior. The combination supports one thing: at x=0.06, where both a continuous phonon evolution and a diffuse Tm2 were established, the TCC curve staying within +/-15% across ~40-340 C (spanning both Tm1 and Tm2) fits a picture in which a smooth, non-abrupt dielectric anomaly produces no sharp property discontinuity - the "no structural transition" reading and the "temperature-stable capacitance" reading are mutually consistent for that one composition.

What it leaves open: the x=0.08 TCC overshoot near 230-290 C, coincident with its Tm2, is not accounted for by any upstream evidence, since Raman was only measured at x=0.06. Whether x=0.08's overshoot reflects a larger-magnitude diffuse anomaly (still no discrete transition) or a genuinely different, more abrupt mechanism at higher x cannot be determined from what's shown - the qualification about compositional generality applies directly here.

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
All four panels viewed directly.

F5d/F5e (ELISA bars): total fibrinogen and gamma-chain both drop roughly by half on BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF and BVLD/NO-PPAmF relative to 316LSS/PPAmF (single *** bracket separates the group of three from the group of two), matching claim A. F6b/F6c (macro photos): 316LSS and PPAmF lumens/foils are dark and fully thrombus-filled; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are partly open with residual clot; BVLD/NO-PPAmF is the cleanest — lumen largely clear, foil largely unstained. This matches claim B, and here the combination coating does look best.

Combined support: the ELISA data justify the conclusion that functionalization (any of the three) suppresses fibrinogen adsorption/activation to roughly the same reduced level, and this is consistent in direction with the macro observation that functionalized coatings resist thrombus better than bare 316LSS/PPAmF. What it leaves open: fibrinogen level alone does not explain why BVLD/NO-PPAmF is macroscopically the cleanest, since it is not the lowest (or even tied-lowest) on fibrinogen/gamma-chain. Some additional mechanism (e.g., synergy between BVLD's anticoagulant action and NO release) beyond fibrinogen adsorption/activation must account for the combination's superior macro patency — this is not demonstrated by the ELISA data shown.

All four panels examined directly.

F5d/F5e (bar charts) confirm the ELISA text exactly: 316LSS and PPAmF sit near 0.10 (fibrinogen) / 0.13-0.15 (gamma-chain) with a *** bracket to the three functionalised bars; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are the two lowest (~0.05), while BVLD/NO-PPAmF sits slightly higher (~0.06) with a visibly larger error bar - so BVLD/NO is indeed not the lowest, on both fibrinogen and its gamma-chain. It does not resolve the fine-grained mismatch already flagged: BVLD/NO-PPAmF looks macroscopically the most patent (best) in the photos yet is not the lowest by ELISA, so the visual thrombus ranking and the biochemical ranking diverge for that one coating. The evidence also still cannot separate a BVLD-specific mechanism from a general PPAmF/NO-associated effect, since NO-PPAmF alone reproduces the same reduction.

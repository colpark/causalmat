THE QUESTION

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
The upstream DPBF assay (F3b) shows bismuthene depletes the probe's absorbance to ~0.73 at 30 min versus ~0.93 for water alone, a standard readout of reactive-oxygen-species (singlet oxygen) generation — establishing that bismuthene has photodynamic activity under whatever irradiation was used for that assay (not specified in the given caption, so it is unclear whether this was 660 nm, 808 nm, or another source). This upstream result supports the general premise that BNs-BSA can act as a photodynamic/photosensitizing agent, which is consistent with the qualitative pattern in F5d: the dual-laser (BNs-BSA+Lasers) group is fully suppressed (0/5 regrowth) while single-wavelength 808 nm monotherapy leaves four of five tumors regrowing after day 14.

What it does not establish: the DPBF data give no wavelength-resolved comparison (no 808 nm-specific ROS curve), so they cannot account for why 808 nm alone underperforms relative to the combination — that gap is more plausibly photothermal-vs-photodynamic mechanism, dosimetry, or synergy, none of which F3b addresses. The individual growth curves also show mouse-to-mouse variability (timing, magnitude of regrowth) that the bulk absorbance assay has no bearing on. So the combination supports "bismuthene generates ROS" as a plausible contributor to efficacy, but leaves the 808 nm-specific shortfall and the mechanism of the observed partial failure/regrowth timing open.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Panels reviewed: F3b (DPBF relative absorbance at 421 nm, bismuthene falling to ~0.73 at 30 min vs H2O ~0.93) and F5d (individual tumor curves for six groups; BNs-BSA+808nm shows 4/5 mice regrowing after ~day 14–20, while BNs-BSA+Lasers, the combined 660/808 nm group, keeps all 5 flat near zero).

The upstream evidence (0.69 eV band gap matched to 660 nm photon energy, plus the DPBF decay difference) supports only that bismuthene can generate a light-associated ROS/singlet-oxygen-consistent signal under 660 nm illumination — i.e., a photodynamic (PDT) capability tied specifically to that wavelength. It says nothing about 808 nm excitation, which in this system is used for photothermal (PTT) heating, not tested in the DPBF or Tauc assays. So the upstream data are consistent with the full tumor suppression in the combined 660/808 nm laser group (PDT+PTT synergy) but do not account for, or even engage, the partial efficacy (4/5 regrowth) seen with 808 nm alone — that outcome depends on photothermal mechanism and dose, which the band-gap/DPBF measurements never addressed.

What remains open: whether singlet oxygen was actually generated in vivo, any dose-response or timing link between the ~0.73 absorbance value and tumor control, why PTT alone underperforms, and whether the PDT and PTT effects are additive, synergistic, or independent in producing the complete-suppression result.

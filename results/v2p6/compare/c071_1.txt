THE QUESTION
how the material performs in service

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
What the two figures show, read directly from the panels:

Figure 5 (static ex vivo assay, PRP, 2 h): SEM (A) shows far fewer, less spread platelets on BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and especially BVLD/NO-PPAmF than on bare 316LSS or plain PPAmF. Bar charts confirm this quantitatively: platelet number (B, ~32-41×10²/mm² on 316LSS/PPAmF vs ~5-15×10²/mm² on the modified surfaces), platelet activation (C, ~65-74% vs ~0-23%), total fibrinogen (D) and fibrinogen γ-chain (E) adsorption are all markedly lower on BVLD/NO-modified surfaces, with *** significance bars against the controls.

Figure 6 (in vivo AV-shunt, carotid-jugular circulation, 2 h): lumen cross-sections (B) and longitudinal cuts (C) show heavy dark thrombus filling 316LSS and PPAmF but largely clear/patent lumens for BVLD-PPAmF and BVLD/NO-PPAmF. SEM of the surface (D) shows dense cell/fibrin coverage on controls versus sparse coverage on modified samples. Occlusion (E) and thrombus weight (F) drop from ~58-74% and ~64-74 mg (controls) to single digits on modified surfaces; blood flow rate (G) is preserved at ~90% of initial for BVLD/NO-PPAmF versus ~18-20% for controls, all differences marked ***.

Conclusion the evidence supports: the BVLD and NO surface modifications, especially combined, substantially reduce platelet adhesion/activation, fibrinogen fouling, and thrombus formation, and preserve blood flow better than bare 316LSS or plain PPAmF, in this short-term (2 h) ex vivo and acute in vivo shunt test.

What it leaves uncertain: only a single short time point (2 h) is shown, so durability, long-term patency, degradation, or chronic in vivo performance are not addressed. Animal numbers/replicates, statistical test details, mechanism of the effect, and other in-service properties (mechanical wear, corrosion, endothelialization, infection risk) are not shown in these panels. Captions for F5b/F5c are truncated in the prompt but match the plotted platelet-count and activation-rate data.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
The quantified platelet data (F5b, F5c) and the ex vivo blood-circulation photographs (F6b, F6c) extend the earlier static-assay findings toward a flow-based "in service" test. F5b/F5c show statistically significant (***) separation: 316LSS and PPAmF carry ~30-40×10² platelets/mm² at ~65-75% activation, while BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF form a distinct lower group (~5-15×10² platelets/mm², ~0-25% activation), with BVLD/NO-PPAmF lowest of all. F6b/F6c, taken from an arteriovenous-shunt circulation model, show grossly clot-covered, dark-red surfaces on 316LSS/PPAmF versus visibly cleaner, metallic-appearing surfaces on the BVLD- and NO-bearing coatings after 2h of actual blood flow. Together this justifies the conclusion that BVLD- and NO-modified surfaces resist platelet accumulation/activation and gross thrombus deposition both in static platelet assays and under flowing blood, i.e., the anti-thrombotic benefit is not an artifact of static incubation alone.

What remains uncertain: F6b/c are qualitative photographs, not matched quantitative thrombus measures, so no magnitude comparison to the F5 platelet counts can be drawn. The shunt model is an animal ex vivo circuit, not a clinical or long-term implant setting, so "in service" here means short-duration flow exposure only, not durability, fatigue, or endothelialization over time.

THE QUESTION
Compare the stress at which this evidence shows deformation along [0001] with the stress step 2 reported for that same orientation. Report both and say what the comparison implies about the deformation route.

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Figure 10a shows that along [0001] at RT this specimen deforms plastically (up to ~45% plastic strain) at a flow stress of only about 55-80 MPa, with serrations and a plateau near 70-80 MPa; Figure 10b confirms this strain is accommodated by dense kink-band formation rather than conventional slip. Figure 2, by contrast, reports the stress reached along [0001] at RT as ~350-360 MPa, plotted in Fig. 2a as a filled square explicitly labeled "fracture" (not yield) and shown in Fig. 2b as a vertical line to an X at ~355 MPa with essentially no plastic strain — i.e., near-elastic loading to brittle failure.

The comparison (~60-80 MPa deforming stress in Fig. 10 vs. ~350 MPa fracture stress in Fig. 2, same [0001] orientation, same temperature) shows roughly a 4-5x gap. This justifies the conclusion that [0001]-oriented loading in this alloy can follow two distinct routes: one in which the crystal remains essentially elastic up to a high stress and fails abruptly by fracture, and another in which kink-band formation nucleates at much lower stress and permits large plastic strain instead of immediate fracture. What the evidence leaves uncertain: why the two specimens diverge (e.g., differences in alignment, surface condition, or kink-band nucleation site) is not shown, nor is which route is more representative or what stress actually triggers kink-band onset.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Fig. 10a (RT tensile test along [0001]) shows continuous plastic flow at only ~60–80 MPa, sustained to ~45% plastic strain, with stress drops/serrations around 15–20% strain that coincide with the onset of deformation kink bands (confirmed in Fig. 10b, kink bands forming along the tensile direction).

Step 2 (Fig. 2a–b), for the same [0001] orientation, reports a fracture stress near ~350 MPa at RT, marked with an X and no accompanying plastic stress-strain curve (unlike the red/blue curves for [11-20] and [01-10], which do show yielding and plastic flow). So Step 2's [0001] data indicate essentially elastic loading to fracture with no measured yield.

Comparison: the kink-banded specimen in Fig. 10 deforms plastically at roughly 4–6x lower stress than the [0001] fracture stress reported in Step 2, and does so with large sustained plastic strain rather than brittle failure. This implies that [0001] loading can follow two distinct routes: an elastic-to-brittle-fracture path near 350 MPa (Step 2), or, when kink bands nucleate, a much lower-stress plastic path (~60–80 MPa) that accommodates large strain through kinking rather than fracture. The evidence supports that kinking is an alternate, low-stress deformation route along [0001]; it does not establish why the two tests diverge (e.g., specimen geometry, alignment, or local stress state differences between the two experiments are not specified in these panels).

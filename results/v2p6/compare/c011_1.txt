ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Both panels were opened and checked against the stated measurements.

F5c (power factor vs T): confirms A exactly — x=0.01 (red) is highest across the whole range, ~29 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² at 300 K falling to ~22 at 623 K; x=0.005 (black) is the lowest of the doped/substituted set; x=0 (green) is far below all of these, topping out near 5.

What the combination supports: the compositional ranking is consistent between the two properties — x=0.01 has both the highest power factor at every measured temperature and the highest peak ZT, so the upstream PF advantage is compatible with, and helps account for, x=0.01's superior ZT. What it leaves open: PF alone cannot explain the ZT curve's shape — PF for x=0.01 declines monotonically above ~350 K, yet ZT keeps rising until ~500–525 K before falling. That rise-then-fall in ZT implies a temperature-dependent thermal conductivity contribution (ZT = S²σT/κ) that is not shown in either panel, so the specific temperature of the ZT peak, and the magnitude of the ZT advantage, are not accounted for by the PF data alone.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Confirmed panels: F5c shows PF(T) exactly as claimed—x=0.01 highest (~29 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² at 300 K, peaking slightly near 320–370 K before falling to ~22 at 623 K), x=0.005 lowest among doped curves, x=0 far lowest (~2–5) and rising with T. The upstream resistivity/PF pairing accounts for the composition ranking: x=0.01 has both the highest PF and the highest ZT, x=0 has both the lowest PF and lowest ZT, consistent with PF (and hence low resistivity) contributing to a higher figure of merit. That much is supported.

It does not account for the temperature location of the ZT maximum. PF for x=0.01 already turns over and falls monotonically after ~350 K, yet ZT keeps rising until ~500–525 K before falling. Since ZT = PF·T/κ, this divergence means thermal conductivity's temperature dependence must also be shaping where ZT peaks—something the resistivity/PF evidence, which contains no κ data, cannot speak to. So the combination supports PF (via low resistivity) as a contributor to the composition ranking of ZT, but leaves the ZT peak's temperature position, and any causal role of thermal conductivity, unestablished.

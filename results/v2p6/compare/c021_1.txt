ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Partial support, with a clear gap at the level of detail needed.

F5d/F5e (ELISA) show total fibrinogen and its gamma chain roughly halved on all three functionalized coatings (BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, BVLD/NO-PPAmF, ~0.05–0.065) versus bare 316LSS/PPAmF (~0.10–0.15), with a single *** bracket spanning controls vs. the functionalized group — the three functionalized surfaces are not shown as significantly different from one another. F6b/F6c show a corresponding qualitative pattern at the coarse level: 316LSS and PPAmF tubes are fully occluded by dark thrombus (high fibrinogen, worst clotting), matching the ELISA. So the upstream reduction in fibrinogen adsorption/activation does account for the broad control-vs-functionalized contrast in thrombus burden.

What it leaves open: within the functionalized group, the photographs show a gradient — BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are only partly open/partly clotted, while BVLD/NO-PPAmF is nearly clean (lumen and foil largely free of clot). The fibrinogen ELISA does not resolve this gradient, since BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF are statistically indistinguishable in fibrinogen level. So the fibrinogen-adsorption evidence explains why functionalized surfaces beat bare metal, but not why the dual (BVLD/NO) surface outperforms either single-agent surface macroscopically — that synergy is not accounted for by this measurement.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Panels viewed: F5d and F5e are the total-fibrinogen and gamma-chain bar charts (matching measurement A); F6b (tube cross-sections) and F6c (foil photos) are the thrombus images (matching measurement B).

The combination supports only the coarse split: on both readouts, 316L SS and plain PPAmF are the worst (highest fibrinogen/gamma-chain values in F5d/e; dark, lumen-filling thrombus in F6b/c), while all three functionalised coatings sit lower/cleaner. That much the ELISA data can account for.

It does not account for the finer pattern the photographs suggest. F6b/F6c show BVLD/NO-PPAmF with the most open lumen and cleanest foil, implying it performs best of the five conditions. But F5d/F5e show BVLD/NO-PPAmF is not the lowest in fibrinogen or gamma-chain - it sits slightly above BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF, all three being roughly equal within the halved range. So the visual "cleanest" impression from the photos is not corroborated, and may even be mildly contradicted, by the quantitative ELISA ordering.

This leaves open exactly what the earlier SEM/ELISA comparison already flagged: (1) fine ranking among the three coatings is unresolved across readouts; (2) since NO-PPAmF alone matches BVLD-PPAmF's reduction, neither readout isolates a BVLD-specific thrombin-inactivation effect from a general PPAmF-functionalisation or NO-associated effect; and (3) the photographs remain a qualitative visual read, not a quantified measure, so they cannot confirm or overturn the ELISA magnitudes.

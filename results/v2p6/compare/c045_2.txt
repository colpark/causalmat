ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Yes, the upstream evidence plausibly accounts for the downstream CE result, but only qualitatively.

F3d/F3e show a clear microstructural contrast: CnC HS (d) has a largely flat, low-relief surface with only a few short protrusions, while C HS (e) is covered edge-to-edge by a dense felt of sharp, interlocking platelets — exactly matching the SEM description in A. F4a shows CnC HS (red) holding ~80–100% CE out to ~200+ cycles, while C HS (black) tracks similarly at first but drops to 0% CE near cycle 40 and again near 48, with no data beyond ~50 cycles — matching B.

Combined, this supports the standard mechanistic story: the flatter CnC HS deposit gives more uniform, lower-surface-area Zn plating with fewer sharp asperities, consistent with more stable, higher CE cycling, whereas the dense sharp-platelet morphology on C HS is consistent with conditions favoring dendrite/short-circuit-type failure and abrupt CE collapse. This is a correlational pairing of morphology and one CE trace at one condition (1 mAh cm⁻², 4 mA cm⁻²) — it does not by itself establish that morphology causes the CE drop, does not identify the failure mechanism at cycles 40/48 (short circuit vs. dead Zn vs. electrolyte depletion), and covers only a single cycling condition and a single pair of representative SEM images, not statistics across replicates.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
The three panels confirm the reported observations as printed. F3d (CnC HS) shows a largely flat, low-relief surface with only a few sparse, sharp protrusions; F3e (C HS) shows a dense, near-uniform mat of interlocking sharp platelets covering the whole field — matching observation A. Together, this justifies the conclusion that the flat, sparsely-platelet morphology on CnC HS co-occurs with markedly more stable, longer-lived Coulombic efficiency (higher service capability), while the dense, sharp-platelet mat on C HS co-occurs with abrupt CE collapse and early cell failure — a pattern consistent with the plating morphology governing electrochemical durability (e.g., via short-circuiting or dead-zinc formation from dendritic/sharp platelets).

What it leaves open: the SEM images are single, likely early-cycle snapshots, not taken at the moment of CE failure, so there is no direct visual link between the imaged morphology and the cycle-40–48 collapse itself. As already noted, CnC HS and C HS differ structurally beyond nitrogen/Zn-N bonding, so the morphology-CE co-occurrence cannot rule out other confounding host-structure differences as the true cause of both the platelet shape and the CE failure. Only one specimen per condition is shown, so this is a qualitative co-occurrence, not a controlled or quantitative causal test.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Combination supports: The upstream, already-settled result establishes that FTIR and TGA jointly demonstrate a real, chemically distinct, thermally quantifiable species (CH2/Si-O-bearing) is present on BST-SH but not on plain BST. F2b's photograph adds an independent, orthogonal line of evidence at the macroscopic/functional level: BST-OH settles into the lower aqueous layer (hydrophilic behavior, consistent with unreacted surface -OH groups) while BST-SH partitions into the upper toluene (organic) layer (hydrophobic/organophilic behavior). This dispersion/settling contrast is exactly what would be expected if a hydrophobic organosilane (MPTMS-type, carrying the CH2 groups FTIR detects) has been introduced onto the BST-SH surface, replacing or masking hydrophilic -OH sites. So the three measurements together are mutually reinforcing: FTIR shows the new species' chemical signature, TGA shows it is a real quantifiable mass addition, and the phase-partitioning photograph shows a macroscopic property change (surface polarity/wettability) that is functionally consistent with that species being organosilane and located at the particle surface.

What it leaves open: this still does not prove covalent Si-O-BST grafting versus strong physisorbed/adsorbed MPTMS — a non-covalently but densely adsorbed hydrophobic layer would produce the same solvent-partitioning behavior. The photograph gives no quantitative measure of surface coverage, hydrophobicity, or how much silane is present, and it is not calibrated against the TGA mass-loss (~1%) or FTIR band intensities. It also cannot rule out other explanations for altered particle-water/toluene affinity (e.g., aggregation state, particle size differences) independent of surface chemistry. So the combination strengthens the case that a new, surface-located, hydrophobic organosilane species is present and functionally alters particle behavior, but bonding mechanism, coverage/density, and a quantitative structure-property (chemistry-to-dispersion) link remain unestablished; direct bonding-verification (e.g., XPS, solid-state NMR, or extraction/control tests) would be needed to close that gap.

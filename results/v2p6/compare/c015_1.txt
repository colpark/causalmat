ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Both panels are consistent with the stated upstream evidence and, taken together, support the downstream claim of an irreversible structural change spanning phase identity and microstructure, but with real limits on precision.

F4a (in operando XRD stack, 16-21 deg 2theta, paired with 0.1C voltage curve to ~24 h): the ~16.4/19.2/20.1 deg reflections are sharp near the bottom (start), progressively broaden and weaken through the discharge traces, and remain a weak, broad, featureless hump through the charge traces at the top — no re-sharpening. This directly confirms observation A: loss of long-range crystalline order that does not recover on charge, i.e., an STR/phase/identity change.

F3a (2D SAXS contour, low-q panel ~0.01-0.08 A^-1, with matched voltage/time axis to ~18 h): the contour/feature position visibly shifts toward lower q during the discharge window and stays shifted through charge, matching observation B — a persistent change in a characteristic length scale, consistent with STR/microstructure/porosity change.

What this combination justifies: co-occurring, non-reversing signatures at atomic (XRD) and nanoscale (SAXS) length scales during the same first discharge, both surviving into charge.

What it leaves open: the two techniques probe different length scales independently; nothing in these panels demonstrates the microstructural/porosity change is causally downstream of the phase-identity loss (versus a parallel, separately driven process). The new amorphous phase's identity, and whether the SAXS shift specifically reflects porosity versus particle/domain coarsening, are not resolved by these panels alone.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Both panels match their captions and the described traces: F4a (SXRD) shows the ~16.4/19.2/20.1° reflections weakening to a broad hump through discharge, staying broad and not sharpening back on charge; F3a (SAXS) shows the low-q contour bending toward lower q during roughly the first 6–11 h of discharge and remaining shifted through charge, never returning to its starting q.

The combination is consistent — both signals move together and stay irreversible — but they probe different length scales, so together they only support a general claim: the same discharge event that destroys the oxide's long-range order and converts Co to a metallic-like local state (established upstream via XRD+XANES) is accompanied by a concurrent, likewise-irreversible change in mesoscale structure (a shift in the characteristic length/size scale seen by SAXS, roughly nm-to-tens-of-nm). The atomic-scale phase/oxidation-state evidence does not explain the SAXS shift's physical origin — it cannot say whether the SAXS bend reflects particle coarsening, pore/void formation, densification, or agglomeration of the new Co-rich phase, nor its magnitude. It also does not establish that the amorphous/metallic domains and the SAXS-visible microstructural feature are spatially the same entity — that inference is plausible but not demonstrated by combining these two data types, which lack a common quantitative or spatial link.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
The combination is suggestive but not demonstrative. F4c (EDS, C10) shows C (blue) and Co (red) concentrated on particle surfaces/edges, consistent with a surface catalyst/coating layer, as already corroborated qualitatively by HRTEM. F6a shows C0's first-cycle hydrogenation is slow (~3.2 wt% at 180 min, needing a clear activation/incubation period before matching cycles 2-3), while F6b shows C10's first cycle already saturates within ~11 min (visually near-identical to cycles 2-3, matching the stated ~5 min figure). Together these support a plausible story: a surface-located C+Co layer on C10 particles removes or shortens the activation barrier that C0 (lacking that addition) shows only on its first hydrogenation.

What this leaves open: C0 and C10 are different compositions (not the same particles with/without the coating removed), so the panels show correlation between surface catalyst presence and fast first-cycle kinetics, not a controlled before/after test isolating the coating as cause. The EDS map's coarse resolution and the HRTEM's single local edge region (per the earlier qualifications) cannot establish that the coating is uniform/complete enough over all C10 particle surfaces to mechanistically explain eliminating the incubation period sample-wide, nor is any direct catalytic or diffusion mechanism shown linking surface C/Co location to the rate-limiting step. So the STR/kinetics improvement is consistent with, but not proven by, the surface-distribution evidence.

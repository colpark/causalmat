THE QUESTION
how the material performs in service

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Across the four panels the material is a cobalt-doped mesoporous bioactive glass (Co-MBG) scaffold, and the measurements characterize its behavior once immersed/implanted, not a mechanical "performance" per se.

F5a-d (ion release in SBF over 7 days): Co2+ release rises with dopant level (0 < 2Co < 5Co-MBG, reaching ~17-21 mg/L by day 7), confirming sustained Co2+ leaching that scales with loading. SiO4^4- and Ca2+ also increase over time (dissolution/apatite exchange), while PO4^3- decreases, consistent with glass dissolution and surface mineralization typical of bioactive glasses. This supports that the scaffold degrades and releases Co2+ in a dose-dependent, time-sustained manner.

F9 (western blot): HIF-1α band intensity increases from MBG to 2Co-MBG to 5Co-MBG, indicating Co2+ dose-dependently upregulates HIF-1α protein in BMSCs — a hypoxia-mimicking signaling effect, not a bulk material property.

F11a/b: panel a shows cumulative ampicillin release plateauing near 700-800 µg by ~24-72 h; panel b shows bacterial survival significantly reduced (vs. blank/plain scaffold) only in the ampicillin-loaded group at days 3 and 7, demonstrating antibacterial efficacy tied to sustained drug release.

Together: the material releases Co2+ and antibiotic in a controlled, sustained, dose-dependent way, drives HIF-1α signaling, and confers antibacterial activity when drug-loaded. Uncertain/not shown: mechanical strength, degradation rate in vivo, long-term durability, or whether HIF-1α upregulation translates to functional angiogenic/osteogenic outcomes — none of that is in these panels.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Evidence across the panels supports a modest, qualified performance picture for Co-MBG scaffolds in service.

Ion release (F5a/F9's companion release curves) shows dose-graded, sustained Co2+ release over 7 days (5Co-MBG > 2Co-MBG > 0Co-MBG, essentially zero from 0Co-MBG), alongside release of SiO4^4-, Ca2+, and PO4^3- consistent with bioactive glass dissolution/apatite exchange — the material is actively degrading and delivering ions rather than being inert. The HIF-1alpha blot (F9) shows only that the highest-Co-release group (5Co-MBG) has the most intense HIF-1alpha band, a two-way association (highest vs. the other two, which are merely "faint" and indistinguishable from each other) rather than a monotonic dose-response, and it is a qualitative band read at an unspecified timepoint. Antibacterial/drug-loaded scaffolds (F11a/b) show ampicillin-loaded scaffolds progressively reducing relative bacterial survival to ~81% and ~52% by days 3 and 7 versus blank/drug-free controls, indicating a real but antibiotic-driven cytotoxic/antibacterial effect — separate from and not evidence for the Co2+/HIF-1alpha hypoxia-mimicking mechanism.

What remains uncertain: whether Co2+ release itself (versus other compositional differences among the three scaffold formulations) drives the HIF-1alpha pattern, whether that pattern is truly dose-graded across all three groups, and whether any of this translates into a functional in vivo angiogenic or bone-repair outcome — no such endpoint is shown in these panels.

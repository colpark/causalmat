ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
What the panels show:

- F5a (soaking-release panel a): Co2+ released into solution increases with cobalt content of the scaffold — 0Co-MBG releases none, 2Co-MBG rises to ~17 mg/L by day 7, 5Co-MBG rises higher/faster to ~21 mg/L. This is a genuine dose-response in cobalt content vs. released Co2+.
- F9 (western blot): HIF-1α band intensity increases across MBG < 2Co-MBG < 5Co-MBG, i.e., qualitatively tracks cobalt content, consistent with a dose-dependent induction of HIF-1α by cobalt loading. It is a single blot with no lane quantification or replicate shown, so it is only a qualitative trend.

However, the F11a/F11b images provided are NOT a VEGF panel — they show (a) an unrelated antibacterial figure and (b) relative bacterial/cell survival percentages for blank control vs. scaffolds vs. scaffolds+ampicillin, tied to ampicillin loading, not to cobalt dose or VEGF expression. No VEGF panel was actually supplied among these four image paths (F11a and F11b point to the same image, which is the antibacterial-effect/ampicillin-release figure).

Conclusion: The evidence here supports a cobalt-content dose-response only for Co2+ release (F5a) and, qualitatively, for HIF-1α induction (F9) — both increase with cobalt loading (0<2<5Co-MBG), consistent with cobalt driving hypoxia-mimetic signaling. But no VEGF data are present in the images given, so the claim that "HIF-1α and VEGF describe one dose response" cannot be evaluated — VEGF's relationship to cobalt content is undetermined from this evidence. The HIF-1α blot alone is also unquantified/unreplicated, so even that dose-response is suggestive, not statistically established.

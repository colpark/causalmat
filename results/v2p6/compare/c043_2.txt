ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
The two panels only share two samples in common: TiO2 and the Er0.5Ce0.2 co-doped sample (F7 also includes Er0.5Ti-O, which never appears in F8; F8 also includes Er0.5Ce0.1 and Er0.5Ce0.3, neither of which appears in F7). For that shared pair, the direction is consistent with a recombination-suppression story: TiO2 has the strongest 390-420 nm PL emission and the lowest degradation (~20% at 60 min), while Er0.5Ce0.2 has the weakest PL emission and the highest degradation (~46% at 60 min). Since PL intensity in this band is commonly read as a proxy for electron-hole recombination, this pairing supports the qualitative claim that the Ce/Er co-doped sample recombines less and photodegrades more than bare TiO2.

What it leaves open: PL was never measured for the Ce0.1 and Ce0.3 samples that anchor the middle of the degradation trend (46% > 41% > 31% > 20%), so the non-monotonic-looking ordering by Ce content in F8 has no PL data to explain it — one cannot say whether recombination suppression scales smoothly with Ce loading or peaks at 0.2 and partially reverses at 0.3. The match is also only a two-point correlation, not a demonstrated causal mechanism, and Er0.5Ti-O's PL value is never checked against any degradation number at all.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
The two datasets overlap on only two samples: TiO2 and Er0.5Ce0.2. For those two, the direction agrees: TiO2 has the highest PL (most radiative recombination, F7) and the lowest degradation (~20% at 60 min, worst catalytic performance, F8); Er0.5Ce0.2 has the lowest PL (least recombination) and the highest degradation (~46% at 60 min, best performance). This is consistent with the expected mechanism that suppressed electron-hole recombination should support better photocatalytic activity, and it extends the earlier PL-EIS convergence by adding a third, functional (catalytic) measure that moves the same way for the same two samples.

What it leaves open: the PL series (TiO2, Er0.5Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.2) and the degradation series (TiO2, Er0.5Ce0.1, Er0.5Ce0.2, Er0.5Ce0.3) only share two members. Er0.5Ti-O's degradation was not measured, and Er0.5Ce0.1/Ce0.3's PL was not measured, so the relationship is untested across the intermediate Ce-doping levels. Notably degradation is non-monotonic with Ce content (0.2 beats 0.3), so even if lower recombination tracks higher activity at the endpoints, the evidence doesn't show whether PL would also be non-monotonic, or that recombination is the dominant driver rather than a correlate. As with the PL-EIS pairing, this is agreement between two proxies on two samples, not a demonstrated causal chain.

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Both panels confirm the reported statistics. F5c: PF for x=0.01 (red) is the highest curve across the entire 300–623 K range, from ~29 down to ~22 µW cm⁻¹K⁻²; x=0.005 (black squares) is the lowest of the four doped curves throughout, and x=0 is far below all doped samples (~2 to ~5). F6a: at 300 K, ZT for x=0.005 (~0.84) is actually higher than x=0.01 (~0.78), and both exceed x=0.02–0.04 (~0.63–0.69); the n-Bi2Te3 reference dashed curve sits near ~0.88 at 300 K, consistent with the stated values.

The combination partially accounts for the observation but leaves a gap: PF ranking (x=0.01 highest, x=0.005 lowest among doped) matches ZT ranking at higher temperatures, where x=0.01 stays on top of the ZT curves through the peak region. But at 300 K specifically, the ZT ranking inverts — x=0.005 outperforms x=0.01 in ZT despite having the lowest PF among doped compositions. Since ZT = PF·T/κ, this reversal implies a lower thermal conductivity for x=0.005 at 300 K, but neither panel shown reports thermal conductivity, so the PF data alone cannot explain why x=0.005 achieves the higher low-temperature ZT. That mechanism is left open by this evidence.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
The upstream resistivity–PF linkage explains the PF panel's own trend, but it does not carry over to explain the ZT ranking. Reading F5c and F6a together shows a clear mismatch: F5c ranks doped PF as x=0.01 (~29) > x=0.02 ≈ x=0.04 (~26–27) > x=0.005 (~23, lowest at 300 K), while F6a ranks 300 K ZT as x=0.005 (~0.84, highest) > x=0.01 (~0.78) > x=0.02/x=0.04 (0.63–0.69). So the composition with the lowest doped power factor (x=0.005) has the highest ZT, the opposite of what a PF-driven picture would predict. The one part that does stay consistent is x=0: lowest PF throughout and also lowest ZT (~0.03–0.22), so at the extremes low PF tracks low ZT.

What the combination supports: qualitative consistency only at the x=0 extreme, and general co-occurrence of "doped, high PF" with "doped, respectable ZT" as a broad contrast to undoped. What it leaves open: since ZT = PF·T/κ, and no thermal-conductivity values appear in either panel, the reversal among doped samples (x=0.005 lowest-PF-highest-ZT) cannot be explained from these two panels alone — thermal conductivity must be doing work that this PF–resistivity account does not capture.

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Yes, the two measurements are mutually consistent and support a phase-change interpretation, though only partially.

F3b (ICP) shows K and Mn falling from cycling while Zn rises from ~0 (pristine) to a nonzero value by the 4th cycle and then to ~50 at% by the 20th, staying near 47 at% through the 100th. F3a (XRD) shows that by the 4th cycle two new diffraction peaks (~16.5°, ~21.9°) have already appeared while the original KMnHCF peaks weaken, and these new peaks persist and strengthen at 20th/60th/100th as the original pattern fades further — matching the reference pattern position for KZnHCF (JCPDS 33-1061) plotted below.

Together this supports the claim that cycling in the Zn-containing electrolyte drives compositional exchange of lattice K (and Mn) for Zn, and that this exchange is accompanied by, and roughly coincides temporally with, a crystallographic transformation from the KMnHCF phase toward a Zn-substituted hexacyanoferrate phase (Zn-KMnHCF), rather than simple surface adsorption.

What it leaves open: neither panel alone proves the new XRD phase's exact structure/stoichiometry (no Rietveld fit or peak-indexing table is shown here), and the two datasets are correlational — they don't establish the mechanism (ion exchange vs. dissolution-reprecipitation) or rule out a mixed-phase intermediate.

Yes — this combination does more than the earlier pristine-phase/ICP pairing, and it directly closes part of what that earlier step left open. It leaves open: no quantitative phase-fraction or Rietveld analysis confirming the new phase is truly Zn-hexacyanoferrate rather than a visually similar pattern; peak weakening shows coexistence, not full conversion; and no site-resolved data proves Zn sits within this new phase's framework versus elsewhere. Mechanism (substitution vs. new-phase growth) remains unestablished.

What the combination supports: the fibrinogen/gamma-chain data justify the coarse conclusion that surface functionalization (versus bare 316LSS/PPAmF) lowers fibrinogen adsorption and activation, consistent with the qualitative reduction in thrombus occlusion seen in the photos — i.e., less fibrinogen deposition/activation tracks with less thrombus on functionalized surfaces as a group.

What it leaves open: the ELISA data do not explain the finer gradient the photos suggest among the three functionalized coatings — BVLD/NO-PPAmF is not the lowest or best-resolved on fibrinogen/gamma-chain, yet appears cleanest in the photos, so this specific downstream ranking (PRP/value and PRF/service_capability differentiation among BVLD, NO, and the combination) is not accounted for by the upstream fibrinogen assay alone. That finer distinction would require other upstream measures (e.g., platelet activation, clotting time) not included in these four panels, and the photographic comparison is qualitative, not quantified.

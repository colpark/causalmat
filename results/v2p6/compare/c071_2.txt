ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
On the actual question — how the material performs in service:

Both figures (F5, F6) compare five surfaces: bare 316LSS, plasma-polymerized amine coating (PPAmF), and PPAmF further modified with BVLD peptide, NO-releasing groups, or both (BVLD/NO-PPAmF). F5 shows short-term (2 h) platelet-rich-plasma exposure: SEM (F5a) shows visibly fewer, less-spread platelets on the modified surfaces than on 316LSS/PPAmF, and the bar charts (F5b platelet number, F5c activation %) confirm this quantitatively, with BVLD/NO-PPAmF lowest and differences marked significant (***).

F6 extends this to a 2 h ex vivo arteriovenous shunt (rabbit carotid-jugular loop, per panel A schematic). Cross-sections (F6b) and thrombus photos (F6c) show markedly less clot on the dual-functionalized surface than on bare/plain-coated ones, matching the quantitative panels (occlusion %, thrombus weight, blood flow rate, all *** or *): BVLD/NO-PPAmF preserves ~90% of blood flow versus ~20% for 316LSS/PPAmF.

Conclusion the evidence supports: over a 2-hour window, the modified surfaces — especially the combined BVLD/NO coating — substantially reduce platelet adhesion/activation and thrombus formation and better maintain blood flow than uncoated steel, in this ex vivo blood-contact test.

What it leaves uncertain: this is a single short (2 h) ex vivo exposure, not chronic in vivo implantation, so durability, coating stability, degradation, healing/endothelialization, immune response, and mechanical performance over service life are not addressed by these panels. Animal numbers/statistics details and long-term hemocompatibility remain unshown here.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
The quantitative panels extend and partly resolve the earlier qualitative picture. Figure 5B/C give matched numeric platelet counts and activation percentages that confirm the SEM trend: 316L SS and PPAmF carry ~32-41×10² platelets/mm² at ~65-74% activation, versus ~15% (BVLD-PPAmF), ~11% (NO-PPAmF), and ~5×10² at near-0% activation (BVLD/NO-PPAmF) — the combination surface is lowest overall. Figure 6's ex vivo arteriovenous-shunt circulation model then shows the functional payoff: after 2 h of real blood flow, bare/PPAmF surfaces are heavily occluded (58-63% occlusion, 64-74 mg thrombus, flow crashed to ~17-20% of baseline), while BVLD/NO-PPAmF sustains ~90% of baseline flow with ~3% occlusion and ~2 mg thrombus, significantly outperforming NO-PPAmF alone on occlusion and thrombus weight (**, *) and BVLD-PPAmF alone on flow rate.

This justifies concluding that BVLD-bearing coatings, especially combined with NO, translate into materially better hemocompatibility under real flowing blood, not just static assays — the anti-adhesive/anti-thrombotic effect is functionally consequential. What remains uncertain: because NO-PPAmF alone already outperforms bare surfaces functionally despite unchanged thrombin activity, the flow-model improvement cannot be attributed to thrombin suppression; platelet-count/activation suppression and/or NO's independent antiplatelet action are more plausible drivers. Which specific mechanism underlies the BVLD/NO synergy in flow performance is not resolved by these panels.

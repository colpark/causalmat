Two answers to the same question about one set of measurements. Both answerers saw the same evidence. The second was additionally handed an earlier established result; the first was not.

THE QUESTION
Does the upstream evidence account for the downstream observation about PRP/value / PRF/service_capability? Say what the combination supports and what it leaves open.

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Both panels confirm the top-line claim. F5c: x=0.01 (red) has the highest PF across the full 300-623K range, ~29 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² near 300K falling to ~22 at 623K; x=0 (green) is far lower, rising only to about 5; x=0.005 (black) is the lowest of the doped set through most of the range. F7c: at Th=623K, x=0.01 reaches ~11.8% efficiency, clearly above the other doped compositions, which cluster near 9.5-10.6%.

So the combination supports: x=0.01's PF advantage, sustained across the whole temperature range, is consistent with and helps account for its highest efficiency at 623K — a coherent story for the top performer.

What it leaves open: the PF ranking does not cleanly track the eta ranking among the other compositions. x=0.005 has the lowest PF of the doped set, yet in F7c its efficiency (~9.5-9.7%) is not the lowest — x=0.04 is comparably low or lower despite a higher PF at most temperatures. So PF alone does not explain relative efficiency ordering beyond the top composition; efficiency must depend on other quantities (e.g., thermal conductivity/ZT) not shown in either panel. Also, x=0 appears in F5c but not in F7c, so its efficiency cannot be checked against the PF claim at all.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Confirmed from the panels: F5c (power factor) shows x=0.01 (red circles) highest across the whole 300-623 K range, falling from ~29 to ~22 µW cm⁻¹K⁻²; x=0 (green) lowest throughout, ~2 to ~5; x=0.005 (black squares) is the lowest of the doped curves, tracking below x=0.02/x=0.04 through most of the range. F7c shows x=0.01 (red) as the highest efficiency curve at every Th shown, reaching ~11.8% at 623 K, while the other three doped compositions (x=0.005, 0.02, 0.04) cluster together at ~9.5-10.6% at 623 K, consistent with the stated values.

What the combination supports: the PF ranking and the efficiency ranking agree on the top performer — x=0.01 has both the highest power factor at every temperature and the highest conversion efficiency at every Th, so the downstream efficiency advantage for x=0.01 is consistent with (not contradicted by) its upstream PF advantage. Since eta is a device-level quantity that depends on PF (via ZT) among other factors, this is a supportive alignment between the two panels.

What it leaves open: efficiency depends on additional inputs not evidenced here — thermal conductivity, ZT, or a full temperature-integrated PF profile — so the panels alone cannot show that PF is what drives the efficiency gap, only that they are directionally consistent. The efficiency ranking among the three lower compositions (x=0.005, 0.02, 0.04) is not cleanly reproduced by the PF ranking (x=0.005 is lowest in PF for most of the range but not distinctly lowest in eta at 623 K), so the correspondence holds clearly for the top performer but not precisely for the ordering of the rest. This remains an association across two panels, not a demonstrated causal or mechanistic link, and the earlier caveats about power factor being a composite quantity (Seebeck not shown) still apply upstream of this comparison.

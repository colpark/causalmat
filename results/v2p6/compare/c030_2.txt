THE QUESTION
Compare the stress at which this evidence shows deformation along [0001] with the stress step 2 reported for that same orientation. Report both and say what the comparison implies about the deformation route.

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Fig. 10a (Mg99.2Zn0.2 single crystal, tension along [0001], RT) shows deformation proceeding at very low stress: the curve oscillates and plateaus in the ~55–80 MPa range, reaching ~45% plastic strain, with the deformed region (Fig. 10b) showing extensive kink-band formation. Fig. 2b, the RT stress–strain plot for [0001] loading ("step 2" comparison), shows a nearly vertical line rising to an X mark at ~350–355 MPa (matching the fracture point in Fig. 2a) with essentially no resolved plastic strain before fracture.

Comparison: the kink-band-mediated deformation in Fig. 10a occurs at a stress roughly 4–6x lower than the ~350 MPa fracture stress plotted for [0001] in Fig. 2b. This implies that loading along [0001] can follow two very different routes: one in which the crystal behaves near-elastically to a high stress and then fractures abruptly (Fig. 2b), and another in which extensive plastic strain accumulates via kinking at a much lower stress plateau (Fig. 10a). The evidence justifies that kinking is a viable, low-stress deformation mechanism along [0001] distinct from brittle fracture at high stress. It does not establish what determines which route a given specimen follows (e.g., buckling constraint, geometry, or loading alignment) — the captions give no basis for that.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Figure 10a: the [0001] tensile specimen deforms plastically via a serrated flow curve running roughly 55-80 MPa (initial rise to ~70 MPa, drop to ~55 MPa, recovery to a ~78-80 MPa plateau) out to ~45% plastic strain, and Fig. 10b confirms this deformation is carried by kink bands, imaged at the site circled in 10a.

Figure 2 (step 2) reports a very different number for the same [0001] orientation: in panel (a) the [0001] data point is plotted as "fracture" at roughly 350 MPa at RT, and panel (b)'s [0001] curve is simply a vertical line to an X at ~355 MPa with no plastic strain shown - i.e., the nominal RT behavior along [0001] is brittle fracture at ~350 MPa, not yielding.

Comparison: the kink-band deformation documented in Fig. 10 occurs at roughly 4-6x lower stress (~55-80 MPa) than the fracture stress (~350 MPa) recorded for [0001] in Fig. 2. This implies [0001] loading can follow two distinct routes - the typical brittle fracture at high stress reported in Fig. 2, versus, in the Fig. 10 specimen, extensive kink-band-mediated plasticity that initiates and proceeds at much lower stress. What remains uncertain: why this specimen took the low-stress kinking route instead of fracturing (geometry, surface condition, or other specimen-specific factors are not shown), and whether kinking is a precursor to or a substitute for the high-stress fracture event.

THE QUESTION

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Both panels confirm the stated values. F3b: bismuthene's DPBF absorbance falls to ~0.73 by 30 min vs ~0.93 for water, showing bismuthene generates reactive oxygen species (ROS) under laser exposure — a photodynamic (PDT) capability. F5d: in the BNs-BSA+808nm panel, tumors are near zero through ~day 12-14 then four of five curves rise sharply by day 20-22 (regrowth); the BNs-BSA+Lasers (dual 660/808nm) panel shows all five curves flat near zero throughout.

What the combination supports: the ROS-generation evidence establishes bismuthene has a genuine PDT mechanism, and it is consistent with — but does not itself prove — the interpretation that adding this PDT effect (via the second, 660 nm laser) is what converts the 808nm-alone group's partial, recurrence-prone response into complete, durable eradication in the dual-laser group. That is, the upstream ROS data supply a plausible mechanistic ingredient for the downstream efficacy/durability gap.

What it leaves open: the DPBF assay is a cell-free dispersion test, not measured in tumor tissue or under the actual in vivo irradiation protocol; its caption does not state which laser/wavelength (660 or 808 nm) was used, so it cannot be tied specifically to one arm of the tumor experiment. No dose, ROS-in-tumor, or statistical comparison links the two datasets directly — the causal chain from measured ROS to reduced recurrence is inferred, not demonstrated, and n=5 per group limits confidence in the regrowth rate itself.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Yes, but only as a coarse correlational link, not a mechanistic or quantitative one.

The upstream pairing (Tauc gap ~0.69 eV plus the DPBF decay to ~0.73 vs ~0.93 in water) already establishes that bismuthene is electronically capable of absorbing 660 nm light and shows a light-associated, bismuthene-specific loss of DPBF signal consistent with singlet-oxygen generation. The downstream tumor-growth data (F5d) add a treatment-outcome layer: BNs-BSA+660/808nm ("Lasers," panel 6) tumors stay flat at 0 in all five mice, matching the photo-activity implied upstream, while BNs-BSA+808nm alone (panel 5) shows 4/5 tumors regrowing after ~day 14, and control arms (PBS, BNs-BSA alone, lasers alone, BNs-BSA+660nm alone) all show unchecked growth (F3b confirms only the absorbance/wavelength evidence, no new information for this pairing).

Combined, this supports the idea that dual-wavelength (660+808 nm) irradiation of bismuthene is required for durable tumor suppression, consistent with a photo-driven mechanism engaging both a singlet-oxygen-generating and a photothermal pathway. It does not establish that the ~0.69 eV band gap or DPBF-measured singlet-oxygen activity specifically drives the 808 nm arm's partial failure or the dual-laser arm's success — no in vivo ROS or temperature measurement links the two mechanisms to the tumor outcome; the connection remains inferential.

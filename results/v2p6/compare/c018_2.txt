Two answers to the same question about one set of measurements. Both answerers saw the same evidence. The second was additionally handed an earlier established result; the first was not.

THE QUESTION
how the material performs in service

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Figures 5 and 6 both compare bare 316LSS, PPAmF, BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and the combined BVLD/NO-PPAmF surface. Fig. 5 (in vitro, 2 h PRP incubation) shows SEM images with visibly fewer, less spread platelets on the modified surfaces, and bar charts (B, C) confirming significantly lower platelet number and lower percent activation for BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and especially the combined coating versus untreated steel/PPAmF (p<0.001). Fig. 6 (ex vivo arteriovenous shunt, 2 h whole-blood circulation) shows less occluding thrombus in cross-section and longitudinal photos, sparser platelet/fibrin coverage by SEM, and quantitatively lower occlusion percentage and thrombus weight, with blood flow rate on BVLD/NO-PPAmF remaining near ~90% of the initial value versus ~20% for bare steel.

Together this justifies the conclusion that the modified coatings, and the combined BVLD/NO-PPAmF treatment in particular, markedly reduce platelet adhesion/activation and thrombus formation and preserve blood flow better than untreated 316LSS or PPAmF over a short blood-contact interval. It does not establish long-term in-service durability, performance beyond 2 h, endothelialization, mechanical/wear stability of the coating in vivo, or which specific factor (topography vs. NO release) drives the effect, since only the combined and single-modification groups are compared, not a mechanistic dissection.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
What the two figures (static platelet assay, panels A-C of the first image; ex vivo blood-circulation assay, panels A-G of the second image) jointly support:

The gross photographs of the shunt-tube lumens and their sliced cross-sections after 2 h of blood circulation (panels B/C of the second figure) show 316L SS and PPAmF lumens filled with dark red clot, BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF with partial clot, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF largely clear/open. This visual ordering matches the quantitative panels in the same figure: occlusion (%), thrombus weight (mg), and blood flow rate (% of initial) all rank BVLD/NO-PPAmF best — lowest occlusion, lightest thrombus, and ~90% of initial flow retained, versus ~20% retained flow and heavy occlusion for 316L SS/PPAmF, with the single-modified surfaces intermediate. Combined with the already-established static SEM/platelet-count/activation result, this indicates the anti-adhesive, low-activation behavior seen on static substrates carries over into maintained vessel patency under actual flowing blood — evidence closer to in-service performance than the static assay alone.

What it does not establish: the photographs are single qualitative images per surface with no stated replication/statistics for that panel specifically; the test is a 2-hour ex vivo arteriovenous shunt in an animal model, not a chronic implant, so long-term durability, wear, or degradation "in service" is not shown; and no mechanism for the reduced thrombosis is given.

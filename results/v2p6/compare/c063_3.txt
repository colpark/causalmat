THE QUESTION

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Panels confirm: F9a (η=1.59, JMAK plot, R²>0.98, four temperatures 280–360°C) is the C0 desorption kinetics; F9b (η=1.32, same format) is C10. F4c is the EDS map of the C10 alloy showing Mg (green) particle bodies with C (blue) and Co (red) signals concentrated on particle surfaces/edges, consistent with the stated observation.

What the combination supports: the EDS map (upstream, structural) and the JMAK kinetics (downstream, mechanistic) are consistent with a shared narrative — a surface-decorating C/Co catalyst phase on C10 particles co-occurring with a lower Avrami exponent (1.32) than the uncatalyzed C0 case (1.59), which the authors would read as a shift toward more interface/surface-controlled (vs. bulk nucleation-and-growth) desorption kinetics upon catalyst addition.

What it leaves open: the EDS map only images C10, so there is no matched EDS comparison for C0 to show the catalyst/surface layer is actually absent or different there — the contrast with η=1.59 is inferred, not imaged. The EDS map is also a single static micrograph (no link to any specific temperature or time point in the JMAK series), so it cannot on its own explain why η changes with temperature within either alloy, nor establish that the surface C/Co layer is mechanistically causal for the lower exponent rather than merely co-present. The linear JMAK fits establish the kinetic exponents but not the physical nucleation/growth model each η value implies. ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
The two lines of evidence sit at very different scales and do not, by themselves, bridge microstructure to kinetic mechanism.

F4c (EDS map, C10 only) shows Mg(green)/C(blue)/Co(red) with C and Co concentrated on particle surfaces and edges — a static, coarse-resolution spatial map for one composition. F9a/F9b (JMAK plots) show excellent linear fits (R²>0.98) for desorption at four temperatures, with exponent η=1.59 for C0 and η=1.32 for C10 — a macroscopic kinetic measurement with no spatial/microstructural information built in.

Together they support only a coincidence-level narrative: the catalyst-containing sample (C10), whose C/Co is shown to sit on particle surfaces, also shows a lower JMAK exponent than the catalyst-free sample (C0). This is consistent with, but does not demonstrate, that surface-located C/Co alters the desorption nucleation/growth mechanism reflected in η.

What is left open: there is no EDS map for C0 to compare against, so the surface-distribution claim cannot be tied specifically to the change in η; the JMAK plots contain no spatial or compositional information, so nothing here directly connects the observed surface sites to the kinetic exponent; and the interpretation of η values as diagnostic of a particular nucleation/growth mechanism is an external assumption, not something these two panels test. The earlier-noted EDS/HRTEM resolution caveats still apply to any claim about where catalyst actually sits.

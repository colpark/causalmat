ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Both panels confirm the printed measurements. The combination supports that these are real, correctly reported trends for both quantities, and it supports that PF alone does not determine ZT ranking: x=0.01 dominates PF at every temperature shown, yet x=0.005 — not x=0.01 — has the highest ZT at 300 K. That divergence is evidence a factor other than PF (most plausibly thermal conductivity, not shown in either panel) must differ between x=0.005 and x=0.01 to produce this crossover.

What it leaves open: neither panel shows thermal conductivity, so the mechanism behind the PF/ZT rank reversal is not established here, only implied. The panels also don't clarify whether the ZT ranking persists or flips at higher T, since only 300 K values were given for B.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result

The upstream resistivity/PF evidence does not account for the ZT ordering. At 300 K the PF ranking (x=0.01 highest, x=0.005 lower) is the reverse of the ZT ranking (x=0.005 highest, x=0.01 second). Since ZT = PF·T/κ, this inversion shows thermal conductivity (not reported in the upstream evidence) must differ enough between x=0.005 and x=0.01 to override the PF advantage — something the resistivity-driven PF story cannot explain, since it never addresses κ.

What is supported: the qualitative composition trend (x=0 far worse than doped samples) is consistent across both PF and ZT. What is left open: why x=0.005 outperforms x=0.01 in ZT despite lower PF, the role of thermal conductivity, and the origin of the doped-vs-reference ZT gap — none of these are addressed by the upstream resistivity/PF pairing.

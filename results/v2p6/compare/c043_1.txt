ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
The two panels are consistent in direction but only partially overlapping in what they test, so they support a limited claim.

F7 (PL) shows three samples — TiO2, Er0.5Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O — with PL intensity in the 390-420 nm band falling in that order (TiO2 highest, Er0.5Ce0.2 lowest, roughly half at Er0.5Ti-O). Lower PL intensity is conventionally read as fewer electron-hole recombination events. F8 (degradation) shows Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O reaching the highest MO degradation (~46% at 60 min) and TiO2 the lowest (~20%), with the same rank order at 20 and 40 min, consistent across the whole time course.

Together, for the two endpoints they share (TiO2 vs Er0.5Ce0.2), the PL trend (least recombination) lines up with the degradation trend (most activity), so the combination supports the qualitative story that suppressed recombination in the Er0.5Ce0.2 co-doped sample tracks with its superior photocatalytic degradation, relative to bare TiO2.

What it leaves open: F8 also includes Er0.5Ce0.1Ti-O and Er0.5Ce0.3Ti-O, whose degradation is non-monotonic in Ce content (0.2 > 0.3 > 0.1 > TiO2), but F7 has no PL trace for the 0.1 or 0.3 Ce loadings, so the PL data cannot confirm or explain why 0.2 outperforms 0.3. It also doesn't rule out other contributors (band gap, surface area, adsorption) to the degradation differences — PL only speaks to recombination, not to degradation mechanism directly.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Reviewed F7 (PL spectra, TiO2 / Er0.5Ti-O / Er0.5Ce0.2-O) and F8 (MO degradation vs time for TiO2, Er0.5Ce0.1Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.3Ti-O).

The upstream PL/EIS convergence (established separately) accounts for the downstream degradation ranking only for the two samples common to both: TiO2 and Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O. TiO2 has the highest PL (most recombination) and no resolvable EIS arc (worst charge transfer), and it also shows the lowest degradation (~20% at 60 min). Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O has the lowest PL and the smallest, well-defined EIS arc, and it shows the highest degradation (~46%). So for these two samples, the optical/electrochemical proxies and the photocatalytic outcome all point the same direction, which is consistent with (but does not prove) lower recombination/lower charge-transfer resistance supporting more efficient photocatalysis.

This does not extend further: Er0.5Ti-O, the sample with the intermediate PL value, was not tested in the degradation assay, so its expected intermediate activity is unconfirmed. Conversely, Er0.5Ce0.1Ti-O and Er0.5Ce0.3Ti-O appear in the degradation data with no PL or EIS measurements at all, and their non-monotonic ordering (0.2 > 0.3 > 0.1) relative to Ce content is not explained by any upstream measurement. So only a two-sample correlation is supported; the mechanistic link and the full doping-series trend remain open.

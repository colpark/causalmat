ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
The three panels are mutually consistent and support the downstream time-to-completion claim, but only via an inference about which curve is which alloy.

F8a (panel a, four temperatures 360-280 C) shows the slower desorption series: at 300 C the curve plateaus around 150-180 min, and at 280 C around 450-480 min — matching the stated C0 values (~150 min, ~450 min) almost exactly. F8b (panel b, same four temperatures) shows a much faster series: at 300 C the curve plateaus around 15 min (close to the ~11 min cited for C10) and at 280 C finishes by ~40 min, matching "C10 finishes in <40 min even at 280 C." F10's Arrhenius plot gives the two slopes/activation energies (S_C0=-15.674 -> Ea=130.3 kJ/mol; S_C10=-9.852 -> Ea=81.9 kJ/mol) cited in measurement A, and the lower Ea for C10 is kinetically consistent with C10's faster, lower-temperature-tolerant desorption seen in F8b versus F8a.

So the combination supports: the two alloys' desorption kinetics differ in the direction and rough magnitude the activation-energy numbers predict (lower Ea, faster desorption for the +10 wt% CQCo alloy). It leaves open: the panel/figure captions as printed are garbled and never explicitly state "panel a = C0, panel b = C10" — that assignment is inferred from matching numbers, not read directly off the image labels, and the mechanism by which CQCo addition lowers Ea is not shown in these panels.

Panels checked: F8a (C0 desorption curves at 360/320/300/280 °C, complete at ~150 min at 300 °C and ~450 min at 280 °C), F8b (C10 desorption curves, complete in ~10-15 min at 300 °C and <40 min at 280 °C), F10 (Arrhenius fits: slopes -15.674/-9.852, Ea = 130.3/81.9 kJ/mol for C0/C10).

Yes, the upstream evidence accounts for the downstream desorption-time observation, but not independently. The Ea values in F10 are extracted directly from the same temperature-dependent desorption-rate data shown in F8a/F8b (the rate constants k used for the Arrhenius fit come from these curves). So C10's much lower Ea (81.9 vs 130.3 kJ/mol) and C0/C10's disparate desorption times (150 vs 11 min at 300 °C; <40 vs ~450 min comparing 280 °C to 300 °C) are two views of one kinetic dataset, not two separate confirmations - faster desorption at a given temperature is mathematically what a lower activation energy predicts, so the panels are internally consistent rather than cross-validating.

What this combination supports: C10's faster hydrogen desorption is quantitatively consistent with its lower measured Ea, and both track the TEM-established Co dispersion. What it leaves open (per the earlier qualifications, still applicable): whether the Co dispersion causes the lower Ea/faster kinetics, since TEM was only imaged for C10, only two Ea points exist, and the TEM/Arrhenius link remains inferred rather than measured on a shared axis.

The upstream UV-Vis measurement establishes that the synthesized material has a strong, reproducibly quantifiable NIR absorption at 808 nm in solution — a necessary optical property for any photoacoustic (PA) contrast agent, since PA signal generation depends on light absorption. In that narrow sense, F3c supports that this material is a plausible candidate to produce the downstream PA readout seen in F4f: it has the requisite absorption capacity at the same wavelength presumably used for PA excitation.

However, the upstream evidence does not account for the downstream observation itself. F3c is an in vitro, solution-phase, concentration-linearity measurement; it says nothing about in vivo behavior — biodistribution, tumor accumulation/clearance kinetics, or blood circulation time. The non-monotonic time course in F4f (initial dip at 1h, rise to a peak at 4h, decline by 10h) reflects pharmacokinetic/EPR-mediated accumulation and clearance dynamics, which the extinction-coefficient measurement cannot predict or explain. Nor does the linear R2 value indicate what magnitude of PA signal to expect in tissue, since in vivo signal also depends on local concentration, tissue optical properties, and instrument gain, none of which are calibrated by F3c. So the combination supports that the material is optically capable of producing PA contrast, but leaves fully open why the signal follows this particular temporal profile and whether the measured coefficient quantitatively predicts the in vivo signal magnitude.

THE QUESTION
how the material performs in service

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Conclusion the evidence justifies: Across both a static in vitro platelet-adhesion assay (Fig. 5, PRP incubation 2 h) and a dynamic ex vivo blood-circulation test (Fig. 6, arteriovenous shunt, 2 h), the combined BVLD/NO-PPAmF surface consistently outperformed bare 316LSS and PPAmF alone. It showed the fewest and least-activated adherent platelets and the least fibrinogen/fibrinogen-γ-chain deposition (Fig. 5B–E, ***), and in the shunt test it produced the least occlusion, lowest thrombus weight, and highest sustained blood flow (~90% of initial vs ~20% for 316LSS/PPAmF; Fig. 6E–G, ***). Cross-sections and photographs (Fig. 6B–C) visually confirm minimal thrombus/occlusion on BVLD/NO-PPAmF versus heavily occluded, thrombus-filled lumens for 316LSS/PPAmF. This supports that the combined topographical (BVLD) and NO-releasing (PPAmF) modification meaningfully improves hemocompatibility relative to unmodified stainless steel.

What it leaves uncertain: the single BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF arms are intermediate between the unmodified controls and the combined surface, but the panels bracket significance only at the group level, so the relative individual contribution of BVLD versus NO release isn't statistically resolved. Test duration is limited to 2 h, the animal/species for the shunt model isn't stated in these panels, and no long-term in vivo durability or clotting-cascade mechanism data are shown, so persistence beyond this short window and cellular activation mechanism remain unaddressed.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
The ex vivo blood-circulation panels (Fig. 6) extend the surface-level platelet findings to device-relevant performance. Cross-sectional photographs (F6b) and split-sample photographs (F6c) show 316LSS and PPAmF lumens filled with dark red thrombus, while BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and especially BVLD/NO-PPAmF show largely clear, unobstructed lumens with minimal red material. This is corroborated quantitatively (panels E-G, which do carry error bars and significance stars): occlusion falls from ~58-63% (316LSS, PPAmF) to ~7-10% (single-modified surfaces) to ~3% (BVLD/NO-PPAmF); thrombus weight follows the same order (~65-75 mg down to ~2-5 mg); and blood flow rate, near-fully preserved on BVLD/NO-PPAmF (~90% of initial), is reduced to ~20% on 316LSS/PPAmF.

This justifies the conclusion that the reduced platelet adhesion/activation seen on retrieved surfaces (Fig. 5) co-occurs with, and plausibly underlies, substantially better maintenance of vessel patency and flow under real circulating blood - BVLD/NO-PPAmF performs best in service among the five surfaces tested.

What remains uncertain: this is a single ex vivo/animal circulation model at one timepoint (2h), so durability, other flow regimes, and long-term device performance are untested; and while occlusion/flow/thrombus mass are statistically compared, no mechanistic link is established connecting the earlier platelet-level suppression to the circulation-level outcome beyond co-occurrence across the same five surfaces.

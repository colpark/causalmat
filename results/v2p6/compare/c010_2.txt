Two answers to the same question about one set of measurements. Both answerers saw the same evidence. The second was additionally handed an earlier established result; the first was not.

THE QUESTION
how the material performs in service

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
The four panels together describe ion-release and functional behavior of Co-doped mesoporous bioactive glass (Co-MBG) scaffolds in simulated fluid, not true clinical "service" performance.

F5a shows SiO4^4- concentration rising over 7 days of soaking for all three scaffolds (0/2/5Co-MBG), with 0Co-MBG and 5Co-MBG climbing to ~190 mg/L by day 7 while 2Co-MBG plateaus near 80-100 mg/L after day 3 — evidence that the glass network degrades and releases silicate steadily, though the non-monotonic ranking by Co content (2Co lowest at day 7, despite being intermediate in dose) is unexplained and not obviously dose-dependent.

F9 (western blot) shows HIF-1α band intensity increasing from MBG to 2Co-MBG to 5Co-MBG, supporting that cobalt release activates hypoxia-mimicking HIF-1α signaling in BMSCs in a Co-dose-related pattern — but this is a single blot with no quantification, replicates, or loading-normalized statistics shown.

F11a/b (same image, two panels) show ampicillin release plateauing near 800 µg by ~24-48 h, and ampicillin-loaded scaffolds significantly reducing bacterial relative survival at days 3 and 7 (marked *) versus blank control and drug-free scaffolds, supporting sustained antibacterial activity.

Together this justifies: the scaffolds degrade, release Co2+/silicate, induce HIF-1α up-regulation, and release ampicillin with measurable antibacterial effect in vitro. It does not establish in vivo efficacy, mechanical performance under load, degradation-strength coupling, or long-term (beyond 7 days) service behavior.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Findings from the four panels:

F5a shows the scaffolds release Co2+ in a clear composition-dependent order (5Co-MBG > 2Co-MBG > 0Co-MBG, plateauing near 21 and 17 mg/L by day 7; 0Co-MBG releases none), alongside SiO4^4-, Ca2+, and PO4^3- exchange typical of bioactive-glass behavior (Ca2+ dips then rises, PO4^3- falls steadily — consistent with ion exchange/mineralization, though apatite formation itself isn't directly shown, only ion trends).

F9 (already settled): 5Co-MBG shows a distinctly stronger HIF-1alpha band than MBG and 2Co-MBG, which are both faint and indistinct from each other — an association between the highest-Co2+-release group and highest HIF-1alpha signal, not a full three-point dose-response, and only a qualitative blot read at an unstated timepoint.

F11a/b (single shared image): ampicillin-loaded scaffolds release drug with a burst then plateau near 700-800 ug by roughly 24-72 h, and these scaffolds show significantly reduced bacterial "survival" (~81% day 3, ~52% day 7) versus blank control (100%) and drug-free scaffolds (110-118%), indicating working antibacterial delivery.

What this justifies: the material releases Co, Si, Ca, and P ions in a controlled, composition-graded way with bioactive-glass-like ion-exchange behavior, and separately delivers ampicillin with measurable antibacterial effect in vitro. The Co2+ release also associates with elevated HIF-1alpha specifically in the highest-release group.

What remains uncertain: whether Co2+ release, rather than other compositional differences between the three scaffold types, drives the HIF-1alpha pattern; whether any of these in vitro ion/drug kinetics or bacterial-survival results predict in vivo degradation, angiogenic signaling, or clinical antibacterial performance; and whether antibacterial and hypoxia-signaling functions interact when combined in one scaffold, since these were tested as separate mechanisms, not together.

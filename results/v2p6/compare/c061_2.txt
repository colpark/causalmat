ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Combining F9 (XRD) and F8 (photographs): the XRD patterns at both 5 and 10 cycles retain the same t-ZrO2 marker peaks (~43°, split ~73-74°) matching the tetragonal reference sticks, with no evidence in either pattern of the additional peaks that would signal monoclinic ZrO2. The insets do show the t-ZrO2 peak intensities somewhat lower at 10 cycles than at 5 cycles, a change that coincides with the photographic transition from an intact coupon at 5 cycles to spalled corners at 10 cycles — consistent with, but not proof of, some phase/microstructural change accompanying spallation onset.

This upstream evidence only partially accounts for the downstream observation. It supports the claim that spallation onset (5→10 cycles) is not obviously driven by a gross tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation, since t-ZrO2 remains the identified phase at both sampled points, while leaving open whether the modest intensity/fraction shift between 5 and 10 cycles reflects a real, mechanistically relevant phase or crystallinity change versus measurement noise. Critically, XRD was only collected at 5 and 10 cycles, but the photographs show spalled corners persisting and growing through 40 cycles — a window with no XRD coverage at all. So the phase data cannot explain, confirm, or rule out any role of phase change in the continued spallation growth from 10 to 40 cycles; that link is untested.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Both panels reviewed. F9 (XRD) shows t-ZrO2 marker peaks (~43 deg, split ~73-74 deg) present at both 5 and 10 cycles, with the 5-cycle pattern showing visibly higher marker intensity than the 10-cycle pattern in the insets. F8 (photographs) shows the coupon intact at 0/5 cycles, corner spallation appearing by 10 cycles, and spalled area persisting/growing modestly through 20, 30 and 40 cycles.

The combination supports a temporal association: the tetragonal-marked phase signature is already detectable by 5 cycles (before spallation is visible) and remains present at 10 cycles, the same cycle count at which spallation first appears. This is consistent with the phase-identity shift being an early, possibly contributing, change that precedes or co-occurs with the onset of coating degradation (service capability loss via corner spallation).

It leaves open several things: (1) whether the amount of phase transformed (fraction) tracks the amount of spallation — no fraction data exist, and spallation keeps growing through 40 cycles while XRD is reported only to 10 cycles, so the phase trajectory during 10-40 cycles is unknown; (2) causation is not established, only co-occurrence; (3) YSZ's own possible phase shift under cycling is untested, so Al2O3-YSZ's degradation cannot be attributed solely to its phase change relative to YSZ.

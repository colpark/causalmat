THE QUESTION
how the material performs in service

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Evidence justifies a clear service-performance conclusion: nitrogen-doped carbon host (CnC HS) suppresses zinc dendrite growth relative to plain carbon host (C HS), extending usable cycle life. Panels d/e show SEM of the two Zn-plated surfaces (both 10 µm): CnC HS (d) has a more open, platelet-like deposit; C HS (e) shows a denser, filamentous/mossy morphology consistent with non-uniform, tip-driven growth. This matches the mechanism schematic (f vs g): zincophilic (Zn–N) sites promote spacious nucleation and lateral growth into a homogeneous Zn layer, while their absence gives dense nucleation, tip growth, and dendrite formation.

Electrochemically (Fig. 4), CnC HS holds Coulombic efficiency near 100% for ~200 cycles, whereas C HS collapses to near-0% CE after ~40–50 cycles (a). In symmetric-cell cycling, C HS develops voltage spikes and outright "battery failure" by ~2700–4300 min, while CnC HS runs stably past 7000 min (b); the same failure pattern recurs at multiple current densities (e), with C HS failing near 2500–3000 min.

What remains uncertain: these are half-cell/symmetric-cell data only, not full-cell energy density, rate capability at higher currents, or long-term performance beyond the tested windows; mechanism is inferred from morphology/schematic, not directly imaged in situ.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Conclusion the evidence justifies: In symmetric-cell service testing, CnC HS clearly outperforms C HS. The Coulombic-efficiency trace (F4a) shows CnC HS holding near 100% steadily across ~200 cycles at 1 mAh cm-2/4 mA cm-2, while C HS is noisier, dips to ~75-80% efficiency twice, and twice drops to 0% (cell failure events). The long-duration cycling trace (F4b), explicitly annotated "C HS battery failure," shows C HS developing large voltage spikes/instability partway through a ~7000-minute test while CnC HS remains flat and stable throughout. The stepped-rate cycling trace (F4e) shows the same pattern as current density is ramped up: CnC HS stays stable across the step sequence while C HS grows increasingly erratic and eventually fails at the higher steps. Together these three panels support that CnC HS provides more reliable, longer-lived, higher-efficiency service performance than C HS, particularly as current density/duration increases.

What remains uncertain: these panels give efficiency and voltage-stability data only — they do not quantify overpotential magnitude (that is F4c/d) or capacity/energy retention, and they do not show *why* C HS fails (e.g., whether by dendrite short-circuit); linking this failure behavior to the earlier-established morphology/plateau divergence is plausible but not directly shown by F4a/b/e themselves.

What it leaves open: these two panels only establish voltage and power output magnitudes at one film thickness (220 μm) and one load sweep; they say nothing about downstream service_capability in the sense of actual functional demonstration (e.g., lighting an LED, charging a capacitor, cycling durability, current/energy density, or efficiency under real load). Any claim that this output performance translates into practical service capability or usable "value" beyond raw voltage/power is not supported by these two panels alone.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Partially, and only indirectly. F7b/F7f together justify that the fiber-PENG's voltage output is a genuine, dose-dependent piezoelectric signal (not artifact) that rises with PZT wt%. That supports the premise behind F7e — that the fiber film has an active, PZT-content-linked piezoelectric source capable of driving external loads — and is consistent with the fiber film outproducing the cast film in F7e, since the fiber-film chemistry is the one shown to be genuinely piezoelectric and scalable with PZT loading.

But the upstream evidence does not account for the specific downstream observations in F7e. First, F7e's power curve versus load resistance (peaking at 136.9 uW at 10 MOhm) reflects impedance matching between the PENG's internal source impedance and the external load — a mechanism neither F7b nor F7f measures or addresses; nothing in the upstream panels explains why the peak sits at 10 MOhm rather than another value. Second, the cast film was never run in F7b/F7f, so the magnitude gap between fiber (136.9 uW) and cast (21.025 uW) power is not directly traced to a dose-dependence or polarity test on the cast film — the fiber-vs-cast difference (likely fabrication/alignment-related) is asserted, not demonstrated, by this evidence pair.

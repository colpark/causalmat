ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Partial support, with a clear gap.

F3e and F3g (IR thermal imaging, 808 nm only) establish that BNs-BSA's photothermal response scales positively with both concentration (25→200 µg/mL) and laser power density (0.5→1.5 W), reaching the highest spot temperatures (~55-60 °C) at 200 µg/mL and 1.5 W, while water stays cold. This is solid, dose-dependent evidence that the material converts 808 nm light into heat and that heating magnitude is tunable — it supports the premise that 808 nm irradiation of BNs-BSA can generate ablative-level hyperthermia.

F5d (in-vivo tumor curves) shows that BNs-BSA+808nm alone (group 5) fails to fully control tumors — 4 of 5 regrow after ~day 14, some reaching ~700 mm³ — whereas BNs-BSA+Lasers (660+808 nm combined, group 6) keeps all 5 tumors at ~0 throughout.

The thermal panels justify that photothermal heating is real and concentration/power-dependent, which is consistent with 808 nm alone being only partially effective if the in-vivo dose/power was on the lower end of the tested range. But F3e/F3g never image the 660 nm condition or the combined-laser condition, and were done on solution spots, not tumors. So the upstream evidence cannot account for why adding the 660 nm laser specifically converts partial regrowth into complete, durable ablation — that mechanistic/synergy gap remains open.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Panels confirm the described patterns: F3e shows the 6-min IR spot brightening/heating with concentration (200>100>50>25 ug/mL 808nm, water cold); F3g shows the same 808nm spot heating with power (0.5->1.5W); F5d shows BNs-BSA+808nm tumors mostly regrowing after ~day 14 (4/5 blue curves rise) while BNs-BSA+Lasers (dual 660/808nm) tumors stay flat near 0 (all 5 red curves).

What the combination supports: the upstream photothermal dose-response (heat rising with both BNs-BSA concentration and 808nm power) accounts for why single-wavelength 808nm treatment produces an initial anti-tumor effect at all — it establishes that this material does generate concentration- and power-dependent heat under 808nm, consistent with some tumor suppression seen in the BNs-BSA+808nm group relative to laser-only or PBS controls.

What it leaves open: the thermal panels say nothing about durability or about the dual-laser condition. They do not explain why 4/5 BNs-BSA+808nm tumors regrow after day 14 (insufficient/incomplete ablation at the single time point and dose tested) nor why adding the 660nm laser (BNs-BSA+Lasers) achieves complete, durable suppression in all 5 mice. That gain plausibly reflects an additional mechanism (e.g., 660nm-driven photodynamic or synergistic effect) not measured by the 808nm-only IR thermal panels, so the upstream evidence accounts for the presence of a photothermal effect but not for the difference in efficacy/durability between the 808nm-alone and dual-laser groups.

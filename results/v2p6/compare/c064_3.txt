ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
The combination supports a correlational link between surface state and first-cycle kinetics, but not a demonstrated mechanism.

F4c (EDS) shows C (blue) and Co (red) concentrated on C10 particle surfaces/edges, consistent with the earlier-established HRTEM corroboration that this is a real carbon(+Co) coating layer at particle edges. F6a and F6b are Sieverts hydrogenation curves at 360°C: C0 (F6a) shows a slow, incubation-limited first cycle (66 min lag before ramp, only ~3.2 wt% at 180 min, far below the ~5 wt% cycles 2-3 plateau), whereas C10 (F6b) shows a fast first cycle (11 min lag, essentially matching cycles 2-3, saturating within ~5 min). Together, this justifies saying that the presence of a surface C@Co layer on C10 co-occurs with, and is temporally consistent with, elimination of the slow-activation behavior seen in the uncoated C0 alloy — i.e., the coating is a plausible surface-mediated contributor to faster first-cycle (activation-free) hydrogen uptake.

What it leaves open: this is a same-sample correlation, not a causal isolation — C0 and C10 differ in bulk composition as well as coating, so the coating's independent contribution to kinetics is not isolated. Neither panel quantifies coating coverage/uniformity or links it mechanistically (e.g., via activation energy) to the kinetic curves, and whether C or Co (or both) drives the effect remains unresolved.

Question: The context describes why this polycrystalline Au film was made and how it was processed. Based on panels F3e and F3f, which coplot the diffraction peak along the q_x, q_y and q_z directions, how does the peak's width vary from one direction to another, and what is the approximate width in the direction where it is largest?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): The measured peak is widest along q_y, with an approximate width of about 10e-3 A^-1; it is narrower along q_x, and compact along q_z (o5).

Grading note: Answer key: the numbers and labels on the evidence node o5, judge-confirmed.

Candidate answer:
Both panels plot the same simulated/experimental diffraction peak sliced through orthogonal q-planes (F3e: qx–qz; F3f: qx–qy), each axis in units of ×10⁻³ Å⁻¹. The peak is clearly anisotropic: it is narrowest and most compact along qz (the film-normal/out-of-plane direction), where the bright core stays confined to roughly ±5×10⁻³ Å⁻¹, giving the pattern in F3e its tall, thin "cross" shape. In-plane, the peak is markedly broader — in F3f the colored intensity (and its star-shaped diffraction fringes) spreads out along qy to roughly ±10–15×10⁻³ Å⁻¹, making qy the direction of largest width, with qx intermediate between qy and qz. So the ordering is qz (narrowest) < qx < qy (widest), consistent with the grain being much more extended/faceted in-plane than through the thin-film thickness. The approximate full width in the largest direction (qy) is on the order of 2–3×10⁻² Å⁻¹ (i.e., a half-width of about 10–15×10⁻³ Å⁻¹), roughly 2–3× broader than the qz width.

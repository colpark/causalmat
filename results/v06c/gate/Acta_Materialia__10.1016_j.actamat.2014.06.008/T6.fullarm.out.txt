Panel F4b plots Δqx (×10^-3 Å^-1) vs temperature for the qx peak, with the red dashed curve/arrow marking the heating leg and the blue dashed curve/arrow marking the cooling leg.

- On heating (room temperature to ~270 C), Δqx rises monotonically and smoothly, from roughly 1.3×10^-3 Å^-1 near 35 C up to about 4.9×10^-3 Å^-1 near 265 C — i.e., the diffraction peak shifts steadily in the same direction (increasing qx-shift) with increasing temperature.
- On cooling back down from ~270 C to room temperature, Δqx decreases again, but the blue (cooling) curve sits above/to the left of the red (heating) curve at intermediate temperatures — the peak shift does not retrace the same path.
- The two curves converge only at the temperature extremes (near the low-T start and high-T peak), producing a hysteresis loop: for a given temperature, the peak shift measured while cooling is larger than the shift measured at the same temperature while heating.

So the answer: the peak shift increases continuously during heating and decreases during cooling, but heating and cooling do not overlap — the cycle traces out a clear hysteresis loop rather than a reversible path, meaning the grain's strain state after a full heat/cool cycle depends on thermal history, not just on temperature.

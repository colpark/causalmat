Across F6a→F6e, the xz phase maps show the film-thickness (z) direction staying uniform at every step (each vertical position along z has a nearly constant color/phase), while the pattern along x changes with temperature. On heating (a: 50 C, b: 140 C, c: 240 C), the number of distinct colored bands/stripes along x visibly increases and the stripes become narrower and more numerous, i.e., the phase pattern gets finer/more subdivided as temperature rises, culminating in the most fragmented, high-contrast striping at 240 C. On cooling back down (d: 140 C, e: 50 C), the stripe pattern partially coarsens again (fewer, broader bands), but the specific color arrangement at 50 C on cooling (e) does not match the 50 C pattern on heating (a) — e.g., the red/blue placement is roughly reversed — indicating the grain's internal pattern does not simply retrace itself step-by-step. The xy phase maps show a mottled, granular texture at all five steps without a clear systematic trend of coarsening or refining, appearing qualitatively similar in speckle density throughout the whole heating-cooling sequence. So step-to-step, the dominant visible change is in the xz view along x: increasing stripe multiplicity/complexity while heating, partial simplification while cooling, but with a different final configuration than the start, while the xy view stays comparably heterogeneous throughout.

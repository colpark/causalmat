Question: Panels F1a-F1d are the SEM images for the Cu2S microspheres made with 0, 5, 10 and 20 mL of GO solution respectively. Based on the insets in these panels, how does the flake structure that builds up each microsphere change as GO solution volume increases?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): In the inset at 0 mL GO the microsphere is built from only a few large flakes. Once GO solution is present (5, 10, 20 mL) the microspheres are instead built from many smaller, thinner flakes, with the finest flakes seen in the 10 and 20 mL insets (panels c and d) (o1).

Grading note: Answer key: the numbers and labels on the evidence node o1 (the SEM inset comparison across F1a-F1d), judge-confirmed.

Candidate answer:
Across the F1a→F1d insets (0, 5, 10, 20 mL GO), the flake architecture of each microsphere becomes progressively denser and more developed, then coarsens. At 0 mL (F1a) only a few sparse, thin, needle-like flakes are scattered on an otherwise smooth spherical surface, with much of the sphere bare. At 5 mL (F1b) the flakes are more numerous and longer, curved petal-like blades radiating from a common center but still leaving visible gaps between them. At 10 mL (F1c) the sphere is fully and densely covered by a compact, uniform array of thin, closely packed radiating flakes/petals with no bare surface showing. At 20 mL (F1d) the flakes appear larger, thicker, and more fused into folded, crumpled sheet-like segments, so individual thin flakes are less distinct and the surface looks coarser/less uniform than at 10 mL. Overall trend: sparse thin flakes → increasing flake density/coverage (peaking in uniformity at 10 mL) → thicker, coalesced/folded flake sheets at 20 mL.

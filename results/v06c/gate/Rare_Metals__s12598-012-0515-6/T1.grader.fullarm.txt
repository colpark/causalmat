Question: Panels F1a, F1b and F1c show SEM images of the as-cast Al-Cu-Mn, Al-Cu-Mn-Sm and Al-Cu-Mn-La alloys. Based on these three panels, how does the appearance of the segregated phase referenced in the alloying hypothesis change from the base alloy through the Sm-containing alloy to the La-containing alloy?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): In Al-Cu-Mn (a) the light-contrast phase forms a continuous network along the dendrite boundaries. In Al-Cu-Mn-Sm (b) it appears as isolated particles instead of a network. In Al-Cu-Mn-La (c) it appears as thin, broken strings.

Candidate answer:
In F1a (base Al-Cu-Mn), the segregated phase forms a thick, continuous, interconnected network of bright regions outlining large polygonal grain/cell boundaries, giving a coarse, fully bounded honeycomb-like structure. In F1b (Al-Cu-Mn-Sm), that network is markedly refined: the bright phase is broken up into short, thin, discontinuous segments and isolated particles scattered along former boundary paths, with much less continuous coverage than in (a). In F1c (Al-Cu-Mn-La), the segregated phase is again refined relative to the base alloy but appears somewhat coarser/more connected than in the Sm case, forming intermediate-length branching segments; two distinct constituents are now resolvable and labeled — arrow A pointing to blocky Al2Cu particles and arrow B to a finer Al6Cu6La phase. So overall, going from base to Sm to La, the continuous coarse network progressively fragments into finer, more discrete segregated features, with Sm producing the most discontinuous/finest morphology and La producing an intermediate, compositionally split (Al2Cu + Al6Cu6La) refined structure. This is consistent with the paper's hypothesis that RE additions (Sm, La) refine the segregated/eutectic phase in the as-cast Al-Cu-Mn alloy.

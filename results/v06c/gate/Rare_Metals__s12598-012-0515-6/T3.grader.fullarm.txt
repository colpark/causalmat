Question: The TEM panels F4a, F4b, and F4c show the aged microstructure of Al-Cu-Mn, Al-Cu-Mn-Sm, and Al-Cu-Mn-La respectively. Comparing the three panels, how does the theta' precipitate distribution change across them?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): Precipitate number density visibly rises from panel (a) to (b) to (c), i.e. it increases going from Al-Cu-Mn to Al-Cu-Mn-Sm to Al-Cu-Mn-La (o8).

Grading note: Answer key: the numbers and labels on the evidence node o8, judge-confirmed against panels F4a/F4b/F4c.

Candidate answer:
Visual comparison of F4a/b/c (Al-Cu-Mn, Al-Cu-Mn-Sm, Al-Cu-Mn-La), all at the same 20 nm scale bar, viewed along [100]Al:

- F4a (Al-Cu-Mn): the theta' cross-hatch pattern is faint and patchy, with only part of the field showing the fine plate contrast; there are also a couple of larger, isolated dark blob/streak features (likely coarser precipitates or other phases) rather than a uniform fine network — overall the theta' plates look sparser and less uniformly distributed.
- F4b (Al-Cu-Mn-Sm): the fine crosshatched theta' contrast is denser and covers most of the field more evenly than in (a), though a small dark cluster is still visible in one corner.
- F4c (Al-Cu-Mn-La): the crosshatch pattern is the densest and most uniform of the three, filling essentially the entire field of view with closely spaced, fine plates and no large isolated dark features.

So going from Al-Cu-Mn to Al-Cu-Mn-Sm to Al-Cu-Mn-La, the theta' precipitates appear to become progressively finer, more numerous, and more uniformly/densely distributed across the matrix, with La giving the most refined and homogeneous distribution — consistent with the paper's claim that RE additions (especially La) refine theta' precipitation via lattice distortion/vacancy binding that provides extra nucleation sites.

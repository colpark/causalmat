Question: For the polished RZSZ-20 sample (20 vol% excess ZrSi2) in panel F8f, given the synthesis route and the microstructure described above, what mechanism accounts for the crack-path evidence shown in that panel?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): Interlocked ZrB2 platelets together with nano-scale SiC trigger grain pull-out, crack bridging, deflection and branching, which lengthens the crack path (m2).

Candidate answer:
The crack-path evidence in F8f (deflection, bridging, grain pull-out, branching into a SiC-rich region) is best explained as extrinsic toughening enabled by the interlocking, anisotropic ZrB2 platelet microstructure that Reaction (1) produces via reactive hot pressing. In RZSZ-20 (20 vol% excess ZrSi2), the exothermic in-situ reaction locally melts/deforms ZrSi2, and the resulting liquid transports Zr and B to the fastest-growing ZrB2 crystal planes, driving anisotropic growth into elongated platelets that interlock most strongly at this composition. As the indentation crack propagates through this interlocked network, it is forced to deflect around the elongated ZrB2 platelets rather than cut straight through them, is bridged by platelets spanning the crack wake, and pulls grains out where platelet/matrix (ZrB2/SiC) interfaces are weaker than the grains themselves — all classic signs of crack-tip shielding from a rigid, elongated second-phase network rather than a homogeneous brittle matrix. The crack's branching upon reaching a SiC-rich region reflects a local change in fracture path/energy dissipation at the ZrB2-SiC phase boundary, where the SiC matrix (formed as the co-product of Reaction 1) offers an alternative, lower-resistance path or additional interfaces that split the advancing crack front. Together, these features indicate that the in-situ reactive synthesis route, by generating interlocking elongated ZrB2 platelets embedded in a SiC-rich matrix, provides the composite's fracture toughness through combined deflection/bridging/pull-out/branching mechanisms rather than through intrinsic matrix toughness alone.

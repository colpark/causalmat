Difference between the two conditions (powder mixture vs. composite), no ranking:

- Powder mixture pattern: dominated by numerous strong ZrSi2 peaks (diamond markers, e.g. the tall (131) peak near 2θ≈39°), plus WC peaks (circle markers) and a couple of small ZrO2 peaks (triangle markers).
- Composite pattern (after hot pressing): the ZrSi2 peaks are gone, replaced by a new set of peaks indexed to ZrB2 (square markers, e.g. (100), (001), (101), (102), (110), (111), (201)) and SiC (triangle-up markers, e.g. (111), (220), (311)); the WC peaks are also gone, replaced by new WSi2 peaks (star markers); a small ZrO2 peak (inverted triangle) persists near 2θ≈30°.

In short, the raw-powder XRD is dominated by ZrSi2 (with WC and trace ZrO2 impurity peaks), while the hot-pressed composite XRD shows those ZrSi2 and WC peaks essentially replaced by ZrB2 and SiC peaks (the intended reaction products) together with new WSi2 peaks and a residual ZrO2 trace — consistent with reaction (1) consuming the ZrSi2/B4C/C precursors to form ZrB2-SiC, with WC (from milling media) converting to WSi2.

Question: The panels given are from the RZSZ-20 sample (20 vol% excess ZrSi2): a polished cross-section image (F5c) and a fracture-surface image (F8d). Based on these two views, what do the ZrB2 grains look like and how do they relate to each other?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): The ZrB2 grains are elongated and connect with one another to form an interlocking arrangement, and this is seen consistently in both the polished (BSE) cross-section and the fracture surface (o7).

Grading note: Answer key: the numbers and labels on the evidence node o7, judge-confirmed against the given panels F5c and F8d.

Candidate answer:
In F5c (polished cross-section, BSE, RZSZ-20), the ZrB2 grains appear as bright, equiaxed to blocky polygonal particles roughly submicron to ~1 µm in size, fairly uniformly and densely distributed within the darker SiC matrix, with faceted (often angular/hexagonal-looking) grain outlines rather than rounded ones.

In F8d (fracture surface of RZSZ-20, region labeled "Interlocking"), the same grain population is seen from the fracture path: the ZrB2 (and SiC) grains are blocky, well-faceted crystals of similar submicron-to-micron scale that are tightly packed and mutually interlock, with irregular, jagged boundaries between neighbors rather than smooth/rounded interfaces.

Together, the two views indicate that the ZrB2 grains are small, equiaxed, faceted crystallites that grew in situ and are densely interlocked with neighboring ZrB2/SiC grains — the fracture surface shows the crack path weaving around and through these interlocked facets (largely transgranular/mixed fracture) rather than pulling apart cleanly along smooth grain boundaries, consistent with the paper's description of a self-toughened, well-bonded ZrB2-SiC microstructure produced by the in-situ reaction and liquid-assisted densification.

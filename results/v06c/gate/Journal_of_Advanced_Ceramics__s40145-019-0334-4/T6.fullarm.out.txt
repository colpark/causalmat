Based on the three fracture-surface SEM panels (each at the same 1 um scale bar):

- F8a (RZSZ-0, 0 vol% excess ZrSi2): No SiC grains are labeled or visibly distinguishable at all; the image only calls out ZrB2 grains and pores, consistent with SiC being present only as very fine, hard-to-resolve grains (or too little in-situ SiC formed) at this composition.
- F8b (RZSZ-10, 10 vol% excess ZrSi2): "Nano-sized SiC" is explicitly labeled, appearing as small, fine-grained particles decorating the fracture surface alongside transgranular fracture features and tiny pores.
- F8c (RZSZ-15, 15 vol% excess ZrSi2): "Nano-sized SiC" is again labeled, but the SiC-associated features look somewhat coarser/more elongated than in F8b, occurring alongside a mix of transgranular and intergranular fracture.

Overall, the panels indicate a trend of increasing SiC grain size with increasing excess ZrSi2 content: essentially unresolved/negligible SiC at 0 vol%, clearly nano-sized SiC grains at 10 vol%, and slightly coarser (though still nano-sized) SiC grains at 15 vol%. This is a qualitative visual read from the labeled SEM images (results/v06/masked/Journal_of_Advanced_Ceramics__s40145-019-0334-4/T6/F8a.jpg, F8b.jpg, F8c.jpg) rather than a quantitative grain-size measurement, since no numeric grain-size data are given on the panels themselves.

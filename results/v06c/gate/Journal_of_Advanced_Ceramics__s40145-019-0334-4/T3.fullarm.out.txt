In F5a (RZSZ-15), the bright/white grains are visibly elongated, plate- or rod-shaped features (arrows mark several of these bright elongated grains) scattered within a darker gray matrix; a distinct darker, irregularly-shaped blob (outlined by the dashed contour) sits among the elongated bright grains, forming a separate dark region embedded in the brighter grain network. The overall texture in F5a is coarser and less uniform, with clear size/shape contrast between the elongated bright grains and the surrounding finer darker matrix, and the dark irregular region appears cordoned off from, rather than blended into, the bright grains around it.

In F5b (RZSZ-20), the microstructure looks finer and more uniform: bright and dark grains are both smaller, more equiaxed/polygonal in shape, and packed closely together in an interlocking mosaic rather than showing the long, elongated bright grains seen in F5a. There is no obvious large, separately-outlined dark blob as in F5a; instead the light and dark phases are more finely and evenly intermixed throughout the field.

Overall, going from 15 to 20 vol% excess ZrSi2 (F5a to F5b), the apparent grain morphology shifts from coarser, elongated bright grains with a distinct dark irregular region toward a finer, more equiaxed, closely intermixed grain structure with no comparably large isolated dark patch.

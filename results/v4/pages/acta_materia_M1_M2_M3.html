end to end
  • Bright-field TEM with g=0002 reveals strong planar fault-like contrast lying on (0001), at a density of roughly ten plates per um obs1.
  • HAADF-STEM shows these plates as bright, heavy-atom-rich bands only a few atomic layers wide, with a 1 nm inset resolving one such bright band obs2.
  • Together the fault-like diffraction contrast and the heavy-atom-rich atomic-scale banding identify the dense basal planar defects as LPSO nanoplates, i.e. aggregates of four-layer Zn/Y-segregated fcc-type stacking faults rather than conventional stacking faults n8.
  • TEM after annealing shows the 520 C/water-quenched specimen retains only one thick dark plate, while the further 400 C anneal restores many thin plates obs3.
  • Quantifying this reversal, nanoplate count falls from 9.8 to 0.3 and back up to 9.0 per um while mean thickness rises from 28 to 84 and falls back to 14 nm across as-grown / 520 C / 520->400 C obs4.
  • These paired counts and thicknesses establish that heat treatment sets nanoplate density and thickness: 9.8 per um and ~28 nm as-grown, ~0.3 per um and ~84 nm after 520 C, ~9.0 per um and ~14 nm after the further 400 C anneal n9.
  • Taking the LPSO nanoplate phase and its density/thickness values as given, the 11-20 RT yield stress is 167 MPa as-grown, falls to 113 MPa after the 520 C anneal (when the nanoplates coarsen to ~84 nm and thin to ~0.3 per um), and recovers to 160 MPa after the further 400 C anneal restores the fine, dense nanoplates obs1 step2.
  • This tracking of yield stress with the nanoplate density/thickness cycle in the same orientation supports treating the nanoplates as the strengthening feature writer inference.
  • RT yield stress otherwise varies strongly with crystal orientation: ~9 MPa for 1 1 -2 1.85, ~148-165 MPa for 11-20, ~143 MPa for 01-10, while 0001 fractures at ~355 MPa without yielding obs2 step2.
  • Between RT and 400 C the alloy's 11-20/01-10 yield stress falls only from ~165 to ~105 MPa, whereas the pure-Mg reference falls from ~270 to ~15 MPa and sits near 10 MPa in the soft orientations obs3 step2.
  • Together these establish that yield stress is strongly orientation dependent and, in 11-20 and 01-10, reaches ~145-165 MPa at RT, about 11x the pure-Mg single crystal n12.
  • Taking this ~145-165 MPa hard-orientation yield stress as given, a tensile 0001 test at RT shows flow stress starting at ~72 MPa, serrated between ~52 and ~80 MPa, with the specimen reaching ~45% plastic strain before fracture obs1 step3.
  • The tensile gauge is filled with fine bands inclined to the 0001 tensile direction, across which the marked basal-plane trace kinks obs2 step3.
  • These observations establish that in tension along 0001 the crystal elongates ~45% at 65-80 MPa by kink-band formation, the first bulk demonstration of tensile ductility by kinking n18.

weakest step: step 3 — Step 3 carries forward the ~145-165 MPa hard-orientation yield stress from step 2, but the service-performance evidence is about the soft [0001] orientation yielding at only 65-80 MPa via kinking, so the numerical and orientation link between the two steps is only nominal rather than a direct continuation.

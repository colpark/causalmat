ANSWER 1
The combination does not establish a mechanistic account for the downstream capacity-fade/service-capability observation.

F3d shows the I(q) curves for OCV, 0.45V, 0.136V, 0.11V, 0.02V, and 3V nearly superimposed across the full q-range, with only a modest separation in the ~0.03–0.1 Å⁻¹ region at the two lowest-voltage states (0.11V, 0.02V) — consistent with the "small drop" described. F3c's 2D contour (paired with the first discharge/charge trace) likewise shows smooth, largely vertical banding with no abrupt feature onset. F2d shows severe capacity fade (870 → 480 → ~70 mAh/g by cycle 40) despite rising Coulombic efficiency.

What this justifies: during the first discharge/charge, SAXS-visible nanostructure (pore/particle scale, q ≈ 0.01–0.4 Å⁻¹) is essentially stable, with at most a minor low-q perturbation near full discharge — i.e., no gross nanoscale structural collapse is detected by SAXS in cycle 1.

What it leaves open: SAXS was only recorded operando for the first cycle, while the dramatic capacity loss (identification/service-capability decline) unfolds over 40 cycles, so this data cannot identify the cause of the long-term fade. It also cannot rule out mechanisms outside SAXS sensitivity (SEI growth, sub-resolution pulverization, irreversible Na trapping). The mechanism behind the observed capacity fade is not identified by this evidence.

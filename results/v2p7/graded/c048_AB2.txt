ANSWER 1
All four panels viewed.

F3a: bare BST — small, smooth, fairly uniform spherical nanoparticles, densely packed, scale 500 nm. F3b: BST-SH — particles appear slightly coarser/rougher and somewhat larger, more irregular clustering than bare BST. F3e: BST@Ag5% — particles are visibly rougher-surfaced, larger agglomerated clumps, consistent with a coating layer on the BST cores. Together these three SEM panels support statement A: a clear morphological progression from smooth bare particles to rougher, coated, larger modified particles.

F5a: DSC traces show a Tg-region step for each material — Pure PAEN 176°C, BST@Ag1% 146°C, BST@Ag3% 149°C, BST@Ag5% 157°C — confirming statement B exactly: all BST@Ag composites show Tg well below neat PAEN, with a modest monotonic increase in Tg as Ag loading rises from 1% to 5%, but none reaching the neat-polymer value.

Does the upstream (SEM) evidence account for the downstream (DSC Tg) observation? Only partially. The SEM panels establish that BST particle surfaces are physically altered (roughened, coated, enlarged) by silanization and Ag deposition — supporting that BST@Ag is a structurally distinct filler from bare BST, which is a plausible precondition for altered polymer-filler interaction. But the SEM images shown are of the fillers themselves (dry particle powders), not composite cross-sections or particle dispersion within the PAEN matrix, so they do not directly visualize filler-matrix interfacial contact, dispersion state, or agglomeration inside the polymer — the mechanistic link needed to explain why Tg drops below neat PAEN at every loading, or why Tg partially recovers (146→149→157°C) as Ag content increases. What the combination supports: successful, dose-progressive surface modification of BST co-occurring with a suppressed-but-loading-dependent Tg. What it leaves open: the causal mechanism (plasticization, reduced crystallinity, disrupted chain packing, or interfacial/Ag-specific effects) driving the Tg suppression and its partial recovery — that requires composite-level microstructural evidence (e.g., matrix cross-section SEM/TEM, dispersion mapping) not present in these four panels.

ANSWER 2
The combination partially accounts for the downstream Tg/value observation but does not fully explain it. SEM (F3a bare BST, ~70 nm smooth spheres; F3b silane-modified BST-SH, rougher/larger; F3e BST@Ag5%, further roughened/coated agglomerates) establishes that surface modification and Ag decoration measurably change particle morphology and presumably interfacial chemistry relative to bare filler. DSC (F5a) shows every composite (BST@Ag 1/3/5%) has a substantially depressed Tg (146, 149, 157 C) relative to pure PAEN (176 C), a ~20-30 C drop. Together these support the qualitative claim that introducing surface-modified/coated filler particles into the PAEN matrix disrupts chain packing or interfacial constraint enough to lower Tg versus the neat polymer — i.e., particle-level surface modification is consistent with, and can plausibly drive, the bulk thermal property shift.

What it leaves open: the SEM series is not graded across 1%, 3%, 5% Ag (only bare, silane-modified, and 5% are imaged), so there is no direct microstructural counterpart to the 146→149→157 C trend, including why Tg rises again from 1% to 5% rather than continuing to drop. SEM also images dry particles, not dispersion within the cured polymer matrix, so filler distribution/agglomeration inside the composite — plausibly relevant to the non-monotonic Tg trend — is not directly evidenced.

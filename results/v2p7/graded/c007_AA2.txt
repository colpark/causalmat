ANSWER 2
The upstream porosity data (F4a) and downstream strength data (F6a) account for the observation only partially, and only for one of three systems.

S1: porosity collapses sharply between 1500-1600°C (7.5%→0.4%), and flexural strength jumps in the same window (188→465 MPa). This co-occurrence supports the standard porosity-controls-strength picture for S1 — densification and strengthening track together in temperature and magnitude.

S2 and S3 break that link. S2's porosity falls to 1.2-0.7% by 1650-1700°C — as low as S1's — yet strength stays at only 145-170 MPa, far below S1's 465 MPa; low porosity here does not produce high strength. So the combined evidence supports a porosity-strength correlation for S1 alone, coincident with its steep densification transition. It does not support porosity as the general explanatory variable across S1/S2/S3, since S2 and S3 both decouple porosity from strength in opposite directions. What is left open: which other microstructural factor (grain size/bonding phase/composition, not addressed by these two panels) drives S1's strength jump and suppresses S2's strength despite low porosity, and why S3 tolerates high porosity without strength loss.

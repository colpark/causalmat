F3c (2D SAXS contour) shows the color/intensity bands running essentially straight (vertically uniform) across time/voltage in both the low-q and mid-q panels, and F3d (overlaid I(q) curves for OCV, 0.45V, 0.136V, 0.11V, 0.02V, 3V) shows the curves closely superimposed, with only a modest, localized separation in the ~0.03–0.1 Å⁻¹ region for the two lowest-voltage curves (0.11V, 0.02V) — matching claim B.

Together this justifies: the bulk crystal structure and nanoscale porosity/microstructure are largely preserved through the first (dis)charge, with only a modest, reversible-looking crystalline-fraction decrease and a small, deep-discharge-specific porosity/microstructure perturbation — not a wholesale phase transformation or structural collapse.

Left open: XRD/SAXS here cannot quantify the actual phase fraction lost, distinguish amorphization from crystallite-size/strain effects as the cause of intensity loss, identify the mechanism behind the small SAXS feature (pore filling vs. contrast change vs. alloying), or establish cycle-life/irreversibility since only the first cycle is shown.

ANSWER 2
Panels viewed: F3c (SAXS 2D contours, two q-windows, plus voltage/time trace), F3d (SAXS I(q) log-log curves at OCV, 0.45V, 0.136V, 0.11V, 0.02V, 3V), F4b (stacked SXRD 2θ waterfall, 16-21°, with 0.1C voltage/time trace — peaks near ~19.3° and ~16.3° persist across the full discharge/charge with visible but modest intensity attenuation).

Combined, the SXRD waterfall (F4b) and SAXS panels (F3c, F3d) support a limited, complementary claim: SXRD shows the same Bragg reflections persisting through the full cycle with only modest intensity loss, consistent with the crystalline oxide phase being largely retained (matching the earlier-settled phase result) rather than a phase fraction collapsing or converting wholesale. SAXS (F3c contours staying essentially straight/unbanded; F3d curves overlapping across states) indicates no large-scale change in nanoscale electron-density contrast — i.e., no major porosity or microstructural development — through most of the voltage range, with only a small, localized deviation at ~0.03–0.1 Å⁻¹ appearing at the deepest-discharge states (0.11V/0.02V). Together this supports that bulk phase retention is accompanied by an essentially intact nanostructure/porosity, with at most a minor, discharge-state-specific microstructural perturbation.

This leaves open: neither technique quantifies phase fraction (SXRD gives qualitative peak persistence, not a converted/retained percentage) or porosity in absolute terms (SAXS gives relative I(q) changes, not pore size/volume). The origin of the small SAXS deviation (new interface, minor pore formation, surface roughening) is unidentified, and no causal link between the modest SXRD intensity loss and the SAXS deviation is established — they are co-observed near the same voltage window, not shown to be mechanistically connected. Length scales differ (SXRD: lattice; SAXS: ~nm features), so this cannot resolve microstructure changes below SAXS's sensitivity.

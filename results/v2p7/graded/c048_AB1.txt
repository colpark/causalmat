ANSWER 1
The panels are consistent with the stated observations but do not by themselves establish a causal mechanism.

F3a shows bare BST as fine, relatively uniform, smooth-textured agglomerated particles; F3b (BST-SH) already looks somewhat coarser/rougher; F3e (BST@Ag5%) shows a visibly rougher, more granular, larger-scale surface texture consistent with claim A (coating and apparent size/roughness increase upon modification). F5a confirms claim B exactly as stated: pure PAEN shows a Tg step at 176 C, while BST@Ag composites show lower, monotonically increasing Tg with Ag loading (146, 149, 157 C for 1/3/5%).

What the combination supports: the SEM series and the DSC series are each internally consistent — particle surface state changes with modification (bare → SH → Ag-coated), and thermal behavior of the composite differs systematically from neat polymer and shifts with Ag content. Taken together they support that incorporating modified BST particles alters the polymer's Tg relative to pure PAEN, and that the degree of surface modification (Ag%) tracks with the size of that Tg shift.

What is left open: the SEM images show only particle/aggregate morphology, not particle dispersion within the polymer matrix or any direct interfacial evidence. Nothing in these panels demonstrates the physical mechanism (plasticization, chain confinement, filler-matrix interaction) linking surface roughness/coating to the Tg depression — that link is inferred, not shown. No mechanical/PRP or value data are visible in these crops to assess.

ANSWER 2
The SEM series (F3a bare BST, F3b BST-SH, F3e BST@Ag5%) shows a progressive increase in particle-surface roughness and apparent coating from smooth ~70 nm particles to visibly coated, agglomerated ones. DSC (F5a) shows all BST@Ag composites (146, 149, 157 C) sit below pure PAEN's Tg (176 C), and among the composites Tg rises monotonically with Ag loading (1%→3%→5% = 146→149→157 C).

The combination supports only a directional co-occurrence: as surface modification/coating becomes more pronounced (SEM) and as Ag content rises, the matrix Tg among the composites also rises (DSC) — i.e., both the roughening/coating trend and the Tg trend move the same way with increasing Ag loading. It also supports that particle modification is associated with a depressed Tg relative to neat polymer.

It leaves open several things: the SEM panels shown are only three discrete points (bare, SH-functionalized, Ag5%), not matched one-to-one to the Ag1%/Ag3%/Ag5% samples used in DSC, so there is no direct morphological image at 1% or 3% loading to confirm the Tg steps track particle roughness continuously. The evidence also cannot separate whether the Tg trend is driven by surface coating/roughening itself, by Ag content, by particle dispersion, or by interfacial adhesion, since these co-vary by construction. No mechanism or causal link between morphology and Tg is established — only that both trends move in the same direction with Ag loading.

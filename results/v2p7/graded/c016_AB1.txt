THE QUESTION
Does the upstream evidence account for the downstream observation about STR/chemistry/bonding / PRP/value? Say what the combination supports and what it leaves open.

ANSWER 1
Panel A (FTIR/GATR) shows new bands in NO-PPAm not present in plain PPAm: a starred NO vibration, N=N stretch, N-N stretch, and N-O stretch (plus a second N-N stretch feature at lower wavenumber). This is static, before/after chemical fingerprint evidence that NO loading created new nitrogen-oxide bonding motifs on the polymer, consistent with formation of NO-donor (diazeniumdiolate-type) functional groups. Panel B (chemiluminescence) shows NO flux rising to a peak of ~35×10⁻¹⁰ mol cm⁻² min⁻¹ around 5 min, then decaying toward ~0 by 60 min.

The combination supports the qualitative claim that the STR/chemistry (new N=N/N-N/N-O/NO bonds formed only after loading) is consistent with a material that can subsequently release NO — i.e., the bonded nitrogen-oxide species detected by FTIR are chemically the kind that decompose to liberate NO, matching the observed burst-then-decay flux. It does not, by itself, quantitatively account for the release profile: FTIR is a single before/after snapshot with no time axis, no measure of bond density or loading amount, and no information on which specific bond(s) decompose during release or why the decay follows that particular burst kinetics (mass-transport limited vs. bond-depletion limited). So the two panels together justify "NO-related bonds form on loading and NO is later released," but leave open the mechanistic/quantitative link between bond identity/amount and the shape or duration of the flux curve.

ANSWER 2
Both panels match their captions and the qualifications already on record. F2a (GATR-FTIR) shows the red NO-PPAm trace carrying starred peaks labeled "NO Vibration," "N=N Stretch," "N-N Stretch," "N-O Stretch" (twice) that are absent from the blue PPAm trace, which instead shows only -OH, -NH stretch, C-H stretch, and -NH vibration bands. F2b (chemiluminescence) shows NO flux rising sharply to ~35 x10^-10 mol cm^-2 min^-1 near 5 min and decaying to near-baseline by ~60 min.

Together, the two measurements support that NO-PPAm contains new nitrogen-oxygen chemical species not present in PPAm (F2a) and that this NO-PPAm material releases a burst of NO gas that decays over about an hour (F2b). This combination is consistent with the interpretation that the FTIR-detected N-O/N=N/N-N adducts are the chemical reservoir from which the measured NO flux is released -- i.e., NO was chemically bound into the coating and is subsequently liberated.

What this leaves open: neither panel establishes a causal or stoichiometric link between the specific FTIR bands and the flux curve -- there is no measurement here tying loss of a particular IR peak to the release kinetics, no post-release FTIR to confirm the N-O species diminish as NO is released, and no control ruling out alternative NO sources (e.g., trapped/physisorbed NO, other byproducts) contributing to the flux. The FTIR band labels also remain generic (not wavenumber-specific), so the exact chemical identity of the reservoir is not pinned down. The pairing is therefore consistent with "chemically stored NO is released over time" but does not prove it.

ANSWER 1
Both panels match the stated numbers exactly. F2a (relative density) shows the PS curve dropping from ~85.5% (0 wt%) to ~78.4% (1), ~70.7% (3), and ~70.0% (5), while the HP curve stays flat near 98–100% across all AlN levels. F2b (Vickers hardness) shows the PS curve falling from ~12.0 GPa to ~9.1, ~7.1, and ~6.8 GPa over the same AlN series, while HP hardness stays flat near 18–20 GPa.

What the combination supports: for pressureless-sintered (PS) ZrB2–30vol%SiC, increasing AlN additive is associated with a monotonic decline in both relative density and Vickers hardness, and the two declines track each other point-for-point (density drop from 0→5 wt% is ~15.5 percentage points; hardness drop is ~5.2 GPa, roughly 43% relative loss). This co-occurrence is consistent with a porosity-mediated link between processing (AlN addition under pressureless sintering) and mechanical property (hardness) — i.e., the density figure supplies a plausible microstructural pathway (retained porosity) for the hardness drop. The flat HP (hot-pressed) curves in both panels show the effect is specific to the PS route, not an intrinsic AlN-hardness relationship, since applied pressure suppresses the density loss and hardness loss together.

What it leaves open: these two plots are density and hardness only — no direct microstructure/porosity micrographs, pore-size distribution, or grain-structure images were provided to confirm porosity (versus phase change, grain growth, or a distinct AlN-related mechanism) as the actual mediating cause. Correlation across the shared AlN axis does not establish that density loss mechanically causes the hardness loss (as opposed to both being independently driven by the same processing deficiency). No PRP or "value" data appear in these panels; that portion of the downstream observation is not addressed by this evidence and cannot be evaluated here.

What this justifies: within the PS series, the density drop and the hardness drop co-vary tightly across the full 0-5 wt% AlN range (not just at one endpoint), consistent with reduced relative density (i.e., greater porosity) accompanying reduced hardness as AlN increases. Combined with the earlier PS0-vs-HP0 SEM finding (lower density = more/larger pores, fewer/smaller necks), this pairing gives a plausible physical story: increasing porosity with AlN plausibly degrades hardness.

What it leaves open: this is a correlation between two macroscopic property curves, not a mechanistic or imaging-based demonstration that porosity specifically drives the hardness loss at 1, 3, or 5 wt%. No SEM/microstructure evidence exists at those compositions, so alternative or additional contributors (AlN reaction phases, grain-size changes, neck geometry beyond what porosity alone implies) cannot be ruled out. The porosity-hardness causal link therefore remains inferred, not directly observed, above 0 wt%.

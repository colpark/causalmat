ANSWER 1
Partially, and only suggestively. F4c (EDS map) shows C (blue) and Co (red) concentrated at particle edges/surfaces against a Mg-rich (green) bulk in the C10 alloy — evidence that the CQCo additive segregates to particle surfaces rather than dispersing through the bulk. F9a/F9b (JMAK plots, ln[-ln(1-a)] vs ln t) show good linearity (R²>0.98) across four temperatures for both compositions, with Avrami exponent η = 1.59 vs 1.32.

Combination supports: it is physically plausible that a surface-concentrated catalytic phase (C, Co) alters nucleation/growth character during desorption enough to shift η downward — a lower exponent is often read as fewer effective nucleation sites or lower-dimensionality growth, consistent with a phase sitting at surfaces/edges rather than penetrating the particle.

It leaves open: no EDS map for the undoped (C0) sample is given, so there is no direct microstructural contrast to explain why C0 gives η=1.59; the surface-localization image and the kinetic fit are never linked mechanistically in the panels themselves — only a shared trend. Also the F9a caption ("+10 wt.% CQCo") suggests that panel may actually be the doped case, creating ambiguity about which η value pairs with which composition. So: surface segregation is documented, but its causal link to the specific Avrami-exponent change is not established here.

ANSWER 2
Conclusion the combination supports: EDS (F4c) shows C and Co co-located on C10 particle surfaces, densest at edges/faces, and prior HRTEM work already corroborates that this surface signal corresponds to an actual carbon phase (curved carbon layers) abutting alloy grains at one particle edge. This is consistent with, and gives a plausible microstructural basis for, a carbon(+Co)-coated surface in the C10 composite that could alter hydrogen desorption pathways relative to uncoated C0. So it is reasonable to say the upstream microstructural picture provides a qualitative rationale for why C0 and C10 might show different desorption kinetics.

What it leaves open: the EDS/HRTEM evidence does not quantitatively connect the carbon/Co surface distribution to the specific JMAK/Avrami exponents (η = 1.59 for C0 vs 1.32 for C10, both R²>0.98). The exponent shift implies a change in nucleation/growth dimensionality or rate-limiting mechanism, but nothing in the EDS map or the single HRTEM edge region measures diffusion pathways, nucleation sites, or growth geometry — it only shows where C/Co sit at one snapshot location. No Co-containing lattice fringe was reported, so atomic-scale Co dispersion in carbon (needed for a catalytic-mechanism argument) is unconfirmed. The mechanistic link between "carbon coats the surface" and "Avrami exponent drops" is inferred, not demonstrated, by this evidence.

ANSWER 1
Yes, the upstream DFT evidence is consistent with the downstream H2-evolution trend, and the match is close. Ranking by |ΔG_H*| (distance from the Sabatier-optimal zero): Pd0.75Ag0.25 (0.08) < Pd0.5Ag0.5 (0.12) < Pd0.25Ag0.75 (0.28) < Pd (0.31) < Ag (0.49). This tracks the measured H2 rate order almost exactly: Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS (125.4) > Ag0.5Pd0.5-ZIS (57.3) > Ag0.75Pd0.25-ZIS (43.5) > Pd-ZIS (34.5) > Ag-ZIS (3.2), and the same ordering appears in the time-resolved H2 evolution curves (F8a), where Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS pulls furthest ahead over 5 h. So the combination supports the claim that alloying Pd with a modest Ag fraction shifts hydrogen adsorption free energy toward the thermoneutral optimum, and that this shift correlates with, and plausibly explains, the observed activity trend across all five compositions as HER cocatalyst.

What it leaves open: the DFT is computed on bare, idealized Pd/PdAg/Ag(111) slabs in isolation, not on the actual ZIS-supported nanoparticles under visible-light illumination. It says nothing about light absorption, charge separation/transfer from ZIS to the alloy, particle size/dispersion, or interfacial contact — factors that also govern measured rates. Correlation across five points is suggestive of the ΔG_H* mechanism but does not, on its own, exclude these confounds or prove causation.

What it leaves open: the DFT panel's "CoFeCrOOH" is not labeled with the same 5:3:2 stoichiometry used in the LSV panel (Co5Fe3Cr2, Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5), so the two panels are not guaranteed to describe the identical composition — the pathway/performance link rests on a name-level, not a stoichiometry-matched, correspondence. Also, DFT free energy alone does not account for other factors (conductivity, active surface area, real defect structure) that jointly determine measured overpotential, so causal attribution of the LSV ranking solely to the computed ΔG trend is not established by this evidence.

Both panels verified by direct image read.

F1a (DFT free energies): x-axis = ΔG(Co2+→Co3+), y-axis = ΔG(Co3+→Co4+). CoCrOOH sits lowest on the x-axis (~1.05 eV, lowest Co2+→Co3+ barrier). Does upstream account for downstream? The combination supports a thermodynamic rationale: CoFeCrOOH's intermediate (neither best-nor-worst) position on both elementary-step free energies is consistent with a mechanistic story for why incorporating both Fe and Cr into the ternary oxyhydroxide (as in Co5Fe3Cr2) could balance the two OER-relevant steps and improve activity relative to unmodified Co(OH)2. It leaves open several things: (1) F1a's species are qualitative labels (CoCrOOH, CoFeOOH, CoFeCrOOH, Co(OH)2), not the specific stoichiometry Co5Fe3Cr2 tested in F5c, so the exact correspondence is inferred, not shown directly; (2) IrO2 is not a DFT panel entry at all, so the LSV comparison to IrO2 has no computed counterpart; (3) the DFT is a static thermodynamic calculation, not conducted under the neutral-electrolyte condition of the LSV experiment, so it cannot by itself explain kinetics, η10 values, or the specific neutral-pH performance ordering. The evidence supports a plausible compositional/thermodynamic trend consistent with the activity trend, but does not establish a direct causal or quantitative link between the computed intermediate energetics and the observed neutral-electrolyte η10 advantage over IrO2.

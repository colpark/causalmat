THE QUESTION
Does the upstream evidence account for the downstream observation about PRP/value / PRP/value? Say what the combination supports and what it leaves open.

ANSWER 1
Both panels viewed.

F5a: Co2+ release rises with dose and time. At every timepoint 5Co releases more Co2+ than 2Co (day1: 8.9 vs 4.7; day3: 17.9 vs 10.5; day7: 20.8 vs 16.9 mg/L); 0Co stays at zero.

F8: VEGF is far higher for 2Co-MBG (~430 pg/mL) than for blank (~60), plain MBG (~100), or 5Co-MBG (~115), with P<0.05 brackets for 2Co vs each. 5Co-MBG's VEGF sits close to the blank/MBG baseline, with no significance bracket shown between 5Co and blank/MBG.

Does the upstream evidence account for the downstream VEGF result? Only partially, and not in the simple direction one might expect. If more Co2+ release straightforwardly drove more VEGF, 5Co (which releases more Co at every timepoint) should out-produce 2Co, but it does not — VEGF at 5Co falls back near baseline. So the combination supports a non-monotonic (inverted-U-type) relationship between Co dose/release and VEGF stimulation, consistent with a stimulatory window at lower Co exposure (2Co) that is lost or reversed at higher exposure (5Co), rather than a linear dose-response.

Left open: why the 5Co response collapses (no cytotoxicity/viability data in these two panels to test a toxicity explanation), the exact Co2+ concentration at which the switch occurs, whether VEGF was measured at a soak time matched to the ion-release curve, and any direct causal (vs. correlational) test linking ion release to VEGF secretion.

ANSWER 2
Both images reviewed.

F5a (Co2+ release into medium) shows a clear dose- and time-dependent pattern: 0Co stays at 0 throughout; 2Co-MBG rises from ~4.7 (day 1) to ~10.5 (day 3) to ~16.9 mg/L (day 7); 5Co-MBG rises faster and higher, from ~8.9 to ~17.9 to ~20.8 mg/L. So at every timepoint, 5Co releases more Co2+ than 2Co, and both increase monotonically with time.

F8 (VEGF secretion) shows a non-monotonic pattern across the same Co-dose series: Blank ~60, MBG ~100, 2Co-MBG ~430 (significantly higher than blank, MBG, and 5Co, each P<0.05), and 5Co-MBG ~115 — statistically indistinguishable from blank/MBG, i.e. back near baseline.

What the combination supports: the ion-release data establish that Co2+ exposure is graded and higher in 5Co than 2Co at every timepoint measured, so the VEGF result is not confounded by 5Co delivering less cobalt — it delivers more. The pairing therefore supports a biphasic/non-monotonic dose-response: a moderate, sustained Co2+ level (2Co regime) stimulates VEGF secretion, while a higher Co2+ level (5Co regime) does not sustain that stimulation despite greater ion availability.

What it leaves open: these two panels give no mechanism for why higher Co2+ fails to elevate VEGF at 5Co — e.g., whether higher Co2+ becomes cytotoxic, saturates or desensitizes the HIF-1α/VEGF pathway, or whether some other Co-dependent factor intervenes. Neither panel reports cell viability, timing alignment between the two assays, or a dose-response curve at intermediate Co levels, so the exact threshold and cause of the drop-off from 2Co to 5Co remain undetermined from this evidence alone.

ANSWER 1
The combination is suggestive but not conclusive. The rough co-occurrence of "resistance rises, then plateaus" with "capacity fades, then slows" is consistent with charge-transfer impedance growth contributing to the capacity decline, and the fact that Rfilm/Re stay flat while only Rct rises points toward a charge-transfer/kinetic origin rather than film or electrolyte resistance.

What this leaves open: the two panels are not shown to be from the same cell/run, and F5d's condition (w/HCl vs another synthesis) is not stated explicitly, so a direct causal link is not established. The timing is only loosely matched — capacity falls fastest in the first 20-50 cycles while Rct's steepest rise is stated through cycle 150. The near-100% CE argues against major continuous side-reaction loss, but doesn't rule out active-material or structural degradation as a co-contributor. Large Rct error bars weaken precision of the trend. No statistical or mechanistic test tying Rct directly to capacity is presented.

ANSWER 2
Panels confirm the described curves. The two curves share a qualitative shape — most rapid change early, slowing/plateauing later — so the timing is broadly consistent with charge-transfer resistance growth (and the associated metal dissolution) contributing to the early, steep phase of capacity fade. That is what the combination supports: co-occurrence of the fast-Rct-rise window and the fast-capacity-fade window.

It leaves open several things. First, the match is only approximate: Rct is still rising through cycle 150 while capacity fade has already slowed well before that; after cycle 150, Rct is roughly flat (large error bars) but capacity keeps declining slowly, so plateaued Rct cannot account for the continued slow fade. Second, near-100% CE throughout argues against gross parasitic/crossover loss but does not distinguish resistance growth from active-material loss as the fade driver. Third, as before, Rct is a lumped fit not attributable to V versus Fe, and no direct mechanistic link (only timing overlap) is shown between resistance growth and capacity loss.

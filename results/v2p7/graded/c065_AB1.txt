ANSWER 1
The EDS map (F4c, C10 alloy) shows C (blue) and Co (red) concentrated on particle surfaces/edges, over a Mg-rich (green) bulk — consistent with a surface-decorating catalyst rather than a bulk-dissolved phase. The kinetics panels (F6a = C0, F6b = C10) show the second, diffusion-controlled absorption stage shortening from 66 min (C0) to 11 min (C10) at 360 °C, i.e., a ~6x acceleration when the C@Co additive is present.

Together this supports a plausible mechanistic story: a surface-located catalyst (C/Co) could lower the barrier for hydrogen dissociation/diffusion into the particle, shortening the diffusion-controlled stage. The spatial coincidence of catalyst with particle surfaces is at least consistent with a surface-catalysis explanation for faster uptake.

What it leaves open: the EDS map is static, single-sample (C10 only) micrography with no C0 comparison, so it cannot itself demonstrate that surface catalyst presence — rather than some other C10-vs-C0 difference — causes the kinetic change; it is correlative, not a direct diffusion measurement. It also gives no quantification of catalyst areal coverage, uniformity, or particle-to-particle variability, and no direct evidence linking the imaged C/Co sites to reduced diffusion barrier or nucleation rate. So the combination supports a coherent surface-catalysis narrative but does not establish causation.

ANSWER 2
Panels reviewed: F6a (Sieverts curves for Mg90Ce5Y5, i.e. C0, at 360C — 66 min diffusion-controlled 2nd stage, slow rise to ~5 wt% over ~180 min), F6b (same alloy +10 wt% C@Co, i.e. C10 — 11 min diffusion-controlled stage, near-saturation by ~15-20 min to ~4.7-5.2 wt%), F4c (EDS map of C10 showing Mg(green) particle bodies with C(blue) and Co(red) signal concentrated along particle edges/surfaces, as described).

What the combination supports: C10 particles carry a surface-concentrated C/Co layer (per EDS), and C10 shows a markedly shorter diffusion-controlled absorption stage (11 min) than the undoped C0 alloy (66 min) at the same temperature. Together these two measurements support a correlation consistent with the surface C/Co phase acting as a short-diffusion-path/catalytic layer that speeds the diffusion-limited stage of hydrogenation — i.e., the location of the additive (surface/edge coating) is compatible with a role in accelerating hydrogen uptake kinetics.

What it leaves open: this is a two-sample comparison (C0 vs C10), not a dose-response or mechanistic study, so it cannot separate whether C, Co, or their combination drives the kinetic change, nor rule out other differences between the alloys (e.g., particle size/milling effects) as contributors. EDS was only reported for C10, not C0, so there is no direct surface-map contrast between the two conditions. No diffusion coefficient, activation energy, or quantified relationship between coating coverage/thickness and absorption rate is given, so the link between "surface location" and "kinetic improvement" remains qualitative/associative, not a demonstrated causal or quantitative mechanism. The EDS map's known limits also apply (coarse spatial resolution, only C10's overall particle population, not proof the coating is uniform or representative at fine scale).

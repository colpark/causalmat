ANSWER 1
Partial support, with a clear gap at the level of detail needed.

F5d/F5e (ELISA) show total fibrinogen and its gamma chain roughly halved on all three functionalized coatings (BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, BVLD/NO-PPAmF, ~0.05–0.065) versus bare 316LSS/PPAmF (~0.10–0.15), with a single *** bracket spanning controls vs. the functionalized group — the three functionalized surfaces are not shown as significantly different from one another. F6b/F6c show a corresponding qualitative pattern at the coarse level: 316LSS and PPAmF tubes are fully occluded by dark thrombus (high fibrinogen, worst clotting), matching the ELISA. So the upstream reduction in fibrinogen adsorption/activation does account for the broad control-vs-functionalized contrast in thrombus burden.

What it leaves open: within the functionalized group, the photographs show a gradient — BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are only partly open/partly clotted, while BVLD/NO-PPAmF is nearly clean (lumen and foil largely free of clot). The fibrinogen ELISA does not resolve this gradient, since BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF are statistically indistinguishable in fibrinogen level. So the fibrinogen-adsorption evidence explains why functionalized surfaces beat bare metal, but not why the dual (BVLD/NO) surface outperforms either single-agent surface macroscopically — that synergy is not accounted for by this measurement.

ANSWER 2
Panels reviewed: F5d, F5e (ELISA bar charts) and F6b, F6c (photographs of tube cross-sections/lumens).

What the combination supports: F5d/F5e confirm that all three functionalized coatings (BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, BVLD/NO-PPAmF) cut total fibrinogen and γ-chain roughly in half versus 316LSS/PPAmF, with overlapping error bars among the three — i.e., statistically similar suppression, and BVLD/NO is not the lowest of the three. So the ELISA data account for the categorical difference: functionalized vs. bare-metal thrombus burden.

What it leaves open: The ELISA data do not account for the graded difference seen among the three functionalized coatings themselves. F6b/F6c show BVLD/NO-PPAmF with a nearly clean lumen and foil, while BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF alone are only partly open/still show clot — a visible superiority for the combination that has no counterpart in the fibrinogen measurements, which are indistinguishable across the three. So fibrinogen/γ-chain suppression appears necessary but not sufficient to explain the dual-coating's extra benefit; some other mechanism (e.g., platelet activation, other clotting factors) not captured by these two panels must be invoked, and that remains unaddressed by the cited evidence.

ANSWER 1
Panel A (FTIR/GATR) shows new bands in NO-PPAm not present in plain PPAm: a starred NO vibration, N=N stretch, N-N stretch, and N-O stretch (plus a second N-N stretch feature at lower wavenumber). This is static, before/after chemical fingerprint evidence that NO loading created new nitrogen-oxide bonding motifs on the polymer, consistent with formation of NO-donor (diazeniumdiolate-type) functional groups. Panel B (chemiluminescence) shows NO flux rising to a peak of ~35×10⁻¹⁰ mol cm⁻² min⁻¹ around 5 min, then decaying toward ~0 by 60 min.

The combination supports the qualitative claim that the STR/chemistry (new N=N/N-N/N-O/NO bonds formed only after loading) is consistent with a material that can subsequently release NO — i.e., the bonded nitrogen-oxide species detected by FTIR are chemically the kind that decompose to liberate NO, matching the observed burst-then-decay flux. It does not, by itself, quantitatively account for the release profile: FTIR is a single before/after snapshot with no time axis, no measure of bond density or loading amount, and no information on which specific bond(s) decompose during release or why the decay follows that particular burst kinetics (mass-transport limited vs. bond-depletion limited). So the two panels together justify "NO-related bonds form on loading and NO is later released," but leave open the mechanistic/quantitative link between bond identity/amount and the shape or duration of the flux curve.

ANSWER 2
Both panels confirm the two evidence claims. F2a: red (NO-PPAm) spectrum shows starred bands for NO vibration (~1900 cm-1), N=N stretch, N-N stretch, N-O stretch (~1550-1450 cm-1 cluster), and a further N-N stretch (~950 cm-1); none of these stars appear on the blue PPAm baseline spectrum, which only shows -OH, -NH, C-H, and -NH vibration bands. F2b: NO flux rises to a peak of ~35x10^-10 mol cm-2 min-1 at about 5 min, then decays through the 10-30 min range and is essentially flat near zero by 50-60 min, matching claim B exactly.

What the combination supports: the new nitrogen-oxide bonds (NO, N=N, N-N, N-O) appear only after NO loading, so PPAm is chemically converted into an NO-donor adduct; the release-profile panel shows this loaded material subsequently discharges NO with a fast burst-then-decay kinetic. Together the panels support that NO loading creates new chemical bonds and that the loaded material is capable of releasing NO over roughly an hour, i.e., the loading chemistry plausibly accounts for the material being an NO donor at all.

What is left open: the FTIR does not by itself prove which specific bond (e.g., a particular diazeniumdiolate vs. nitrosamine species) is the one that decomposes to release NO, there is no time-resolved FTIR tracking band loss during the 60-min release window, no quantification of total NO loaded versus released, and no replicate/error data in either panel. So a correlative link between the two panels is visually reasonable, but a mechanistic, bond-specific causal chain from a named bond to the observed flux curve is not demonstrated by these two panels alone.

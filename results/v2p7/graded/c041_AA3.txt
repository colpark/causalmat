THE QUESTION
Does the upstream evidence account for the downstream observation about STR/microstructure/distribution / PRF/service_capability? Say what the combination supports and what it leaves open.

ANSWER 1
F5e confirms the aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 particles' chemical identity: C, Zn, and N (inherited from the ZIF-8 framework) fill the particle outline in both the DNA and RNA aptamer samples, while P — attributable to the aptamer's phosphate backbone — appears only as sparse dots within that same outline, consistent with a low but real aptamer loading co-located on/in the framework. F6b shows a downstream functional readout: aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 produces brighter (green, higher on the 0–2500 scale) ex vivo lymph-node fluorescence than the non-aptamer BSA@ZIF-8 control (cyan) in both inguinal and axillary nodes, for both subcutaneous and footpad injection. Together these support that the material actually injected and imaged was verified to carry the aptamer, and that aptamer-bearing particles are associated with greater lymph-node accumulation than the neat/BSA control — i.e., structural identity plus a correlated in vivo outcome. What the combination does not establish is mechanism: the EDS map is static, dry-particle elemental data and cannot show that the same sparse P/aptamer signal persists in vivo or is what drives node accumulation (versus size, charge, or EPR-type effects). There is also no quantification linking P density (aptamer loading) to fluorescence intensity, and the fluorescence images are representative single examples with the color scale saturating information, so reproducibility/statistics across animals are not shown here. In short: identity of the aptamer-functionalized particle and a qualitative advantage in node targeting are both supported; a causal, dose-dependent link between the aptamer loading seen in F5e and the enhanced accumulation seen in F6b is not.

ANSWER 2
Both panels viewed directly.

F5e (EDS mapping): For both aptamer constructs (anti-CCL21 DNA aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 and A10 RNA aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8), C, Zn, and N signals densely fill the particle outline (elements inherited from the ZIF-8/BSA scaffold), while P appears only as sparse, discrete dots confined to the same outline. Because P has no other source in this system, its presence (even sparse) is read as evidence that aptamer (which carries a phosphate backbone) is physically associated with the particle.

F6b (ex vivo fluorescence): Inguinal and axillary lymph nodes from mice given the aptamer-MOF show a brighter, more green signal than nodes from mice given the neat/control MOF (BSA@ZIF-8, cyan-shifted, lower on the shared 0-2500 scale), consistent across both subcutaneous and footpad injection routes.

What the combination supports: EDS establishes that the aptamer is chemically present on/in the particle (a precondition for any aptamer-specific targeting), and the fluorescence data show a qualitative difference in lymph-node accumulation between aptamer-bearing and neat MOF particles. Together they are consistent with the aptamer contributing to enhanced lymph-node delivery.

What is left open: the EDS map does not quantify aptamer loading, surface density, orientation, or bioactivity — sparse P dots only confirm presence, not "how much" or whether that amount is sufficient to drive targeting. The fluorescence panel is representative imaging, not quantified/statistically compared, and does not control for probe/fluorophore differences between the two constructs. So the upstream EDS evidence supports that aptamer is present on the particle but does not, by itself, establish that this presence causally accounts for the brighter downstream lymph-node signal — that mechanistic/causal link (STR/microstructure → distribution/service_capability) remains inferred, not demonstrated, by these two panels alone.

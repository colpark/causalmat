ANSWER 2
Yes, but only as a correlational, not mechanistic, account. The XRD (F3a) shows that milling in 10 wt% C@Co does not change the bulk phase identity of the Mg90Ce5Y5 matrix: the same reflections (CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17, Mg) appear in both the 0% and 10% patterns, with the only addition being a weak Co line near 44.5°. The PCT curves (F11a, F11b) show a single flat plateau per temperature for both compositions, at essentially the same pressures (~0.15, 0.27, 0.45 MPa across 320-360 °C). Together these support the conclusion that the plateau pressure — a thermodynamic property tied to which hydride/dehydride phases are forming — is set by the unchanged matrix phase assemblage, and that C@Co addition does not introduce a new bulk phase or shift the underlying thermodynamics; it behaves as a minor, likely surface/catalytic addition.

What is left open: the data do not demonstrate a causal mechanism linking phase identity to plateau pressure (no per-phase pressure assignment is given); the weak Co line does not rule out solute incorporation below XRD detection limits; and although pressures track closely, the panels show small differences at 360 °C that are not quantified or discussed, so exact equivalence is not established, only near-equivalence.

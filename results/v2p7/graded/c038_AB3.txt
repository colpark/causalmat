ANSWER 1
The three panels support the upstream summary and, by extension, a downstream reading of "little change" in phase/fraction and microstructure/porosity, but only qualitatively.

F4b (the stacked line-scan pattern, despite its "Na/PCO cell" caption) shows the ~19.3° reflection continuously present across the full 0.1C discharge/charge trace, weakening somewhat but never vanishing — consistent with claim A (SXRD peaks persist, modest intensity loss). F3c's 2D SAXS contour is smooth and vertically banded (no abrupt color breaks) across both the low-q (0.02–0.08 Å⁻¹) and higher-q (0.1–0.4 Å⁻¹) windows, i.e. contours stay "nearly straight" as claimed. F3d shows the six voltage-state I(q) curves overlapping almost everywhere, with a visible small separation of the 0.11 V and 0.02 V (magenta) traces from the rest in roughly the 0.03–0.1 Å⁻¹ range — matching claim B exactly.

Together this justifies: no new/collapsing crystalline phase and no gross change in scattering-length-density contrast (pore/particle structure) occurs on cycling — arguing against a large phase-fraction swing or major porosity development. What it leaves open: the panels give no quantitative phase-fraction or porosity numbers, no explanation for the small mid-discharge SAXS deviation (particle rearrangement vs. minor void formation vs. electrolyte ingress), and being bulk/ensemble-averaged techniques, neither rules out local/heterogeneous microstructural change.

ANSWER 2
Panels viewed: F4b (XRD waterfall, 16-21° 2θ, ~32 stacked traces vs. time/voltage) shows the ~19.2° reflection persisting across the full discharge/charge with only modest intensity change — this is the visual basis for claim A. F3c (2D SAXS contour, log-q from 0.02 to 0.4 Å⁻¹ vs. time/voltage) shows essentially flat, vertically-banded contours with no new streaks or intensity fronts developing during cycling. F3d (overlaid I(q) curves at OCV, 0.45V, 0.136V, 0.11V, 0.02V, 3V) shows near-total overlap of all six curves across the measured q-range, with the curves at 0.11V/0.02V sitting slightly below the others only in the mid-q window (~0.03-0.1 Å⁻¹) — this is claim B.

What the combination supports: the invariant SAXS contours/curves (no new low-q upturn, no shift in the mid-q shoulder, near-complete overlap) indicate that PCO's nanoscale electron-density contrast — i.e., its particle/pore-scale microstructure — is not measurably reorganized during sodiation, in the same window where SXRD shows the crystalline lattice itself is retained. Together they support that both the crystal phase and the nanoscale microstructure/porosity of PCO stay essentially unchanged through cycling, i.e., no major amorphization-driven pore formation, particle cracking, or phase-separated domain growth accompanies the modest XRD intensity loss.

What it leaves open: neither measurement quantifies porosity or phase fraction. The small SAXS intensity dip at 0.03-0.1 Å⁻¹ confined to the two most-reduced states (0.11V, 0.02V) is not explained — it could reflect a minor, localized nanostructural change (e.g., slight pore/domain nucleation) coincident with the discharge end, or simply a contrast/normalization artifact; SAXS alone cannot attribute it to porosity versus particle-scale effects, and nothing here ties it quantitatively to the "modest" XRD intensity loss. So the claim that structure AND microstructure are both largely retained is supported qualitatively but not mechanistically or quantitatively.

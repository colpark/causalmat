ANSWER 2
The upstream evidence does not account for the downstream VEGF observation — in fact it is in tension with it.

Upstream (settled): Co2+ release is monotonic with embedded Co content at every timepoint, 5Co > 2Co > 0Co (Fig. 5a, confirmed by direct read: 5Co curve sits above 2Co curve at days 1, 3, 7; 0Co flat at zero).

Downstream (Fig. 8, confirmed by direct read): VEGF secretion is non-monotonic. 2Co-MBG (~430 pg/mL) is far above blank (~60), MBG (~100), and, critically, above 5Co-MBG (~115), with P<0.05 for 2Co vs each comparator including 5Co. So the group releasing more Co2+ (5Co) produces VEGF at roughly blank/MBG background level, while the group releasing less Co2+ (2Co) produces the peak response.

What the combination supports: only that "more Co released" cannot be the whole story for VEGF induction — a simple monotonic dose-response from Co2+ concentration to VEGF secretion is contradicted by these two panels taken together. The release data explain the exposure gradient across groups but not the biological outcome, since the outcome peaks at an intermediate dose and falls at the highest dose.

What is left open: the mechanism for the drop at 5Co (e.g., a biphasic/hormetic Co2+ effect, possible cytotoxicity or altered cell behavior at the higher concentration, or a different pathway threshold) is not addressed by either panel and would require separate evidence (e.g., viability or signaling data) to resolve.

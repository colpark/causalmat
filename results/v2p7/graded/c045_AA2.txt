ANSWER 1
Yes, the upstream evidence plausibly accounts for the downstream CE result, but only qualitatively.

F3d/F3e show a clear microstructural contrast: CnC HS (d) has a largely flat, low-relief surface with only a few short protrusions, while C HS (e) is covered edge-to-edge by a dense felt of sharp, interlocking platelets — exactly matching the SEM description in A. F4a shows CnC HS (red) holding ~80–100% CE out to ~200+ cycles, while C HS (black) tracks similarly at first but drops to 0% CE near cycle 40 and again near 48, with no data beyond ~50 cycles — matching B.

Combined, this supports the standard mechanistic story: the flatter CnC HS deposit gives more uniform, lower-surface-area Zn plating with fewer sharp asperities, consistent with more stable, higher CE cycling, whereas the dense sharp-platelet morphology on C HS is consistent with conditions favoring dendrite/short-circuit-type failure and abrupt CE collapse. This is a correlational pairing of morphology and one CE trace at one condition (1 mAh cm⁻², 4 mA cm⁻²) — it does not by itself establish that morphology causes the CE drop, does not identify the failure mechanism at cycles 40/48 (short circuit vs. dead Zn vs. electrolyte depletion), and covers only a single cycling condition and a single pair of representative SEM images, not statistics across replicates.

Sample size (single cell/condition) and reproducibility are not shown here. So the combination supports a correlation between deposit morphology and CE-based service capability, but does not by itself establish the causal mechanism connecting platelet density to the abrupt CE failure.

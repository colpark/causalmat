ANSWER 1
Yes, the upstream FTIR evidence plausibly accounts for the downstream thermal observation, but only as a chemistry precursor, not a direct explanation of the Tg trend across silver loading.

F2a shows new peaks at 2930/2864 (C–H, thiol-linked alkyl chain) and 1122 cm⁻¹ appearing uniquely in the BST-SH trace, confirming successful surface thiolation of BST particles (matches measurement A exactly). This establishes that BST-SH carries a distinct organic/thiol surface chemistry relative to bare BST and BST-OH — the kind of surface functionalization commonly invoked to justify altered filler–matrix interfacial bonding.

F5a (DSC) shows Tg drops sharply from 176 C (pure PAEN) to 146 C at 1% Ag loading, then rises with further Ag loading (149 C at 3%, 157 C at 5%), all remaining well below the neat polymer. This is consistent with the filler (functionalized BST-SH-type particle route) disrupting polymer chain packing/free volume relative to neat PAEN, with some recovery of Tg as Ag content increases.

What is supported: the FTIR confirms the thiol-functionalized filler chemistry exists and differs from unfunctionalized BST, providing a plausible chemical basis for altered polymer–filler interaction. What is left open: F5a's samples are labeled BST@Ag at 1/3/5%, not BST-SH directly, so the panels do not show a direct A→B causal chain — no panel here demonstrates that the specific thiol bonding is the mechanism producing the Tg depression-then-partial-recovery pattern with silver content. The link between "thiol surface chemistry" and "Tg vs. Ag%" trend is inferential, not shown by these two panels alone.

ANSWER 2
Partial support, with a real gap in the chain.

What F2a (FTIR) shows: BST-SH alone carries new bands at 2930, 2864 cm⁻¹ (C–H stretches) and 1122 cm⁻¹, absent from both BST and BST-OH spectra. This is direct evidence that the thiol surface treatment introduced new organic/sulfur-bearing functional groups on the particle surface — i.e., successful surface functionalization, distinct in chemistry from the untreated or hydroxylated particle.

What F5a (DSC) shows: pure PAEN has Tg = 176 °C; BST@Ag composites in PAEN show Tg = 146, 149, 157 °C at 1, 3, 5% Ag — all depressed relative to neat polymer, with a rising trend as Ag loading increases from 1% to 5%.

Does the combination connect them? Only loosely. The FTIR evidence is measured on bare BST/BST-OH/BST-SH particles, not on the BST@Ag/PAEN composites that DSC characterizes — there is no FTIR spectrum tied to the specific Ag-loading series, and no Ag-content-dependent chemistry is shown at all. So FTIR supports that surface chemistry (thiol grafting) was successfully altered, which is a plausible precursor for changed filler–matrix interfacial bonding, but it does not itself explain the composite Tg depression, nor the non-monotonic-looking recovery from 146→149→157 °C with increasing Ag%. Whether BST-SH is the actual filler surface used in the BST@Ag composites, and why Tg rises with Ag loading, remain open — the two panels are not run on the same sample series.

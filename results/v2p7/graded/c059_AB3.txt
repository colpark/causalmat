ANSWER 2
The combination is consistent with the downstream degradation ranking but does not establish it. In F4b, absorbance among the co-doped samples in the visible region tracks Ce0.2 > Ce0.3 > Ce0.1 > TiO2 (TiO2's edge falls off sharply near 410 nm while all three co-doped curves extend well past it, with Ce0.2 sitting highest). That ordering matches F8's degradation ranking at every timepoint: Er0.5Ce0.2 (~46%) > Ce0.3 (~41%) > Ce0.1 (~31%) > TiO2 (~20%). So the upstream optical data (itself grounded in EDS-confirmed Er/Ce presence) is at least correlationally compatible with the photocatalytic performance ordering — greater visible-light absorption co-occurring with greater MO degradation is what one would expect if extended absorption were contributing to activity.

What this leaves open: F4b and F8 are separate measurements (optical vs. catalytic assay), so correlation in ranking is not evidence of a causal mechanism connecting them — the qualitative edge-position comparison never yields a quantitative band gap, and no photocurrent, radical-trapping, or surface-area data here isolates absorption as the driver of degradation versus other factors (crystallinity, surface area, charge-carrier separation). Also, since Er and Ce are co-present, the UVVIS/assay pairing still cannot attribute the effect to one dopant over the other.

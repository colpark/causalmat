Panel B shows NO flux rising sharply to a peak of ~35×10-10 mol cm-2 min-1 around 5 min, then decaying toward baseline by ~60 min, matching claim B's burst-then-decay description.

Together, the two panels support a coherent story: new chemical bonds (N-N/N-O/NO) formed only upon NO loading (A), and that loaded material releases NO in a burst that decays over an hour (B). What it leaves open: the FTIR alone does not prove the new bonds are the actual NO-donor species responsible for the release kinetics — no time-resolved FTIR, no quantitative bonding-to-flux correlation, and no control (e.g., partial loading or intermediate time points) linking bond loss to flux decay is shown. So causal linkage (bonding change directly drives the release profile) is plausible but not demonstrated by these panels alone.

So the pairing supports "chemical incorporation enables sustained NO release" as a qualitative, correlational claim, not a mechanistic or quantitative one.

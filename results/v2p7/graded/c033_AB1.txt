ANSWER 2
Both panels confirm the stated data. F5d: Re flat ~0.5 ohm, Rfilm flat ~2-3 ohm (peaking near 3-4), Rct rises steeply from ~3 ohm at cycle 0 to ~17-20 ohm by cycle 60, continues climbing to ~26 ohm at cycle 150, then plateaus at ~24-28 ohm with large error bars through cycle 250. The combination only partially accounts for the capacity fade. The two curves overlap in their early, steepest-change window (0-50/60 cycles), where both Rct and capacity loss are moving fastest, which is consistent with growing interfacial resistance contributing to reduced accessible capacity. But the shapes diverge afterward: Rct keeps rising substantially from cycle 60 to 150 before flattening, while capacity fade has already decelerated by ~50 and flattens earlier and more gradually. So growing Rct cannot explain the front-loaded sharpness of the initial capacity drop (which occurs while Rct is still comparatively low), and it is not clear it is needed to explain the long slow tail either, since CE~100% throughout indicates no ongoing parasitic/Faradaic loss that would track resistance growth directly.

What this leaves open: no mechanistic link is demonstrated, only rough co-occurrence in the early window; the post-150 "flat" Rct carries large error bars; and other unmeasured processes (e.g., structural degradation, early SEI/active-material loss) remain candidates for the initial steep fade. Cannot rule in or out causation from this evidence alone.

F7a (tensile strength bar chart): confirms claim B exactly — 79.3 MPa (pure PAEN), 78.3 MPa (BST@Ag 1%), 74.4 MPa (3%), 73.5 MPa (5%), a monotonic decline with increasing filler content.

Does the upstream evidence account for the downstream observation? Partially. The SEM at 1% loading, showing no interfacial gaps and matrix coverage of particles, is consistent with the very small strength drop from pure PAEN (79.3) to 1% Ag (78.3) — good adhesion would be expected to preserve most of the matrix's load-bearing capacity, and that is what is observed. This supports a link between interfacial bonding quality and retained tensile strength at low filler content.

What it leaves open: no SEM image is provided for the 3% or 5% Ag samples, so the further, larger decline in strength (74.4, 73.5 MPa) has no corresponding interfacial micrograph in this pair — it is not shown whether debonding, gaps, or agglomeration appear at higher loading. The single 1% SEM image is also qualitative (no quantitative interfacial-adhesion or void-fraction metric), so it cannot by itself establish that interface quality, rather than other factors (e.g., particle agglomeration, stress concentration, filler stiffness mismatch), is the mechanism behind the strength trend across all four compositions. The evidence supports the low-loading part of the story but does not fully account for the continued decline at higher loadings.

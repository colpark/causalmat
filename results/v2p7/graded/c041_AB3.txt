F6b shows a downstream functional readout: aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 produces brighter (green, higher on the 0–2500 scale) ex vivo lymph-node fluorescence than the non-aptamer BSA@ZIF-8 control (cyan) in both inguinal and axillary nodes, for both subcutaneous and footpad injection. Together these support that the material actually injected and imaged was verified to carry the aptamer, and that aptamer-bearing particles are associated with greater lymph-node accumulation than the neat/BSA control — i.e., structural identity plus a correlated in vivo outcome. What the combination does not establish is mechanism: the EDS map is static, dry-particle elemental data and cannot show that the same sparse P/aptamer signal persists in vivo or is what drives node accumulation (versus size, charge, or EPR-type effects). There is also no quantification linking P density (aptamer loading) to fluorescence intensity, and the fluorescence images are representative single examples with the color scale saturating information, so reproducibility/statistics across animals are not shown here. In short: identity of the aptamer-functionalized particle and a qualitative advantage in node targeting are both supported; a causal, dose-dependent link between the aptamer loading seen in F5e and the enhanced accumulation seen in F6b is not.

ANSWER 2
The two measurements are complementary but operate at different scales, so they only partially account for each other.

What is supported: EDS (F5e) shows P confined within the same C/Zn/N particle outline for both DNA- and RNA-aptamer samples, consistent with the earlier-established conclusion that anionic aptamer is associated with the cationic BSA@ZIF-8 particle.

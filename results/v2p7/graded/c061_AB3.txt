Together, this supports only a temporal correlation: the interval (5→10 cycles) over which the t-ZrO2 signature weakens is the same interval over which corner spallation first appears, consistent with a link between tetragonal-phase degradation and onset of local coating loss/service-capability decline.

What it leaves open: the XRD trace is not shown to be taken from the spalled corner specifically, so there is no spatial co-registration proving the phase change occurred at the failure site rather than elsewhere on the coupon. Causation (phase change driving spallation, vs. both being consequences of a third process) is not established, only coincidence in cycle number.

ANSWER 2
Panels checked: F9 (XRD after 5 and 10 cycles, insets showing t-ZrO2 marker peaks near 43° and ~73-74°, more intense in the 5-cycle trace than the 10-cycle trace) and F8 (photos at 0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40 cycles: coupon intact through 5 cycles, corner spallation appearing by 10 cycles and persisting/growing through 40 cycles).

Other plausible drivers of spallation (thermal-mismatch stress, sintering, bond-coat oxidation) are also unaddressed here. Conclusion: co-occurrence at early cycles only, not a causal or quantitative account of the spallation trajectory.

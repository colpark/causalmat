ANSWER 1
The upstream thermal-imaging evidence (F3e, F3g) establishes only that BNs-BSA's photothermal heating under 808 nm is dose-dependent: 6-min spot temperature rises with nanoparticle concentration (200>100>50>25 ug/mL, water inert) and with laser power density (0.5→1.5 W at 200 ug/mL), reaching ~55-60°C at the highest settings. This supports that the material has a real, tunable photothermal conversion capacity (PRP) sufficient in principle to reach ablative temperatures under 808 nm alone.

It does not, however, account for the downstream tumor-curve result in F5d. There, BNs-BSA+808nm (single wavelength, group 5) shows regrowth in 4/5 tumors after ~day 14, while BNs-BSA+Lasers (combined 660+808 nm, group 6) holds all 5 tumors flat at 0. Both thermal panels image only single-wavelength (808 nm) heating at varied dose/power in vitro/ex vivo spot tests — they contain no imaging or temperature data for 660 nm alone or the 660/808 combination, and no in-tumor temperature trace over the ~20-day treatment course. So the heating dose-response justifies that 808 nm photothermal effect is real and scalable, but leaves open why the single-wavelength regimen fails to sustain suppression while the dual-wavelength regimen does — that durability/efficacy gap (service_capability of the combined treatment) is not addressed by this thermal-imaging evidence.

ANSWER 2
Upstream (F3e, F3g): 6-min IR imaging confirms BNs-BSA is a genuine photothermal agent under 808 nm — spot temperature rises with both concentration (25→200 ug/mL) and power density (0.5→1.5 W), with water staying cold. This establishes photothermal capability (PRF) and that heating scales with dose/power (PRP/value dependence).

Downstream (F5d): BNs-BSA+808nm alone produces an initial tumor response (volumes near zero through ~day 10-14) but 4/5 tumors regrow by day ~14-22; only the dual-wavelength BNs-BSA+660/808nm arm keeps all 5 tumors suppressed through day 20+.

What the combination supports: the IR data justify that 808nm irradiation of BNs-BSA does generate a real, dose/power-graded thermal effect, which is consistent with the initial tumor shrinkage/stasis seen in the 808nm-alone curves — i.e., photothermal heating is a plausible driver of the early suppression phase.

What it leaves open: the IR measurements are short (6 min), ex vivo/in-solution spot readings, not in vivo tumor-core temperatures at the actual treatment settings, so they cannot establish whether the heat generated was sufficient, or sufficiently sustained, to fully ablate tumors. Nothing in the thermal panels explains why 808nm-alone treatment fails in 4/5 mice after day 14 or why adding 660 nm (F5d group 6) prevents regrowth — that requires a mechanistic link (e.g., depth penetration, incomplete ablation margin, dual-wavelength synergy) not shown in these three panels.

The three panels do support a link between the DFT hydride-binding energetics and the measured photocatalytic rates, but only as a qualitative, descriptor-level correlation, not a demonstrated causal chain.

F9a shows ΔG_H* nearest zero for Pd0.75Ag0.25 (-0.08 eV), with Pd (-0.31) too strong-binding and Ag (0.49) too weak-binding, and the two intermediate alloys spaced monotonically between. F8b/F8a show Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS (same composition as Pd0.75Ag0.25, just written in reverse order) as the fastest H2 evolver (125.4 µmol/h, and the steepest time-course), with Ag0.5Pd0.5 and Ag0.75Pd0.25 next, and Pd and Ag alone slower — an ordering consistent with a volcano relationship where near-zero ΔG_H* favors HER activity.

What this combination supports: the composition with the most thermoneutral computed hydrogen adsorption energy is also the experimentally fastest H2-evolving cocatalyst, and the alloy series' rate ranking tracks reasonably with distance from ΔG_H*=0.

What it leaves open: the DFT is on isolated bare metal (111) facets, not the actual Ag-Pd/ZIS heterojunction, so it says nothing about charge transfer from ZIS, particle dispersion, or interface effects that also govern the measured rate; only five points are compared with no error bars or replicates; and no quantitative kinetic model bridges eV differences to the observed rate magnitudes — correlation, not proven causation.

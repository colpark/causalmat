The three panels do support a coherent upstream-to-downstream story, but only qualitatively and only by parallel ranking, not by direct causal linkage.

F5a (SEM, platelets in PRP, 2h) shows the same rank order as F6b/F6c (whole-blood circulation, 2h): 316LSS and PPAmF have abundant spread, dendritic (activated) platelets and match photographs of tubes and foils fully filled with dark, adherent thrombus; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF show fewer, less-spread platelets and correspondingly partially open lumens/less clot; BVLD/NO-PPAmF combined shows mostly round, unspread platelets and near-clean lumen and foil. This concordant ranking across an isolated-platelet assay and a whole-blood macroscopic thrombus assay supports the inference that reduced platelet adhesion/activation on the drug-eluting surfaces is consistent with, and plausibly contributes to, the reduced downstream thrombus burden.

What it leaves open: the SEM data come from PRP alone, so they cannot establish that platelet behavior is sufficient or necessary to explain the whole-blood outcome — coagulation-cascade activity, fibrin formation, and red-cell entrapment (all visible as bulk in the dark clots) are not measured or excluded. No quantitative platelet counts or statistics accompany F5a, and only one timepoint (2h) is shown, so the SEM-to-thrombus link is correlational and qualitative, not a demonstrated causal mechanism.</message>

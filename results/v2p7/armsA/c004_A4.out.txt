Both panels were opened and inspected directly.

F3c (upstream, PRP/value): A/L at 808 nm vs. concentration is linear (R2=0.9999, slope alpha=6.4 L g-1 cm-1) — a clean Beer-Lambert calibration established in a controlled solution/cuvette measurement, confirming that absorbance (and by extension photoacoustic signal, which derives from absorbed light) tracks agent concentration monotonically and quantitatively over the tested range.

F4f (downstream, PRF/service_capability): tumor PA signal after IV injection rises from 0.26 (0h) to a peak of 0.94 at 4h, then declines to 0.31 by 10h, consistent with the printed values.

Combination supports: the calibration in F3c justifies treating the tumor PA signal time-course as a proxy for the relative amount of agent present in the tumor, so the 0-1-2-4-6-10h trace is reasonably read as accumulation peaking near 4h post-injection followed by clearance/washout, not merely instrument noise or a non-monotonic optical artifact.

What it leaves open: F3c's calibration was done in a homogeneous solution, not tissue — tissue scattering, depth, and heterogeneity are not addressed, so the PA values in F4f cannot be converted into absolute intracellular/tissue concentrations. Neither panel establishes the accumulation mechanism (EPR vs. targeting) or links concentration to any therapeutic/service outcome (e.g., photothermal efficacy) — only imaging signal is measured.

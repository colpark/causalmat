All three panels viewed directly.

F5a (SEM, PRP 2h): 316LSS and PPAmF show numerous spread, dendritic platelets with pseudopodia; BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF show markedly fewer platelets, mostly small and round/unspread. F6b (tube-lumen macro photos, 2h circulation): 316LSS and PPAmF lumens are filled with dark thrombus; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are partly open; BVLD/NO-PPAmF is nearly clean. F6c (cross-sections) shows the same ordering — heavy dark/red thrombus coating on 316LSS/PPAmF, progressively less on the modified surfaces, least on BVLD/NO-PPAmF.

What this combination supports: the three readouts rank the five surfaces consistently — the same surfaces that show low platelet adhesion/activation in the static PRP assay also show the least occlusive thrombus and cleanest lumens/cross-sections in the dynamic circulation model. This is a coherent, reproducible correlation across an in vitro platelet measure and an ex vivo whole-blood thrombosis outcome, consistent with platelet adhesion/activation being one contributor to the downstream thrombus/patency result.

What it leaves open: causation is not established — PRP is a static, isolated-platelet assay, while F6b/c reflect dynamic whole-blood flow with coagulation, plasma proteins, and possibly complement also acting; no quantitative thrombus mass/area or platelet counts are given (all three panels are qualitative images); and sample size/reproducibility across replicates is not shown here. So the evidence supports association, not a demonstrated mechanistic pathway from platelet spreading to thrombus occlusion.

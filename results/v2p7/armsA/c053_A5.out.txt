What the combination supports: the SEM evidence is consistent with, and can partly explain, why the drop from pure PAEN to 1% Ag is small (79.3→78.3 MPa) — the interface at that loading shows no gaps, i.e., no gross debonding that would sharply degrade load transfer.

What it leaves open: the SEM image covers only the 1% composition, not 3% or 5%, so there is no direct micrographic evidence for the larger strength drops at those higher loadings (74.4 and 73.5 MPa). The data alone cannot attribute even the small 1%-loading decrease specifically to interfacial cause versus other factors (e.g., filler rigidity, local stress concentration, minor agglomeration) since the SEM shows adhesion quality, not a quantified stress-transfer mechanism. Whether interfacial quality degrades progressively with Ag content, tracking the strength decline, is not shown by these two panels.

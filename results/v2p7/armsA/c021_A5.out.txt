Upstream ELISA (F5D total fibrinogen, F5E gamma-chain) shows all three functionalized coatings — BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF — sit at roughly half the value of 316LSS/PPAmF, with the significance bracket (***) drawn between the bare pair and the functionalized trio as a group; there is no significant separation among the three functionalized bars, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF is not the lowest (in D it is marginally highest of the three, with the widest error bar).

Downstream, the macro photos (F6B tube lumens, F6C thrombus on foil) show a clear gradient: 316LSS and PPAmF are solidly clotted; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are only partly open/partly clot-covered; BVLD/NO-PPAmF alone is essentially clean.

Combination supports: reduced fibrinogen adsorption/activation is a plausible shared mechanism explaining why all three functionalized coatings resist thrombus better than bare 316LSS/PPAmF — the ELISA and photo evidence agree on that binary contrast.

What it leaves open: the ELISA gives no basis for the graded difference among the three actives themselves. Since BVLD/NO-PPAmF's fibrinogen/gamma-chain values are statistically indistinguishable from (or slightly above) the single-agent coatings, the visibly superior patency of the dual coating is not accounted for by this fibrinogen assay and must arise from some other, unmeasured mechanism (e.g., combined effects on platelets or coagulation further downstream).

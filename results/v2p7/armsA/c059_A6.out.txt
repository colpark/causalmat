Panels checked: F4b (UV-Vis, panel b) and F8 (degradation kinetics).

What the combination supports: The upstream UV-Vis data (F4b) show that all three Er0.5Ce-doped TiO2 samples (Ce0.1, Ce0.2, Ce0.3) have absorption edges shifted well past 420 nm and higher visible-region absorbance than the sharp ~410 nm edge of bare TiO2. Downstream, F8 shows all three doped samples degrade MO faster than bare TiO2 at every timepoint (20/40/60 min), with TiO2 lowest (~20% at 60 min) and all doped variants higher (31-46%). This much is well accounted for: extended visible-light absorption from co-doping plausibly explains why doped catalysts outperform undoped TiO2.

What is left open: The claim "Ce0.2 absorbs most" is not clearly borne out by F4b itself — the three doped curves (Ce0.1 navy, Ce0.2 red, Ce0.3 yellow) are closely overlapping across the visible tail (450-800 nm), with Ce0.3 (yellow) if anything sitting slightly above Ce0.2 in places, and no clean monotonic separation matching the degradation ranking. So the specific non-monotonic activity order in F8 (Ce0.2 > Ce0.3 > Ce0.1 > TiO2) is not fully explained by absorbance magnitude alone from this panel. The evidence supports "co-doping broadens visible absorption and improves activity over pure TiO2" but does not by itself justify why Ce0.2 is the activity optimum rather than Ce0.3; that would require additional structural/electronic evidence (e.g., recombination, defect density, crystallinity) not present in these two panels.

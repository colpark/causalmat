The combination partially accounts for the downstream observation but does not fully explain it. F9 shows the t-ZrO2 peaks (~43 deg split, ~73-74 deg split) are already present and essentially unchanged in shape/position between 5 and 10 cycles — only modest intensity differences, no new monoclinic-ZrO2 peaks or phase-transformation signature appear between these two time points. F8 shows the coupon is visually intact at 5 cycles but has spalled corners by 10 cycles, with spalling persisting and growing only slightly out to 40 cycles.

So the upstream XRD evidence (A) establishes that the zirconia in the coating remains predominantly tetragonal at both 5 and 10 cycles — it does not show a t-to-m phase transformation coinciding with the onset of spallation. This supports ruling out a bulk tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation as the trigger for the 5-to-10-cycle corner spallation event, since no corresponding phase change is detected. However, XRD alone cannot explain the mechanical/geometric cause of spallation (e.g., thermal-stress mismatch, interfacial defects, corner stress concentration) — that mechanism is left open by this evidence, as is why spallation localizes at corners and plateaus after 10 cycles rather than propagating further.

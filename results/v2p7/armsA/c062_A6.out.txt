Findings after viewing F4b, F4c, and F8.

F4b/F4c: SEM micrographs of the YSZ crack network in one field, captioned "before thermal cycles" and "after five thermal cycles." The crack pattern (fine interconnected microcracks plus a few larger dark pores/clusters) looks essentially the same in both panels — consistent with observation A (unchanged crack network at 5 cycles).

F8: Six photographs of an Al2O3-YSZ coated coupon at 0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40 cycles. At 0 and 5 cycles the coating is intact and uniform. By 10 cycles a corner has visibly spalled (dark/exposed substrate at the edge), and that spalled region persists and grows slightly through 20, 30, 40 cycles — matching observation B.

What the combination supports: the SEM field shows that whatever microcrack network exists in the bulk coating does not visibly change between 0 and 5 cycles, and this is consistent with (does not contradict) the macro-photo finding that the coupon is still visually intact at 5 cycles — i.e., no macroscopic damage has yet appeared at the point where the SEM comparison stops. So the two pieces of evidence are compatible in timing: unchanged microstructure at 5 cycles pairs with no spallation at 5 cycles.

What it leaves open: the SEM comparison only spans 0→5 cycles and is taken from a general/representative field, not specifically the corner region where spallation later occurs at cycle 10. The evidence therefore does not show what the microstructure looks like at 10+ cycles, nor whether corner-localized crack coalescence (rather than the field-average network shown) is what triggers spalling. No causal mechanism linking the imaged crack network to the corner-specific spallation onset and growth is established — only a temporal non-contradiction at the single early time point both were sampled.

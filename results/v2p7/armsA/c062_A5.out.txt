Findings from the panels:

- F4b/F4c: SEM images of the same field before cycling and after 5 thermal cycles. The crack network (fine cracks plus dark porosity/spots) looks essentially the same in both — no visible new cracking or coarsening is apparent at 5 cycles.
- F8: Macro photographs at 0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40 cycles. The coupon is visually intact (uniform white coating, sharp corners) at 0 and 5 cycles. At 10 cycles a corner has spalled (dark substrate exposed); by 20–40 cycles the spalled region has grown somewhat but the rest of the coating remains largely intact.

What the combination supports: for the 0→5 cycle interval, the microstructural (SEM) and macroscopic (photograph) records agree — neither shows damage. This is consistent with, and supports, a picture in which early-stage cycling (through 5 cycles) causes no detectable microcracking or spallation; the coating's crack density/network is stable and the macroscopic state matches (intact).

What it leaves open: the SEM comparison stops at 5 cycles, before spallation begins, so there is no microstructural imaging bridging to the 10-cycle onset of corner spallation or its slow growth to 40 cycles. The mechanism by which spallation initiates at a corner — and whether it correlates with local crack-network changes not captured in this (likely interior/bulk) SEM field — is not addressed. So the evidence justifies "no micro/macro damage through 5 cycles" but cannot account for, or rule in/out any specific cause of, the corner-spallation damage that appears afterward.

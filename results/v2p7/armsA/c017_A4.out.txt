Panels viewed directly: F5a (SEM, platelets after 2h PRP), F6b (macro photos of tube lumens after circulation), F6c (photos of longitudinally-cut thrombus).

What I see: F5a shows a consistent gradient across the five surfaces in the same left-to-right order — numerous large, spread dendritic platelets on 316LSS and PPAmF; fewer, still mostly spread platelets on BVLD-PPAmF; fewer and partly round/unspread on NO-PPAmF; very few, small, mostly round unspread platelets on BVLD/NO-PPAmF. F6b and F6c show the same ordering downstream: 316LSS and PPAmF lumens/cross-sections are filled with dark thrombus; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are partially open/thinner thrombus with visible clean foil; BVLD/NO-PPAmF is largely clean/reflective with minimal red material.

This combination supports the claim that the surfaces producing less platelet adhesion/spreading in static PRP also produce less thrombus under blood circulation, and that the reduction tracks a consistent rank order across three independent, qualitatively concordant readouts (SEM, gross lumen photos, cross-section photos) for the same five materials.

It leaves open: whether platelet spreading is causally responsible for the thrombus difference versus both being downstream of another shared factor (e.g., NO/BVLD release affecting coagulation independently); no quantitative counts/statistics are shown, only qualitative/visual comparison; and the PRP static assay and the flowing-blood circulation model are different conditions, so correspondence between them is associative, not demonstrated as mechanistic.

Micrographs b–f (increasing SiCp content, with d/e/f captioned 15/20/25 wt%) show dark SiC particles distributed across the whole matrix at every content level, with local clustering visible in all panels and clustering/particle density visibly increasing from b to f (b and c look more dilute and more homogeneous; d–f show denser, more contiguous dark regions). The UCS series rises monotonically (~310→363 MPa) as SiCp content increases.

What the combination supports: the micrographs establish that the microstructural change accompanying the UCS increase is a rising areal fraction of SiC particulate in the Mg matrix, i.e., the strength gain co-occurs with, and is spatially consistent with, more particulate reinforcement being present at every field of view — supporting a straightforward particle-content–to-strength association across this series.

What it leaves open: the images alone do not prove particulate content is the mechanism driving the UCS rise (versus grain refinement, porosity change, or interfacial effects not shown here); they cannot quantify particle volume fraction per panel to match against the numeric UCS values; and the visible local clustering (present at every wt%) is not evaluated for whether it helps, hinders, or is neutral to strength — so a clustering-driven ceiling or scatter in UCS at higher loadings cannot be ruled out or confirmed from these panels.

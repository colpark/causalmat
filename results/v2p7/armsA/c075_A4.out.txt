Both panels were opened and inspected directly.

F5d (EIS) shows Rct climbing sharply from ~3 to ~26 Ω between cycle 0 and 150, then plateauing (noisily, ~24–28 Ω with wide error bars) out to 250 cycles, while Rfilm (~2–3 Ω) and Re (~0.5 Ω) stay essentially flat throughout. F2a (GCD) shows the "w/ HCl" trace (red) losing capacity steeply over the first ~20–50 cycles and then declining slowly to ~55 mAh/g by cycle 250, with coulombic efficiency pinned near 100% the whole time.

What the combination supports: the timing/shape correspondence — most of the impedance growth and most of the capacity fade both occur early (roughly the first 50–150 cycles) followed by a slower, near-plateau phase — is consistent with charge-transfer resistance growth (not electrolyte or surface-film resistance, which are flat) being a plausible contributor to the fade. High CE argues against bulk side-reaction/irreversible-capacity loss as the main driver, pointing instead toward a kinetic/impedance-limited mechanism.

What it leaves open: neither caption states that the EIS cell and the "w/HCl" GCD cell are the same sample/run, so the correspondence is a temporal correlation across panels, not a demonstrated causal or same-cell link. Mechanism behind the Rct rise (e.g., SEI/interface change, particle isolation) is not specified, and quantitative coupling between Ω increase and mAh/g loss is not established.

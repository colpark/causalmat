Yes, within limits. The DFT free-energy panel (F9a) gives |ΔG_H*| deviations from thermoneutral (0 eV) of 0.08 (Pd0.75Ag0.25), 0.12 (Pd0.5Ag0.5), 0.28 (Pd0.25Ag0.75), 0.31 (Pd), 0.49 (Ag). This ranking by closeness to zero tracks almost exactly the measured H2-evolution rates in F8b/F8a: Ag0.25Pd0.75-ZIS (same composition as the −0.08 case, just written reversed) is fastest at 125.4 µmol/h and shows the steepest cumulative curve in F8a, followed by Ag0.5Pd0.5-ZIS (57.3), Ag0.75Pd0.25-ZIS (43.5), Pd-ZIS (34.5), and Ag-ZIS slowest (3.2). So the combination supports a Sabatier/volcano-type account: cocatalyst compositions with H* adsorption energy nearest thermoneutral give the highest measured HER rates, and the two metal extremes (too strong binding on Pd, too weak on Ag) are the poorest performers — a coherent, monotonic correspondence across all five points.

What it leaves open: the DFT calculations are for bare AgPd(111) alloy slabs, not for the actual ZIS-supported nanoparticle cocatalysts tested; no structural/compositional characterization (e.g., particle size, real surface composition, interface coupling with ZIS) is shown to confirm the modeled surfaces match the synthesized material. So the evidence supports ΔG_H* as a plausible thermodynamic descriptor correlating with rate, but does not independently rule out interfacial charge-transfer, light-absorption, or dispersion differences as contributing or confounding causes of the ZIS-composite rate trend.

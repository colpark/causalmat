Both panels were opened and verified byte-consistent with the stated values. Panel a (HP, red curve): ~97.5% (0 wt%), ~100% (1 wt%), ~99.3% (3 wt%), ~100% (5 wt%). Panel b (HP, red curve): ~18.0 GPa (0), ~20.3 GPa (1), ~19.4 GPa (3), ~19.2 GPa (5). These match items A and B exactly.

What the combination supports: the rise in HP relative density from ~97.5% to ~100% between 0 and 1 wt% AlN tracks the hardness jump from 18.0 to 20.3 GPa — consistent with porosity elimination (fewer/smaller pores) raising hardness over that interval. That much is directly supported by the paired curves.

What it leaves open: from 1 to 5 wt% AlN, relative density stays essentially flat at 99–100% (even nominally highest at 5 wt%), yet hardness monotonically falls (20.3 → 19.4 → 19.2 GPa). Density/porosity therefore cannot account for this later decline — the downstream drop in hardness at 3 and 5 wt% must trace to some other microstructural change (e.g., AlN phase distribution, grain size, secondary-phase effects) that these two panels do not show. So the density-porosity story explains the 0→1 wt% hardness gain but not the 1→5 wt% hardness loss; that mechanism is unresolved by this figure pair alone.

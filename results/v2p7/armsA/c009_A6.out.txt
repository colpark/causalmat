F10's Arrhenius fit (Ea = 130.3 kJ/mol for C0 vs 81.9 kJ/mol for C10, over 1000/T ≈ 1.58–1.81, i.e. ~280–360 °C) is derived from rate constants k extracted from the very desorption curves shown in F8a (C0) and F8b (C10) — it is not an independent measurement. Within that constraint, the two panels are consistent with each other and with claim B: F8a shows C0 needing ~150–450 min to finish desorbing across 300→280 °C, while F8b shows C10 finishing in ~11–40 min over the same temperature range, and a lower Ea does predict faster kinetics at a given T. What it leaves open: (1) since the Ea values were fit to the same desorption data, this is corroboration by construction, not an independent cross-check; (2) the Arrhenius lines also differ in intercept/pre-exponential factor (C0 ~ -5.9 to -9.5 ln k vs C10 ~ -4.1 to -6.4), so Ea alone does not account for the full ~10-15x speed difference seen between C0 and C10 at matched temperatures — the prefactor difference contributes too, and these panels don't decompose that. (3) No mechanistic evidence (e.g., microstructure/catalyst dispersion) is shown here to explain why Ea drops; that remains outside the scope of F8a/F8b/F10.

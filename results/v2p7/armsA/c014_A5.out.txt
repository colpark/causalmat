Both panels match their stated captions and the reported numbers. F3b shows RGO-Cu2S adsorbing consistently more N2 than Cu2S across the full P/P0 range, with the two curves diverging most near P/P0=1 (roughly consistent with the ~53 vs ~32 cm3/g figures), and the inset pore-size distribution shows RGO-Cu2S with a markedly higher dV/dD peak in the ~10-20 nm diameter range versus a much flatter Cu2S trace. F5a shows three J-V curves with RGO-Cu2S (gray triangles) above Cu2S (open circles) above Pt (filled squares) in current density across most of the voltage range, and the curves converge near similar Voc values around 0.53-0.56 V — consistent with the stated Jsc/Voc ordering.

So the combination supports: RGO-Cu2S has higher surface area/porosity than plain Cu2S, and separately, RGO-Cu2S-based devices give higher Jsc and slightly higher Voc than Cu2S or Pt counter electrodes. That is, both the porosity claim and the PV performance claim are each individually borne out by their own panel.

What it leaves open: neither panel establishes a causal or even correlative link between the porosity/pore-size data and the photovoltaic figure of merit. They are two independent measurements on possibly different samples/contexts (powder isotherm vs. full device J-V); no intermediate data (e.g., impedance, active surface area of the actual electrode used in the cell, charge-transfer resistance) bridges "more mesoporosity" to "higher Jsc/Voc" in these two figures. The mechanistic connection is plausible but unverified by this evidence alone.

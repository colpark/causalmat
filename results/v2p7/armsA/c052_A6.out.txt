Yes, within the narrow scope of surface chemistry → phase behavior, but not beyond that.

F2a (FTIR): The BST-SH spectrum (red) alone shows new bands at 2930 and 2864 cm⁻¹ (aliphatic C–H stretches) and 1122 cm⁻¹, absent from both the plain BST and BST-OH traces. This is direct upstream chemical evidence that thiol-silane grafting introduced new alkyl/organosilane functionality onto the particle surface, distinct from the hydroxyl-modified particles.

F2b (photograph): After 2 h standing in a water–toluene mixture, BST-OH settles into the lower (aqueous) layer while BST-SH disperses in the upper (toluene) layer — a macroscopic hydrophilic-vs-hydrophobic partition.

Combined, the two panels support that the new FTIR bands correspond to a real, functionally consequential surface change: the chemistry seen only in BST-SH tracks with a switch from water-wetting to organic-solvent-wetting behavior, i.e., the thiol modification made the particle surface more hydrophobic/organophilic than the OH-modified one.

What is left open: FTIR shows new bands are present but cannot establish covalent grafting versus physisorption, nor assign 1122 cm⁻¹ definitively (e.g., Si–O–Si vs C–S). The photograph is a qualitative, single-timepoint dispersion test, not a quantitative wettability or bonding-strength measurement, and neither panel speaks to any downstream composite/PRP performance or "value" — that link is not evidenced here.

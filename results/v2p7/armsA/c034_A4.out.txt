Yes, the upstream DFT evidence is consistent with the downstream activity ranking, but only as a qualitative correlation, not a mechanistic proof.

F9a shows ΔG_H* magnitudes: Pd0.75Ag0.25 (-0.08 eV) is closest to the thermoneutral ideal, followed by Pd0.5Ag0.5 (0.12), then Ag0.75Pd0.25 (0.28) and Pd (-0.31) roughly tied, with Ag (0.49) worst. F8b/F8a report H2 rates in the same order: Ag0.25Pd0.75 (=Pd0.75Ag0.25) highest at 125.4 umol/h, then Ag0.5Pd0.5 (57.3), Ag0.75Pd0.25 (43.5), Pd (34.5), Ag lowest (3.2), with ZIS as the inert baseline (1.5). So the alloy with the best-tuned (near-zero) hydrogen adsorption free energy is also the best-performing catalyst, and the ordering tracks |ΔG_H*| reasonably well across the series. This supports the standard volcano-type argument that HER activity on these AgPd alloys is governed by hydrogen-binding thermodynamics on Pd0.75Ag0.25-type sites.

What it leaves open: the DFT is a static (111)-surface, gas-phase model with no light, no ZIS support, no charge-transfer or band-alignment effects, and no kinetic barriers, so it cannot establish that binding energy—rather than photoabsorption, charge separation, or exposed facet/particle size differences—causes the measured rate differences. F8a only confirms the same ranking is stable over 5 h; no error bars or replicate data are shown.

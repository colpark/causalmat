Both panels are genuine SEM fractographs of the same tensile fracture surface. F2c (low mag, 100 μm scale) shows tear ridges and elongated grooves that track the lamellar-colony morphology, with scattered dark pits/holes distributed across colonies. F2d (high mag, 10 μm scale) zooms into that same texture and explicitly marks (red arrows) small holes and (red/blue boxes/circles) microcracks concentrated at lamella ends and colony edges.

Together, these two images justify the claim that fracture-surface damage (microvoids and microcracks) nucleates preferentially at lamellar-boundary/lamella-end sites, and that the macroscopic ridge/groove tearing pattern is structurally organized by the underlying lamellar colony microstructure — i.e., the STR/microstructure link to fracture damage morphology is directly supported by these micrographs.

What is left open: the panels are pure morphology (SEM topology) with no EDS/composition data, so the phase identity or chemical nature of the pitted/cracked sites is not established. There is no stress-strain, hardness, or elongation data shown, so no quantitative behavior_class (ductile vs. brittle, overall toughness) can be derived from these two crops alone — only that the fracture mode is a mixed tear/quasi-cleavage pattern tied to lamellae. No statistics on hole/crack density or comparison across alloy conditions is given, so causal strength (versus mere co-location) of microstructure driving the damage class remains unverified by this evidence pair.

What the two panels show:

F5b (EDS): A single spectrum with a dominant C peak (likely surface/adventitious carbon), Ti and O peaks, and small but distinct Er (~1.4 keV) and Ce (~4.8 keV) peaks. This confirms Er and Ce are present as elements in the analyzed sample alongside Ti and O — i.e., the co-doped composition (Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O) was actually incorporated, not just nominal.

F7 (PL): The 390-420 nm emission band decreases monotonically from pure TiO2 (highest) to Er0.5Ti-O (about half) to Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O (lowest), a clean, ordered trend across the three compositions.

What the combination supports: The EDS confirms the presence of Er and Ce dopants in the material, and the PL trend shows that this same compositional series (undoped → single-doped → co-doped) is accompanied by progressive quenching of the near-band-edge/defect emission — consistent with dopant-induced changes to recombination behavior. Together they support a compositional identity for the quenching trend: it tracks with Er and Ce content.

What is left open: The EDS spectrum shown is a single spectrum (apparently the co-doped sample), not one per composition, so it does not directly verify elemental content for the TiO2 or Er0.5Ti-O samples individually — that identification for those two rests on labeling, not shown EDS. EDS is also qualitative/point-based, giving no quantification, spatial distribution, or dopant valence/site. The mechanism linking dopant presence to PL quenching (e.g., charge trapping, defect states, particle-size/crystallinity effects) is not established by these two panels — only the correlation is.

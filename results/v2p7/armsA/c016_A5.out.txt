Panel F2a (GATR-FTIR) shows the red NO-PPAm trace carrying starred peaks — NO vibration (~1900 cm⁻¹), N=N stretch, N-N stretch, N-O stretch, and a second N-N stretch (~900 cm⁻¹) — that are absent from the blue PPAm trace, which shows only -OH/-NH stretch, C-H stretch, and -NH vibration bands. Panel F2b shows chemiluminescence NO flux rising to a peak of ~34-35×10⁻¹⁰ mol cm⁻² min⁻¹ around 5 min, then decaying to near-zero by ~55-60 min.

Together, the FTIR evidence (A) establishes that new nitrogen-oxide/diazonium-type bonds (N=N, N-N, N-O, NO) were chemically incorporated into PPAm upon NO loading, distinguishing NO-PPAm from PPAm at the level of bonding structure. The chemiluminescence evidence (B) independently shows that this loaded material releases NO gas over time with a burst-then-decay kinetic profile. The combination supports the claim that the new bonds seen by FTIR are the chemical species responsible for the NO release measured by chemiluminescence — i.e., NO-PPAm's bonded N-oxide/diazonium groups are a plausible chemical reservoir that discharges as free NO.

What this leaves open: the two panels are separate measurements on presumably the same material batch but not a mechanistic linkage — FTIR does not track loss of these bands over the 60-min release window, so it cannot confirm that band A specifically depletes as NO flux falls in B, nor which specific bond (N=N vs N-N vs N-O) is the kinetically active donor. No time-resolved FTIR-during-release data is shown, so causal/kinetic correspondence between structure and release rate remains inferred, not directly demonstrated.

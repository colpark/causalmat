Panels confirm the two claims. F4c is an EDS map of the C10 alloy: green Mg grains with blue (C) and red (Co) speckle concentrated along particle edges/rims and patchily on faces, matching claim A. F6a (C0) shows a slow first hydrogenation (~3.2 wt% at 180 min) with cycles 2-3 saturating in minutes; F6b (C10) shows all three cycles, including the first, saturating within ~5-11 min, matching claim B.

What the combination supports: since C10 alone carries a surface layer of C and Co decorating particle boundaries, and C10 alone shows a first-cycle absorption rate already matching later cycles, the data are consistent with the additive's surface coating removing (or bypassing) whatever first-cycle activation barrier limits the undoped C0 sample - i.e., a plausible surface/catalytic role for the C@Co layer in eliminating the incubation period.

What it leaves open: the EDS map exists only for C10, so there is no direct visual comparison to C0's surface state - the inference that C0 lacks such decoration rests on formulation, not imaging. The map is static and gives no mechanistic evidence (catalytic dissociation, crack/defect generation, oxide disruption) linking surface distribution to hydrogenation rate. It also does not explain why C10's cycles 2-3 plateau lower (~4.7 wt%) than C0's (~5.2 wt%), nor whether coating uniformity affects cycling capacity retention.

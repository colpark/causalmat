The EDS spectrum (F5b) confirms the doped TiO2 material contains Ti and O (host lattice), a dominant C peak (likely surface/adventitious carbon), and small Er (~1.4 keV) and Ce (~4.8 keV) peaks — i.e., it verifies that Er and Ce dopants are present in the sample's chemistry. F10c and F10d (dark bars) reproduce the assay claim closely: Er0.5Ce0.1Ti-O ~28-33%, Ce0.2 ~43%, Ce0.3 ~45-47%, TiO2 ~6%, control near 0, with a monotonic Ce-dose trend and significance brackets shown in both panels.

Combined, the evidence supports that the tested composite does contain Ce (and Er) alongside Ti/O, and that samples nominally varying in Ce content show a graded increase in dark antibacterial ratio, undoped TiO2 being much lower. It leaves open: the EDS spectrum is a single, qualitative spectrum (unclear which sample/dose it was taken from) and does not quantify Ce loading across the 0.1/0.2/0.3 series, so it cannot itself establish that Ce content scales as reported or mechanistically causes the antibacterial trend — the composition-performance link remains a correlation from separate panels, not a verified dose-resolved chemical measurement.

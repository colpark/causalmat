Yes, but only for a limited claim. The XRD (A, F3a) shows milled C10 retains every C0 phase (CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17, Mg) with just one weak extra Co line — so the structure/phase identity of the hydrogen-absorbing matrix is unchanged by the 10 wt% C@Co addition; Co appears as a minor, largely separate phase rather than altering the host intermetallics. The Sieverts data (B) and the F7a/F7b plateau curves at 250 C confirm the downstream property value: capacity falls from ~5.1 wt% (C0) to ~4.56 wt% (C10), and both panels show similarly shaped, similarly fast rises to plateau at 250 C, with parallel ordering at lower temperatures (200/150/100 C) too. Because phase identity is preserved, this combination supports interpreting the capacity drop as a dilution/mass effect from the inert 10 wt% additive rather than a change in the active hydride-forming phases — notably 5.1 x 0.9 ≈ 4.59, close to the observed 4.56. What is left open: whether the residual Co line reflects unreacted Co or a reacted Co-bearing phase invisible to XRD; whether Co measurably changes absorption kinetics (rates) rather than just capacity, since the two curve panels look qualitatively similar and were not quantitatively compared here; and any role of milling itself (versus Co) in the small phase/microstructural changes.

Findings after opening all four panels (F5d, F5e, F6b, F6c):

F5d/F5e show a two-tier pattern: total fibrinogen and its gamma-chain drop by roughly half on all three functionalized coatings (BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, BVLD/NO-PPAmF) relative to 316LSS and plain PPAmF, with the *** bracket spanning "316LSS+PPAmF" vs "the three functionalized surfaces" as one group — the three functionalized bars are statistically indistinguishable from each other (overlapping error bars, no separating asterisks).

F6b/F6c show a three-tier pattern: 316LSS and PPAmF lumens/foils are fully packed with dark thrombus; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are partly open with residual clot; BVLD/NO-PPAmF is nearly clean in both cross-section and thrombus photo — visibly better than either single-drug coating.

What the combination supports: the upstream fibrinogen/gamma-chain reduction is sufficient to account for the coarse, categorical split in the downstream photos — why any functionalized coating (single- or dual-drug) outperforms bare 316LSS/PPAmF, which show no fibrinogen suppression and are the only tubes fully occluded.

What it leaves open: the assay does not explain the finer gradient among the three functionalized surfaces. Because BVLD-PPAmF, NO-PPAmF, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF are not statistically distinguished on total fibrinogen or gamma-chain content, this evidence cannot account for why BVLD/NO-PPAmF's lumen/foil is visibly cleaner than the two single-agent coatings in F6b/F6c. That graded improvement from combining BVLD and NO must rest on some other mechanism not captured by these two ELISA readouts — the upstream data are silent on it.

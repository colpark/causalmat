Yes, but only as two independent, complementary lines of evidence — the upstream EDS data do not mechanistically explain the downstream blood-chemistry result.

F5e (EDS mapping) shows C, Zn and N densely filling the particle outline for both the anti-CCL21 DNA aptamer and A10 RNA aptamer BSA@ZIF-8 samples, while P appears only as sparse, scattered dots within the same outline. This is compositional/microstructural evidence: it confirms the ZIF-8 framework elements are present throughout the particle and that phosphorus (attributable to the aptamer backbone) is detectable but present at much lower density and non-uniformly, consistent with the aptamer being a minor, surface-associated component rather than a bulk constituent.

F7b/F7c (blood biomarker tables for tail-vein and subcutaneous administration) show ALT, AST, BUN and CREA at 3–14 days all within the printed reference ranges for both routes — supporting that the administered aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 material did not cause detectable hepatic or renal toxicity over that window.

Together, this supports: the particles were successfully fabricated with the intended elements including aptamer-derived P, and short/medium-term systemic exposure did not perturb these liver/kidney markers. It leaves open: whether sparse P reflects true low aptamer loading, incomplete surface coverage, or EDS detection-limit effects on light elements; and whether biomarker normalcy generalizes beyond 14 days, beyond the tested dose, or to organs/markers not measured.

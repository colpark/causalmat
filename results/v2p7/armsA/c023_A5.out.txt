Panel F2a (relative density vs AlN) and F2b (Vickers hardness vs AlN) confirm the stated HP values: density ~97.5% at 0 wt%, ~100% at 1 wt%, ~99.3% at 3 wt%, ~100% at 5 wt%; hardness ~18.0, ~20.3, ~19.4, ~19.2 GPa at the same points. PS curves in both panels are far lower and monotonically declining, shown only for contrast.

What the combination supports: for the HP series, the rise in density from 0 to 1 wt% AlN (porosity elimination) tracks with the rise in hardness over that same interval — a standard densification-raises-hardness relationship, consistent for HP material.

What it leaves open: from 1 to 5 wt% AlN, HP density stays essentially saturated (99.3–100%), yet hardness declines (20.3 → 19.4 → 19.2 GPa). Since porosity/density is flat-to-full in this range, density alone cannot account for the hardness drop at higher AlN loading. The two panels give no microstructural detail (grain size, phase distribution, AlN intrinsic hardness) to explain that decline — so the downstream hardness trend beyond 1 wt% is left unexplained by this density evidence alone; some other structural or compositional factor must be invoked, which these two figures do not supply.

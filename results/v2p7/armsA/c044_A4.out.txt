Both panels reproduce the stated numbers exactly. F2a (relative density) shows PS falling monotonically ~85.5→78.4→70.7→70.0% across 0/1/3/5 wt% AlN, while HP stays essentially flat and high (~97.5–100%). F2b (Vickers hardness) shows PS falling ~12.0→9.1→7.1→6.8 GPa over the same AlN series, while HP stays flat and high (~18–20.5 GPa).

This combination supports the conclusion that, in the pressureless-sintered (PS) process specifically, increasing AlN content is accompanied by a co-occurring decline in relative density and hardness — consistent with (but not proof of) density loss/porosity being the proximate driver of the hardness drop, since the two PS curves track each other closely while the AlN-free-of-defect HP process shows neither decline. It also establishes that this degradation is process-dependent (PS only), not an intrinsic AlN-hardness effect, since HP with the same AlN loadings shows no such loss.

What it leaves open: neither panel contains a micrograph, so "microstructure/porosity" is only inferred from the density numbers, not directly imaged or quantified (no pore size/distribution/STR data here). No mechanism (e.g., AlN reaction products, grain growth, pore clustering) is shown. And there is no PRP/value or performance-relevance panel among these two, so any downstream claim linking this density-hardness pair to PRP/value is not supported by F2a/F2b and would need separate evidence.

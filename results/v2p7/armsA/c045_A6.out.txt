The SEM pair (F3d/F3e) and the CE plot (F4a) are internally consistent and support a direct structure-to-performance link. F3d (CnC HS) shows a comparatively smooth, largely flat deposit with only sparse, short protruding platelets; F3e (C HS) shows a dense, uniform mat of interlocking sharp platelet/flake features covering the whole field. F4a shows CnC HS (dark points) sustaining ~85-100% CE out to roughly 200+ cycles, while C HS (red points) tracks similarly only through ~35-40 cycles, then drops to two points near 75% and two points at 0% by ~48 cycles, after which no further C HS data are plotted.

This combination supports the claim that the flatter, less platelet-dense zinc morphology on CnC HS is associated with markedly better/longer-lived Coulombic efficiency than the sharp, dense platelet mat on C HS, and that the C HS cell's CE collapse (to 0%) coincides with, and is plausibly caused by, its more jagged/protruded deposit morphology (consistent with dendrite-driven micro-short or dead-Zn/side-reaction failure). What it leaves open: the SEM crops are single-timepoint snapshots (not explicitly tied to the exact cycle number of failure), so the images alone don't prove the morphology at the moment of CE drop, only a plausible morphological correlate; and no direct causal mechanism (e.g., internal short vs. corrosion) is shown in these three panels alone.

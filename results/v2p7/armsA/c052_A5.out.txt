Peaks at 2930, 2864 and 1122 cm⁻¹ are labeled and visible only on the red BST-SH trace; they are absent from the gray BST and blue BST-OH traces, consistent with claim A. F2b (photo) shows two vials of a water-toluene biphasic mixture after 2h: BST-OH particles form a visible cloudy/white band settled in the lower (aqueous) layer, while BST-SH particles are dispersed as haze in the upper (toluene) layer, consistent with claim B.

What the combination supports: the new IR bands are consistent with C–H (2930/2864) and a C–S/related (1122) signature unique to the thiol-modified particles, and this distinct surface chemistry co-occurs with a distinct partition behavior — BST-SH favoring the organic (toluene) phase versus BST-OH favoring the aqueous phase. This is a plausible chemistry-to-behavior link: thiol grafting making the surface more organophilic than the hydroxyl-only surface.

What it leaves open: the FTIR alone does not chemically assign 1122 cm⁻¹ unambiguously, nor quantify degree of functionalization. The photo is a single qualitative snapshot (one time point, no replicate, no quantified partition coefficient), and does not by itself rule out other contributors to dispersion (e.g., particle size/aggregation state) besides surface chemistry. So the pairing is consistent and suggestive, not a controlled mechanistic proof of causation.

F9 (XRD, 5 vs 10 cycles): both patterns share the same major peaks; the insets show a t-ZrO2-marked peak near 43° and a split peak near 73-74° matching the t-ZrO2 reference sticks. Notably, these t-ZrO2 features are clearly taller in the 5-cycle (pink) trace and markedly weaker in the 10-cycle (black) trace — i.e., a decline in the tetragonal signal between 5 and 10 cycles.

F8 (photographs, 0-40 cycles): the coupon is visibly intact at 5 cycles, corners have spalled by 10 cycles, and that spalled-corner damage persists and grows slightly through 40 cycles.

Combination: the two datasets are temporally aligned — the interval (5→10 cycles) over which the t-ZrO2 signal weakens is the same interval in which corner spallation first appears. This supports the idea that a change in zirconia phase content accompanies, and is plausibly linked to, the onset of coating failure/service-capability loss, consistent with a phase/fraction contribution to spallation.

What it leaves open: XRD here is a bulk, whole-coupon measurement, not localized to the spalled corners, so it cannot show the phase change occurred where the spalling occurred. No monoclinic-ZrO2 tracking, stress, or microstructural data are given, so whether the phase shift causes spallation, results from it, or is merely coincident cannot be distinguished. Causation is not established.

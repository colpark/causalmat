Both panels are SEM fractography of the same Al0.9CoFeNi2 EHEA tensile fracture surface. F2c (low mag) shows tear ridges and elongated grooves running along lamellar colonies, with scattered dark holes; F2d (high mag) shows arrowed small holes and boxed microcracks concentrated at lamella ends and edges. Together they support a structure→damage link: fracture morphology follows the lamellar microstructure, and void/microcrack nucleation is spatially tied to lamella boundaries/tips rather than occurring randomly in the matrix. This is consistent with STR (lamellar colony architecture) controlling where damage initiates on the fracture surface, and with a ductile-mixed fracture mode (tear ridges plus discrete microvoids/microcracks) rather than pure cleavage.

What it leaves open: the images are a static post-mortem fracture surface from one specimen/condition, so they cannot establish that lamella-edge damage is causally rate-limiting for macroscopic behavior_class (e.g., ductility, strength, fracture toughness) — no in-situ or interrupted-loading sequence is shown to link nucleation to failure progression. There is no quantification (density, size distribution) of the holes/microcracks, no comparison to other compositions/conditions, and no explicit mechanical property values tied to this image pair. The correlation between microstructure and damage site is visually clear; the strength or generality of any downstream PRP/behavior_class effect is not established by these two panels alone.

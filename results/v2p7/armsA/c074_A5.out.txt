Panels confirm both claims. F1a (Fig 1a) plots ΔG for Co2+→Co3+ (x) and Co3+→Co4+ (y) for CoCrOOH, CoFeCrOOH, CoFeOOH, and Co(OH)2: CoCrOOH has the lowest ΔG(Co2+→Co3+) (~1.05 eV), CoFeOOH the lowest ΔG(Co3+→Co4+) (~6.2 eV), CoFeCrOOH sits between both on each axis, and Co(OH)2 is highest on both. F5c (Fig 5c) shows neutral LSV curves where Co5Fe3Cr2 (black) rises first, crossing 10 mA cm⁻2 at η10=452 mV, ahead of Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5, pure Co, and IrO2.

Together the evidence supports a qualitative, compositional story: the ternary (Co,Fe,Cr)OOH occupies an intermediate/balanced position between the two limiting binary/pure phases on both redox steps computed by DFT, and this same ternary composition family is the one that reaches OER benchmark current fastest experimentally — consistent with a "balanced barrier" rationale for why mixing three metals outperforms either binary or the pure hydroxide.

What it leaves open: the DFT panel uses generic labels (CoCrOOH, CoFeOOH, CoFeCrOOH) without the specific 5:3:2 stoichiometry tested in LSV, and there are no DFT points corresponding to Co5Fe5 or Co5Cr5 individually, so no one-to-one quantitative mapping between computed ΔG and the measured η10 ranking is established — the link is associative/qualitative, not a verified causal or stoichiometry-matched mechanism.

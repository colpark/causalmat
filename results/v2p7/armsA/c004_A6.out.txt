Both panels reviewed directly (F3c and F4f images).

F3c shows a clean in-vitro calibration: A/L at 808 nm rises linearly with agent concentration (R2=0.9999, alpha=6.4 L g-1 cm-1), establishing that absorbance/PA-type signal at this wavelength is a valid, quantitative proxy for agent concentration under controlled optical conditions (fixed path length, no tissue scattering).

F4f shows the downstream in-vivo readout: tumor PA signal drops from 0.26 (baseline) to 0.13 at 1 h, then rises to a peak of 0.94 at 4 h, and falls back to 0.31 by 10 h after IV injection.

What the combination supports: the upstream Beer-Lambert-type linearity justifies treating the tumor PA signal's rise and fall as tracking real changes in relative agent concentration in the tumor over time — i.e., the 4 h peak is consistent with maximal agent accumulation, and the decline toward 10 h with clearance/washout. This is the basis for using PA signal as a semi-quantitative biodistribution/accumulation tracer.

What is left open: the calibration (F3c) was done in a simple solution, not in tissue, so it does not account for scattering, depth attenuation, or blood/background absorption that could distort the in-vivo signal's proportionality to concentration. It also gives no mechanistic account for why the signal dips below baseline at 1 h before rising, or why 4 h specifically is the accumulation peak (pharmacokinetics, EPR effect) — those remain unexplained by this evidence pair.

The four panels are SEM fracture-surface micrographs (1 µm scale) and a tensile-strength bar chart, not a single unified figure set. F4c (BST@Ag1%/PAEN) and F4e (BST@Ag5%/PAEN) show small, fairly evenly distributed particulate features on the fracture surface without conspicuous large agglomerated clumps — F4e shows somewhat more numerous/visible particles than F4c but still no obvious clustering into big agglomerates. F7a confirms a modest, monotonic tensile-strength decline: 79.3 (pure PAEN) → 78.3 (1% Ag) → 74.4 (3%) → 73.5 (5%).

Together, this supports a limited claim: at 1% and 5% Ag loading, dispersion appears reasonably uniform, and the associated strength loss is small and gradual rather than catastrophic — consistent with (but not proof of) good filler dispersion avoiding severe stress-concentration failure. What it leaves open: F4b (BST-SH/PAEN) is a different, non-Ag-doped composite not represented among the four bars in F7a, so it cannot be tied to the tensile trend at all. There is no SEM shown for the 3% Ag composite or for pure PAEN, so the intermediate/baseline points in the tensile series are unverified against morphology. The images alone also cannot establish that dispersion (rather than interfacial adhesion or intrinsic filler stiffness) causes the strength decline — only correlation across two of four data points is visible.

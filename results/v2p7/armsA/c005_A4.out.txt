The three panels do support a photothermal-service-capability claim, but only up to the point of heat generation, not therapeutic efficacy.

What's shown: F3e demonstrates that in vitro spot temperature rises monotonically with BNs-BSA concentration (200 > 100 > 50 > 25 ug/mL) over 6 min under 808 nm irradiation, with water staying near baseline — i.e., the material itself, not the solvent, generates heat, and more material makes more heat. F3g shows the same spot rising monotonically with laser power density (0.5 to 1.5 W) at fixed concentration — heat output is laser-dose-dependent too. F5c then shows that in vivo, tumors injected with BNs-BSA and irradiated reach ~49 C by ~5 min versus ~41 C for PBS-injected tumors under identical irradiation.

Combined, this chain (in vitro dose-response and power-response photothermal heating -> in vivo tumor heating well above the PBS control and into a range conventionally associated with hyperthermia/ablation, ~45-50 C) supports that BNs-BSA confers a real photothermal service capability that transfers from solution to tumor tissue in the animal, i.e., PRF (photothermal conversion) plausibly accounts for the elevated downstream tumor temperature (PRP/value) relative to control.

What it leaves open: none of these three panels report actual therapeutic outcome — tumor growth suppression, ablation histology, survival, or recurrence — so "service_capability" here is demonstrated only as heat generation, not as the value of tumor destruction/cure. F5c also shows a single averaged/representative curve without stated group size, variance, or statistics, so the PBS-vs-BNs-BSA gap, while large, isn't quantified for significance in this panel alone. The concentration/power sweeps (F3e/g) are in vitro/solution measurements, not the actual intratumoral concentration or effective in vivo power density, so the specific dose used in F5c isn't directly cross-referenced to the F3e/g curves. Mechanistic attribution (photothermal conversion of the BNs-BSA nanomaterial specifically, as opposed to another accompanying effect) is inferred from the PBS control, not independently isolated.

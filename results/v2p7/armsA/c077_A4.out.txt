Both panels are as described. F3b (ICP) shows Zn at 0 at% in the pristine sample, ~22% by the 4th cycle, jumping to ~51% by the 20th cycle, then leveling near 46-47% at the 60th and 100th cycles — matching statement A closely. F3a (XRD) shows the pristine KMnHCF pattern is essentially replaced starting at the 4th-cycle trace: two new peaks emerge near the dashed guide lines (~16.5 and ~21.9 degrees), and the original main reflections weaken; by the 20th-100th cycle the pattern increasingly resembles the JCPDS 33-1061 (KZnHCF) reference below, matching statement B.

The combination supports a coherent picture: Zn uptake (composition, panel B) and emergence/strengthening of a KZnHCF-like diffraction pattern with suppression of the original KMnHCF pattern (phase identity, panel A) proceed together and both plateau by the 60th-100th cycle, consistent with progressive conversion of KMnHCF into a Zn-substituted hexacyanoferrate phase during cycling.

What it leaves open: ICP is a bulk elemental measurement and cannot itself prove the two new XRD peaks are specifically the Zn-substituted phase rather than a coexisting/mixed phase; the panels do not establish mechanism (ion exchange vs. dissolution-redeposition) or spatial homogeneity of the transformation, only correlated timing and bulk composition/phase trends.

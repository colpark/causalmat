Both panels were opened directly.

F4b (UV-vis): pure TiO2 has a sharp absorption edge near 410 nm; all three Er/Ce co-doped samples show edges extended past 420 nm into the visible, plus extra f-f transition peaks (~525, ~650 nm) from Er. In the visible region the co-doped curves rank roughly Ce0.2 (red) ≥ Ce0.3 (yellow) > Ce0.1 (blue), consistent with the stated "Ce0.2 absorbs most."

F8 (degradation assay): at 60 min the order is Er0.5Ce0.2 (~46%) > Ce0.3 (~41%) > Ce0.1 (~31%) > TiO2 (~20%), and this same ranking holds at 20 and 40 min.

The combination supports a correlation: co-doping that extends visible-light absorption tracks with higher photocatalytic degradation activity, and the sample with the strongest visible absorption (Ce0.2) is also the most active catalyst — the two independent measurements agree on the same doping-level ordering. It does not establish causation: the UV-vis panel alone cannot show whether the assay was run under visible or UV illumination, whether the extra absorption actually translates into more photogenerated charge carriers reaching the surface, or whether other doping-induced changes (defect/oxygen-vacancy density, crystallite size, surface area, recombination rate) instead drive the degradation differences. So the optical data are consistent with, but do not prove, the mechanistic link to the degradation results.

The three panels are consistent with, but do not prove, a causal link between deposit morphology and cycling reversibility.

F3d (CnC HS) shows a largely flat surface with only a few sparse, thin protruding platelets. F3e (C HS) shows the same field of view uniformly covered by a dense mat of sharp, interlocking platelets — matching claim A. F4a shows CnC HS (black circles) holding ~85-100% Coulombic efficiency out past 200 cycles, while C HS (red circles) tracks it briefly, then collapses to 0% near cycles 40 and 48 and has no further points after ~50 — matching claim B.

Together, the combination supports an associative story: the smoother, sparsely-decorated Zn deposit on CnC HS coincides with stable long-term Coulombic efficiency (higher service capability), while the dense sharp-platelet morphology on C HS coincides with early efficiency collapse — plausibly via increased side-reaction area, soft-short risk, or dead-Zn formation from sharp features.

It leaves open: (1) the cycle number/state at which each SEM was taken relative to the CE trace, so temporal correspondence to the failure point isn't established; (2) any direct mechanistic evidence (cross-sections, dendrite penetration, corrosion analysis) tying platelet sharpness to CE loss rather than mere correlation; and (3) sample size/reproducibility, since each panel shows a single field/cell.

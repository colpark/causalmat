Findings after opening both panels.

F2a (FTIR) shows three overlaid traces (BST, BST-OH, BST-SH). The peaks at 2930, 2864 and 1122 cm-1 are labeled only on the red BST-SH curve, not on the BST or BST-OH curves — consistent with claim A. These are plausibly C–H (alkyl chain from the silane coupling agent) and C–S/Si–O–C related bands, so the panel supports that thiol surface functionalization chemically modified the particle surface (BST → BST-SH), distinct from the plain hydroxylated intermediate (BST-OH).

F5a (DSC) shows Tg-type transitions for pure PAEN (176°C) and BST@Ag composites at 1/3/5% (146, 149, 157°C), matching claim B: a large drop from neat polymer to filled composite, then a partial rise with increasing Ag loading.

What the combination supports: BST particles were successfully surface-modified with thiol chemistry (new IR bands), and PAEN composites containing BST@Ag filler show a lower, loading-dependent Tg than neat PAEN.

What it leaves open: F2a's samples are BST/BST-OH/BST-SH (surface-chemistry stage), while F5a's samples are BST@Ag-loaded PAEN composites — the figures never show DSC for BST-SH particles alone or for composites without thiol/Ag modification. So the panels do not establish that the thiol bonding chemistry specifically (rather than particle loading, dispersion, or Ag content) causes the Tg shift; that causal link is not tested here.

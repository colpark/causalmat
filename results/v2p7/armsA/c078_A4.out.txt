Panels confirm both claims individually. F3b (ICP) shows K/Fe/Mn/Zn at% by cycle: Zn is absent pristine, rises to ~50% by the 20th cycle, and stabilizes near 46-47% at 60th/100th cycles — matching claim A exactly. F5d/e/f (DFT structural models) show a stepwise progression as nominal Zn content increases (K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6 → K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6 → ZnMnFe(CN)6): the Mn site goes from MnN6-type octahedral toward MnN3 (panel e) and then MnN4 (panel f), and in panel f grey ZnN4 tetrahedra appear linked to orange/magenta FeC6 octahedra — matching claim B.

Together, the two lines of evidence support a coherent story: as Zn substitutes into the Prussian-blue-analogue framework during cycling (quantitatively documented by ICP), the local coordination/site occupancy of Mn and Zn reorganizes from octahedral toward lower-coordinate motifs, with ZnN4 tetrahedra ultimately bridging to FeC6 octahedra — i.e., composition change plausibly drives the site-occupancy/coordination change reported downstream.

What is left open: the DFT panels are structural models keyed to nominal Zn stoichiometries (0.25, 0.5, 1.0 Zn per formula unit), not to the ICP's cycle-indexed at% values. No panel directly maps a given cycle's measured Zn at% onto a specific DFT structure, so the quantitative correspondence between "how much Zn" (ICP) and "which coordination state" (DFT) is inferred by analogy, not demonstrated. Also, the DFT structures are computational models, not direct experimental proof (e.g., XRD/EXAFS) that these coordination states exist in the actual cycled material at those compositions. So the combination justifies that composition change and coordination/site-occupancy change co-occur and are mechanistically plausible, but not that the specific measured at% values at specific cycles correspond one-to-one with the specific MnN3/MnN4/ZnN4 structures shown.

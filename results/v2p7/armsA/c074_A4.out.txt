Does upstream account for downstream? The combination supports a thermodynamic rationale: CoFeCrOOH's intermediate (neither best-nor-worst) position on both elementary-step free energies is consistent with a mechanistic story for why incorporating both Fe and Cr into the ternary oxyhydroxide (as in Co5Fe3Cr2) could balance the two OER-relevant steps and improve activity relative to unmodified Co(OH)2. It leaves open several things: (1) F1a's species are qualitative labels (CoCrOOH, CoFeOOH, CoFeCrOOH, Co(OH)2), not the specific stoichiometry Co5Fe3Cr2 tested in F5c, so the exact correspondence is inferred, not shown directly; (2) IrO2 is not a DFT panel entry at all, so the LSV comparison to IrO2 has no computed counterpart; (3) the DFT is a static thermodynamic calculation, not conducted under the neutral-electrolyte condition of the LSV experiment, so it cannot by itself explain kinetics, η10 values, or the specific neutral-pH performance ordering. The evidence supports a plausible compositional/thermodynamic trend consistent with the activity trend, but does not establish a direct causal or quantitative link between the computed intermediate energetics and the observed neutral-electrolyte η10 advantage over IrO2.

F5e (EDS mapping): For both the anti-CCL21 DNA aptamer and A10 RNA aptamer BSA@ZIF-8 particles, C, Zn, and N signals densely fill the particle outline, while P appears only as sparse scattered dots within the same outline. This supports the claim that P is a minority/exclusive marker attributable to the aptamer-BSA cargo, co-located with (but far less abundant than) the ZIF-8 framework elements (C, Zn, N) — i.e., structural evidence that aptamer material is present on/in the particle, for both aptamer identities.

F6e (ex vivo fluorescence): At 9h, the tumor image for A10 RNA aptamer-FITC-BSA@ZIF-8 is visibly brighter (more green/yellow) than for BSA@ZIF-8 alone, consistent with panel B's claim. Liver remains the brightest organ in both rows.

What the combination supports: the two panels are independently plausible and mutually consistent with a narrative in which aptamer-functionalized MOF particles are structurally confirmed to carry aptamer material (P signal) and separately show greater tumor accumulation than the non-aptamer particle. This is consistent with — but does not itself establish — that aptamer loading causes the enhanced tumor signal.

What is left open: the EDS panel does not quantify P loading or link it mechanistically to targeting; it uses a different aptamer (anti-CCL21 DNA and A10 RNA) sample than the biodistribution study's readout basis (FITC label). No dose-response, replicate counts, or statistics are shown in either panel, and liver uptake dominates in both conditions, so no causal chain from "P present" to "tumor brighter" is demonstrated — only a qualitative, indirect correlation across two separate experiments.

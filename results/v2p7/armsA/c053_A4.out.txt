Combination: the good, gap-free interface seen at 1% loading is consistent with the very small strength drop at that same loading (79.3 to 78.3 MPa) — i.e., strong interfacial bonding plausibly limits stress-concentration losses at low filler content. That is what the pairing supports.

What it leaves open: no SEM interface image is given for the 3% or 5% samples, so the evidence cannot explain why strength continues to fall at higher loadings — that could equally be attributed to filler agglomeration, increased defect density, or other causes not shown here. The evidence also does not explain why good interfacial adhesion produces no strength gain (reinforcement), only a smaller loss; it addresses magnitude of decline at 1% only, not the mechanism at higher loadings or the absence of reinforcement.

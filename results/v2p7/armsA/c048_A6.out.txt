All four panels viewed. F3a shows bare BST as a dense field of smooth, uniformly small (~70 nm) particles. F3b (BST‑SH) and F3e (BST@Ag5%) show visibly rougher, larger, more agglomerated particle textures — consistent with claim A that surface modification/coating changes morphology and particle size. F5a (DSC) shows Tg = 176 °C for pure PAEN and 146/149/157 °C for BST@Ag 1/3/5%, i.e. all composites depress Tg relative to neat polymer, with Tg rising monotonically with Ag loading (146→149→157) — matching claim B's numbers exactly.

What the combination supports: the SEM series corroborates that chemical/particle modification produces a real, visible microstructural change (smooth→rough, larger, coated particles), and the DSC series shows this modification step is accompanied by a shifted, loading-dependent Tg. Together they support a link between the surface-modified particle microstructure and an altered thermal property value (Tg), with a clear trend across Ag content.

What is left open: these crops show particle/composite surface morphology only — none is a matrix cross-section or dispersion map, so particle distribution/dispersion homogeneity within the bulk PAEN is not evidenced here. No mechanism connecting the SEM-visible roughening to the specific magnitude of Tg depression is shown; the DSC-microstructure link is correlational, not demonstrated causally, and no other property (PRP) values are addressed by these four panels.

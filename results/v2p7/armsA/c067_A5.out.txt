Both panels open and inspected.

F2a shows, for the (004)/(400)/(242)/(224) reflections, an orthorhombic-tetragonal step near 150 K, with a shaded coexistence band (~150-175 K) evident on the red trace; a second (blue) trace shows a similar step near 150 K without the extended shading. Per the caption, red=PC, blue=SC, black=HOC, but only two colored traces (red, blue) are actually visible in this crop — no black/HOC trace appears, so the panel does not itself confirm HOC's phase-transition behavior.

F3a (as-grown) shows PC developing two emission branches plus a low-energy tail that appears below ~150 K, matching the XRD step/coexistence temperature; the same tail/second band persists across the 50-370 uW fluence range at 80 K. HOC shows a visually intermediate branching pattern; SC shows a single, essentially fluence- and near-temperature-invariant band despite also displaying a structural step near 150 K in F2a.

So the combination supports: for PC, the temperature at which structural phase coexistence appears (~150-175 K) coincides with the onset of the dual-emission/IR-tail behavior — consistent with, but not proof of, a structural origin. It leaves open: (1) HOC's structural data isn't shown here, so its "intermediate" PL status can't be tied to XRD in this panel; (2) SC undergoes a comparable structural step yet shows no PL splitting, so coincidence-in-temperature alone doesn't explain why PC/HOC split and SC doesn't; (3) XRD is temperature-only and says nothing about the fluence-dependence of the PC branching.

Panels confirm the stated numbers. F3d shows the six voltage-state SAXS curves (OCV, 0.45, 0.136, 0.11, 0.02, 3V) essentially overlapping across the full q-range, with only a minor separation in the ~0.03–0.1 Å⁻¹ region at 0.11/0.02V — matching claim A. F3c's 2D contours are correspondingly flat/vertical-striped, showing no evolving feature over the discharge/charge time axis. F2d shows discharge capacity at ~870 mAh/g (cycle 1), dropping sharply to ~480 (cycle 2), then decaying smoothly to ~70 mAh/g by cycle 40, while coulombic efficiency climbs toward ~95%+ — matching claim B.

Together these support only a negative/mechanistic-exclusion conclusion: the operando SAXS signal, which would register mesoscale structural or porosity changes, stays essentially unchanged across the voltage window while capacity fades severely — so the severe capacity loss is not accompanied by (and is not explained by) any SAXS-detectable morphological transformation. This does not identify (MEC) what does cause the fade — SEI growth, irreversible Na trapping, electrolyte side reactions, or sub-nanometer changes below SAXS resolution all remain open possibilities. It also leaves service_capability/PRF unresolved: capacity is still declining, not plateaued, at cycle 40, so no claim of stabilized or adequate long-term service capability is supported by this data; only that mesoscale structure is not the visible driver of the fade.

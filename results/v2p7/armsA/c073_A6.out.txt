Both panels were opened and inspected.

F2c (low mag, ~100 µm scale) shows a fracture surface with tear ridges and elongated grooves that track the orientation of lamellar/dendritic colonies, plus scattered dark holes within and between colonies. F2d (high mag, ~10 µm scale) resolves this same surface and marks specific small holes (red arrows) and microcracks (red/blue boxed regions) sitting at the ends and edges of individual lamellae.

Together the two images support a modest, purely descriptive claim: fracture-surface topography (tear ridges, grooves) is spatially organized by the lamellar/dendritic microstructure, and the discrete damage features present (microvoids, microcracks) preferentially localize at lamella boundaries/tips rather than appearing randomly across the surface. That is consistent with lamella boundaries acting as preferred sites for local damage nucleation during fracture, and with a fracture mode that is not simple brittle cleavage (ridges/grooves plus voids imply some tearing/ductile component along with cracking).

What is left open: these are morphological SEM observations only — no EDS/phase identification ties the holes or lamella boundaries to a specific phase or composition, no quantitative crack/void density is given, and there is no linkage shown to bulk mechanical properties (strength, elongation, strain rate) or to a broader behavior-class classification. Causality (boundary → damage vs. co-located artifact) is not established, only spatial association.

The optical panels (F1b-e) show SiCp particles distributed fairly uniformly through the Mg-alloy matrix but with visible local clustering, and the clustering/density appears to increase from panel b through d/e (consistent with rising reinforcement content, e.g., 10-20% SiCp per the captions). F5a (SEM, AZ91/10SiCp) shows a mixed fracture surface: fibrous/tear-ridge regions interspersed with flatter, facet-like patches, i.e., a mixed ductile-plus-brittle-like fracture character rather than uniform ductile tearing.

Together, the upstream evidence is consistent with the idea that a non-uniform (clustered) particle distribution could produce a mixed fracture morphology — matrix-dominated tear ridges alongside more brittle facets near particle-rich zones. That is the extent of what this pairing supports: a plausible, qualitative link between microstructural heterogeneity and mixed fracture character.

It does not establish causation or spatial correspondence: no panel shows the fracture surface registered against a particle map, so whether the facets actually sit at cluster sites (versus matrix cleavage, interfacial debonding, or porosity) is unconfirmed. Also, F5a is only the 10% SiCp sample, while the optical panels span multiple higher fractions (15%, 20%), so the SEM evidence does not directly cover the distributions shown in F1d/F1e.

Findings from the three panels:

F5b (EDS spectrum): confirms qualitative composition — Ti, O, C peaks with small Er (~1.4 keV) and Ce (~4.8 keV) peaks, matching claim A exactly. It shows Er and Ce are present in the sample but gives no quantitative wt%/at% breakdown, so it cannot confirm the specific 0.1/0.2/0.3 Ce doping levels used in the antibacterial series — only that both dopants exist alongside the Ti-O host.

F10c and F10d (antibacterial bar charts, Dark vs Light): both panels reproduce the dark-condition trend in claim B — Control ~0%, TiO2 ~6%, Ce0.1 ~28–33%, Ce0.2 ~43%, Ce0.3 ~45–47%, with significance brackets — so the dose-dependent rise up to Ce0.2 (and near-plateau at Ce0.3) is well supported. Both panels also show a Light condition (not covered by claim B): ratios rise much further (TiO2 ~45%, Ce0.2 ~92–94%, Ce0.3 ~87–88%), and notably Ce0.3 falls slightly below Ce0.2 under light — a non-monotonic reversal at the highest dose that the dark-only ASSAY description doesn't capture.

Net: the EDS confirms the elements (Ce, Er) responsible for the composition are present, and the assay panels confirm the dark-condition dose trend quantitatively. What's left open: quantitative dopant concentration from EDS, the mechanism linking composition to antibacterial value, and why Ce0.3 underperforms Ce0.2 under light.

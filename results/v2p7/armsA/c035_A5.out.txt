The two panels are consistent with each other and support the stated observations. F5c (power factor vs T) shows x=0.01 (red circles) as the highest curve across the whole range, ~28-29 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² near 300 K declining to ~22 near 623 K — matching claim A's ~29 at 300K/~22 at 623K. x=0.005 (black squares) is the lowest among the doped compositions (~23 down to ~13), and x=0 (green) sits far below all doped samples, at most ~5, also matching. F6a (ZT vs T) shows x=0.01 (red) peaking highest among all curves, reaching about 1.25 in the 500-525 K window before declining — matching claim B.

Together, the panels support that x=0.01 has both the highest power factor and the highest ZT among the compositions tested, and that its ZT peak (~1.25, ~500-525 K) occurs at a temperature past where its PF has already begun declining (PF peaks earlier, near 300-350 K, then falls smoothly). This indicates the ZT peak is not simply tracking the PF maximum — some other factor (most plausibly reduced thermal conductivity at higher T, not shown in either panel) must be contributing to the ZT peak. The panels alone do not show thermal conductivity data, so they cannot establish *why* ZT peaks near 500-525 K despite PF already falling; that mechanistic link remains open and unverified by this pair of figures.

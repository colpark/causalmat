Both panels confirm the tabulated numbers: F4a plots apparent porosity vs. temperature for S1/S2/S3 matching series A, and F6a plots flexural strength (σ) vs. temperature matching series B.

What the combination supports: for S1, porosity collapses from 8.7% to 0.4% between 1400 and 1600 °C while strength jumps from ~165 to ~465 MPa over the same range — a clear, large inverse co-movement consistent with densification (porosity loss) driving strength gain. For S3, porosity stays comparatively high (11.2% down to only 7.7%) and strength stays essentially flat (~165-195 MPa), which is also consistent — little porosity change, little strength change.

What it leaves open: S2 breaks a simple porosity-controls-strength story — porosity keeps falling slightly from 1650 to 1700 °C (1.2% to 0.7%) yet strength drops (170 to 145 MPa), so something other than porosity (grain growth, phase change, microstructure not shown in these two panels) must be acting. The two panels only show porosity and strength together with temperature as the common axis; they do not show microstructure/grain data directly, so they cannot establish that porosity is the causal mechanism rather than a co-varying proxy for a shared underlying process (sintering/densification stage). Whether "STR" or "PRP/value" denotes something beyond flexural strength is not resolvable from these two panels alone.

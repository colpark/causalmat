Both panels were opened and inspected.

F3 (polarization): Mg-Zn-Y-Nd shows E_corr ≈ -1.49 V vs. -0.25 V for 317L SS, with I_corr of 24.93 µA/cm² vs. 0.017 µA/cm² — a roughly three-order-of-magnitude difference, confirming the Mg alloy is electrochemically far more active/anodic than the steel.

F5a (pH): Mg-Zn-Y-Nd pH climbs steadily from 7.4 to ~10 by ~day 22 and then plateaus; 317L SS instead falls to a minimum of ~6.1 by day 20 before recovering slightly; blank control rises briefly then declines gently to ~7.5.

What the combination supports: the polarization data establish that Mg-Zn-Y-Nd corrodes far more actively than 317L SS, a mechanism (Mg + 2H2O → Mg(OH)2 + H2, consuming H+/releasing OH-) qualitatively consistent with the sustained, large pH rise seen only for the Mg alloy. The much lower corrosion current for 317L SS is consistent with its far smaller pH excursion in that direction.

What it leaves open: the polarization curve is a single-timepoint snapshot, not time-resolved over the 30-day immersion, so it cannot account for the pH plateau's timing or magnitude quantitatively. It also gives no mechanism for the 317L SS pH decrease (its corrosion is even lower than in the polarization test, so something else — e.g., media buffering, secondary reactions — must drive that drop) or for the blank control's trajectory, neither of which the electrochemical data address.

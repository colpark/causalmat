Panels viewed: F5d (MnN6, low-Zn structure "K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6"), F5e (MnN3, "K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6"), F5f (MnN4 with grey ZnN4 tetrahedra linked to brown FeC6 octahedra, "ZnMnFe(CN)6"), and F3b (ICP bar chart: Zn at% ~0 at pristine/4th cycle, jumping to ~50% by the 20th cycle and holding ~46–47% at 60th/100th).

The combination supports a composition-to-coordination correspondence: the ICP series (F3b) establishes that Zn content in the cycled material rises from none to roughly half the transition-metal content, and the DFT structural series (F5d→e→f) shows that as the modeled Zn stoichiometry increases (0.25→0.5→1), the Mn coordination sheds N ligands stepwise (N6→N3→N4) while a grey ZnN4 tetrahedron appears and bridges to an FeC6 octahedron — exactly the qualitative trend claim B describes. So together the panels justify saying that increasing Zn incorporation (measured) tracks with, and plausibly explains, the modeled shift in Mn coordination geometry and the emergence of ZnN4–FeC6 linkage.

What is left open: the DFT models are for three fixed nominal compositions, not indexed to the ICP's cycle numbers or exact at% values, so no panel directly ties a given measured Zn at% to a specific occupancy/coordination state. No diffraction/EXAFS confirms the real cycled material actually adopts these computed local structures. The link is a plausible qualitative correspondence, not a quantitative or experimentally verified site-occupancy mapping.

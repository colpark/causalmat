What the four panels show:

- F3a (bare BST): smooth, discrete, roughly spherical particles matching the ~70 nm SEM description.
- F3b (BST‑SH): particles appear coarser, less distinctly spherical, and more aggregated/clumped — consistent with a surface coating.
- F3e (BST@Ag5%): surface texture is further roughened, with a denser, cauliflower-like packed appearance, consistent with additional Ag decoration building on the modified filler.
- F5a (DSC): Tg drops from 176 °C (pure PAEN) to 146, 149, and 157 °C for BST@Ag at 1%, 3%, 5% loading — all composites below the neat polymer, and Tg rising with Ag content from 1% to 5%.

What the combination supports: the SEM series is consistent with a stepwise surface modification (bare → thiol-functionalized → Ag-decorated), each stage visibly rougher/coarser than the last, so the particles used to make the DSC composites are plausibly the coated, larger species claimed. Pairing this with the DSC panel supports that incorporating this modified filler into PAEN lowers Tg relative to neat polymer, with a partial recovery (146→149→157 °C) as Ag loading increases.

What it leaves open: the SEM images are of isolated particles, not the polymer composite, so there is no direct micrograph of dispersion/distribution within the PAEN matrix (no cross-section or embedded-particle image) to explain the Tg shift mechanistically. The DSC panel only includes BST@Ag composites — not composites with bare BST or BST-SH — so these four panels cannot isolate which modification step (thiolation vs. Ag decoration) drives the Tg depression, and no strength/mechanical (STR) or "value" property panel is among these four to link microstructure to those outcomes.

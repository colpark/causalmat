I opened all five crops (F1b–F1f). They form a composition series: b=5, c=10, d=15, e=20, f=25 wt% SiCp. Dark particle density visibly increases from b to f, with the particles distributed across the whole field and forming small local clusters in every panel (b-f) — this matches observation A, and the clustering does not disappear at higher loadings, it just occurs among a denser population.

Against the creep result (15/20/25 wt% below AZ91; 5/10 wt% overlapping or above), the micrographs support a content-dependent story: the panels with reduced creep rate (d, e, f) are exactly those with visibly higher, more continuous particle coverage than the panels that failed to beat AZ91 (b, c). So the imaging is consistent with "more SiC particles present" tracking "lower creep rate," i.e., particle content plausibly underlies the crossover in creep behavior.

What it leaves open: the micrographs only show particle spatial distribution, not particle size distribution, interfacial bonding, or matrix grain size — any of which could be the real mechanistic driver of creep resistance. They also cannot establish causation, only that density and creep behavior co-vary across the same specimen series. Whether the persistent local clustering (present at every wt%) helps, hurts, or is neutral for creep is not addressed by these images.

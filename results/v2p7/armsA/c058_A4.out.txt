F5b (EDS): a single spectrum shows dominant C, plus Ti and O peaks, and small but distinct Er (~1.4 keV) and Ce (~4.8 keV) peaks. This is consistent with successful incorporation of Er and Ce into the Ti-O matrix in the doubly-doped sample — it supports the compositional/STR claim that Er and Ce are present as dopants, not that they are absent or that the base is anything other than Ti-O. It does not give quantitative doping levels, has no replicate spectra, and the dominant C peak (likely coating/contamination, unlabeled as such) is not explained.

F7 (PL): PL intensity in the 390-420 nm band decreases in the order TiO2 > Er0.5Ti-O > Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O, roughly matching the description (Er0.5Ti-O about half of TiO2, Er0.5Ce0.2 lowest). This trend is consistent with dopant-induced suppression of radiative electron-hole recombination, which is often used as an indirect proxy for improved charge separation/photocatalytic potential.

Together, the two panels support that Er/Ce doping is compositionally real (EDS) and correlates with progressively suppressed PL (PL), i.e., composition tracks a recombination-related optical signal. What is left open: EDS is a single spot spectrum (not quantified, not replicated), PL curves show no error bars or replicates, and neither panel directly measures photocatalytic/PRP performance (e.g., degradation rate) — the causal link from PL quenching to actual value/performance is inferred, not demonstrated here.

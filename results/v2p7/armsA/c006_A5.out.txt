Upstream (F3e, F3g): IR thermal imaging shows BNs-BSA heats under 808 nm irradiation in a graded, monotonic way — hotter spots with higher concentration (200>100>50>25 ug/mL, water inert) and with higher power density (0.5->1.5 W). This establishes that the material does possess a real, dose- and power-scaling photothermal conversion capability at 808 nm — i.e., it supports the "PRF/service_capability" claim that 808 nm alone can drive heating.

Downstream (F5d): individual tumor curves show BNs-BSA+808nm (group 5) fails in most animals — 4/5 tumors regrow after ~day 14 — while BNs-BSA+660/808nm dual laser (group 6) suppresses all 5 tumors to zero.

Does the upstream evidence account for this? Only partially. The IR data confirms the material genuinely converts 808 nm light to heat and that this scales with concentration/power, so the photothermal mechanism is real and not negligible — it is consistent with 808 nm alone producing some antitumor effect (which is seen: growth is far slower than PBS/BNs-BSA/laser-only controls). But the IR panels are bench measurements (fixed exposure window, unspecified whether in vivo tumor concentration/power matches the 200 ug/mL, 1.5 W conditions shown) and say nothing about tumor accumulation, ablation completeness, or depth penetration in vivo. They therefore cannot explain why single-wavelength treatment is incomplete in most animals or why adding 660 nm yields full, durable suppression — that gap (synergy vs. dose vs. penetration) remains open.

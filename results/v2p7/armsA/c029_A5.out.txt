Together this justifies two things: (1) increasing PZT content in the fiber-based PENG increases piezoelectric output voltage across the full 0–10 wt% range tested, and (2) at its optimal load (~10 MOhm) the fiber-film PENG delivers roughly 6.5x the peak power of the cast-film device, with both curves showing a rise-then-fall (impedance-matching) shape rather than monotonic increase with resistance. This supports a claim that the fiber morphology confers greater power/service capability than the cast morphology under matched-load conditions.

What it leaves open: no mechanism for why fiber outperforms cast is shown in these panels (only the output curves, not structural/impedance data); PZT content beyond 10 wt% is untested; power was only measured for the single f-P0.10 composition, so the voltage trend (A) and the power/load trend (B) are not shown as a joint function of composition and load together; and there is no stability/durability or absolute "value" metric beyond these two figures.

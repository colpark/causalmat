Both panels were opened and match the stated data ranges. F5c (PF): x=0.01 (red) is highest across the whole range, ~29 µW cm⁻¹K⁻² at 300 K falling to ~22 at 623 K; x=0.005 (black) is the lowest of the doped compositions (~23→13); x=0 (green) is far lower still (~2–5). F6a (ZT): at 300 K, x=0.005 (~0.84) is actually the highest doped curve, slightly above x=0.01 (~0.78), with x=0.02/0.04 lower (~0.63–0.69) and the n-Bi2Te3 reference near 0.88.

So the upstream PF evidence does NOT fully account for the downstream ZT ordering: PF ranks x=0.01 as the best composition, but ZT at 300 K ranks x=0.005 slightly above x=0.01. The combination supports only that PF is one contributing factor to ZT (both are far above the undoped x=0 baseline, and PF's temperature-decreasing trend is broadly mirrored by ZT's shape at high T), not that PF alone determines the ZT ranking. What is left open: neither panel shows thermal conductivity, so the reason x=0.005 edges out x=0.01 in ZT despite having lower PF (presumably a lower thermal conductivity for x=0.005) cannot be confirmed from these two panels alone.

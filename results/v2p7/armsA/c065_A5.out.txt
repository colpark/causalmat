F4c (EDS map, captioned "EDS mapping of the C10 alloy"): shows Mg (green) particle bodies with C (blue) and Co (red) signal concentrated on particle surfaces, especially edges and some faces — matching evidence A exactly.

F6a (..._A.jpg): a Sieverts absorption curve at 360 °C with a "66 min" bracket marking the second-stage plateau approach — matches evidence B's C0 value.
F6b (..._B.jpg): the companion Sieverts curve at 360 °C with an "11 min" bracket — matches evidence B's C10 value, and the curve visibly saturates faster and reaches its plateau within ~15 min versus ~75 min in F6a.

What the combination supports: the EDS map confirms that in the C10 material, carbon and cobalt sit as a surface/edge decoration on Mg-alloy particles rather than being bulk-dissolved, and the paired kinetics curves confirm that this composition's diffusion-controlled absorption stage is roughly six times shorter than the plain alloy's. Together this is consistent with a surface-catalyst story: C@Co decoration at particle boundaries plausibly shortens the diffusion path/barrier for hydrogen uptake.

What it leaves open: this is spatial co-occurrence, not a demonstrated mechanism — no EDS map of the C0 particles is shown for contrast, so "surface decoration present only in C10" is assumed, not shown side-by-side. Also, neither F6a nor F6b crop itself carries a visible alloy label (only cycle numbers 1st/2nd/3rd); the C0/C10 assignment relies entirely on matching the externally supplied 66 min / 11 min values, not on text within the panels.

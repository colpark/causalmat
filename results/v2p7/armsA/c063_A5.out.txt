All three panels reviewed (F9a, F9b JMAK plots; F4c EDS map).

F4c shows Mg (green), C (blue), and Co (red) signals for the C10 alloy: C and Co are co-located and concentrated on particle surfaces, edges, and some faces — consistent with a discrete surface catalyst phase rather than uniform bulk dissolution. F9a/F9b are JMAK plots of desorption (ln[-ln(1-a)] vs ln t) at four temperatures, both highly linear (R2 > 0.98), with a single fitted Avrami exponent eta per alloy: 1.59 for the plot in F9a (C0) and 1.32 for F9b (C10).

The combination supports: the linear JMAK fits indicate a single, temperature-independent kinetic mechanism governs desorption in each alloy, and the C10 alloy (with surface-segregated C/Co catalyst per F4c) shows a lower eta than the C0 case — plausibly consistent with catalyst-modified nucleation/growth (e.g., surface-catalyzed nucleation reducing dimensionality). This is a reasonable qualitative link between catalyst placement and altered kinetics.

What it leaves open: there is no EDS map of C0 to confirm the surface distribution is specific to (or absent in) the uncatalyzed alloy; the panels don't quantitatively connect surface coverage/density to the magnitude of eta; and JMAK linearity alone doesn't uniquely identify the physical mechanism eta reflects. The mechanistic attribution remains inferential, not directly demonstrated by these three panels.

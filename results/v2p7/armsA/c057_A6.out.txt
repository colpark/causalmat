Panels viewed:
- F5b (EDS spectrum): confirms Ti and O (host oxide) plus small Er (~1.4 keV) and Ce (~4.8 keV) peaks, with a dominant C peak (likely conductive coating/adventitious carbon). This is a single representative spectrum, not one per composition.
- F10c and F10d (dark/light antibacterial ratio bar charts, both n=3 mean±SD): both show the same monotonic pattern — Control ~0-7%, TiO2 ~6-45%, Er0.5Ce0.1 ~28-33%(dark)/~68-73%(light), Er0.5Ce0.2 ~42-43%/~92-93%, Er0.5Ce0.3 ~45-47%/~87-88%, matching the stated ASSAY numbers for the dark condition, with significance brackets (*, **, ***) between adjacent compositions.

What the combination supports: the EDS spectrum verifies that Ce and Er are genuinely incorporated into the Ti-O material (not merely nominal formula labels), so the antibacterial dose trend can plausibly be attributed to the doped composition rather than an artifact. The two nearly identical bar-chart panels corroborate that the Ce0.1<Ce0.2≈Ce0.3 ordering (all exceeding TiO2 and control) is reproducible across triplicate runs (and apparently across two related assays/organisms, consistent with the truncated caption "...and E. coli").

What it leaves open: the EDS spectrum is qualitative and from one sample, so it does not confirm a quantitative, monotonic increase in measured Ce content across the Ce0.1→0.2→0.3 series, nor does it explain why activity plateaus (even slightly reverses) from Ce0.2 to Ce0.3. No mechanistic link between the Ce/Er peaks and the antibacterial ratio is shown — composition presence and activity trend are only correlated, not causally connected, by this evidence.

Evidence and what it supports:

- F2b (DFT charge-transfer, top/hollow/bridge sites): N-doped graphene withdraws roughly 30-37×10⁻³ e from adsorbed Zn at every site tested, versus only ~22-27×10⁻³ e for pristine graphene — a consistent, site-independent increase in charge transfer with N-doping.
- F3a/F3c (N1s NEXAFS on pristine vs. Zn-exposed CnC HS): the N1s spectrum shifts/sharpens after zinc exposure, consistent with N sites coordinating Zn (Zn-N bond formation), supporting the charge-transfer result chemically.
- F4c/F4d (galvanostatic cycling, voltage profiles): C HS (undoped) fails abruptly (CE collapses near cycle 40-50, large voltage spikes), while CnC HS (N-doped) cycles stably past 200 cycles with high, steady CE; the nucleation-overpotential comparison shows CnC HS at 39 mV vs. C HS at 43 mV.

Together these three lines (charge transfer, spectroscopic Zn-N interaction, and cycling behavior) justify the conclusion that N-doping increases zincophilicity (stronger electron donation to Zn), and this correlates with more stable, higher-efficiency Zn plating/stripping — i.e., improved reversibility of the Zn electrode.

What remains uncertain: the panels show correlation, not a proven causal chain — no panel here directly links the measured charge-transfer magnitude to the specific overpotential/CE numbers. The nucleation/growth "stage" schematics are illustrative, not independently measured. Performance at higher current densities, longer cycle counts, or full-cell scale is not shown in these panels.

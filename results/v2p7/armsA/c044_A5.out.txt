Both panels are confirmed exactly as described. F2a shows relative density vs. AlN wt% for PS and HP routes: PS falls monotonically from ~85.5% to ~70.0% (0→5 wt% AlN), while HP stays essentially flat and near-full density (~98–100%) across all AlN levels. F2b shows Vickers hardness vs. AlN wt%: PS falls from ~12 to ~6.8 GPa, while HP stays roughly flat/high (~18–20 GPa).

This upstream evidence (A, B) is directly reproduced in the figure and does support a link between the downstream density/porosity decline and the hardness decline for pressureless-sintered (PS) samples: as AlN increases, PS density drops and PS hardness drops in parallel, consistent with rising residual porosity degrading mechanical property (value). The HP contrast shows that when density is held near-full, hardness stays high regardless of AlN content, which is consistent with porosity (not AlN itself) being implicated in the PS hardness loss.

What it leaves open: these two panels give only density and hardness numbers, with no microstructural imaging (grain size, pore morphology, secondary-phase distribution) to confirm porosity is the mechanistic cause rather than a co-varying effect. Whether AlN independently degrades hardness (e.g., via reaction products) beyond its effect through density cannot be determined from these panels alone.

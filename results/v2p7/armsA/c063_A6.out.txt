The two lines of evidence are compatible but only loosely linked.

F4c (EDS, C10 only) shows Mg (green) particles with C (blue) and Co (red) signal concentrated along edges and on some faces — i.e., the catalyst/carbon phase sits at the particle surface rather than being uniformly dispersed through the bulk. F9a/F9b (JMAK plots) show that desorption in both the undoped alloy (eta = 1.59) and the CQCo-doped C10 alloy (eta = 1.32) is well described by a single linear Avrami regime (R2 > 0.98), with the doped alloy showing a lower exponent.

A surface-located catalyst is a plausible physical basis for a shifted growth exponent, since desorption nucleates at reactive interfaces and a catalyst sitting at those interfaces could alter nucleation/growth dimensionality. So the combination supports: the C10 microstructure is consistent with a catalyst-modified desorption mechanism, and the kinetic data independently confirm a mechanism change (different eta) in that same alloy.

What it leaves open: EDS is static and reported only for C10 (no comparable C0 map here), so it cannot show that surface distribution — rather than composition, defect density, or something else — is what causes eta to drop from 1.59 to 1.32. No causal or quantitative link between the mapped distribution and the specific exponent value is established.

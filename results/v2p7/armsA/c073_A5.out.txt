Combination supports: the fracture surface is spatially organized by the lamellar microstructure — crack/tear paths and grooves follow colony boundaries, and the damage features (holes, microcracks) preferentially nucleate at lamella ends/edges rather than randomly. This is consistent with a mixed fracture mode (tear-ridge/dimple character mixed with brittle-looking microcracking) whose initiation sites are structurally located by the lamellar architecture — i.e., microstructure (STR) plausibly governs where and how damage nucleates on this fracture surface.

What it leaves open: the two SEM panels alone do not establish the phase/chemical identity of the holes (second-phase decohesion vs. porosity vs. void coalescence), do not quantify hole/microcrack density or link it to strain, and give no mechanical data (strength, elongation, or behavior_class assignment) — so a causal link from this fracture morphology to a specific PRP/behavior_class outcome is not demonstrated, only a spatial/qualitative association between lamellar microstructure and local damage sites.

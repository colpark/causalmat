The optical panels (b-f) show SiCp particles as dark regions scattered through a lighter Mg-alloy matrix, with density and local clustering increasing steadily from b through f — consistent with panels d/e/f being confirmed as 15, 20, 25 wt% SiCp, and b/c presumably the lower (5, 10 wt%) members of the same series, though the provided captions only confirm d-f explicitly. This progression supports the general premise that SiC volume fraction rises monotonically across the panel set and that particles are not uniformly dispersed but form local clusters/stringers at every loading, worsening in apparent size and density at higher content (especially f).

This is consistent with, but does not by itself explain, the creep result: that 15/20/25 wt% suppress minimum creep rate below AZ91 while 5/10 wt% overlap or exceed it. A rising particle density with wt% is compatible with a volume-fraction-driven strengthening/load-transfer mechanism that only becomes effective above some threshold, but the static micrographs give no direct evidence of load transfer, interfacial bonding, dislocation pinning, or diffusion pathways — the actual mechanisms that would explain a threshold rather than a smooth trend. Nor do they explain why clustering, present at every loading including low content, does not simply degrade creep performance uniformly. So the combination supports a qualitative content/dispersion trend consistent with the creep ranking, but leaves the causal mechanism for the 5/10 wt% underperformance, and the precise b/c wt% identity, open.

The four panels are consistent with both stated measurements. F3a (bare BST) shows small, densely packed, comparatively smooth round particles at 500 nm scale. F3b (BST-SH) and F3e (BST@Ag5%) show visibly rougher, more irregular, larger, agglomerated/coated particle surfaces — consistent with claim A that modification (silane functionalization, then Ag coating) roughens and enlarges the particles relative to bare BST. F6a shows dielectric constant vs. frequency for Pure PAEN, BST@Ag1%, 3%, 5%: the curves are cleanly ordered 5%>3%>1%>>PAEN across the full frequency range, with ~100 Hz values near 6.05 (5%) and ~3.35 (PAEN), matching claim B exactly.

Together, this justifies: surface modification changes bare BST particle morphology from smooth to rough/coated/larger, and increasing Ag loading correlates monotonically with higher composite dielectric constant, well above the pure polymer.

It leaves open: F3a/b/e are powder-level SEM of isolated particles, not composite cross-sections, so they don't show microstructure or particle distribution within the PAEN matrix — the link between particle morphology and dielectric enhancement (e.g., via dispersion, interfacial effects) is not demonstrated, only implied by juxtaposition. F6a also has no dielectric data for bare BST or BST-SH alone, so whether morphology change without Ag affects dielectric constant is untested here. No PRP/value data beyond dielectric appear in these panels.

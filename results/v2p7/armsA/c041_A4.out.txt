Combination supports: F5e's EDS maps confirm that both aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 preparations (DNA and RNA aptamer variants) retain the ZIF-8 backbone (C, Zn, N filling the particle outline) while also carrying phosphorus, the aptamer's diagnostic element, present as sparse but outline-confined dots rather than absent or randomly scattered. This establishes that the aptamer is physically associated with the particle (composition/microstructure), i.e., the aptamer-MOF conjugate exists as claimed. F6b then shows that this same aptamer-MOF conjugate produces brighter (green, near the 2500 end of scale) ex vivo fluorescence in both inguinal and axillary lymph nodes than the non-aptamer BSA@ZIF-8 control (cyan, lower on scale), under both subcutaneous and footpad injection — a distribution/service_capability observation of enhanced lymph-node accumulation.

What is left open: the EDS panel only confirms that phosphorus/aptamer is present on the particle surface; it gives no quantitative loading value and includes no non-aptamer control map in this same panel to compare P signal against, so it cannot establish how much aptamer is present or that loading level tracks with targeting efficiency. Critically, nothing in either panel demonstrates a causal/mechanistic link between the aptamer's presence and the improved lymph-node accumulation — the fluorescence difference could reflect the aptamer's intended CCL21-binding function, or alternatively surface-charge, size, or dosing differences introduced by the BSA-aptamer coating itself. No fluorescence quantification, replicate counts, or statistics accompany F6b (only a qualitative color-scale comparison), and no biodistribution outside these two lymph nodes is shown. So the upstream (composition) and downstream (distribution) evidence are consistent and additive but not mechanistically connected by the data shown.

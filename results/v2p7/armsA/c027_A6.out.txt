F5a: Co2+ release into medium rises with soaking time for both doped scaffolds and is dose-ordered — 5Co-MBG releases more Co2+ than 2Co-MBG at every timepoint (1/3/7 d), while 0Co stays at zero. This matches measurement A: ion release is monotonic in scaffold Co content.

F8: VEGF secretion is non-monotonic in Co content. 2Co-MBG (~430 pg/mL) is significantly higher than blank, plain MBG, and 5Co-MBG (all ~60–115 pg/mL, P<0.05 both comparisons), but 5Co-MBG — the higher-ion-release condition — sits near baseline, statistically indistinguishable in magnitude from blank/MBG.

What the combination supports: Co doping and its associated ion release do modulate BMSC VEGF secretion, and the effect is dose-dependent with an apparent optimum at the lower Co loading (2Co); simply releasing more Co2+ (5Co, per panel A) does not translate into more VEGF.

What it leaves open: the two panels don't establish why the response is non-monotonic — panel A gives no data at concentrations between 2Co and 5Co, and neither panel addresses cytotoxicity, cell viability, or dose-response mechanism at higher Co exposure that could explain the drop-off at 5Co. No direct link is shown between the specific ion concentration cells experienced during the VEGF assay and the medium-soak measurements in A (different experimental contexts). Causal mechanism connecting ion level to VEGF signaling is not demonstrated, only correlation across three discrete formulations.

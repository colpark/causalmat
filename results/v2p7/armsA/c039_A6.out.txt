I reviewed F7b (tail-vein biomarker table), F5e (EDS elemental maps), and F7c (subcutaneous biomarker table). They are two independent lines of evidence, not a causal chain, so the upstream EDS finding does not "account for" the downstream biomarker result in a mechanistic sense — it merely accompanies it in the same paper.

F5e shows C, Zn, N distributed densely across the whole particle outline for both aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 samples, while P appears only as sparse, scattered dots — consistent with P being confined to the aptamer coating rather than the bulk ZIF-8 framework, i.e., confirmation that the aptamer is conjugated/localized on/near the particle rather than uniformly incorporated. F7b and F7c report ALT, AST, BUN, and CREA at 3–14 days after IV and subcutaneous dosing, respectively, with all values falling within the printed reference ranges for both aptamer constructs.

Combined, this supports: the material was synthesized with the aptamer present but compositionally distinct from the ZIF-8 core, and dosing with this material did not push standard liver/kidney biomarkers outside normal ranges over the observed 3–14 day window, by either route. It leaves open any mechanistic link between P's sparse/surface-like distribution and the normal biomarker readout, animal numbers/statistics, effects beyond 14 days, other organs, and whether EDS mapping (semi-quantitative) reliably reflects true P loading rather than detection-limit sparsity.

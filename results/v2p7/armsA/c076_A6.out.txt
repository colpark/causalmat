F3b shows bismuthene drives DPBF absorbance down to ~0.73 in 30 min versus ~0.93 in water, an in vitro, cell-free readout that supports bismuthene having intrinsic photoreactive/ROS (singlet-oxygen-like) generating capacity — a mechanistic PRP for photodynamic activity. F5d shows the downstream in vivo picture: BNs-BSA+808nm tumors are suppressed early but 4/5 regrow by ~day 14–21, while BNs-BSA+Lasers (dual 660/808nm) keeps all five at ~0 volume through day ~20.

Combined, the two panels justify only a general, qualitative link: bismuthene's demonstrated ROS-generating capability under light is a plausible basis for the partial tumor suppression seen with 808nm alone, and the dual-laser condition's full, durable ablation is consistent with an added or complementary photothermal/photodynamic contribution from the second wavelength. What the evidence does not establish is why the 808nm-alone effect is incomplete rather than durable — the DPBF assay is a bench dispersion measurement with no stated wavelength-matching to the in vivo laser, no dose, depth, or biodistribution information, and no companion 660nm ROS/heat data. So it cannot adjudicate whether regrowth reflects insufficient ROS yield at 808nm, incomplete light penetration into tumor tissue, surviving subpopulations, or a service_capability gap distinct from photoreactivity. The mechanism-outcome link is directional/plausible, not quantitatively demonstrated.

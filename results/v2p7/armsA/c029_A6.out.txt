Both panels were opened and checked against the numeric claims.

F7b (voltage vs. PZT content) shows peak voltages of 7.5, 12.5, 26.5, 32.0, 43.0, 62.0 V for the 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 wt% PZT fiber-based PENGs — an exact match to measurement A. F7e (power vs. load resistance) shows the fiber film's power curve peaking at 136.9 uW at 10^7 Ω (10 MOhm), with the cast film's much lower curve peaking at 21.025 uW near the same load region — an exact match to measurement B.

What this combination supports: within this fiber-PENG system, increasing PZT filler content raises peak output voltage monotonically across the tested range, and the fiber-film architecture delivers substantially higher peak electrical power (~6.5x) than the cast-film comparator at the same optimal external load. So the upstream transport measurements (piezo_output) are directly and specifically the source of the downstream PRP/value and PRF/service_capability figures — the numbers are traceable one-to-one to the plotted curves.

What it leaves open: the panels show only the described trend and peak values, not the underlying mechanism (e.g., dielectric constant, poling degree, or effective piezoelectric coefficient) linking PZT content or fiber structure to output. There is no indication of replicate measurements, error bars, or statistical spread, so robustness/reproducibility is unclear. Nor do the panels address behavior beyond the tested composition/load range (saturation, degradation) or long-term device stability. The two panels are consistent with each other but were not shown to come from strictly identical sample batches beyond the stated f-P0.10 designation.

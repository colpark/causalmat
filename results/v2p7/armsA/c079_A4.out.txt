Panels b–f show a monotonic increase in dark-particle (SiCp) density from a sparse field with modest local clustering (b) to a dense, heavily clustered field (f), tracking the printed d=15%, e=20%, f=25 wt% labels (b, c are presumably the lower, unlabeled fractions in this set). This dispersion trend is consistent with claim A (particles spread field-wide with local clusters, clusters intensifying at higher loading). The YS series (93→142 MPa) rises monotonically over the same 0–25 wt% range.

Combined, the evidence supports a correlation: as SiCp content and areal particle density increase across b–f, yield strength increases in step, and this rise is not obviously undermined by the increasing clustering seen in d–f, since YS keeps climbing even where agglomerates are visually more prominent. That is what the pairing justifies — a consistent, monotonic co-trend between particle loading/microstructure and strength.

What it leaves open: the micrographs alone give no particle size, volume-fraction, porosity, interfacial-bonding, or grain-size data, so they cannot establish the strengthening mechanism (Orowan, load transfer, grain refinement, or CTE mismatch) nor rule out that clustering is locally detrimental even while bulk YS rises. Causal attribution of the YS trend to the observed microstructure change is therefore only supported qualitatively, not mechanistically or quantitatively.

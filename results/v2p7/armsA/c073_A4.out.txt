Both panels viewed. F2c (low-mag, 100 µm scale) shows a fracture surface with tear ridges and elongated grooves that visibly follow the lamellar colony structure of the alloy, plus scattered dark holes distributed across colonies. F2d (high-mag, 10 µm scale) zooms into the same type of region and marks (red arrows) small holes and (red boxes) microcracks, both located at the ends/edges of individual lamellae, with a blue circle marking a larger hole.

Together these two panels support a fractographic observation: crack/void formation is spatially associated with the lamellar microstructure — tearing propagates along lamellar colonies (low mag) and microvoids/microcracks nucleate preferentially at lamella ends and interfaces (high mag). This is consistent with a microstructure-guided, interface-associated damage mechanism (STR/microstructure → damage link) and a locally ductile-tearing fracture character.

What this leaves open: the images are qualitative SEM fractography from one specimen/region, with no quantitative measure (crack/void density, size statistics) tying damage extent to a specific behavior_class or PRP. There is no comparison across alloys, compositions, or loading conditions to show this pattern is what distinguishes behavior classes, and no direct mechanical data (strength, elongation) is shown alongside the fractographs. So the evidence supports "microstructure sites damage nucleation," not a demonstrated causal or classifying link to a particular PRP/behavior_class.

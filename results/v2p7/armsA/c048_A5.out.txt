The four panels: F3a shows bare BST nanoparticles as small, densely packed, comparatively smooth spheres; F3b (BST-SH) and F3e (BST@Ag5%) show visibly rougher, more irregular, larger-looking agglomerated particle surfaces — consistent with the SEM claim (A) that modification/coating roughens and enlarges the particles. F5a's DSC traces confirm claim (B): pure PAEN's glass transition sits at 176 C, while the BST@Ag composites show depressed transitions at 146, 149, and 157 C, rising modestly with Ag loading (1%→3%→5%) but staying well below the neat polymer.

Together, this justifies a morphology-plus-thermal-signature story: surface modification changed particle texture, and incorporating these modified particles into PAEN measurably lowers and shifts the polymer's Tg in a loading-dependent way, consistent with altered particle-matrix interaction. What it leaves open: these SEM images are single-magnification surface snapshots (not cross-sections or quantified particle-size/dispersion statistics), so they cannot establish uniformity of distribution through the bulk matrix. Nothing in these four panels shows mechanical, electrical, or other performance/value (PRP) data, so no causal link from morphology/Tg shift to any downstream property or value claim can be drawn from this evidence alone — that connection is unsettled by what's shown here.

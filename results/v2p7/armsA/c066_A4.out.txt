Both panels reviewed (F6a XRD, F2b hardness vs. AlN in HP series).

What's supported: The HP5 XRD pattern (Fig. 6a) indexes only to ZrB2, SiC, graphite, and BN—no AlN or Al2O3 reflections appear at the highest AlN loading (5 wt%). Combined with the HP hardness curve (Fig. 2b, red trace), which rises from ~18.0 GPa at 0% to a peak ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt% AlN and then eases to ~19.4 and ~19.2 GPa at 3 and 5 wt%—all still above the 0% baseline—the pair supports that whatever AlN does in the hot-pressed composites, it is not persisting as a distinct AlN (or Al2O3) phase detectable by XRD, and it is not degrading hardness relative to the unadditized material even at 5 wt%. So the absence of AlN/Al2O3 reflections is consistent with AlN being consumed (reacted, dissolved, or below detection limit) rather than remaining as a separate hardness-lowering inclusion.

What's left open: XRD was only shown for the HP5 sample, not for 0/1/3 wt%, so the phase evolution across the series that might explain the rise-then-plateau shape of the hardness curve (grain refinement, densification, or a reacted/interfacial phase invisible to XRD) is not established. The identity/mechanism behind the 1 wt% hardness peak remains uncertain from this evidence alone.

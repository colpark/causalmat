Both panels were opened and confirm the reported numbers. F4a (apparent porosity vs. temperature, S1/S2/S3) shows a clear downward trend for all three series: S1 drops steeply from ~8.7% at 1400C to ~0.4% at 1600C; S2 falls from ~7.9% at 1400C to ~0.7% at 1700C; S3 declines more gradually from ~11.2% to ~7.7% over its narrower 1400-1600C range. F5 (Vickers hardness vs. temperature) shows the mirror-image rise for all three series: S1 from ~9.3 to ~15.6 GPa, S2 from ~9.4 to ~14.8 GPa, S3 from ~9.0 to ~14.1 GPa, tracking the same temperature axis and the same three-series scheme as F4a.

What this combination supports: within each series, increasing sintering temperature is co-observed with decreasing apparent porosity and increasing hardness, consistent with densification (pore elimination) accompanying temperature increase and correlating with hardness gain — a plausible microstructure-to-mechanical-property (porosity-to-hardness) relationship. The three series (S1, S2, S3) diverge in their porosity floor and in the temperature at which the steepest porosity drop occurs (S1 and S2 fall sharply by 1600C, S3 stays higher through 1600C), and hardness likewise separates by series at high temperature, so the two figures are consistent with each other and with the upstream porosity/hardness numbers given.

What this leaves open: neither panel nor caption states a causal mechanism linking porosity reduction to hardness increase, nor controls for other variables (e.g., grain size, phase content, binder chemistry) that differ across S1/S2/S3 and could independently drive hardness. The figures establish correlation across temperature, not a demonstrated causal pathway from porosity to hardness, and do not address STR (strength) or PRP/value claims directly — those remain unaddressed by this pair of panels.

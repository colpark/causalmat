The upstream EDS evidence and the downstream blood-chemistry evidence answer two different questions and do not mechanically account for each other.

F5e shows C, Zn, and N filling the whole particle outline for both aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8 samples, while P appears only as sparse, isolated dots within the same outline — consistent with the caption's claim that C/Zn/N come from the ZIF-8 framework and P is contributed specifically (and sparsely, i.e., low-copy-number) by the conjugated aptamer. This is purely a compositional/structural characterization of the particle, confirming aptamer presence on/in the MOF.

F7b/F7c report ALT, AST, BUN, and CREA values at 3–14 days after intravenous or subcutaneous dosing of aptamer-BSA@ZIF-8, all falling within the printed reference ranges — supporting an absence of detectable hepatic or renal toxicity over that window, in both routes.

Together, the two datasets support that the aptamer-conjugated ZIF-8 construct verified by EDS was the material subsequently dosed and found non-toxic by standard liver/kidney panels — i.e., a qualified, aptamer-loaded particle with acceptable short-term systemic safety. But the EDS mapping does not explain or predict the biomarker result: it says nothing about biodistribution, dose, clearance, or why blood chemistry stayed normal. Left open: mechanistic link between composition and safety, histopathology/organ accumulation beyond blood markers, sample sizes/statistics, longer-term chronic effects, and whether P sparsity indicates low aptamer loading with functional consequences.

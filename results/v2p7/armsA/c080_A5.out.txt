All five panels reviewed (F1b–e optical micrographs, F5a SEM fractograph).

F1b–e show SiC particles (dark) dispersed through the Mg-alloy matrix at what appear to be increasing volume fractions (captions for d/e cite "15 and" / "20 and" — likely wt.% or vol.% labels cut off), with fairly uniform scatter but visible local clustering/stringering of particles, especially in c–e. This matches claim A. F5a shows a fracture surface with a mixed morphology: fibrous/tear-ridge regions (ductile matrix tearing) interspersed with smoother, flatter facet-like patches — matching claim B, and consistent with a mixed ductile-matrix/brittle-particle (or particle-decohesion) fracture mode.

Together, the two lines of evidence are compatible: a matrix containing scattered, locally clustered hard SiC particles is a plausible microstructural cause of a fracture surface combining ductile tearing with flatter facets (particle clusters acting as crack-initiation/brittle sites). But the images do not establish this causally. F5a's caption ("AZ91/10 SiCp") does not clearly match any specific optical panel — b–e are not labeled with matching fractions in the fragments given, and no panel is explicit for 10%. There is no spatial registration linking specific clusters seen in b–e to the facet regions in F5a, and no chemistry/EDS confirming the flat facets are SiC-related rather than intermetallic or matrix cleavage. So the pairing supports plausibility of a distribution→fracture-mode link but leaves the direct correspondence, composition match, and facet identity unconfirmed.

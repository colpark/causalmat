Findings on the actual question:

F4c confirms claim A: the EDS map of the C10 alloy shows Mg (green) grains with C (blue) and Co (red) speckled predominantly along particle edges and on some faces — i.e., the catalyst additive sits on/near particle surfaces rather than being dissolved into the bulk. F6a and F6b confirm claim B: at 360 °C the second, diffusion-controlled absorption stage takes 66 min for C0 but only 11 min for C10, a roughly six-fold acceleration once the catalyst is present.

Together, the two pieces of evidence support a plausible structure–property link: a catalyst phase decorating grain/particle surfaces coincides with markedly faster diffusion-limited hydrogen uptake, consistent with surface-catalyzed dissociation/shortened diffusion paths improving absorption kinetics (the downstream "value"/PRP claim). This is what the combination justifies — a surface-distribution-to-kinetics correlation for this specific pair of samples.

What it leaves open: the EDS map is shown only for C10, so there is no matching micrograph proving C0 lacks such surface decoration — the comparison is inferred, not directly imaged side by side. The evidence also does not establish causality (versus confounds like particle-size or porosity changes from adding 10 wt% C@Co), nor does it quantify coverage density against kinetic rate. No mechanism for how surface Co/C sites accelerate diffusion is shown in these panels.

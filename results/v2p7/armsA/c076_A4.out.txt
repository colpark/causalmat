What the combination supports: the ROS-generation evidence (F3b) is consistent with bismuthene having a genuine photodynamic mechanism, and the tumour data (F5d) show that adding this mechanism (dual-laser group) achieves durable, complete tumour control where single-wavelength (808 nm) treatment does not — i.e., the combined regimen's superior durability is compatible with a photodynamic contribution beyond photothermal ablation alone.

What it leaves open: F3b is an in vitro dispersion assay with unspecified irradiation match to the 808 nm in vivo regimen; it does not measure ROS generation in tumour tissue, at 808 nm specifically, or at the doses/depths used in vivo. So it cannot establish that insufficient ROS (rather than incomplete photothermal ablation, heat penetration, or dosing) caused the day-14 regrowth under 808 nm alone. The mechanistic link between measured ROS capacity and the specific in vivo failure mode remains unconfirmed by these two panels.

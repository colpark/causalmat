F4a confirms porosity falling with temperature for all three series (S1 8.7→~0.4%, S2 7.9→~0.7%, S3 11.2→~7.7%), matching the stated PHYS:porosity values, though the curves show S1 dropping sharply between 1500 and 1600°C while S3 declines much more gradually and never reaches low porosity even at 1700°C. F5 confirms hardness rising with temperature for all three series (S1 9.3→15.6, S2 9.4→14.8, S3 9.0→14.1 GPa), matching MECH:hardness, with S1 and S3 tracking closely up to 1600°C and S2's curve extending further (to 1700°C) at generally lower hardness than S1 at matched temperatures.

What the combination supports: within each series, as sintering temperature increases, porosity decreases and hardness increases in parallel — a co-occurrence consistent with densification (pore elimination) accompanying hardness gains, i.e., the two figures together are compatible with reduced porosity/improved microstructure being associated with higher PRP (hardness) as temperature rises.

What it leaves open: neither figure establishes a causal mechanism linking porosity reduction to hardness increase, nor rules out other temperature-driven changes (grain growth, phase transformation, binder/matrix chemistry, cBN content differences between S1/S2/S3) as the actual driver of hardness. The series also differ in composition/processing (different temperature ranges, different porosity floors), so cross-series comparison at a single temperature is confounded — S2 has lower hardness than S1 at 1600°C despite comparable porosity trends. No statistical or regression link (e.g., porosity vs. hardness scatter) is shown, so the strength or functional form of the porosity-hardness relationship is not quantified by these panels.

All five panels (b-f) were viewed. They show optical micrographs of Mg-matrix composites with increasing SiCp loading, with dark angular particles distributed across the field and visible local clustering/stringering that becomes denser and more pronounced from b through f (consistent with rising SiCp wt%, panels d/e/f captioned 15/20/25 wt%).

The combination supports a straightforward correlation: as SiCp content increases, particle areal density in the micrographs increases in step with the reported monotonic rise in yield strength (93→142 MPa across 0-25 wt%). This is consistent with particle addition contributing to strengthening (via some combination of load transfer, Orowan effect, or CTE-mismatch dislocation generation), and shows that the microstructural change (more/denser particles, more visible clustering) does not suppress the strengthening trend — YS keeps rising even as clustering becomes visually more evident at higher loadings.

What this leaves open: low-magnification optical micrographs cannot identify which strengthening mechanism dominates, cannot quantify actual clustering severity, interparticle spacing, or porosity, and cannot establish causation beyond correlation — no grain-size, dislocation-density, or fractographic data is present in these panels. Whether clustering at 20-25 wt% eventually degrades properties (e.g., ductility, or a plateau/decline in strength beyond 25 wt%) is also not addressed by this evidence.

Both panels were opened and inspected directly.

F4b shows TiO2 with a sharp absorption edge near 410 nm and negligible absorbance beyond ~450 nm, while all three Er0.5Ce-Ti-O co-doped curves show a red-shifted edge and sustained absorbance tailing out past 600–800 nm (plus small Er f-f peaks near 520/650 nm) — consistent with claim A. F8 shows TiO2 degrading MO the least at every time point (~20% at 60 min), while all three co-doped samples degrade substantially more (~31–46% at 60 min), consistent with claim B's TiO2-vs-doped contrast.

Together, the two panels support a qualitative link: doping narrows the bandgap/extends visible absorption, and doped samples also degrade MO faster than undoped TiO2. This justifies the general conclusion that co-doping improves both visible-light absorption and photocatalytic activity relative to bare TiO2.

What is left open: the fine ranking among the three doped compositions is not cleanly supported by F4b. In the visible region the Ce0.1, Ce0.2, and Ce0.3 curves overlap closely with no clear, consistent separation, so the absorption panel does not visibly distinguish which composition "absorbs most" in a way that matches the degradation order (Ce0.2 > Ce0.3 > Ce0.1) reported in F8. No mechanistic link (charge separation, recombination, surface area) is shown, so absorption-edge extension is only a plausible, not demonstrated, cause of the degradation ranking.

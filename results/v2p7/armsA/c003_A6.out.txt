Yes, the upstream XRD evidence is sufficient to account for the downstream PCT observation, within limits.

F3a shows that milling C0 with 10 wt% Co (giving C10) reproduces every C0 reflection (CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17, Mg) with only one weak added line (Co, ~44.5°) — i.e., phase identity of the matrix/hydride-forming phases is preserved; Co appears as an added but largely unreacted second phase, not a new compound. F11a and F11b confirm each alloy shows a single flat plateau per isotherm (320/340/360 °C), and the plateau pressures track closely between the two alloys (~0.15, ~0.27–0.3, ~0.4–0.5 MPa), consistent with statement B.

Since plateau pressure reflects the thermodynamics (enthalpy/entropy) of the same absorbing/desorbing phase, unchanged phase identity plausibly explains why the plateau pressures are nearly unchanged after Co addition — the combination supports "Co addition does not alter the phase-level thermodynamics governing plateau pressure."

What it leaves open: XRD/PCT together say nothing about kinetics (rate of (de)hydrogenation) or the modest apparent capacity difference visible between F11a (~5.2 wt%) and F11b (~4.7–5 wt%), and cannot confirm Co is fully inert rather than a minor catalytic surface additive.

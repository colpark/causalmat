ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Both panels open as described. F3b (Fig 3b): bismuthene drives DPBF relative absorbance down to ~0.73 by 30 min, while water controls only fall to ~0.93 — confirming bismuthene generates reactive oxygen species (a photosensitizing/PRP-type material property) far more effectively than the no-photosensitizer control. F5d shows six individual-mouse panels; the "BNs-BSA+808nm" group has four of five curves flat near zero before rising sharply after ~day 14–15 (matching the stated regrowth), whereas the "BNs-BSA+Lasers" (combined 660/808nm) group stays at ~0 across all five mice through day 20.

The combination supports that bismuthene possesses genuine ROS-generating capacity in solution, consistent with a photodynamic mechanism contributing to the therapy, and that this mechanism correlates with better tumor control when both lasers are used together versus 808nm alone. It does not establish causation for the downstream difference: F3b is a cell-free DPBF assay with no stated laser wavelength match to the 808nm-alone in vivo arm, no dosing/irradiance link, and no direct test of why 660nm co-irradiation prevents the regrowth seen with 808nm monotherapy. Whether the regrowth reflects insufficient ROS versus insufficient photothermal effect, incomplete tumor coverage, or dosing is left open by this evidence pair.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
F3b (UVVIS) shows bismuthene accelerates DPBF photobleaching relative to water (falling to ~0.73 vs ~0.93 relative absorbance at 30 min), establishing that bismuthene generates reactive oxygen species (singlet oxygen) under laser irradiation — a generic photodynamic capability. F5d (individual tumor curves) shows that BNs‑BSA+808 nm alone lets 4/5 tumors regrow by ~day 14–22, whereas BNs‑BSA+combined 660/808 nm lasers keeps all 5 tumors flat at ~0 through day ~20.

Together, the two panels support that bismuthene's demonstrated ROS-generating (photodynamic) activity is a plausible mechanistic contributor to the antitumor effect seen when both lasers are combined — the UVVIS assay gives independent evidence that the "value" (PDT capability) claimed for bismuthene is real. But the combination does not establish why 808 nm alone is insufficient for durable control while adding the 660 nm channel is: the DPBF assay doesn't specify which wavelength/dose produced the absorbance drop, doesn't measure photothermal contribution, and offers no in vivo dosimetry. So it accounts for bismuthene having PDT capacity in principle, but leaves open the mechanistic split between PDT and PTT contributions, why single-wavelength treatment fails long-term, and whether ROS generation alone (versus combined heat+ROS) is what prevents regrowth.

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Yes, within limits. F3a shows the milled C10 pattern (top, blue) carries the same indexed peaks (1 CeMg12, 2 Y5Mg24, 3 Ce2Mg17, 4 Mg) as the C0 pattern (bottom, black), with only one extra, weak line (5, Co) near 44-45 deg — consistent with claim A: Co sits as a separate, minor phase rather than altering the host hydride-forming phases. F11a (0 wt% C@Co) and F11b (+10 wt% C@Co) both show single flat-ish plateaus at 320/340/360 C in roughly the same 0.15-0.5 MPa band, consistent with claim B that plateau pressures are nearly unchanged by the Co addition.

Together this supports: catalyst addition preserves phase identity of the Mg-Ce-Y hydride system and leaves its equilibrium plateau pressure (thermodynamic value) essentially the same — i.e., any benefit from C@Co is not achieved by changing which phases form or their thermodynamic hydrogen affinity.

Left open: F11b's plateaus (especially 360 C) are visibly less flat/more sloped than F11a's, so "nearly the same pressure" is approximate, not exact equality, and kinetic or homogeneity differences aren't ruled out. The single weak Co line also doesn't establish Co's dispersion, reactivity, or behavior over cycling — only its presence as-milled.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Yes, the two upstream measurements are mutually consistent and jointly support the downstream claim that adding 10 wt% C@Co does not change the phase identity or the thermodynamic plateau pressures of the Mg90Ce5Y5 hydrogen-storage alloy. F3a (XRD) shows the milled C10 pattern retains all four host phases (CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17, Mg) seen in C0, with only one weak extra peak (labeled 5, Co) near 44.5°, indicating the additive is present in small amount without forming new bulk compounds or disrupting the host structure. Together, unchanged phase identity (structure) plausibly accounts for unchanged plateau pressure (thermodynamic value), since plateau pressure is set by the hydride-forming phases, which the additive does not alter.

What is left open: the panels don't establish a causal mechanism linking the Co line's presence to the pressure match (only correlation), don't quantify Co's actual loading beyond "weak," and don't address whether capacity, plateau slope/flatness, hysteresis, or kinetics differ between C0 and C10 — F11b's plateau appears to end at slightly lower H2 content than F11a, but no capacity value is claimed here.

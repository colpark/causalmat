ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
All three panels confirm the stated measurements. F3e shows spot heating scaling with concentration (200>100>50>25 ug/mL, H2O flat) over 0-6 min, colorbar 25-60C. F3g shows the same 200 ug/mL sample heating with laser power (0.5-1.5 W), 0-6 min. This upstream evidence (dose- and power-dependent photothermal conversion in vitro, plus elevated in-vivo tumor heating with the nanomaterial vs control) directly supports the claim that the material's photothermal property (PRP/value: temperature rise under NIR laser) is concentration- and power-tunable and translates in vivo to a therapeutically relevant hyperthermia range (>45-50C, associated with ablation). It justifies saying the material converts light to heat in a dose-dependent way and that this heating is reproduced, and amplified over PBS, in the tumor setting.

It leaves open the causal link to actual service capability/outcome (PRF): none of these three panels show tumor shrinkage, survival, histology, or any therapeutic endpoint — they are thermal-imaging/temperature data only. So the combination supports "material generates tunable, therapeutically relevant heat in vivo" but does not by itself establish that this heat produced tumor ablation or improved treatment efficacy; that inference requires additional (unreviewed) efficacy panels.

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Both panels are visually confirmed. F1a (DFT ΔG plot) matches claim A: CoCrOOH sits lowest on the Co2+→Co3+ axis (~1.05 eV), CoFeOOH lowest on the Co3+→Co4+ axis (~6.2 eV), CoFeCrOOH intermediate on both (~1.2, ~6.4 eV), and Co(OH)2 highest (~6.7 eV). F5c confirms B: the black Co5Fe3Cr2 curve crosses j=10 mA/cm2 first, with η10=452 mV marked, ahead of IrO2 and the other Co-Fe-Cr/Co5Fe5/Co5Cr5 curves.

What the combination supports: the ternary Co-Fe-Cr composition is both computationally predicted to have a more balanced (not extreme-high, not extreme-low) two-step Co redox energetics and is empirically the fastest catalyst to reach the 10 mA/cm2 benchmark in neutral electrolyte. This is consistent with — but does not prove — a mechanistic link between moderated Co-redox thermodynamics and improved OER kinetics.

What it leaves open: the DFT panel's "CoFeCrOOH" is not labeled with the same 5:3:2 stoichiometry used in the LSV panel (Co5Fe3Cr2, Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5), so the two panels are not guaranteed to describe the identical composition — the pathway/performance link rests on a name-level, not a stoichiometry-matched, correspondence. Also, DFT free energy alone does not account for other factors (conductivity, active surface area, real defect structure) that jointly determine measured overpotential, so causal attribution of the LSV ranking solely to the computed ΔG trend is not established by this evidence.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Both panels verified by direct image read.

F1a (DFT free energies): x-axis = ΔG(Co2+→Co3+), y-axis = ΔG(Co3+→Co4+). CoCrOOH sits lowest on the x-axis (~1.05 eV, lowest Co2+→Co3+ barrier). CoFeOOH sits lowest on the y-axis (~6.2 eV, lowest Co3+→Co4+ barrier). CoFeCrOOH sits between them on both axes (~1.2–1.3 eV, ~6.4 eV). Co(OH)2 is highest on both (~1.4 eV, ~6.68 eV). This matches claim A exactly.

F5c (neutral LSV, 0.5 M KHCO3): among Co5Fe3Cr2, Co5Fe5, Co5Cr5, Co, and IrO2, the Co5Fe3Cr2 curve (black) rises earliest and crosses the 10 mA/cm2 dashed line first, with η10 = 452 mV labeled at that crossing; IrO2 (grey) requires higher potential to reach 10 mA/cm2. This matches claim B.

Does upstream account for downstream? The combination supports a thermodynamic rationale: CoFeCrOOH's intermediate (neither best-nor-worst) position on both elementary-step free energies is consistent with a mechanistic story for why incorporating both Fe and Cr into the ternary oxyhydroxide (as in Co5Fe3Cr2) could balance the two OER-relevant steps and improve activity relative to unmodified Co(OH)2. It leaves open several things: (1) F1a's species are qualitative labels (CoCrOOH, CoFeOOH, CoFeCrOOH, Co(OH)2), not the specific stoichiometry Co5Fe3Cr2 tested in F5c, so the exact correspondence is inferred, not shown directly; (2) IrO2 is not a DFT panel entry at all, so the LSV comparison to IrO2 has no computed counterpart; (3) the DFT is a static thermodynamic calculation, not conducted under the neutral-electrolyte condition of the LSV experiment, so it cannot by itself explain kinetics, η10 values, or the specific neutral-pH performance ordering. The evidence supports a plausible compositional/thermodynamic trend consistent with the activity trend, but does not establish a direct causal or quantitative link between the computed intermediate energetics and the observed neutral-electrolyte η10 advantage over IrO2.

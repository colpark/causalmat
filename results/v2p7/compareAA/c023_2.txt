ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Figure 2a (relative density) and 2b (Vickers hardness), HP series only, plotted against AlN content: a) HP rises from ~97.5% (0 wt%) to ~100% (1 wt%), ~99.3% (3 wt%), ~100% (5 wt%); b) HP hardness rises from ~18.0 GPa (0 wt%) to ~20.3 GPa (1 wt%), then falls to ~19.4 GPa (3 wt%) and ~19.2 GPa (5 wt%). Both panels match the stated PHYS:density and MECH:hardness values closely.

The combination supports densification (porosity elimination) as the driver of the hardness jump from 0 to 1 wt% AlN: relative density climbs sharply from ~97.5% to ~100% over that interval, and hardness rises in step, consistent with residual porosity limiting hardness at 0 wt%.

It does not account for the hardness decline from 1 to 5 wt% AlN: over that range HP relative density stays essentially flat and near-full (~99.3–100%), so porosity/density cannot explain the drop from 20.3 to 19.2 GPa. That decline is left open — the panels give no microstructural detail (grain size, AlN distribution, phase boundaries) to identify its cause, and neither caption nor the plotted curves address it. The PS (pressureless) series shows a different, monotonic co-decline of both density and hardness, but that is a separate process condition and does not bear on the HP trend.

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Both panels confirm the two measurements as stated. F5d: Rct climbs sharply from ~3 to ~17-20 ohm by cycle ~60, keeps rising with wide error bars to ~24-28 ohm by cycle 150-250; Re and Rfilm stay flat and low (~0.5 and ~2-4 ohm) throughout. F2a: the "w/ HCl" (red) trace drops steeply from ~90 to ~65 mAh/g over the first ~20-50 cycles, then declines slowly to ~55 mAh/g by cycle 250, with CE pinned near 100% across all three synthesis conditions shown.

What the combination supports: the timing of the two curves is compatible — both the steep phase and the subsequent slow phase of capacity fade co-occur with the steep-then-plateauing rise in Rct, while Re and Rfilm stay essentially unchanged. This is consistent with charge-transfer (interfacial) resistance growth, rather than bulk electrolyte or surface-film resistance, being the resistive component that tracks the fade pattern. Near-100% CE indicates the loss is not a coulombic/side-reaction efficiency problem, narrowing the fade to a capacity/kinetic-accessibility issue, for which rising Rct is a plausible correlate.

What it leaves open: this is temporal correlation only, not a mechanistic or causal link — no measurement ties Rct rise directly to loss of active capacity on the same electrodes/cycles, and F2a's three-curve comparison (synthesis routes) isn't matched one-to-one with EIS data for each route. The large Rct error bars also limit how tightly the correspondence can be read quantitatively.

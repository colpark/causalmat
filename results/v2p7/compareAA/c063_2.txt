ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Partially, and only suggestively. F9a/F9b (JMAK plots, ln[-ln(1-a)] vs ln t) show good linearity (R²>0.98) across four temperatures for both compositions, with Avrami exponent η = 1.59 vs 1.32.

Combination supports: it is physically plausible that a surface-concentrated catalytic phase (C, Co) alters nucleation/growth character during desorption enough to shift η downward — a lower exponent is often read as fewer effective nucleation sites or lower-dimensionality growth, consistent with a phase sitting at surfaces/edges rather than penetrating the particle.

So: surface segregation is documented, but its causal link to the specific Avrami-exponent change is not established here.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
All three panels reviewed (F9a, F9b JMAK plots; F4c EDS map).

This is a reasonable qualitative link between catalyst placement and altered kinetics.

What it leaves open: there is no EDS map of C0 to confirm the surface distribution is specific to (or absent in) the uncatalyzed alloy; the panels don't quantitatively connect surface coverage/density to the magnitude of eta; and JMAK linearity alone doesn't uniquely identify the physical mechanism eta reflects. The mechanistic attribution remains inferential, not directly demonstrated by these three panels.

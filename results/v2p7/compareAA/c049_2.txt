THE QUESTION

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Findings from the four panels (paper s42114-021-00366-2):

F3a (bare BST): smooth, densely packed spherical nanoparticles, roughly 70 nm, scale bar 500 nm.
F3b (BST-SH, i.e., silane-modified): particles visibly rougher-surfaced and somewhat larger/more clumped than F3a — consistent with a surface coating.
F3e (BST@Ag5%): similarly rough, coated, agglomerated morphology, comparable to F3b, not obviously coarser than the 5% loading alone would suggest.
F7b (breaking elongation bar chart): monotonic decrease — Pure PAEN 6.9%, BST@Ag1% 5.8%, BST@Ag3% 4.5%, BST@Ag5% 4.4% — errors bars shown, largest drop between 1% and 3%, near-plateau from 3% to 5%.

What the combination supports: the SEM series (A) confirms that BST particles undergo a real, visible morphological change upon surface modification/Ag decoration — from smooth ~70 nm spheres to rougher, coated, larger-looking aggregates — establishing that a physical/interfacial change accompanies functionalization. The mechanical data (B) independently show elongation falling as filler content rises. Together this is consistent with a plausible mechanism (rougher/coated/agglomerated filler disrupting matrix ductility), but the two datasets do not directly connect.

What it leaves open: SEM images are shown only for bare BST, BST-SH, and BST@Ag5% — there is no micrograph for BST@Ag1% or BST@Ag3%, the intermediate loadings where elongation is actually measured, so the SEM series cannot be matched point-by-point to the elongation trend. The panels also give no quantitative measure of roughness, coating thickness, particle size, or dispersion/agglomeration state that could be correlated with the elongation numbers, and no panel addresses composite-level microstructure (particle distribution within the PAEN matrix, interfacial voids, etc.) — only the isolated filler particles. So the evidence supports that surface modification changes particle morphology and that elongation drops with Ag content, but it does not establish a mechanistic or quantitative link between the two observations; that connection is asserted by the paper's narrative, not directly shown by these four panels.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
All four panels reviewed.

F3a shows bare BST as small (~70 nm-scale, sub-resolution at this 500 nm scale bar) smooth spherical particles, uniformly packed. F3b (BST-SH) shows particles that are visibly larger, rougher, and more agglomerated — consistent with a surface coating. F3e (BST@Ag5%) shows an even more textured, densely packed, rough surface, consistent with further surface decoration/aggregation at the highest Ag loading. F7b shows breaking elongation falling monotonically from 6.9% (pure PAEN) to 5.8%, 4.5%, 4.4% as Ag content rises from 1% to 5%.

Together, the SEM progression (bare → coated/rough → more aggregated at 5% Ag) and the monotonic elongation drop are qualitatively consistent: increasing particle modification/roughening and apparent aggregation track with increasing loading and with reduced ductility, supporting a general "more filler surface modification/aggregation → stiffer, more brittle composite" narrative.

This is as far as the evidence goes. The panels do not show SEM images at the specific 1% or 3% Ag loadings (only bare BST, BST-SH, and BST@Ag5% are shown), so the SEM series cannot be matched point-for-point to the four elongation values. No cross-sectional/fracture-surface or dispersion-quantification images are given, so interfacial adhesion, void formation, or actual dispersion uniformity — the usual mechanistic link to embrittlement — remain unverified by these images; the correspondence is suggestive, not demonstrated.

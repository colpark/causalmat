ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
The evidence only partially accounts for the STR decline. F4b shows the neat PAEN fracture surface (rough, particle-free, as expected for a matrix control). F4c (BST@Ag1%) and F4e (BST@Ag5%) show embedded spherical particles distributed across the fracture surface without visible large agglomerates or clustering — this is consistent with claim A (even dispersion, no large agglomerates) even at the highest loading shown. F7a confirms the tensile-strength trend in claim B: 79.3, 78.3, 74.4, 73.5 MPa for PAEN, 1%, 3%, 5% Ag, a monotonic decline with increasing filler content.

Together, the panels support that good particle dispersion (no gross agglomeration) coexists with a steady drop in tensile strength as loading increases — i.e., dispersion quality alone does not explain or prevent the strength loss. What the evidence leaves open: the SEM images cannot show interfacial adhesion strength, debonding, voids, or stress-concentration effects at the particle-matrix boundary, which would be the more likely mechanistic driver of the strength decline. No panel for the 3% composite is included, so the intermediate dispersion state is not directly visualized. Causal attribution of the strength drop to filler rigidity, interfacial weakness, or particle content per se cannot be settled from these images alone.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
The four panels are SEM fracture-surface micrographs (1 µm scale) and a tensile-strength bar chart, not a single unified figure set. F4c (BST@Ag1%/PAEN) and F4e (BST@Ag5%/PAEN) show small, fairly evenly distributed particulate features on the fracture surface without conspicuous large agglomerated clumps — F4e shows somewhat more numerous/visible particles than F4c but still no obvious clustering into big agglomerates. What it leaves open: F4b (BST-SH/PAEN) is a different, non-Ag-doped composite not represented among the four bars in F7a, so it cannot be tied to the tensile trend at all. The images alone also cannot establish that dispersion (rather than interfacial adhesion or intrinsic filler stiffness) causes the strength decline — only correlation across two of four data points is visible.

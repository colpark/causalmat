ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Both panels reviewed. F3 (polarization curves + fitted values) shows Mg-Zn-Y-Nd with Ecorr = -1.49 V and Icorr = 24.93 µA/cm2, versus 317L SS at Ecorr = -0.25 V and Icorr = 0.017 µA/cm2 — roughly three orders of magnitude difference in corrosion current, i.e. the Mg alloy is far more electrochemically active/anodic. F5a shows Mg-Zn-Y-Nd's pH climbing steadily from 7.4 to ~10 by day ~22 then plateauing, while 317L SS falls to a minimum of ~6.1 by day 20 before partially recovering, and the blank control rises briefly to ~8.4 then declines to ~7.5-7.6.

The combination supports a directional, mechanistically consistent story: the polarization data establish that Mg-Zn-Y-Nd corrodes far more readily than 317L SS, and active Mg dissolution is known to consume water/release hydroxide, so a high Icorr for the Mg alloy is consistent with its pH climbing well above neutral, while 317L SS's very low Icorr is consistent with it not producing comparable alkalinization. This much the upstream electrochemistry can account for — the qualitative ranking of which sample should push pH up and by how much relatively.

What it leaves open: the polarization curves are a single-timepoint (presumably early-immersion) snapshot, not a 30-day series, so they cannot by themselves explain the specific plateau at day 22, the sigmoidal shape of the pH climb, or the timing. They also give no account of the 317L SS pH decline to 6.1 (an acidifying trend that a purely anodic-dissolution/alkalinization framework does not predict) or of the blank control's rise-then-fall pattern, which likely reflects solution buffering/CO2 exchange rather than any electrode process. So the evidence justifies the qualitative link between higher corrosion activity and higher solution pH, but the quantitative kinetics and the SS/blank pH trajectories remain unaccounted for by these two panels alone.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Both panels were opened and inspected.

F3 (polarization): Mg–Zn–Y–Nd sits at Ecorr ≈ –1.49 V, far more active than 317L SS at Ecorr ≈ –0.25 V, with the Mg alloy's anodic branch shifted 2–3 decades higher in current (Icorr 24.93 vs 0.017 µA/cm²), i.e., much faster, less-passivated metal dissolution.

F5a (pH): Mg–Zn–Y–Nd solution rises steadily from 7.4 to ~10 by day 22 and plateaus; 317L SS instead falls, reaching a minimum near 6.1 by day 20 before a partial rebound; the blank control rises modestly then declines slowly to ~7.5.

The combination supports the qualitative direction of the effect: the electrochemical activity ranking (Mg alloy >> SS in corrosion rate/activity) is consistent with the pH ranking (Mg alloy solution becomes strongly alkaline, consistent with Mg corrosion consuming water/H+ and releasing hydroxide, while SS — the more noble, lower-Icorr material — does not alkalinize and instead trends acidic). So upstream electrochemistry and downstream pH trajectories point the same direction and are mutually consistent.

What it leaves open: the polarization data are a single-timepoint measurement, not a kinetic model, so they cannot quantitatively predict the 30-day pH curve shape (e.g., the plateau after day 22, or the SS minimum-then-rebound after day 20). The blank control's own non-monotonic pH (rise then decline) is not addressed by either metal's electrochemistry. No direct mechanistic bridge (ion release, buffering) is shown here linking Icorr magnitude to pH rate.

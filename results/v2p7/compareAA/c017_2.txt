ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Yes, the upstream evidence is consistent with and supports the downstream thrombosis observation, but only as a qualitative, cross-surface association.

F5a (SEM) shows a gradient across the five surfaces: 316LSS and PPAmF have numerous spread, dendritic (activated) platelets; BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF show fewer, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF shows the fewest, mostly small/round unspread platelets. F6b (tube-lumen photos) and F6c (cross-sectional thrombus photos) show the same ordering: 316LSS and PPAmF lumens/cross-sections are filled with dark thrombus, BVLD-PPAmF and NO-PPAmF are partially open with mixed dark/light tissue, and BVLD/NO-PPAmF is largely clean/white with minimal dark thrombus.

Combined, this supports the claim that surfaces promoting greater platelet spreading/activation in PRP also produce more extensive thrombus and lower vessel patency in the ex vivo circulation model — i.e., the PRP platelet-adhesion result and the PRF/service_capability (thrombus/occlusion) result move together across the same five conditions.

What it leaves open: these are qualitative, macroscopic/single-timepoint (2h) images, not counts, clot mass, or statistics, so the strength and significance of the gradient are not established here. The panels show correlation across surfaces, not a demonstrated causal mechanism linking platelet spreading directly to thrombus volume; other coagulation factors could contribute, and replicate/sample-size information is not visible in these crops.

This is a coherent, reproducible correlation across an in vitro platelet measure and an ex vivo whole-blood thrombosis outcome, consistent with platelet adhesion/activation being one contributor to the downstream thrombus/patency result.

What it leaves open: causation is not established — PRP is a static, isolated-platelet assay, while F6b/c reflect dynamic whole-blood flow with coagulation, plasma proteins, and possibly complement also acting; no quantitative thrombus mass/area or platelet counts are given (all three panels are qualitative images); and sample size/reproducibility across replicates is not shown here. So the evidence supports association, not a demonstrated mechanistic pathway from platelet spreading to thrombus occlusion.

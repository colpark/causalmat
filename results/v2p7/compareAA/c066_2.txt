ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
The XRD trace (F6a) for the HP5 specimen indexes reflections only to ZrB2, SiC, C (graphite), and BN — no AlN or Al2O3 peaks are marked. The hardness curve (F2b, HP series) shows ~18.0 GPa at 0 wt% AlN, rising to ~20.3 GPa at 1 wt%, then settling to ~19.4 and ~19.2 GPa at 3 and 5 wt%.

Together, this supports the claim that in the hot-pressed samples the added AlN does not persist as a separate detectable crystalline phase at the level XRD can resolve (no AlN/Al2O3 lines), while hardness nonetheless changes non-monotonically with AlN content — an initial jump at 1 wt% followed by a mild decline that still stays above the 0 wt% baseline. So the combination is consistent with AlN acting via a mechanism other than forming a residual AlN/Al2O3 second phase detectable by XRD (e.g., reaction, incorporation into grain boundaries/other phases, densification, or grain-size effects), coinciding with the hardness shift.

What it leaves open: XRD absence of AlN/Al2O3 peaks does not prove AlN is absent — it could exist below detection limit, as an amorphous phase, or dissolved/reacted into ZrB2, SiC, or grain-boundary phases; the panel does not show diffraction data for the 1, 3, 5 wt% specimens specifically (only "HP5," identity of which AlN loading is unstated in the given crop), so a direct per-composition phase-hardness link cannot be confirmed. No causal mechanism (density, grain size, second-phase toughening) is established by these two panels alone — only a coincidence between undetected AlN phases and a hardness trend.

THE QUESTION

ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
The four panels are consistent with the stated measurements but only partially close the gap between the upstream SEM evidence and the downstream tensile-strength trend.

F4b (BST-SH/PAEN), F4c (BST@Ag1%/PAEN) and F4e (BST@Ag5%/PAEN) show fracture-surface SEM images at increasing Ag loading. F4b and F4c show a fairly featureless, cohesive fracture surface with no obvious discrete particles. F4e (5% Ag), by contrast, shows a field of distinct, evenly distributed spherical particles (roughly sub-micron, per the 1 µm scale bar) with no visible large agglomerates or clustering — this is the panel that most directly supports claim A ("embedded particles spread evenly without large agglomerates"). F7a is the bar chart reproducing the tensile values in claim B: 79.3, 78.3, 74.4, 73.5 MPa for pure PAEN, 1%, 3%, and 5% Ag composites, a modest monotonic decline with increasing filler content.

Together, this combination supports the description of good particle dispersion (from SEM, mainly F4e) co-occurring with a mild, progressive drop in tensile strength as filler content rises. It does NOT establish that dispersion quality is the cause of the strength decline, since no SEM panel for the 3% composite was shown here and no comparison to a poorly-dispersed (agglomerated) control is provided — so whether agglomeration would have produced a larger drop, or whether the decline instead reflects interfacial adhesion, filler stiffness mismatch, or porosity, is left open. Only F4e directly matches the "no large agglomerates" claim; F4b/F4c show no clear particles to judge dispersion from.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Findings from the four panels (paper: Advanced Composites and Hybrid Materials, s42114-021-00366-2):

- F7a matches claim B exactly: bars labeled 79.3, 78.3, 74.4, 73.5 MPa for Pure PAEN, BST@Ag1%, BST@Ag3%, BST@Ag5%, a monotonic decline with increasing Ag loading. Confirmed.
- F4e (BST@Ag5%/PAEN) shows a fracture surface with numerous small, roughly spherical particles distributed fairly uniformly across the field, without any obvious large agglomerate clusters — this supports claim A, but only for the 5% composition shown here.
- F4c (BST@Ag1%/PAEN) shows a fibrillar, striated fracture surface with only faint particulate texture; discrete embedded particles are not clearly resolved.
- F4b (BST-SH/PAEN, no Ag) shows a rough, porous fracture surface with no clearly visible discrete particles at all.

What the combination supports: the tensile-strength decline (B) is confirmed by F7a, and the "even dispersion without large agglomerates" claim (A) is visibly supported specifically at 5% Ag (F4e). What it leaves open: the SEM crops document filler morphology/dispersion, not a fracture mechanism — there is no visible debonding, voiding, or interfacial gap in these images that would explain why even, well-dispersed particles cause strength to drop. So the panels do not themselves establish that dispersion state is the cause of the downstream strength decrease; that link is an inference beyond what the images show. Additionally, since F4b and F4c don't clearly show comparable particle dispersion, claim A's uniformity across the whole series (not just 5%) is not fully corroborated by these four crops alone.

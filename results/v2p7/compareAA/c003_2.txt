ANSWER 1 -- evidence only
Yes, the combination supports the downstream claim, within limits.

XRD (F3a) shows the milled +10 wt% C@Co sample (C10) contains the same crystalline phases as the base C0 sample (CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17, Mg), with only a weak added Co line (~44.5°) — so the additive does not introduce a new hydrogen-storage phase or alter the parent alloy's crystal structure/identity. The PCT panels (F11a = C0, F11b = C10) each show a single flat plateau per temperature (320/340/360 °C), and the plateau pressures in F11b are nearly the same as in F11a (~0.15–0.45 MPa range), matching the numeric claim.

Together, this justifies: the C@Co addition does not change the phase composition of the milled alloy and does not measurably shift the thermodynamic plateau pressure (hence van't Hoff enthalpy/entropy) of hydride formation — the additive's effect, if any, is not one of altering bulk phase identity or equilibrium pressure.

Left open: the panels say nothing about absorption/desorption kinetics, cycling behavior, hysteresis width, or capacity differences (the two PCT curves' capacities look slightly different but that's not addressed here). Whether C@Co improves kinetics — the presumed reason for adding it — is not evidenced by these three panels; only phase and equilibrium-pressure identity are supported.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
Yes, but only as a correlational, not mechanistic, account. The XRD (F3a) shows that milling in 10 wt% C@Co does not change the bulk phase identity of the Mg90Ce5Y5 matrix: the same reflections (CeMg12, Y5Mg24, Ce2Mg17, Mg) appear in both the 0% and 10% patterns, with the only addition being a weak Co line near 44.5°. The PCT curves (F11a, F11b) show a single flat plateau per temperature for both compositions, at essentially the same pressures (~0.15, 0.27, 0.45 MPa across 320-360 °C). Together these support the conclusion that the plateau pressure — a thermodynamic property tied to which hydride/dehydride phases are forming — is set by the unchanged matrix phase assemblage, and that C@Co addition does not introduce a new bulk phase or shift the underlying thermodynamics; it behaves as a minor, likely surface/catalytic addition.

What is left open: the data do not demonstrate a causal mechanism linking phase identity to plateau pressure (no per-phase pressure assignment is given); the weak Co line does not rule out solute incorporation below XRD detection limits; and although pressures track closely, the panels show small differences at 360 °C that are not quantified or discussed, so exact equivalence is not established, only near-equivalence.

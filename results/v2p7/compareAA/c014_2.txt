What the combination supports: a correlation across the same ranking — the sample with greater N2-adsorption capacity and larger mesopore volume (RGO-Cu2S) is also the sample with the best J-V performance (highest Jsc and Voc), with Cu2S intermediate and Pt-free comparisons trailing. This is consistent with a porosity/surface-area-driven mechanism (more accessible surface for catalytic/charge-transfer sites) improving photovoltaic output, and the two panels do not contradict that reading.

What it leaves open: neither panel establishes causation — there is no direct measurement here linking pore structure to interfacial charge transfer, catalytic activity, or series/shunt resistance. Fill factor and power-conversion efficiency (the actual figure-of-merit/PRF) are not shown in either crop, so the porosity-to-efficiency link is unverified by this pair; only Jsc/Voc trends are matched. The isotherm/pore data and the J-V data are also on different specimens/conditions typical of separate characterization runs, so the association is circumstantial rather than mechanistically demonstrated by these two figures alone.

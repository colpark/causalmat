The combination supports a porosity-strength link only for S1: its porosity collapses toward near-zero exactly where its strength jumps to ~465 MPa, consistent with densification driving strength. It does not generalize: S3 keeps high porosity (~8%) and flat, modest strength across the whole range, while S2's porosity also falls sharply at higher temperature yet its strength stays low (~80–170 MPa), well below S1's. So porosity reduction alone is not sufficient to explain the strength differences across systems — some other factor (composition, phase, grain/microstructure) must differ between S1, S2, and S3.

Left open: neither panel shows microstructure (grain size, phase, defects) directly, so the mechanism behind S1's outsized gain versus S2/S3's muted response is not established here. Neither panel addresses PRP/value at all — that link is not evidenced by this pair of figures.

ANSWER 2 -- the same evidence, plus the earlier result
The upstream porosity data (F4a) and downstream strength data (F6a) account for the observation only partially, and only for one of three systems.

S1: porosity collapses sharply between 1500-1600°C (7.5%→0.4%), and flexural strength jumps in the same window (188→465 MPa). This co-occurrence supports the standard porosity-controls-strength picture for S1 — densification and strengthening track together in temperature and magnitude.

S2 and S3 break that link. S2's porosity falls to 1.2-0.7% by 1650-1700°C — as low as S1's — yet strength stays at only 145-170 MPa, far below S1's 465 MPa; low porosity here does not produce high strength. S3's porosity stays high throughout (11.2→7.7%, never below 7%) while its strength stays flat and moderate (170-195 MPa), showing no strong degradation despite persistent high porosity.

So the combined evidence supports a porosity-strength correlation for S1 alone, coincident with its steep densification transition. It does not support porosity as the general explanatory variable across S1/S2/S3, since S2 and S3 both decouple porosity from strength in opposite directions. What is left open: which other microstructural factor (grain size/bonding phase/composition, not addressed by these two panels) drives S1's strength jump and suppresses S2's strength despite low porosity, and why S3 tolerates high porosity without strength loss.

The panel at /home/aid1/Documents/causalmat/.v07work/rr/0b14ba5b68f7379a.jpg is a line/scatter plot, not a micrograph — there is no imaging technique or scale bar present. It shows engineering stress (MPa, y-axis, 0–400) versus engineering strain (x-axis, 0–0.30) for three sample conditions tested at 45°: "A-45°" (black inverted triangles), "ACA-45°" (red circles), and "AC-45°" (blue triangles). All three curves show typical tensile stress-strain behavior: a steep linear-elastic rise from the origin, transitioning to a gradually flattening plastic/hardening region, followed by a peak and then a short drop (indicating fracture/necking) at the curve's end. The AC-45° and ACA-45° curves nearly overlap and reach the highest peak stress (~390–400 MPa at strain ~0.25–0.28), both clearly outperforming the A-45° curve, which plateaus at a lower peak stress (~320 MPa) and fractures at a somewhat smaller strain (~0.25). This indicates the "AC" and "ACA" treatments/conditions improve both strength and ductility relative to the "A" condition at this 45° orientation.

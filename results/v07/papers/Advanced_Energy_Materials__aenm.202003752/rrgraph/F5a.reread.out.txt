Panel (a): a line/scatter plot of battery cycling (rate-capability) data, not a microscopy image — no scale bar present.

- X-axis: "Cycle Number" (0–35). Y-axis: "Discharge Capacity (mAh g⁻¹)" (0–200).
- Three series compared across stepped C-rates (labeled above the curves): 0.2C, 0.5C, 1C, 3C, 5C, 10C, then back to 0.5C, each held for roughly 5 cycles.
  - Red circles = LHCE (localized high-concentration electrolyte): highest and most stable capacity throughout, starting ~155 mAh g⁻¹ at 0.2C, declining gradually with increasing rate to ~80 mAh g⁻¹ at 10C, and recovering to ~150 mAh g⁻¹ when returned to 0.5C.
  - Black squares = "commercial" electrolyte: intermediate performance, starting ~150 mAh g⁻¹, dropping to ~45 mAh g⁻¹ at 10C, recovering to ~145 mAh g⁻¹.
  - Blue triangles = ILE (ionic liquid electrolyte): lowest and least stable, starting ~135 mAh g⁻¹, falling sharply to ~20–25 mAh g⁻¹ at 10C, only partially recovering to ~120 mAh g⁻¹ at 0.5C.
- Overall trend: capacity decreases stepwise as C-rate increases for all three electrolytes, with LHCE showing the best rate capability and reversibility, ILE the worst, and commercial electrolyte intermediate.

Question: Panels F3a-F3b, F3c-F3d, and F3e-F3f are SEM images of Li deposited on Cu under, respectively, the commercial electrolyte, the ILE, and the LHCE. Based on those six panels, how does the shape and packing of the deposited Li differ across the three conditions?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): The deposit is needle-like and porous in the commercial electrolyte, nodular in the ILE, and flat and dense in the LHCE.

Grading note: Answer key: the reading given directly on evidence node o11, judge-confirmed against the panel set (F3a-F3f).

Candidate answer:
Findings from the six SEM panels (F3a-b commercial electrolyte, F3c-d ILE, F3e-f LHCE):

- Commercial electrolyte (a,b): cross-section shows a thick, loose, mossy/fuzzy deposit with an uneven, whisker-covered surface; top view confirms fine, interconnected filamentous/dendritic Li strands with abundant open voids between them — the most porous, loosely packed, dendrite-like morphology of the three.
- ILE (c,d): cross-section still shows a rough, moss-textured layer of similar thickness to (a); but top view reveals the Li has coalesced into larger rounded plate-/petal-like particles that pack together more tightly, with fewer fine filaments but still visible gaps and irregular particle boundaries — an intermediate morphology (larger, flake-like grains, moderately dense packing).
- LHCE (e,f): cross-section shows a markedly thinner, smoother, compact layer with a flat top surface (and clean optical inset, unlike the darker/patchier insets for a and c); top view shows small, fine, smooth, closely and uniformly packed Li grains with minimal void space — the most compact, dense, and uniform deposition.

Overall trend: commercial electrolyte to end, the deposited Li becomes progressively less filamentous/dendritic and more compact/dense — from loose mossy dendrites (commercial), to larger flake-like particles with moderate packing (ILE), to fine, smooth, densely packed grains (LHCE).

Panel F3b (Fig. 4b) plots BJH pore-size distribution (dV/dD vs. pore size, nm) for the three scaffolds. All three curves peak in the same mesopore range (~4-6 nm), but their shapes differ markedly and non-monotonically with Co content: 0Co-MBG (black) shows a moderate peak (~0.011 cm3/g) at ~5 nm with a long tail extending out to ~25-30 nm, i.e., a fairly broad, right-skewed distribution; 2Co-MBG (red) shows the lowest, flattest peak (~0.006) and the broadest, most poorly defined distribution, remaining nearly flat from ~6 to ~20 nm; 5Co-MBG (green), the highest-Co sample, shows the tallest and narrowest peak (~0.016) at ~4-5 nm, dropping sharply to near-baseline by ~8 nm - i.e., the most uniform, sharply defined pore size, not the most disordered.

So the panel does show real differences among the three samples in pore-size distribution shape, but the pattern is non-monotonic with Co/Ca ratio (2Co is broadest, 5Co is narrowest), which does not cleanly track a "more Co = more disorder" trend. More importantly, pore-size distribution width is a distinct property from the claimed effect - "perturbation of ordered orientation of mesopore channels" refers to long-range hexagonal/mesostructural packing order (typically evidenced by small-angle XRD or TEM), not the BJH pore-diameter dispersity from a nitrogen isotherm. Two samples can have identical average pore size and narrow size distributions while having completely disordered channel orientation, or vice versa. Therefore F3b, while showing that Co substitution alters pore size and distribution breadth, does not directly substantiate the specific claim about disrupted channel orientation, and its non-monotonic trend across the three samples further weakens it as supporting evidence for that particular mechanism.

Question: The claim under test: dielectric properties stay stable from 100 Hz to 1 MHz and up to about 135-140 C. Does the loss data in panel F6b support this?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): No. On panel F6b the BST@Ag1% loss climbs above 100 kHz, reaching about 0.065 at 1 MHz, and the low-frequency loss values exceed 0.026, so the panel does not bear out the claimed stability of loss across 100 Hz to 1 MHz.

Candidate answer:
Panel F6b (Fig. 5b) plots dielectric loss vs. frequency (100 Hz to 1 MHz) only — it contains no temperature axis or data, so it cannot support or refute the "stable up to ~135-140 C" portion of the claim at all; that would require a different panel (a loss-vs-temperature plot).

For the frequency-stability portion: loss values for Pure PAEN, BST@Ag3%, and BST@Ag5% stay low and roughly flat (~0.01-0.04) across the full 100 Hz–1 MHz range, which does support frequency stability for those three curves. However, BST@Ag1% deviates clearly — its loss rises sharply above ~100 kHz, reaching ~0.065 near 1 MHz, roughly 4-6x higher than the other curves at that point. This is not "stable" across the full measured band.

Conclusion: Panel F6b partially supports the frequency-stability claim (true for pure PAEN, 3%, and 5% Ag samples) but contradicts it for the BST@Ag1% sample, which shows a marked loss increase at high frequency. The panel provides no evidence at all regarding thermal stability up to 135-140 C, since it has no temperature axis.

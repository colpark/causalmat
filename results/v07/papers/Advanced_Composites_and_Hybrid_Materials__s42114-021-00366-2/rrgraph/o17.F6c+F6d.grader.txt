Panel F6c: Panel (c) is a line plot, not a microscopy image — it shows dielectric constant versus temperature (26–180 °C) for four samples: Pure PAEN (gray), BST@Ag1% (teal/blue), BST@Ag3% (orange), and BST@Ag5% (red). No scale bar is present since this is a graph, not a micrograph. All four curves start near room temperature with dielectric constant values of roughly 3–5 and remain fairly flat/slowly rising up to about 130–140 °C, after which each curve rises sharply toward 180 °C. At 180 °C, the values reach approximately 11 (Pure PAEN, lowest), 17 (BST@Ag1%), and about 19–20 (BST@Ag3% and BST@Ag5%, which are highest and nearly overlapping at the high-temperature end). The overall trend shows that Ag-doped BST composites have consistently higher dielectric constants than pure PAEN across the whole temperature range, with the enhancement most pronounced near the high-temperature upturn, and BST@Ag3% and BST@Ag5% performing similarly and above BST@Ag1%.

Panel F6d: Panel (d) is a line plot of dielectric loss versus temperature (25-180 degrees C) for four samples: Pure PAEN, BST@Ag1%, BST@Ag3%, and BST@Ag5%, distinguished by color-coded curves (gray, blue, orange, red respectively) with a legend in the upper area. No scale bar is present, as this is a data plot rather than a micrograph. All curves show a shallow, roughly linear rise in dielectric loss from about 0.02-0.03 up to about 0.05-0.08 between 25 and 140 degrees C, then a sharper increase above ~140 degrees C. Pure PAEN shows the steepest late rise, peaking highest (~0.33) near 175-180 degrees C, while the Ag-doped BST composites (1%, 3%, 5%) peak earlier (~160-170 degrees C) at lower maxima (~0.21-0.27) and then decline slightly toward 180 degrees C, with BST@Ag3% and BST@Ag5% tracking closely together above BST@Ag1%. Overall trend: silver-doped BST composite fillers shift and suppress the high-temperature dielectric loss peak relative to pure PAEN, with dose-dependent separation among the 1%, 3%, and 5% Ag loadings.

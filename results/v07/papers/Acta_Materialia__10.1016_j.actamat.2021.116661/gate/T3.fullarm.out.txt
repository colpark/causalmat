At low magnification, the two H-charged fracture surfaces show different macroscopic zoning patterns rather than differences that can be ranked as better/worse.

Ref steel (panel b): the fracture surface is clearly divided into two distinct regions separated by a sharp, traceable boundary (outlined with the dotted line) — a darker-gray central/interior region (region c) and a lighter-gray surrounding region (region d) near the specimen edges. This produces a visually heterogeneous, two-tone fracture surface.

Mo steel (panel f, from the boxed region in e): the fracture surface instead shows a single, relatively uniform light-gray texture across the entire imaged area (regions g and h), with no comparable sharp boundary separating darker and lighter zones — the surface appears macroscopically homogeneous in tone/texture.

In short, the Ref steel fracture surface exhibits a clearly demarcated dark-gray/light-gray duplex zoning, whereas the Mo steel fracture surface is uniform in appearance (light-gray throughout) without such zonal division — consistent with the caption's statement that the Ref steel's overall fracture surface is divided into dark- and light-gray areas while the Mo steel's consists of a light-gray area alone.

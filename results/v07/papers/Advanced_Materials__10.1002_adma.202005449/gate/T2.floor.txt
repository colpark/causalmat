Context from a materials paper:
- Flexible wearable electronics need micro-scale energy storage and sensors printable without binders, additives or metal collectors
- One aqueous additive-free MXene ink, if its rheology suits screen printing, can act as electrode, current collector, interconnect and sensor
- Take additive-free aqueous Ti3C2Tx MXene ink as the only solid of every printed component
- Selectively etch Ti3AlC2 in LiF/HCl and delaminate the product into an aqueous few-layer MXene nanosheet ink
- The H ink has the elastic shear-thinning rheology screen printing needs; the dilute ink does not
- Choose the H-MXene ink for all printing and drop the L-MXene ink
- Screen-print the H-MXene ink through a patterned mesh onto paper, PET, SiOx-coated steel, rubber, glass, wood and fabric
- Shear flow through the mesh aligns the nanosheets while the instantaneous viscosity recovery freezes the printed line in place

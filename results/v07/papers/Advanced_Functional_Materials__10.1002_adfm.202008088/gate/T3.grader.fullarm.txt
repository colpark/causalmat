Candidate answer:
Panel F2d (three stacked sub-panels, all vs. Temperature 100–270 K, y-axis Δσ/σ (%) relative to 700 K):

- SC (top, blue): Δσ/σ is roughly flat/high (~100–110%) below ~150 K, then drops sharply at ~150 K to a much lower plateau (~40–80%) that persists up to 270 K. This is a step-down discontinuity at the transition, not a peak.
- PC (middle, red): Δσ/σ sits near baseline (~100%) below ~140 K, then rises sharply to a pronounced peak (~250–280% for the (242) reflection) right around 150–160 K, followed by a gradual decay back toward ~100% by 270 K. This is a sharp spike/peak at the transition.
- HOC (bottom, black): similar shape to PC but smaller in magnitude — near-baseline (~100%) at low T, a modest peak (~120–130%) at ~150 K, then a gradual decline below baseline (down to ~85–90%) by 270 K.

Comparison across samples: PC shows the largest anomaly (biggest spike, ~150–180 percentage points above baseline), HOC shows a much smaller spike (~20–30 points), while SC shows the opposite sign of behavior — a sudden decrease rather than an increase — dropping to a lower plateau rather than peaking and relaxing. This ordering (largest anomaly in PC, smaller in HOC, qualitatively different/suppressed in SC) is consistent with domain size scaling inversely with the strength of the FWHM (strain-broadening) anomaly at the low-temperature transition.

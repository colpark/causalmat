Panel B (two 3x1 stacked color-map grids, labeled PC, HOC, SC from top to bottom in each grid) shows photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy data for a sample "after grinding," plotted as false-color heatmaps of normalized PL intensity (dark red/black = min, white/bright = max in the left set; blue = min, dark red = max in the right set) versus wavelength (700–850 nm, x-axis) against two different variables.

Left triptych: temperature-dependent PL, with temperature (120–300 K) on the y-axis for each of the three subpanels (PC, HOC, SC). In all three, the PL emission band (bright red-to-yellow arc) is centered near 750–800 nm at low temperature and shifts/broadens toward longer wavelength with a redshift as temperature increases toward 300 K, with intensity appearing to decrease (dimmer/more diffuse) at higher temperature — a typical thermal quenching and bandgap redshift trend, generally consistent across the PC, HOC, and SC samples.

Right triptych: fluence-dependent PL at fixed 80 K, with excitation fluence (log scale, ~10^2 to 3×10^2 µW) on the y-axis. Here the emission peak position (a narrow vertical colored band, green-to-red) stays essentially constant in wavelength (~780–800 nm) across the fluence range for all three samples, indicating no significant fluence-dependent spectral shift; only subtle color/intensity banding is visible with fluence. No scale bar (axes are wavelength/temperature/fluence, not spatial); each subplot has its own colorbar to the right of its triptych.

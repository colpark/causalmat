Panel (a) shows Nyquist plots from electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), with -Z'' (imaginary impedance, ohm·cm²) on the y-axis versus Z' (real impedance, ohm·cm²) on the x-axis, both axes scaled from 0 to 8.0×10^5. The data are labeled "EP" and compare five immersion times: 1d (red squares), 2d (green circles), 3d (blue triangles), 6d (cyan inverted triangles), and 9d (magenta diamonds). The 1d, 2d, and 3d curves form large, overlapping semicircular capacitive loops spanning nearly the full x-axis range (up to ~8×10^5 ohm·cm²) with a small inductive-like upturn near the right end; the 3d curve has the largest arc diameter, followed closely by 1d then 2d. The 6d and 9d curves are much smaller depressed semicircles confined to roughly 0–2×10^5 ohm·cm², indicating a marked decrease in impedance (and thus degradation of the protective/passive layer) with longer immersion time. There is no scale bar (this is a plotted graph, not a micrograph), and no other sub-panels are visible in this crop.

Technique: X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), shown as a 2x2 grid of high-resolution binding-energy spectra labeled panel (b), comparing pristine MoS2 and PEGSH components against a crosslinked PEGSH/(2% MoS2) hydrogel ("Gel").

Left column (Mo 3d region, x-axis Binding Energy 226–240 eV, y-axis Intensity a.u., no scale bar as is standard for XPS): top spectrum is pristine MoS2 showing two deconvoluted peaks, Mo 3d5/2 (~229 eV) and Mo 3d3/2 (~232 eV); bottom "Gel" spectrum shows the same two Mo 3d doublet peaks plus deconvoluted shoulder/interaction components labeled (MoS2-PEG) and a new peak near 235–236 eV attributed to (MoS2-PEG) bonding, indicating a shift/broadening upon crosslinking.

Right column (C 1s region, x-axis 281–290 eV): top spectrum is pure PEGSH deconvoluted into sp2, C-C (~284.5 eV), C-O (~285.5 eV), and C-S (~286.5 eV) components; bottom "Gel" spectrum shows the same sp2/C-C/C-O components plus an added C-S/(MoS2-PEG) peak near 286–287 eV, again indicating new chemical bonding/interaction in the composite.

Overall trend: the paired comparison (precursor vs. gel) demonstrates that crosslinking/composite formation introduces new deconvoluted XPS peaks (labeled MoS2-PEG interactions in both Mo 3d and C 1s regions), used as evidence of chemical bonding between MoS2 and the PEGSH polymer network in the hydrogel.

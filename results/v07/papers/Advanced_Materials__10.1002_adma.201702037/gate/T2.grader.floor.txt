Question: The claim on the table is that PEG thiols chemisorb at MoS2 vacancy sites, forming C-S-Mo crosslinks, and you're given the fitted Mo 3d XPS panel (Figure 4b) for the crosslinked PEGSH/MoS2 gel, with its peak assignments (Mo3d5/2, Mo3d3/2, MoS2, PEGSH, C-C, C-O, C-S, sp) labelled. Does this fitted Mo 3d spectrum confirm the claimed bonding?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): No. The fitted gel Mo 3d components do not pair into the expected 3.1 eV spin-orbit doublets; instead the fitted peaks sit about 1.6 eV and about 6.8 eV apart. The peak at 235.3 eV falls at the binding energy of Mo6+ 3d3/2, not at a signature that confirms the C-S-Mo crosslink assignment (o14).

Candidate answer:
Partially, and only in combination with the companion C1s data — the Mo 3d panel alone provides supporting but indirect evidence. What the fitted Mo3d spectrum shows is two peak sets: the native MoS2 Mo3d5/2/Mo3d3/2 doublet plus an additional "PEGSH" component appearing in the crosslinked gel that isn't present in pristine MoS2. The emergence of this new Mo-associated peak is consistent with a new bonding environment at Mo sites (i.e., chemisorption at vacancies), which supports the crosslink claim. However, the Mo3d region cannot by itself specify a C-S-Mo linkage — it just shows an altered/new Mo environment; the direct evidence for the C-S bond piece comes from the accompanying C1s spectrum (C-C, C-O, C-S peak shift/appearance), which the caption explicitly says shows "the change in binding energy of carbon (C1s) peak due to C-S-Mo formation." So per the caption itself: Raman and XPS (C1s + Mo3d) together suggest the new bond formation — the Mo3d panel is one necessary piece of corroborating evidence (new Mo environment) but does not alone confirm the C-S-Mo bond; that requires the joint C1s + Mo3d + Raman evidence described in the paper.

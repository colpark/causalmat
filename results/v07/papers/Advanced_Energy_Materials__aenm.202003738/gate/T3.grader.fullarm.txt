Candidate answer:
Panel A (EC-based, EC:EMC 3:7) and Panel B (EC-free, EMC) show clearly different elemental distributions, not "no visible difference."

In panel A, the F, Co, Mn, and Ni EDX maps all show strong, spatially localized signal concentrated in distinct rounded blobs/patches that coincide with the lighter-contrast regions in the SEM image — i.e., Co, Mn, and Ni (transition metals from the cathode) are co-located with F in the same discrete spots on the graphite anode.

In panel B, the F signal is spread broadly and roughly uniformly across the whole imaged surface rather than concentrated in discrete patches, while the Co and Ni maps show essentially no detectable signal (near-background/dark), and the Mn map shows only faint, diffuse speckling spread evenly over the field rather than localized clusters.

So the comparison is: EC-based anode exhibits colocalized, spatially clustered F/Co/Mn/Ni "hot spots," whereas the EC-free anode lacks those localized transition-metal clusters (Co and Ni are essentially absent, Mn is faint and diffuse) and F is dispersed rather than clustered — a clear qualitative difference in spatial distribution and intensity between the two conditions.

Panel labeled "(b) 1.0 M LiPF6 in EMC" showing a row of five co-registered micrographs of the same sample area: an SEM (secondary/backscattered electron) image on the left, followed by four EDX (energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy) elemental maps for F, Co, Mn, and Ni, each labeled in the top-right corner of its panel. The SEM image shows a grainy, particulate/porous surface texture typical of an electrode or deposited film, with a 100 µm scale bar in the bottom-left corner. The EDX F map (red) shows dense, widespread bright speckling indicating strong, uniform fluorine signal across the field of view (consistent with LiPF6-derived fluorine, e.g., LiF or PF-containing decomposition products). The EDX Co map (blue-black) is essentially dark/near-blank, indicating little to no detectable cobalt. The EDX Mn map (blue) shows moderate, fairly uniform speckled signal across the field, and the EDX Ni map (dark green) shows sparse, faint speckling, indicating low but nonzero nickel signal. Overall the panel demonstrates that after exposure to this electrolyte, the surface is fluorine-rich with low/undetectable Co and only trace Mn/Ni signal, likely representing a surface deposit/passivation layer overlying an NMC-type cathode particle.

Panel F5c shows simulation (top row) and experiment (bottom row) for four conditions: air/710nm, air/890nm, oil/710nm, oil/890nm. The claim is largely but not fully borne out:

- Air, 710 nm: Experimental "D" is reasonably clear and matches the simulated D shape, though with some background speckle noise.
- Air, 890 nm: Experimental "C" is visible but weak/faint, with the letter shape only partially resolved against noisy background.
- Oil, 710 nm: This is the weakest panel — the experimental image is dominated by a bright, diffuse red blob/speckle pattern in the center; the "I" (a simple vertical bar in simulation) is not clearly discernible as a distinct letter shape, essentially buried in noise/glare.
- Oil, 890 nm: Experimental "U" is visible and reasonably recognizable, matching the simulated U shape, though with speckle background similar to the other panels.

So while 3 of the 4 conditions (D, C, U) show experimentally recognizable letters roughly matching simulation, the oil/710nm "I" condition does not clearly reproduce a legible letter in the experimental data — it looks like a saturated/noisy blob rather than a distinct "I" bar. Thus the measured data do not fully and cleanly bear out the DC-to-IU switching claim across all four wavelength-medium conditions; the oil, 710 nm case is the weak link.

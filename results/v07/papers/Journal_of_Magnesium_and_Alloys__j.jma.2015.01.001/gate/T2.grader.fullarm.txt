Question: Do the F7a and F7b creep-rate panels for the AZ91-SiCp series show that this threshold-stress behavior occurs only in the SiC-reinforced compositions?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): No. Unreinforced AZ91 itself carries a threshold stress of about 24 MPa (the 80 MPa applied condition plots at an effective stress of about 56 MPa), close to the composites' threshold-stress range of about 24-35 MPa, so the panels do not confine this behavior to the SiC-reinforced samples (o16).

Grading note: Answer key: the audit ruling on the panel (o16).

Candidate answer:
No. Panel F7a (applied stress) shows apparent stress exponents that rise with SiC content: AZ91 (no reinforcement) already has n=7.2, and this increases progressively to n=8.9 for AZ91+25SiC. Panel F7b shows that once an effective (threshold-corrected) stress is used, all compositions — including the unreinforced AZ91 baseline — collapse to a much lower, similar true stress exponent (n_t ≈ 5.4–5.8), with AZ91 itself at n_t=5.4 versus its apparent n=7.2. This means a threshold-stress correction is applied to and needed for the unreinforced AZ91 alloy too, not just the SiC-containing composites — so the panels indicate threshold-stress behavior is present across the whole series (present even without SiC), with SiC addition simply increasing the magnitude of the apparent-exponent anomaly, rather than showing threshold behavior exclusively in the reinforced compositions.

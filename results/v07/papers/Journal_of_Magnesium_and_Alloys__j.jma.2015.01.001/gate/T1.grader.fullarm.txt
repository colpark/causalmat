Question: The claim under test is that matrix grain size falls from about 65 um in plain AZ91 to about 34 um at 5 wt% SiCp, 22 um at 10 wt%, and roughly 21 um at 25 wt%, based on the six optical micrographs (F1a-F1f) spanning the AZ91-SiCp composite series. Do these micrographs actually let you trace grain boundaries and read off grain size across all six conditions shown?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): No. The grain network is clearly resolved only in panel a; across panels b through f the grain boundaries are at most faintly traceable (in panel c) and are not measurable, so the stated grain-size progression cannot be read off the figure (o5).

Grading note: Answer key: the audit ruling on the panel, node o5.

Candidate answer:
Reviewed all six panels (F1a–F1f).

F1a (unreinforced AZ91) shows a genuinely resolvable microstructure: a fine, mottled two-phase network with visible dark beta-Mg17Al12 outlining polygonal grain boundaries, so a boundary network can plausibly be traced and a mean linear intercept grain size extracted here.

F1b through F1f, however, are increasingly dominated by dense clusters/agglomerates of dark SiC particles rather than by clearly delineated alpha-Mg grain boundaries. In F1b–c the particle clusters partially obscure the matrix but some lighter matrix background with faint boundary contrast is still visible; by F1d–F1f (nominally 15–25 wt%) the field of view is so saturated with SiC particle agglomerates that the underlying alpha-Mg grain boundaries are largely hidden or indistinguishable from particle/porosity contrast — there is no continuous, traceable grain-boundary network visible in these crops.

So the micrographs do not uniformly support tracing grain boundaries and reading grain size across all six conditions: it is credible for F1a (and marginally for F1b) but becomes progressively unverifiable optically for the higher-SiC panels (F1d–F1f), where particle clustering, not grain-boundary contrast, dominates the image. The reported grain-size trend (65→34→22→~21 µm) cannot be independently confirmed from these crops at the higher loadings; it would require the paper's separate quantitative method (e.g., linear intercept counts or higher-magnification/etched images not shown here) rather than direct visual inspection of F1a–F1f.

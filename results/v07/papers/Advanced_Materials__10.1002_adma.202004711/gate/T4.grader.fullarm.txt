Question: The claim under test is that LiFePO4 cells built with PEOm-Li21Si5 cycle stably for 100 cycles at 30, 45 and 60 C with about 100% Coulombic efficiency, and panel F7c (Coulombic efficiency axis marked 20% to 100%) is offered as the supporting evidence. Does the panel actually back this claim once you look at how capacity behaves across the three C-rates?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): No. Panel F7c shows the opposite ordering from what the claim implies: the 60 C curve has the highest capacity and the 30 C curve the lowest, which is the reverse of the text's stated 149.7/140.9/129.2 mAh/g at 30/45/60 C.

Grading note: Answer key: the audit ruling on the panel, node a3.

Candidate answer:
Looking at F7c: the right-axis Coulombic-efficiency traces for all three temperatures (30, 45, 60 C) do sit essentially flat near ~100% across the full 100 cycles, so that part of the claim is visually supported. But the left-axis specific-capacity traces tell a different story: the 30 C and 60 C capacity curves sit in a plausible physical range (~130-165 mAh/g) and both show a gradual downward drift over the 100 cycles rather than being perfectly flat/stable. More problematic, the 45 C capacity curve is plotted at ~290-305 mAh/g, essentially overlapping the ~100% CE cluster near the top of the plot — a value that exceeds LFP's theoretical capacity (~170 mAh/g) and looks like it was mistakenly plotted on/near the CE scale rather than as a distinct, realistic capacity trace. So the panel does not cleanly back the "stable capacity at all three temperatures" part of the claim: two of the three capacity curves show mild fade rather than true stability, and the third (45 C) is not credibly shown as a real capacity trajectory at all — only the CE-near-100% portion of the claim is well supported by the image.

This is a photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy plot, not a microscopy image, so there is no scale bar. The x-axis is Wavelength (nm), ranging from 350 to 550 nm, and the y-axis is PL intensity (a.u.), ranging from 0 to 800. Four emission spectra are overlaid, each corresponding to a different reaction condition: TA+H2O2 (black), TA+H2O2+Cu(II) (red), TA+H2O2+MoO3 (green), and TA+H2O2+Cu(II)+MoO3 (purple). All four curves show a broad peak centered around 420-430 nm, consistent with 2-hydroxyterephthalic acid fluorescence used to detect hydroxyl radical (•OH) generation. The peak intensity increases progressively from the baseline TA+H2O2 condition (~250 a.u.) through the single-additive conditions (Cu(II) ~280 a.u., MoO3 slightly lower/similar to baseline) to a marked jump for the combined Cu(II)+MoO3 condition (~490 a.u., nearly double the others), indicating a synergistic enhancement of hydroxyl radical production when both Cu(II) and MoO3 are present together.

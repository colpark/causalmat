Panel C is a set of colorized SEM (scanning electron microscopy) micrographs comparing bacterial capture/killing by an unmodified enzyme-mimetic material (POD-M) versus a modified version (V-POD-M), each with H2O2, tested against two bacterial strains (S. aureus, top row; E. coli, bottom row).

Left column (POD-M+H2O2): low-magnification views (5 μm and 10 μm scale bars) show a collapsed, sheet-like POD-M structure with bacteria (false-colored yellow/tan) lying loosely nearby, labeled "POD-M structure collapse" and "No bacterial capture" — indicating the material fails to physically trap the bacteria.

Middle and right columns (V-POD-M+H2O2): higher-magnification images (1–2 μm and 500 nm scale bars) show an intact, rough/particulate scaffold with bacteria (yellow) embedded within and adhering directly to the material surface, labeled "Bacterial capture." The rightmost, highest-magnification panels (500 nm scale bars) show red arrows pointing to ruptured, collapsed, or deformed bacterial cell walls/membranes, labeled "Membrane destruction" — for S. aureus this appears as flattened/burst coccoid cells, and for E. coli as wrinkled, deflated rod-shaped cells. Overall the panel trend argues that the V-POD-M modification enables physical capture of bacteria followed by membrane rupture under H2O2, whereas the unmodified POD-M does neither.

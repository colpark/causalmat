Panel F5c (SEM comparison, labeled C) directly supports the CFU result. In the left-hand POD-M+H2O2 panels for both S. aureus and E. coli, the image is explicitly annotated "POD-M structure collapse" and "No bacterial capture" — the SEM shows the MOF/POD-M scaffold fragmented into thin, sheet-like collapsed debris with bacteria scattered loosely nearby rather than adhered to it. In contrast, the right-hand V-POD-M+H2O2 panels are annotated "Bacterial capture" and "Membrane destruction," showing intact, robust particulate structures with bacteria (pseudo-colored yellow-green) closely adhered/embedded in the material and red arrows marking visibly ruptured/damaged bacterial cytomembranes.

This indicates the mechanistic reason POD-M+H2O2 performs so much worse: H2O2 exposure causes the POD-M octahedral structure itself to collapse/degrade, which both destroys its catalytic architecture and eliminates its ability to physically capture/localize bacteria at the reactive surface. Without structural integrity and bacterial capture, the hydroxyl radicals generated cannot be delivered locally to the bacterial membrane, explaining the residual 45-58 CFU. V-POD-M, by remaining structurally stable under H2O2, retains bacterial capture capability and thus achieves localized membrane destruction and zero colonies.

Question: The claim under test holds that MoO3 binds H2O2 into a peroxo-complex intermediate that only becomes catalytically active once paired with Cu, implying MoO3 by itself should barely touch TMB. Based on the TMB-oxidation comparison in panel F2c across the separate and combined MoO3/H2O2/Cu(II) conditions, does the panel support that exclusivity?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): No. Panel F2c shows MoO3 alone already gives an absorbance of 0.055, versus 0.017 for H2O2 alone and 0.082 for Cu(II) alone. MoO3 by itself measurably oxidizes TMB, so it is not an inert partner that only turns active in combination with Cu; the panel qualifies the claim rather than confirming it (a1).

Grading note: Answer key: the audit ruling on the panel (a1), as named by the trace's grader field.

Candidate answer:
Panel F2c (bar chart, absorbance a.u.) shows four conditions: TMB+H2O2 alone (~0.02, lowest), TMB+H2O2+Cu(II) (~0.08), TMB+H2O2+MoO3 (~0.05), and TMB+H2O2+Cu(II)+MoO3 (~0.61, dramatically higher, marked *** vs both single-additive conditions).

This partially but not fully supports the exclusivity claim. The combined Cu(II)+MoO3 system does show a large, statistically marked synergistic jump (roughly 7-12x higher than either additive alone), consistent with MoO3 requiring Cu to unlock strong peroxidase-mimetic activity. However, MoO3 alone is not negligible — at ~0.05 it is about 2.5x the H2O2-only baseline (~0.02) and comparable to (even slightly exceeding) the Cu(II)-only condition (~0.08 vs 0.05, similar order of magnitude). So MoO3 by itself does measurably oxidize TMB above background, contradicting a strict "barely touch TMB" reading; the panel supports strong synergy/amplification with Cu but not complete exclusivity of MoO3's catalytic contribution to the paired state.

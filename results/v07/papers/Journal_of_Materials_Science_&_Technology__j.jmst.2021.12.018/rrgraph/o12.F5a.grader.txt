This is a nitrogen adsorption-desorption isotherm plot (BET analysis), not a microscopy image — there is no scale bar since it's a graph, not a micrograph. The x-axis is "Relative pressure, P/P0" (0 to 1.0) and the y-axis is "Quantity Adsorbed (cm³ g⁻¹, STP)" (0 to 600). Four sample curves are plotted with adsorption/desorption branches (hysteresis loops): g-C3N4 (black squares), g-C3N4-E (teal triangles), S@g-C3N4-E (blue inverted triangles), and S-g-C3N4-E (red circles).

Three of the samples (g-C3N4, g-C3N4-E, S@g-C3N4-E) show low, relatively flat adsorption (rising only to ~50-70 cm³/g at P/P0 near 1), indicating low surface area/porosity, while S-g-C3N4-E shows a much higher, steeply rising isotherm reaching ~580 cm³/g at high relative pressure with a pronounced hysteresis loop, characteristic of mesoporous/macroporous structure with substantially greater surface area. An inset table lists BET surface area (S_BET, m² g⁻¹) and pore volume (V_pore, cm³ g⁻¹) for each sample: g-C3N4 = 5.51 m²/g, 0.038 cm³/g; g-C3N4-E = 12.68 m²/g, 0.085 cm³/g; S@g-C3N4-E = 15.69 m²/g, 0.073 cm³/g; S-g-C3N4-E = 173.53 m²/g, 0.888 cm³/g — confirming S-g-C3N4-E has by far the largest surface area and pore volume among the four samples.

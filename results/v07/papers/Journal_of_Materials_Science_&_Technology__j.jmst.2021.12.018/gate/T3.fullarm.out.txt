Panel F8a: PL intensity ranks g-C3N4 (black, highest peak) > g-C3N4-E (teal) > S@g-C3N4-E (blue) > S-g-C3N4-E (red, lowest), with S-g-C3N4-E showing the weakest and broadest-suppressed peak of all four. Taken at face value (lower PL = less radiative recombination = better charge separation), this ordering does support the claim that S-g-C3N4-E separates/retains charges better than plain g-C3N4 and g-C3N4-E.

However, the picture is complicated rather than clean: S@g-C3N4-E (the sulfur-loaded intermediate, before pyrolysis forms the C-S-C-bonded, in-framework S-scheme heterojunction) already shows PL almost as low as S-g-C3N4-E, far closer to it than to the two undoped samples. Since the S-scheme/internal-field mechanism is specifically attributed to the pyrolyzed S-g-C3N4-E (with its R-type/O-type domain junction), but most of the PL quenching is already present in S@g-C3N4-E where that heterojunction presumably hasn't formed yet, this suggests the PL drop may partly reflect surface-loaded sulfur acting as a simple optical/physical quencher (or non-radiative defect/trap recombination) rather than solely the proposed charge-separation mechanism unique to the final material. PL intensity by itself also cannot distinguish "fewer recombination events because charges are usefully separated" from "fewer radiative events because charges are lost to non-radiative trap recombination" — so it needs the photocurrent and H2-yield trends (which the paper reports as increasing S@g-C3N4-E < ... < S-g-C3N4-E, consistent with genuine charge separation and not just defect quenching) to corroborate the interpretation. So: the PL ranking is directionally consistent with the claim for S-g-C3N4-E vs. the two undoped samples, but the near-equal quenching in S@g-C3N4-E means PL alone does not cleanly isolate the S-scheme heterojunction as the cause, and corroboration from photocurrent/H2 data is needed to rule out simple sulfur-related PL quenching as an alternative explanation.

Candidate answer:
No — the XPS survey spectrum in Fig. 4a, on its own, does not support the specific claim that sulfur is bonded as C-S-C (S on N sites) in S-g-C3N4-E. A survey (wide-scan) XPS spectrum only reveals the elemental composition of the sample — i.e., it can show that S is present alongside C and N in S-g-C3N4-E, confirming successful sulfur doping/loading. It cannot resolve chemical bonding states or coordination environments; that requires high-resolution, element-specific spectra (e.g., S 2p, and possibly N 1s) with peak deconvolution and binding-energy assignment, which per the caption are separate panels alongside the survey spectrum in Fig. 4. So F4a provides only indirect support (evidence S is incorporated into the material) but not direct evidence for the specific C-S-C/S-on-N-site bonding configuration asserted in the claim.

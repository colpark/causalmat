Panel 4c shows n-type defect density (Mott-Schottky derived) for the three multilayer systems: Al/Ti ≈ 4.5×10^20 cm⁻³, Al/Mg ≈ 1.2×10^20 cm⁻³, and Al/Cu ≈ 1.6×10^20 cm⁻³. Al/Ti has by far the highest defect density, roughly 3-4x that of Al/Mg and Al/Cu.

This does not support the claim as framed. If Ti beneath the Al surface raised the work function and lowered surface (electrochemical) activity, one would expect Al/Ti to show a comparatively low or at least not-elevated defect density (fewer point defects/donor states, more stable passive film) relative to Al/Cu and Al/Mg. Instead, the panel shows the opposite ordering: Al/Ti has the highest defect density of the three, while Al/Mg (the presumed least noble/most reactive pairing) actually shows the lowest defect density. A higher n-type defect density is generally associated with a more defective, less protective passive film and greater susceptibility to localized electron transfer, which is not consistent with "lowering surface activity."

So based on Figure 4c alone, the comparison across Al/Ti, Al/Cu, and Al/Mg does not support the stated claim — the data pattern runs counter to it.

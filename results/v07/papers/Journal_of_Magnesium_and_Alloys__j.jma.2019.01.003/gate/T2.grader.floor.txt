Question: The paper sets up the stir-cast versus friction-stir-processed (FSP) AZ31/10 vol% fly-ash composites, notes that the FSP route gives a homogeneous fly-ash dispersion with no particle-free zones, and shows the wear-debris SEM comparison for the two composites. What mechanism accounts for the difference in wear-debris character between the two composites during sliding, and how does it curb material removal in the FSP sample?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): Because the fly ash is uniformly dispersed through the FSP composite, it stops the sliding asperities from penetrating into the subsurface; the load-bearing fly-ash layers themselves then fracture into fine debris, which rolls between the mating surfaces (three-body rolling) rather than being cut away, so cutting-type material removal is reduced (m2).

Candidate answer:
Mechanism: In the stir-cast composite the fly-ash (FA) particles are unevenly distributed, leaving particle-free (soft, unreinforced) regions of the AZ31 matrix. During sliding these soft zones undergo extensive plastic shear and subsurface deformation that nucleates cracks and delamination, producing the large flat flakes (~20-40 µm) seen in debris (a) — a classic delamination/adhesive wear mode that removes bulk material in big sheets.

In the FSP composite, the homogeneous FA dispersion with no particle-free zones means hard, thermally stable SiO2-rich particles are present everywhere across the stir zone to share and interrupt the applied contact load. This uniform reinforcement restrains localized plastic flow and subsurface crack propagation in the matrix, so instead of large-scale delamination the surface degrades by localized micro-fragmentation/abrasive action, yielding the fine (<5 µm) particles and loose agglomerates seen in debris (b). Because material is shed in much smaller increments rather than as large detached flakes, the overall volumetric material removal (wear rate) in the FSP sample is curbed compared to the stir-cast sample.

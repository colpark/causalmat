The three calculated-structure panels (d, e, f) show a progressive Mn coordination change as Zn content increases: panel d (K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6) shows octahedral MnN6 (Mn–N distances ~1.98–2.28 Å) in an intact 3D framework of alternating Mn and Fe polyhedra; panel e (K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6) shows a distorted MnN5→MnN3-type site (one Mn–N bond elongated/broken, ~2.15–2.28 Å range) with a grey polyhedron appearing near the Zn/K-occupied channel; panel f (ZnMnFe(CN)6) shows a clearly tetrahedral MnN4 site (Mn–N ~2.08–2.26 Å) alongside multiple grey ZnN4 tetrahedra now directly linked to the brown FeC6 octahedra, replacing part of the original Mn-centered octahedral sublattice.

Mechanism supported by the images and caption: as Zn2+ progressively occupies lattice N-coordinated sites (not the Fe(CN)6 vacancies) rather than simply sitting in interstitial channels, it competes for and elongates/breaks specific Mn–N bonds, lowering Mn's coordination number stepwise from 6 to effectively 3–4. This is attributed in the caption to an intensified Jahn-Teller effect induced by Zn2+, which distorts the MnN6 octahedra. As Zn content rises further, Zn itself adopts tetrahedral ZnN4 coordination and these ZnN4 units become directly linked to the FeC6 octahedra, replacing Mn-Fe octahedral connectivity with a mixed Zn-tetrahedra/Fe-octahedra network. This substitution and bond reorganization is what drives the breakdown of the original 3D Mn-Fe open framework and enables the subsequent phase transition.

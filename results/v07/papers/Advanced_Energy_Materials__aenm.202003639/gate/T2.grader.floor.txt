Question: The Mn coordination environment shifts from an octahedral MnN6 arrangement toward MnN3 then MnN4 as Zn is incorporated, with grey ZnN4 tetrahedra linking to FeC6, as shown across panels F5d, F5e and F5f (conditions K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6, K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6, and ZnMnFe(CN)6). What mechanism drives this change in the Mn polyhedron and the breakdown of the original framework?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): Zn2+ relieves the electronic degeneracy at the Mn site and drives an intense Jahn-Teller distortion that breaks the MnN6 framework (n11).

Candidate answer:
The shift from octahedral MnN6 toward MnN3 then MnN4 is driven by an intensified Jahn-Teller effect induced by Zn2+ incorporation into the lattice sites of the framework. As Zn2+ content rises (from K0.25Zn0.25MnFe(CN)6 to K0.25Zn0.5MnFe(CN)6 to ZnMnFe(CN)6), this distortion progressively strips coordinating N atoms from Mn, converting its polyhedron from six-coordinate to three- then four-coordinate. Concurrently, tetrahedral ZnN4 units emerge and become linked to the octahedral FeC6 units, replacing the original Mn-N/Fe-C connectivity of the 3D open framework. This new ZnN4–FeC6 linkage structurally enables the subsequent phase transition, i.e., it is the structural precursor/trigger that breaks down the original monoclinic P21/n framework as Zn2+ substitution proceeds.

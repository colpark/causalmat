Panel F1d (TEM close-up) shows the boundary between the dark, featureless gold nanoparticle core (upper-left, solid black) and the surrounding mottled gray silica matrix (lower-right, speckled/granular texture typical of amorphous SiO2). Right at that interface there is a thin, continuous lighter-toned (brighter/whiter) rim that traces the irregular, scalloped outline of the gold particle edge before the contrast drops into the grainy gray silica background.

Measured against the 10 nm scale bar in the lower right, this bright rim is very thin — on the order of 1–2 nm wide, i.e. a small fraction of the scale bar length. This is consistent with the caption's statement that the conformal silica shell has a thickness of about 2 nm. The rim is uniform in width as it follows the undulating gold surface, supporting the interpretation that it represents the thin, conformal TMAPS/TEOS-derived silica coating (the DNA-STROBE shell) directly enveloping the gold nanoassembly rather than a gap or artifact.

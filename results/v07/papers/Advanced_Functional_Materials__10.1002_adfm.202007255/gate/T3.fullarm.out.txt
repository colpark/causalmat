Panel F3d (top-view, x1.00k, 50 um scale bar): the deposited Li surface is not smooth/dendritic but consists of large, discrete, roughly circular/nodular mounds with finely rippled, wavy or "cauliflower-like" microtexture on their surface, separated by lower-lying, similarly textured regions between the mounds — no sharp needle-like or whisker dendrites are visible. Each individual dome-like mound spans roughly 20-30 um in diameter (i.e., a substantial fraction of the ~50 um scale bar), making these the largest surface features.

Panel F3e (cross-section, x500, 100 um scale bar) shows how this surface is built up in depth: it reveals a distinct dual-layer/stratified deposit — a rougher, more nodular/spiky top layer sitting on a denser, more compact underlying layer that sits directly on the Cu foil, with a void/gap visible beneath part of the deposit. This is consistent with the described "dual layer (sheets over spheres)" growth mode, where a denser base layer forms first and a coarser, mound-covered layer builds up above it, and the overall deposit thickness in this cross-section is on the order of several tens of micrometers.

Question: The text above proposes that adding Ca to Mg-9Li-1Al should form Al2Ca particles, and that both the base LA91 ingot and the LA91-0.2Ca ingot were cast for comparison. Reading panels F1c and F1d, what differentiates the two XRD scans, and at roughly which diffraction angles does the difference show up?
state the difference between the two conditions; do not rank

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): The LA91-0.2Ca scan (panel F1d) contains extra diffraction markers for Al2Ca appearing near 29.5, 37 and 80.5 degrees, while the base LA91 scan (panel F1c) shows none of these markers (n5).

Grading note: Answer key: the numbers and labels on evidence node n5, judge-confirmed.

Candidate answer:
Both XRD patterns (F1c = LA91, F1d = LA91-0.2Ca) show the same α-Mg (filled triangle) and β-Li (open triangle) reflections at essentially the same 2θ positions (e.g., ~33°, ~37°, ~48°, ~58°, ~65°, ~70°, ~78°). The differentiator is that panel F1d contains an additional phase, Al2Ca, marked with a diamond symbol in the legend, that is entirely absent from F1c's legend and pattern.

These extra Al2Ca peaks in F1d appear at roughly three locations: (1) a low-angle peak near ~29-30° (2θ), just before the first α-Mg peak; (2) a peak/shoulder coinciding with or immediately adjacent to the strong α-Mg peak near ~37°, shown with a diamond marker next to the triangle; and (3) a cluster of several small diamond-marked peaks in the higher-angle region roughly between ~75° and ~83°.

So the qualitative difference is presence vs. absence of Al2Ca diffraction peaks (at ~29-30°, ~37°, and ~75-83°) in the Ca-containing alloy, consistent with the text's proposal that Ca addition forms Al2Ca particles; no ranking of intensity or amount is being made here, just presence/absence of these extra reflections.

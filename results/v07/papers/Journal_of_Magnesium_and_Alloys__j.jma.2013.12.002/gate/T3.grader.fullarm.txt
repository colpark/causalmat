Question: Looking at the BSE panels F2a and F2b of the as-cast LA91-0.2Ca alloy, what do the newly formed Al2Ca particles look like and how are they arranged with respect to the two matrix phases?

Two arrangements are visible: strings of particles strung out along the lines separating the two matrix phases, and separate particles sitting alone within one of the phases, away from those lines.

Candidate answer:
In the low-magnification BSE image (F2a), the newly formed Al2Ca particles appear as small, bright (high-Z-contrast) specks and short chains that trace out a network of curved, wavy lines across the field — these bright lines/dots decorate what are the boundaries between the darker alpha-Mg matrix and the beta-Li phase, i.e., the particles are concentrated along the interphase (alpha/beta) boundaries rather than scattered randomly through the bulk of either phase. The dashed circle in F2a marks one such boundary segment for closer inspection in the higher-magnification image.

In F2b (the zoomed-in view of the circled region), the Al2Ca particles along the boundary (labeled "B") are seen to form a discontinuous, chain-like/beaded ("necklace") string of small angular/blocky bright particles strung out along the curving alpha/beta interface, rather than a continuous film. A separate, isolated particle (labeled "A") of similar size and brightness sits within the matrix away from the boundary, showing that while most Al2Ca particles nucleate and align along the two-phase boundary, a minority also form isolated within one of the matrix phases.

Overall: Al2Ca particles are small, bright, roughly equiaxed/blocky particles that preferentially precipitate as discontinuous chains decorating the alpha-Mg/beta-Li phase boundaries, consistent with them acting as boundary-pinning refiner particles, with occasional isolated particles also present within the matrix grains.

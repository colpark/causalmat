Yes, the two PFM panels together support the claim of a CIPS network embedded in an IPS matrix, but they show different aspects of it. Panel F3f (out-of-plane amplitude) is the clearer evidence: it displays a continuous, branching, tree-like network of bright (high-amplitude) CIPS lamellae threading through the orange IPS background across the whole imaged area, consistent with an interconnected ferroelectric network. Panel F3e (out-of-plane phase) shows the same region contrast-coded by domain polarity/phase (purple vs. orange), but the purple (CIPS-phase) regions appear as smaller, more scattered, less continuous patches rather than a fully connected web — so phase contrast alone under-represents the network's connectivity compared to amplitude. The dashed grid lines and "1 IPS / 2 CIPS" labels in both panels mark the same co-registered locations, confirming the two channels are sampling identical flake regions for direct comparison. Taken together, the pair supports that CIPS lamellae form an extended, spatially structured network within the IPS matrix, with the amplitude channel (F3f) giving the more complete picture of network extent and the phase channel (F3e) confirming the ferroelectric/paraelectric identity of the two phases at specific points. Neither panel by itself resolves in-plane vs. out-of-plane migration paths — these are single out-of-plane PFM channels, not evidence for a distinct in-plane pathway.

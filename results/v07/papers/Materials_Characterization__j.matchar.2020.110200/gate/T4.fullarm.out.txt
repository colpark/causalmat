In both F6c (side disc) and F6d (top disc), the {100} pole figures show a distinct pole sitting essentially at the center of the stereographic projection (the crosshair intersection), which is the position corresponding to the disc-normal/punch axis. This central pole is high intensity (deep red/orange, near the plot's maximum) in both discs, indicating that a <100> crystallographic direction is preferentially aligned close to parallel with the punch (loading) axis in both the side and top sections. Beyond the center, F6d (top) additionally shows two more strong, sharp <100> poles at the periphery along both X and Y (a near-complete cube-type four/five-pole pattern), whereas F6c (side) shows peripheral poles that are more diffuse and asymmetric (a strong pair along Y but weaker/broader lobes along X), suggesting a somewhat less sharp overall <100> texture in the side disc. So crystallographically, the punch axis is close to <100> in orientation in both discs — the pole figures do not show the punch axis sitting near a <110> or <111> pole in either case. This central-<100> similarity means the pole figures alone do not directly explain the claimed Schmid-factor difference; that anisotropy claim rests on how the columnar grain long axis (BD) is oriented relative to the punch axis (perpendicular in the side disc, parallel in the top disc), not on a difference in which <100>-type pole sits at the disc-normal position.

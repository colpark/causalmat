Panel F5f (image: /home/aid1/Documents/causalmat/matmech/Journal_of_Magnesium_and_Alloys/j.jma.2020.11.023/panels/crops/e97b843ad7d9410688d4372c412c5ca0b5c2d658982b414c251282f8eda38559_F.jpg) plots κL+κb vs T (300–625 K) for x=0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04, all with the same Bi:Sb ratio (1.4:0.6) but increasing Se content. All Se-doped samples sit well below the undoped x=0 curve across the entire temperature range (~0.55–0.9 W/m/K vs ~1.1–1.4 W/m/K), a clear, monotonic-with-composition reduction consistent with additional point-defect/mass-contrast phonon scattering from alloying.

However, because Bi/Sb ratio is held fixed across all five curves and only Se content x varies, this panel isolates the effect of Se substitution, not Bi/Sb disorder specifically. It shows that alloying in general (here, adding Se on the Sb site) lowers κL, which is consistent with the broader "alloy scattering lowers κL" mechanism invoked in the text, but it does not directly test or vary Bi/Sb ratio, so it cannot on its own confirm that Bi/Sb disorder per se is the operative scattering source versus Se disorder. In short: the panel supports the general claim that alloy/point-defect scattering suppresses κL, but it is not a controlled test of the Bi/Sb-disorder mechanism specifically, since that variable is constant across all traces shown.

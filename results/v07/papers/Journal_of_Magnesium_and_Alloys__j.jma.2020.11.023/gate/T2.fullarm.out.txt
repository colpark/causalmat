Answer to the materials question:

Panels F2c (Mg3Bi1.375Sb0.625, Se-free) and F2d (Mg3Bi1.375Sb0.5Se0.125, Se-substituted) show the same underlying band topology (near-degenerate conduction-band minima at M, K, L, plus a higher Γ valley ~0.35–0.4 eV above E_F), but with E_F shifted relative to those bands. In F2c the M/K/L conduction minima sit visibly above the E_F=0 dotted line (~0.15 eV), while in F2d they have dropped to touch E_F (visible as a band crossing/kissing point at K and along M–K–Γ). This is consistent with a rigid-band-like doping effect: substituting Se (group VI, one more valence electron than Sb, group V) on the Sb site injects extra electrons per the Mg3.2Bi1.4Sb0.6-xSex composition, raising the carrier (electron) concentration and pushing E_F upward through the conduction bands rather than changing their dispersion shape. Because the M/K/L valleys were already close to E_F, this small upward shift is enough to bring them into degeneracy with E_F, converging multiple conduction-band valleys at the Fermi level — a favorable "band convergence" for n-type thermoelectric performance (higher effective mass/degeneracy for a given carrier concentration). The Γ valley, being much farther above E_F, is essentially unaffected by this shift and remains ~0.35–0.4 eV higher in both panels, confirming the change is a doping-induced E_F shift/valley convergence rather than a change in the intrinsic band structure itself.

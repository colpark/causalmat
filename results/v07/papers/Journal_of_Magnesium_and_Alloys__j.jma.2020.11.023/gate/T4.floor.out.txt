No. The panel varies Se content x (0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04) while holding the Bi/Sb ratio fixed (1.4:0.6) across all samples. Any trend in kappa_L+kappa_b with x therefore reflects the effect of Se substitution/alloying, not Bi/Sb disorder — since Bi/Sb disorder is constant across the whole series, this panel cannot isolate or demonstrate its contribution to lowering kappa_L. To support the specific claim that Bi/Sb disorder lowers kappa_L, one would need a comparison across samples with varying Bi/Sb ratios (e.g., against end-member Mg3Sb2 or Mg3Bi2), which this panel does not provide. So the panel is evidence (at most) for Se-alloying scattering effects, not for the Bi/Sb-disorder claim as stated.

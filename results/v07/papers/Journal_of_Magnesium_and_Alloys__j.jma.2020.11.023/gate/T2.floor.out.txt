The mechanism is Se acting as an electron donor when it substitutes for Sb, which adds extra conduction electrons (increasing n-type carrier concentration) and thereby raises the Fermi level relative to the band structure. Because the M, K, and L conduction-band minima sit only ~0.15 eV above E_F in the undoped case (panel c), this Fermi-level rise (essentially a rigid band-filling/Moss-Burstein-type shift) is enough to pull E_F up to meet those valleys in panel d, making them appear degenerate with the band edge. The Gamma-point conduction minimum, however, lies much deeper (~0.35–0.4 eV above E_F), so the same Fermi-level shift is insufficient to bring it down to E_F, leaving it essentially unchanged between panels c and d. In short, the apparent band-structure difference reflects a shift of the Fermi level due to Se donor doping rather than an intrinsic change in the calculated band dispersions themselves, and this selectively brings the near-degenerate M/K/L valleys into play for multi-valley transport while leaving the higher Gamma valley inactive. This is consistent with the stated goal of using Se substitution on Sb sites to enhance n-type carrier concentration and band convergence for improved thermoelectric performance.

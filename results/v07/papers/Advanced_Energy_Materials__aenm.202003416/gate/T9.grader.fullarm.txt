Question: Panel F1e plots Coulombic efficiency of the Li|Cu pouch cells with and without external pressure. Does this panel support the claim that external pressure raises and stabilises Coulombic efficiency over cycling?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): No. Even with pressure, Coulombic efficiency still falls, from about 95% down to about 84%, over cycles 15 to 21, and the panel only shows 21 cycles total, so it does not establish a stable, raised CE.

Candidate answer:
Panel (e) plots CE (%) vs. cycle number (1-21) for Li|Cu cells "With pressure" (red) and "Without pressure" (blue).

Partial support. The panel does support the "stabilises" part of the claim: the red (with-pressure) points track tightly in a narrow ~85-95% band across all ~20 cycles, whereas the blue (without-pressure) points are visibly more scattered, dipping to ~81% (cycle 17) and spiking to ~107-110% (cycles 14 and 20) — i.e., without pressure shows larger cycle-to-cycle fluctuation/instability.

However, the panel does not clearly support the "raises" part: for most of the cycling range the red and blue points overlap closely (both ~88-95%), and the without-pressure series even shows some values equal to or higher than with-pressure (including the >100% outlier spikes, which are likely measurement artifacts rather than true efficiency gains). Only toward the later cycles (~17-21) does with-pressure appear modestly higher/more consistent than the erratic without-pressure trace.

So the image best supports increased CE stability under pressure, with only weak/qualified support for a mean CE increase; it does not show a large, unambiguous overall boost in CE magnitude from external pressure.

Candidate answer:
Panel (a) is a line plot of a biaxial yield locus in normalized principal stress space (σ2/σ0 vs σ1/σ0), both axes ranging from -2 to 2 with gridlines at integer values. Two yield surfaces are overlaid: a black solid curve labeled "Isotropic (von Mises)," and a blue dotted curve labeled "Transverse isotropic Hill48 (this study)." The isotropic von Mises curve is a symmetric ellipse tilted at 45°, elongated along the σ1=σ2 diagonal, passing through approximately (±1,0), (0,±1), and (1,1)/(-1,-1) near the axis extremes. The Hill48 (transverse isotropic) curve is a smaller, more rounded/near-circular dotted ellipse nested mostly inside the von Mises curve, indicating a reduced yield stress envelope reflecting material anisotropy compared to the isotropic case. Both curves are symmetric about the origin and cross near σ1/σ0 = 0, σ2/σ0 = ±1 and vice versa. There is no scale bar (not a micrograph); this is a schematic/computed stress-locus diagram, not an imaging technique.

Panels F1c (TEM), F1d (SEM), and F1e (HAADF-STEM) all show populations of discrete, roughly spherical particles on the order of ~100-200 nm in diameter (scale bars 100 nm in c/d inset, 1 µm for the wider SEM field, 200 nm in e), consistent with the target monolayer hollow vesicle size regime for a Dox-carrying nanovesicle.

In F1c, the low-magnification TEM view shows many similarly sized spheres each displaying a dense, granular/speckled rim (the nanoparticle-decorated BCP shell) surrounding a lighter, less-contrasted interior, i.e., a hollow-looking core consistent with an aqueous lumen. The inset (white arrow) zooms on one sphere with a partially broken/torn edge, directly revealing a continuous particulate shell (a single monolayer of tethered manganese-ferrite nanoparticles) enclosing an empty-looking cavity rather than a solid nanoparticle aggregate.

F1d (SEM) shows the vesicles as solid-looking, uniformly round exterior surfaces at lower magnification (with some fused/aggregated clusters and film-like debris in the background), and the inset again highlights (arrow) a sphere with a broken shell, exposing what appears to be an open/hollow interior rather than solid fill, confirming the shell-and-cavity architecture from the outside.

F1e (HAADF-STEM), which is Z-contrast sensitive and thus highlights the electron-dense Mn-ferrite nanoparticles specifically, shows the same spherical particles with bright, densely packed nanoparticle rims forming a continuous, closely packed monolayer shell around each sphere, while the interior appears comparatively dim/hollow, reinforcing that the ferrite nanoparticles are confined to a single-particle-thick membrane rather than filling the core.

Together, the three panels are mutually consistent: they show uniform, roughly spherical Dox-MV particles (~100-200 nm) with a shell built from a monolayer of tethered manganese-ferrite nanoparticles and an optically/electronically less dense, apparently hollow lumen — the broken-membrane examples in each inset (arrows) providing direct structural evidence of a hollow vesicular interior rather than a solid nanoparticle sphere.

This is a 4x2 grid of SEM (scanning electron microscopy) micrographs comparing surface morphology at two time points, "0 d" (left column) and "7 d" (right column), across four sample rows (labeled by faint letters at left edge, partially cropped out of frame). Each panel has a 50 µm scale bar in the lower right. At 0 d, the top two rows show smooth surfaces with fine parallel striations/polishing lines and scattered small dark pits, while the bottom two rows show a darker, faintly striated background with sparse bright specks. By 7 d, the top two rows develop pronounced networks of dark branching/mud-crack-like fissures dividing the surface into irregular polygonal cells (larger, more open cracks in row 2 than row 1), the third row develops a dense field of bright granular/particulate aggregates or corrosion nodules covering much of the surface, and the fourth row shows a comparatively subtle change with fine cracking and a cluster of bright particulate deposits near the lower-middle. Overall trend: progressive surface degradation, cracking, and/or particulate deposition (consistent with corrosion or degradation testing) increasing from day 0 to day 7, with the severity and morphology of change differing by sample row.

F3a (ATR-FTIR) shows four stacked spectra: AZ31 (featureless), AZ31-OH (labeled 3700 cm-1 Mg-OH peak), PCL, and PCL-LS. The PCL-LS trace is essentially superimposable on the plain PCL trace — same ester carbonyl peak at 1723 cm-1, same CH2 stretches at 2944/2865 cm-1, and the same fingerprint-region peak pattern (1000–1300 cm-1). No extra or shifted bands attributable to lawsone (e.g., a distinct quinone C=O or phenolic O-H band) are visible or labeled in the PCL-LS spectrum relative to pure PCL.

This is consistent with, but does not directly confirm, the claim that lawsone is buried beneath a lawsone-free PCL top layer: ATR-FTIR is a surface-sensitive technique (probes roughly the top ~1–2 µm), so if lawsone were confined to an inner layer under a pure PCL top coat, the surface spectrum would indeed look like plain PCL, exactly as shown. However, the panel equally cannot rule out that lawsone is simply absent or present at too low a concentration to produce a detectable signal — the data show an absence of a lawsone signature, not positive evidence of a buried lawsone layer. So F3a is compatible with the claimed stratified architecture but does not by itself prove it; it would need to be paired with other evidence (e.g., cross-sectional imaging, depth profiling, or release/color data) to substantiate the "inner layer beneath a lawsone-free top layer" claim.

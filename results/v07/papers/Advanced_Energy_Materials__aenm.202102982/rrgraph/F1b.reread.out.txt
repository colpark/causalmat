This is panel (b), a computational DFT-style adsorption/binding-energy bar chart, not a microscopy image (no scale bar present). It compares the binding energy (eV, y-axis, range 0 to -5) of two adsorbates — NH4+ and Zn2+ — on a Zn (101) substrate. Above each bar is a small ball-and-stick/space-filling model showing the ion (NH4+ tetrahedron and Zn2+ sphere, shown in blue and gray respectively) adsorbed on a gray hexagonal-lattice slab representing the Zn(101) surface. The NH4+ bar (red-shaded gradient) shows a binding energy of -2.10 eV, notably more negative than the Zn2+ bar (blue-shaded gradient) at -0.99 eV, indicating NH4+ binds roughly twice as strongly to the Zn(101) surface as Zn2+ does. The trend illustrated is that ammonium ions are preferentially/more strongly adsorbed on the Zn substrate compared to zinc ions themselves, consistent with a electrolyte-additive or corrosion-inhibition context in a battery/materials paper.

Panel (A) shows fluorescence microscopy (live/dead or cell-viability style staining, green fluorescent signal on black background, likely calcein-AM/live-cell stain) comparing cell attachment/proliferation on two material surfaces over time. It is a 2-row by 3-column grid: rows are labeled "Mg-Zn-Y-Nd" (top) and "317L SS" (bottom, stainless steel), columns are time points "4 h", "1 day", and "3 days". Each micrograph has a small white scale bar in the lower-right corner (label barely legible but present in each panel). Trend: on the Mg-Zn-Y-Nd surface, green fluorescent (cell) signal is sparse at 4 h, decreases further by 1 day, and is markedly diminished/fragmented by 3 days, with some scattered small bright punctate spots at 3 days. On the 317L SS surface, cell density is denser than Mg-Zn-Y-Nd at all time points and increases progressively from 4 h to 1 day to 3 days, culminating in a dense, near-confluent network of green-stained cells at 3 days. Overall the panel indicates better/increasing cell viability and proliferation on 317L SS versus poor and declining cell coverage on the Mg-Zn-Y-Nd alloy over the 3-day culture period.

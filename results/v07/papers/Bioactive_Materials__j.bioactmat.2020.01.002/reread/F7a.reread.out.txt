Panel (A) shows fluorescence microscopy images (live/dead or cell-adhesion staining, green fluorescent signal on black background) comparing two materials — "Mg-Zn-Y-Nd" (top row) and "317L SS" (stainless steel, bottom row) — at three time points: 4 h, 1 day, and 3 days (six panels total in a 2x3 grid). Each panel has a white scale bar in the lower-right corner (label too small to read exactly, appears to be ~100 µm). Trend: for Mg-Zn-Y-Nd, sparse green puncta/aggregates at 4 h, increasing slightly and clustering by 1 day, then decreasing in density/brightness by 3 days. For 317L SS, green punctate signal is present and fairly dense already at 4 h and 1 day, then dramatically increases to near-confluent, densely packed green spots covering the whole field by 3 days — indicating much greater cell attachment/proliferation (or fluorescent signal) on the stainless steel substrate over time compared to the Mg-alloy, which shows a decline at the 3-day timepoint.

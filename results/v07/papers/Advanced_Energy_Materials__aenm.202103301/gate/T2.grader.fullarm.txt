Question: In panel F1c, how does the alignment between the W 5d states and the 3d states of the substituted atom differ between pure WO2 (the W1/W2 pair) and the heteroatom-substituted W-M structures shown for the Fe, Co, Ni, Cu sweep?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): In pure WO2 the W1/W2 5d states overlap with each other across the whole plotted energy range, whereas in the M-substituted structures the M 3d peaks sit apart from the W 5d states (o2).

Grading note: Match to the labels of o2, judge-confirmed.

Candidate answer:
In panel F1c, the pure WO2 pair (W1 3d, pink; W2 5d, gray) shows broad, delocalized density of states that continuously straddle both sides of EF, indicating the two W 5d orbitals hybridize strongly and nearly symmetrically across the Fermi level (a metallic-like, largely overlapping alignment).

In the heteroatom-substituted W-M structures, the M 3d states (colored curves) become sharp, discrete peaks that sit mostly below EF, while the residual W 5d states (gray) remain a broad, low background underneath them — so the alignment is no longer symmetric/continuous but consists of localized M 3d peaks partially overlapping a diffuse W 5d band, mainly on the occupied (below-EF) side.

The degree of alignment with EF also varies with the heteroatom: for Cu and Ni the 3d peaks sit further below EF with little density crossing to the unoccupied side; for Co the peaks move somewhat closer to EF; and for Fe the 3d states span both below and above EF (peaks on each side of the dashed line), most closely resembling the W1-W2 pattern and suggesting Fe 3d couples most strongly/closely with W 5d near the Fermi level among the substituents.

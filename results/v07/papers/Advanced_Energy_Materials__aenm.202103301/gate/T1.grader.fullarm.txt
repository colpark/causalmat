Question: Using the context above and panel F1b (the COHP plot), how does the antibonding-state region near the Fermi level compare across the W-W, W-Fe, W-Co, W-Ni, and W-Cu bonding pairs shown?
state the difference between the two conditions; do not rank

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): The antibonding-state area just below the Fermi level is essentially absent for the W-W bond and grows steadily larger through the series, reaching its largest extent for the W-Cu bond (o1).

Candidate answer:
Panel F1b shows stacked COHP curves for W-W, W-Fe, W-Co, W-Ni, and W-Cu, with a shaded band (~-1 to 0 eV) marking the antibonding-state region just below EF.

The key difference is in how much shaded/filled antibonding intensity each pair contributes within that near-EF window versus elsewhere in the spectrum: W-Cu shows a large, clearly filled antibonding peak that occupies most of the shaded window right up to EF, indicating substantial antibonding character close to the Fermi level. W-Ni also has a shaded antibonding contribution in that window, but its dominant feature is instead a large bonding-state peak located further away, around -2 to -2.5 eV (explicitly labeled "bonding state" with a dashed arrow), so its antibonding weight near EF is comparatively small next to that bonding peak. W-Co's curve is nearly flat through the -1 to 0 eV window, with only a slight bump right around/after EF rather than a distinct shaded antibonding feature. W-Fe shows a shaded peak, but it sits mostly just past EF (roughly 0 to 0.5 eV) rather than within the -1 to 0 eV window itself. W-W is essentially flat with minimal curve variation near EF, showing no pronounced antibonding peak at all.

So the pairs differ in whether the antibonding weight is concentrated right at/near EF (W-Cu, and to a lesser extent W-Fe just past EF) versus diffuse/absent (W-W, W-Co) or overshadowed by a stronger bonding peak well below EF (W-Ni).

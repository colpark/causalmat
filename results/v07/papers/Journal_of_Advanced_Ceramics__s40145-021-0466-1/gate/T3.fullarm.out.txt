Findings from Fig. 4b (LSV curves, panel crop 562a39bf...B.jpg):

The panel plots J (mA/cm2) vs E (V vs RHE) for NC-1000 (light purple), NPC-800 (green), NPC-900 (blue), NPC-1000 (red), and Pt/C (black).

- NC-1000 (the non-porous, ZnCl2-free N-doped carbon control) tracks almost on top of Pt/C for most of the curve: its onset/half-wave potential is among the best of all samples (comparable to or slightly better than NPC-1000's), and it reaches the same diffusion-limited current density (~-5 mA/cm2) as NPC-1000.
- NPC-1000 (the porous sample carbonized at the same 1000 C) has a somewhat more gradual/right-shifted drop and a slightly lower half-wave potential than NC-1000, though it also plateaus near -5 mA/cm2.
- NPC-800 and NPC-900 are the clear underperformers (NPC-800 plateaus at only ~-3 mA/cm2, an incomplete/2-electron-like limiting current; NPC-900 is intermediate).

So this specific NC-1000 vs NPC-1000 comparison does not straightforwardly support the pore-confinement narrative: the non-porous control (no ZnCl2-templated fine/medium pores) performs as well as or marginally better than the porous NPC-1000 in onset potential, and both reach essentially the same limiting (4-electron-equivalent) current density. If pore-confined peroxide re-reduction were the dominant driver of ORR efficiency at 1000 C, one would expect NPC-1000 to clearly outperform NC-1000 in this panel, but the curves instead show near-parity (NC-1000 even edging ahead in half-wave potential). The clearer pore-volume-dependent trend in this figure is among the NPC series itself (NPC-800 < NPC-900 < NPC-1000), not the NC-1000-vs-NPC-1000 contrast.

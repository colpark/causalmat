Question: The claim under test is that the particle/matrix interface in the AZ31/Ti-6Al-4V friction-stir composite is continuous and void-free. Given SEM panels F11e and F11f (fracture surface at 21 vol% Ti-6Al-4V) alongside F1a (the starting powder), what does a cross-check across these three panels show about the particles, and does it support the claim?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): The cross-check finds a mismatch, not confirmation: the particles seen on the fracture surface in F11e and F11f are near-spherical, unlike the angular flake powder shown in F1a. This observation qualifies rather than simply confirms the claim that the interface is continuous and void-free.

Grading note: Answer key: the audit ruling on the panel (o12), which is the sole graded target for this trace.

Candidate answer:
Cross-check: F1a shows the starting Ti-6Al-4V powder as angular, faceted, blocky particles (tens of microns), not spheres. In the fracture-surface panels F11e and F11f, the particles embedded at 21 vol% appear as smooth, rounded/spherical protrusions sitting essentially intact on top of (or partly proud of) the torn Mg matrix, with a visible boundary/gap or socket-like rim around their base rather than matrix material conforming tightly and continuously up to the particle surface. This shape mismatch (angular starting powder vs. rounded fracture-surface particles) combined with the particles appearing pulled whole out of their sockets, rather than fractured through or coated by torn ductile matrix ligaments, is a classic fractographic signature of interfacial decohesion/pull-out, not of a strongly bonded, void-free interface. If the interface were truly continuous and void-free, one would expect the matrix to have torn around/through the particle with dimpled ductile tearing hugging the particle contour, or the particle itself to have fractured, not to sit as a clean, intact sphere with a surrounding gap. So this three-panel cross-check (F1a vs F11e/F11f) undermines rather than supports the "continuous, void-free interface" claim — it points instead toward particle pull-out/debonding at the particle-matrix interface in the fractured composite.

Candidate answer:
Panel F10c plots elongation (%) vs. volume fraction (%) for two series: AZ31/Ti-6Al-4V (blue, ~14.5% → ~9.3% as fraction rises to ~21%) and AZ31B/SiC (purple, oscillating between ~2.6% and ~6.6% across similar 0-20% fractions). The Ti-6Al-4V curve does sit well above the SiC curve at every matched volume fraction, so the panel does support the qualitative claim that AZ31/Ti-6Al-4V retains more elongation than AZ31B/SiC at comparable reinforcement levels.

However, it does not support treating this as a fair, like-for-like comparison of the reinforcement effect itself: the two curves start from very different unreinforced-matrix baselines at 0% volume fraction — AZ31/Ti-6Al-4V begins near 14.5% elongation while AZ31B/SiC begins near only 4.2%, a roughly 3.5x gap before any particles are added. That baseline gap indicates the two base materials/processing routes (different matrix designation AZ31 vs AZ31B, and almost certainly different fabrication methods, e.g., this paper's FSP vs. whatever process produced the cited SiC composites in ref [39]) already differ substantially in ductility. So the plotted superiority of AZ31/Ti-6Al-4V conflates an inherent matrix/process difference with the effect of the reinforcement type, meaning the panel shows the comparison is confounded rather than controlled, even though it visually corroborates the retained-elongation claim.

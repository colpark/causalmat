This is a line/scatter plot (panel labeled "(a)"), not a micrograph — no scale bar is present. It plots Critical Resolved Shear Stress (CRSS, in MPa, y-axis 0–80) versus Temperature (°C, x-axis 0–400) for a hexagonal (likely Mg-alloy) material, showing three competing deformation modes: {10-12} twinning (blue triangles/circles with blue fitted curve), {1-100} prismatic slip (red triangles/circles with red fitted curve), and (0001) basal slip (green diamonds with green fitted curve, near-flat). An inset schematic shows a hexagonal unit cell/crystal orientation diagram with crystallographic directions [1120], [0001], [01-10], and a "45° [1 1 -2 1.85]" label, indicating the loading orientation used for CRSS extraction. Trends: basal slip CRSS stays low and essentially flat (~3–5 MPa) across the whole temperature range, while twinning CRSS starts highest (~55–70 MPa) at low temperature and decreases with increasing temperature, converging toward ~40–50 MPa by 300–400°C; prismatic slip CRSS starts lower than twinning at room temperature but decreases more slowly, crossing over the twinning curve around 150–250°C so that at higher temperatures prismatic slip CRSS exceeds or matches twinning CRSS. Data points at each temperature show two or three symbols (triangle/circle) representing repeat measurements or different sample conditions, scattered around each fitted curve.

Context from a materials paper:
- Percutaneous implants need a tight skin seal; bare Ti lets epithelium grow down along the implant and a fibrous capsule form
- Ti implant surface carrying a hydroxyapatite nanorod coating as the base
- Hydrothermal growth of HA and Si-HA nanorod coatings on Ti
- Si is incorporated in the Si-HA nanorods at about 3-5% and is absent from HA
- Si sits on phosphate sites as orthosilicate SiO4 4- replacing PO4 3- in the HA lattice
- Si substitution distorts the HA lattice: reflections shift to lower angle and the a-axis lengthens slightly (9.425 to 9.437 A)
- Si-HA releases more Ca than HA at every immersion time and also releases Si (~0.5-0.6 ppm by 28 d)
- Ions released from Si-HA (Si and extra Ca) stimulate fibroblast TGF-b1/CTGF signalling, driving alpha-SMA, Col-I and collagen production
- Si-HA up-regulates fibrogenic genes (TGF-b1, CTGF, alpha-SMA, Col-I) in fibroblasts above HA and Ti

Candidate answer:
Panel (a) shows X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns, not a micrograph. It plots intensity (a.u.) versus 2θ (°) from about 10° to 100° for three samples processed/annealed at 1400 °C, 1500 °C, and 1600 °C, stacked vertically (black = 1400 °C, red = 1500 °C, teal/blue = 1600 °C) with an offset baseline for clarity; there is no scale bar since this is a diffractogram, not an image. Peaks are labeled with symbols identifying phases: filled squares (α-Al2O3), stars (cBN), filled inverted triangles (Si3Al3O3N5, written as Si_xAl_xO_xN_x-type sialon), and open inverted triangles (a second sialon phase, Si1.8Al0.2O1.2N1.8). The 1400 °C trace shows mostly broad/weak features with only a couple of labeled α-Al2O3/cBN peaks, indicating limited crystalline phase formation. With increasing temperature (1500 °C then 1600 °C), the number and intensity of sharp peaks increase markedly, especially a very strong cBN peak near 2θ ≈ 43° and growing sialon peaks in the 30–40° region, indicating progressive crystallization and increased formation of secondary sialon phases (and retained cBN and α-Al2O3) as processing temperature rises.

Question: The context above establishes the three binder systems and notes that cBN/binder bonding is strong in S1 but weak in S2 and S3. Panels F8c and F8f show Sample S3 sintered at 1600C. Looking at these two panels, what path does the crack take through the microstructure?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): The crack runs along the rim of a large particle, tracing through the binder (marked with a dotted trace) rather than cutting straight through the grain itself.

Grading note: Answer key: the numbers and labels on the evidence node o19, judge-confirmed.

Candidate answer:
In both F8c and F8f (Sample S3, 1600 C), the crack (marked by the yellow dashed line) runs along the boundary between adjacent grains/phases rather than cutting through grain interiors — i.e., it follows an intergranular path along the cBN/binder (or grain-grain) interface. In F8c the crack traces a dark, winding boundary channel between two rounded grains, with the yellow circle highlighting a small bridging particle it deflects around. In F8f the crack follows a fairly straight but interfacial line separating a smooth-faced grain on the right from a rougher, angular grain cluster on the left, again tracking the boundary rather than transgranular cleavage. This intergranular crack path is consistent with the weak cBN/binder bonding described for S3 (and S2), where the interface is the mechanically weakest link, so cracks preferentially propagate along grain/interface boundaries instead of through the grains themselves.

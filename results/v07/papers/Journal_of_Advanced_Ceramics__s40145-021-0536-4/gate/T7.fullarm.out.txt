The crack consistently runs along grain boundaries rather than cutting straight through grain interiors — i.e., it follows an intergranular path, hugging the facets of individual grains and turning at each triple junction/boundary. The grains bordering the crack path appear as distinct, angular, blocky/polygonal particles with visible facets and boundaries that remain intact (not fractured through their bulk), and the crack appears to pry open the boundary/interface between adjacent grains rather than splitting a grain in two. This zig-zag, boundary-following morphology is consistent with weak grain-boundary (binder) bonding in the S2 (Al2O3-ZrO2) sample, where the crack preferentially opens along the poorly-bonded cBN/binder interfaces rather than propagating transgranularly through stronger grain bodies.

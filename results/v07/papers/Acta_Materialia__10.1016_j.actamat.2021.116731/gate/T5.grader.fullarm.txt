Question: You are given the identification claim that the operative transformation is the herringbone B2-B19' martensitic transformation producing (001)B19' compound twins (not the conventional B2-B19' transformation with [011]B19' type II twins), along with the CRSS map for the herringbone transformation across the orientation triangle, panel F8c. Reading that panel, is the critical resolved shear stress for this transformation uniform across orientations, and specifically how does the [001] corner compare to the rest of the triangle?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): No, CRSS is not orientation independent for the herringbone transformation either: in the CRSS map the [001] corner cell is still several times lower (~40-60 MPa) than the rest of the triangle (~150-300 MPa).

Grading note: Answer key: the audit ruling on the panel, node a2, read from panel F8c.

Candidate answer:
Reading panel F8c (the herringbone B2-B19' transformation CRSS map over the [001]-[1̄11]-[011] standard triangle, scale 0-400 MPa): the CRSS is clearly not uniform across orientations. The [001] corner is dark blue, the lowest value on the entire map (roughly 50-80 MPa), while the rest of the triangle is mostly green-yellow (~150-250 MPa) with an orange local maximum (~300-350 MPa) near the middle of the triangle close to the [1̄11]-[011] edge. So [001] stands out as the orientation with the minimum CRSS for the herringbone transformation, markedly lower than essentially everywhere else in the triangle, consistent with [001] being the orientation where stress-induced transformation is most favored.

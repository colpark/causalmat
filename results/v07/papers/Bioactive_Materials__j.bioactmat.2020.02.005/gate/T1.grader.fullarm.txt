Candidate answer:
Findings from the two XPS panels (F6c = Er 4d, F6d = Ce 3d):

Panel F6c (Er 4d): The raw red trace is dominated by high-amplitude, spike-like noise with no clearly resolved peak shape; a smooth black curve is overlaid and labeled "168.8 eV," but this curve looks like an imposed/fitted envelope rather than something that emerges naturally from the raw data — the raw signal itself does not show an obvious, well-defined maximum at 168.8 eV distinguishable from the noise floor.

Panel F6d (Ce 3d): The plot shows only a raw, noisy black trace with eight dashed vertical guide lines labeled u, u′, u″, u‴, v, v′, v″, v‴ — the standard nomenclature for Ce3+/Ce4+ mixed-valence Ce 3d satellite/main peaks. However, there is no background subtraction, peak fitting, or deconvolution shown, and several of the labeled positions (e.g., u‴, v″) do not correspond to any visually distinguishable local maximum above the surrounding noise; the spectrum reads as essentially flat/noisy across most of the range with only marginal bumps near a few of the marked positions (v region shows a modest rise).

Conclusion: The panels do not convincingly confirm the labeled peak assignments. Both spectra have very poor signal-to-noise ratio, and neither shows proper curve fitting/deconvolution that would substantiate the specific oxidation-state assignments (Er3+ at 168.8 eV; Ce3+/Ce4+ mixed valence via the eight-line Ce 3d pattern). The labels appear to be asserted based on expected literature binding energies rather than demonstrated by resolved features in the actual data, so the claim of confirmed incorporation/oxidation states is not well supported by this XPS evidence alone.

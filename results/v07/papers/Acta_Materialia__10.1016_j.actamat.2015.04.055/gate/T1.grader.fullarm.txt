Question: You're told that a carbon-free TNM alloy is compared with a carbon-added TNM0.75C alloy, both hot-forged and homogenised before sectioning, and you're handed the two SEM fields (one per alloy). What difference in grain morphology do the two images show between the alloys?
state the difference between the two conditions; do not rank

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): The carbon-free alloy shows globular grains throughout. The carbon-added alloy still has globular grains but also shows coarse lamellar colonies mixed in among them.

Grading note: Answer key: the description on the evidence node o2, judge-confirmed against the panel content.

Candidate answer:
Panel a (carbon-free TNM, hot-forged and homogenised) shows a fully equiaxed microstructure: the γ, α2, and βo phases all appear as compact, roughly polygonal grains of broadly similar size, with no elongated or aligned features.

Panel b (carbon-added TNM0.75C, hot-forged and homogenised) shows a mixed/heterogeneous grain morphology: part of the field (left/center) contains elongated, parallel, lamellar or needle-like grains arranged in colony-like clusters, while the remainder of the field (right side) consists of equiaxed polygonal grains similar to those in panel a.

So the difference is that the carbon-free alloy has a uniformly equiaxed grain structure throughout, whereas the carbon-added alloy shows a coexistence of elongated/lamellar grain regions alongside equiaxed grain regions within the same field.

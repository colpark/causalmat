Panel (b) shows two atomic force microscopy (AFM) topography images comparing a film/coating surface "without additive" (top) and "with additive" (bottom), each with a 1 μm scale bar. The color scale runs from dark brown/black (low regions) to bright yellow/white (high/peak regions), typical of AFM height-mapped topography.

Without additive: the surface shows large, smooth, rounded grain-like features with broad dark valleys/depressions between them, giving a coarse, undulating morphology with relatively low apparent roughness contrast within grains.

With additive: the surface is markedly finer-grained and more densely packed, with many small, tightly packed nodular/granular features and less pronounced dark valley regions, indicating a smoother, more uniform, and refined microstructure compared to the additive-free sample.

Overall trend: addition of the additive appears to refine/homogenize the surface grain structure, reducing large-scale roughness features and producing a more compact, fine-grained morphology relative to the coarser, more irregular surface seen without the additive.

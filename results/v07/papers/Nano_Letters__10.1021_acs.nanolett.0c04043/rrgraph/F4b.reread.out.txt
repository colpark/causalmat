Panel "b" shows two atomic force microscopy (AFM) height maps of the same striped/dumbbell-shaped patterned structure (alternating narrow "neck" and wider "bulb" segments arranged in a repeating stack), compared "Before peeling" (left) and "After peeling" (right). Both panels have a 1 μm scale bar and a color-coded height scale bar below: the "Before peeling" image uses a 0–120 nm range (dark brown/black background with bright yellow-gold raised features), while the "After peeling" image uses a much narrower 0–12 nm range, giving the surface a more uniform, lighter gold/tan appearance with less height contrast between features and background. The pattern geometry (stripe positions, necking regions, bulb widths) is essentially identical in both images, indicating the underlying patterned structure is preserved through the peeling process. The key visual/quantitative difference is the collapse of height contrast (topographic relief) after peeling — the background substrate and the patterned features become much closer in height (max scale drops 10-fold, from 120 nm to 12 nm), consistent with removal of a thick overlying layer (leaving only a thin residual or the underlying pattern) by the peeling step. Some small bright particulate/dot features are visible scattered on the patterned bands in both images, more prominent in the "before" image.

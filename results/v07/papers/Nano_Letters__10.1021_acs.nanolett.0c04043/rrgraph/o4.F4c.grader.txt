Candidate answer:
Panel (c) shows AFM topography images comparing a patterned strip before and after a "peeling" process, with an accompanying line-profile plot.

Left AFM image ("Before peeling"): a bright, granular/nanoparticle-textured strip on a black background, with a 500 nm scale bar; the color scale spans 60–100 nm height (yellowish-orange colormap), and there is a visible gap/notch between two strip segments with a couple of bright particulate features at the break.

Right AFM image ("After peeling"): the same type of patterned strip, now imaged with a much finer height scale (0–12 nm, same 500 nm scale bar), showing a smoother, less textured surface and a cleaner, more well-defined gap between the two strip segments.

Below, a line plot of height z (nm) versus lateral position x (µm, 0–3.0 µm) overlays two cross-sectional profiles: the "Before peeling" trace (orange/salmon) shows large excursions — a deep negative dip to about −15 nm followed by a sharp positive spike to about +13 nm near x ≈ 1.4–1.7 µm, plus smaller bumps elsewhere; the "After peeling" trace (blue) is comparatively flat and stays within roughly ±5 nm across the whole scan, with only a modest dip near the same gap location. The overall trend indicates that peeling substantially reduces surface roughness/height variation and produces a much flatter, cleaner gap/edge profile compared to before peeling.

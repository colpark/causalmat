Panel d shows atomic force microscopy (AFM) topography maps comparing a patterned thin-film/2D-material stack peeled off a substrate under two conditions: "Peeled in air" (left) and "Peeled in water" (right). Both images show the same striped/rectangular patterned region (~1.5 µm field of view based on the scale bar of 500 nm) with a false-color height scale (yellow = high, dark = low): the air-peeled sample uses a 45–100 nm range and shows large bright blob-like residues/tears plus irregular dark voids within the stripes, indicating rough, damaged, non-uniform peeling with residual material or torn flakes. The water-peeled sample uses a much smaller 0–35 nm range and shows cleaner, more uniform stripe edges with small scattered dark speckles/pits but no large blobs, indicating a smoother, more controlled peel. Below the images is a line profile (z in nm vs. x in µm, 0–1.5 µm) labeled "Peeled in water," showing periodic rectangular trenches ~10–13 nm deep with widths matching the striped pattern, consistent with clean removal of material down to a well-defined depth at the stripe locations. Overall the panel demonstrates that peeling in water produces a smoother, more uniform, lower-height-variation surface compared to peeling in air, which leaves large residual clumps and greater roughness.

Across F3a→F3f the GB filler's surface visibly gains a wrinkled carbon coating in two stages.

- F3a/F3b (bare GB): smooth, spherical particles ~20 µm with a subtly rough but largely bald surface — no sheet-like features, just a fine granular texture.
- F3c/F3d (GB@GO): spheres now show thin, crumpled, paper-like sheets draped loosely over parts of the surface, with visible wrinkles/folds and patches of the underlying smooth GB still exposed — a partial, delicate wrapping.
- F3e/F3f (GB@rGO): the spheres are covered by a denser, more continuous shell of wrinkled/ridged material with much rougher, corrugated texture and fuller coverage than the GO stage; little to no bare GB surface remains visible.

So the progression is smooth bare sphere → sparse, thin, wrinkled GO sheet partially coating the surface → thicker, rougher, more fully-enclosing rGO shell with pronounced wrinkles/ridges after thermal reduction. The rGO stage appears visibly rougher and more completely coated than the GO stage, consistent with the described thermal reduction step increasing shell coverage/corrugation on the hollow glass bubble core.

Question: The claim under test is that the Ce layer on the Al nanoparticles is very thin and non-uniform, leaving the particles only partly covered by Ce oxides/hydroxides. Does the O 1s high-resolution XPS spectrum in Figure 2c support this claim, and what does the spectrum show about the relative amounts of the Ce-related oxygen species?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): The O 1s spectrum resolves three oxygen-species components with distinct relative intensities: the Ce2O3 component is the tallest (~275), the Ce-OH component is next (~250), and the CeO2 component is the smallest (~100) (o21). This mix of Ce2O3, Ce-OH and CeO2 signals, rather than a single dominant, uniform CeO2 signature, is the panel reading that qualifies the thin/non-uniform, partial-coverage claim.

Panel F2c (O 1s high-resolution XPS spectrum, image at /home/aid1/Documents/causalmat/matmech/Progress_in_Organic_Coatings/j.porgcoat.2016.09.010/panels/crops/3f6843d04010f6847dcf522c1c8efef9c729d9f3707ef77bff15b79edfa3f409_C.jpg) supports the claim. The envelope is deconvoluted into four components: Al-O (dashed, centered ~531 eV, peak intensity ~380, the largest single component), Ce-OH (dotted, ~532 eV, peak intensity ~370, nearly as large as Al-O), Ce2O3 (dashed red, ~529.5-530 eV, peak intensity ~270), and CeO2 (dash-dot, ~528.5-529 eV, peak intensity ~100, the smallest component). Because the Al-O signal remains the dominant (or co-dominant) contribution to the O 1s spectrum rather than being fully suppressed, this indicates the Al2O3/Al-O surface is still substantially exposed and not fully overcoated by cerium species — directly supporting the claim that the Ce conversion layer is thin and non-uniform, leaving particles only partly covered. Among the Ce-related oxygen species, Ce-OH (hydroxide) and Ce2O3 (Ce3+ oxide) are present in greater relative amounts than CeO2 (Ce4+ oxide), with CeO2 being a minor shoulder, indicating the Ce film is predominantly hydroxide/Ce(III)-oxide in character with only a small fraction of fully oxidized CeO2.

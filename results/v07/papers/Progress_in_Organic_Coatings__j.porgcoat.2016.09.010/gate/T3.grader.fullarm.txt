Candidate answer:
Panel F2b is an Al 2p high-resolution XPS spectrum (not a Ce spectrum) fitted with two components: a dominant peak at ~78.1 eV assigned to metallic Al (intensity ~72, the large majority of the signal) and a smaller peak at ~75.5 eV assigned to Al2O3 (intensity ~17, a minor shoulder). No Ce-related peaks are shown or fitted in this panel at all.

This peak fit does lend indirect support to the "thin, non-uniform, partial coverage" reading, but not directly. XPS is a surface-sensitive technique (probing depth of only a few nm), so the fact that the strong metallic Al 2p signal is still clearly detected—rather than being attenuated or eliminated—implies that whatever surface layer exists (oxide and, by extension, any Ce conversion film) cannot be thick or fully continuous; a thick, uniform overlayer would suppress the underlying metallic Al signal. However, the panel itself only resolves Al-metal vs. Al2O3 chemical states and contains no Ce 3d or Ce-oxide component, so it cannot directly confirm partial/non-uniform Ce coverage—only that the Al core beneath any coating remains XPS-detectable, which is consistent with, but not direct proof of, the claim about the Ce layer specifically.

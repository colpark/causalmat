In Fig. 2c (O 1s high-resolution XPS spectrum), the three Ce-related deconvoluted peaks differ notably in height: Ce2O3 (red dashed, ~529.7 eV) is the tallest of the three Ce components at roughly 270 intensity units; Ce-OH (black dotted, ~532 eV) is comparable but somewhat lower, around 250-260; and CeO2 (red dash-dot, ~528.7 eV) is distinctly the smallest, only about 100 intensity units — roughly a third the height of Ce2O3 and Ce-OH. This uneven, comparatively small contribution from the Ce oxide/hydroxide species relative to the Al-O signal is consistent with the claim that the Ce layer is thin and only partly covers the Al nanoparticle surface, though the fitted peak heights alone do not directly establish "non-uniformity" — that inference relies on the relatively low overall Ce signal intensity compared to Al-O, and the differing proportions among Ce2O3, Ce-OH, and CeO2 (with CeO2 being the smallest contributor).

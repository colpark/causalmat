Candidate answer:
Figure 3d plots X-ray absorption edge energy (eV) versus normalized charge capacity (C/C1.15V) for both Fe (top, K-edge position at half-height) and V (bottom, pre-edge peak position), overlaid on dashed reference lines labeled for each oxidation state (Fe2+, Fe3+; V3+, V4+, V5+).

Both curves show a monotonic increase in edge energy with increasing charge capacity: Fe edge energy rises from roughly the Fe2+ reference line (~7124.7 eV) at low capacity to near the Fe3+ line (~7125.8 eV) at full charge (capacity ~1.0), and V pre-edge energy rises from near the V3+ line (~5469.8 eV) at low capacity, through the V4+ line (~5470.0 eV) around capacity 0.15-0.2, up toward the V5+ line (~5470.3-5470.5 eV) at capacity 0.5-1.0.

This supports the tested claim: the panel shows simultaneous, gradual oxidation-state shifts in both metals during charging — Fe moving from Fe2+ toward Fe3+ and V moving from V3+ through V4+ toward V5+ — consistent with charge compensation by both V and Fe valence changes as stated.

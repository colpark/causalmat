Candidate answer:
Panel F3c shows V K-edge XAS spectra (pre-edge/main-edge region, ~5465-5495 eV) stacked in order of state of charge: 0.45 V, 0.60 V, 0.90 V, 1.15 V, flanked by reference standards VO2 and V2O3 (lower V oxidation states) plotted below/behind the cycled spectra, and V2O5 (highest V oxidation state) plotted at the top.

As the potential (state of charge) increases from 0.45 V to 1.15 V, the pre-edge feature (marked by the colored triangles, ~5470 eV) progressively shifts to higher X-ray energy, tracking a shift of the spectral lineshape from resembling the lower-valence VO2/V2O3 standards toward more closely resembling the higher-valence V2O5 standard.

The two conditions differ as follows: at 0.45 V (low potential/low charge capacity), the vanadium K-edge spectrum sits at lower energy, closer in position/shape to the VO2 and V2O3 reference spectra, consistent with vanadium in a lower oxidation state. At 1.15 V (high potential/high charge capacity), the spectrum shifts to higher energy, approaching the V2O5 reference spectrum, consistent with vanadium in a higher oxidation state. This indicates that vanadium undergoes a reversible change in oxidation state (redox) as the electrode is charged and discharged, in tandem with the potential-dependent changes tracked by the normalized charge capacity axis.

The arrowheads marking the Fe K-edge/pre-edge peak position are shifted to progressively higher X-ray energy as the applied potential increases from 0.45 V through 0.60 V and 0.90 V to 1.15 V — the peak visibly moves rightward (toward higher eV) along the 7115–7130 eV region across this series, and the same rightward shift is echoed by the dotted-circle pre-edge features in the magnified inset panel. Relative to the two reference standards, the curve at 0.45 V sits with its edge/pre-edge feature close in energy to the FeO reference trace positioned at the bottom, while the curve at 1.15 V sits with its edge/pre-edge feature close in energy to the Fe2O3 reference trace positioned at the top. In other words, the difference between the low-potential condition (0.45 V) and the high-potential condition (1.15 V) is a shift of the Fe K-edge/pre-edge position toward higher photon energy, tracking a change in local Fe electronic/oxidation environment from one resembling FeO to one resembling Fe2O3, rather than any change in overall spectral shape or intensity scale. This is consistent with the galvanostatic charge process in the acidic Na2SO4/H2SO4 electrolyte driving oxidation of Fe centers in the PBA framework as the cell is charged from 0.45 V to 1.15 V vs Ag/AgCl.

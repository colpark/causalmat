Panel F2c shows three galvanostatic Na/PCO discharge-charge curves (1st black, 2nd red, 40th blue) plotted as voltage (0–3.5 V vs Na/Na+) against capacity (mAh/g). The 1st discharge (black) sloping curve extends out to roughly 870 mAh/g before reaching the low-voltage cutoff (~0.05 V), consistent with the stated "~870 mAh/g" first-discharge value in the context. This first-discharge capacity is actually comparable to, or even larger in relative terms than, typical first-discharge capacities reported for Co3O4/CoO lithiation (which for full conversion to Co metal + Li2O is theoretically ~800–1000 mAh/g), so on its face the huge Na-side number looks like it should indicate a comparably deep conversion reaction as in the Li case. However, the claim under test is that the structural/spectroscopic evidence (SXRD, XAFS, ab initio MD) shows the crystalline Co3O4+CoO phases are largely retained after sodiation and no metallic Co forms — i.e., true chemical conversion is minor. That mismatch is the key point: most of the large first-discharge capacity (~870 mAh/g) must therefore be attributed to non-conversion processes (SEI/electrolyte decomposition, interfacial/pseudocapacitive Na storage, and irreversible side reactions on the large surface area of the porous oxide) rather than actual Co-oxide-to-Co reduction. This is corroborated by the drastic, irreversible capacity fade already visible in the curves themselves — 2nd cycle drops to roughly 400–450 mAh/g and by the 40th cycle to only ~50–70 mAh/g — showing that the large 1st-cycle capacity was not sustained or associated with a stable structural conversion. So the curve is consistent with the paper's argument: the big first Na discharge number is misleading if read as evidence of deep conversion (as it would be by analogy to Li), and the real structural data show only slight/minor sodiation, meaning capacity fade is driven by inherently poor sodiation (redox) activity rather than volume-change-induced mechanical failure of a heavily converted material.

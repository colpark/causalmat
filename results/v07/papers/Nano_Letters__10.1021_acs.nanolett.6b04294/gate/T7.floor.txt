Context from a materials paper:
- Bulk cobalt oxide anodes fade fast: low electronic conductivity and large volume change on cycling
- Make a porous cobalt oxide (PCO) to shorten ion diffusion paths and buffer volume change
- Thermal decomposition of a Co-based MOF in air (300 C, 6 h) yields PCO
- As-prepared PCO is a two-phase Co3O4 + CoO mixture
- Na/PCO fades from ~870 (1st discharge) to ~70 mAh/g after 40 cycles at 0.1 C
- Why cobalt oxide sodiates so poorly vs lithiates is unexplained; volume change is usually blamed
- Compare lithiation and sodiation by in operando SAXS, SXRD and Co K-edge XAFS plus ab initio MD
- First galvanostatic discharge/charge of Na/PCO during in operando measurement
- Sodiation reduces PCO only slightly: crystalline oxide largely retained, no metallic Co
- Na capacity fading stems from poor sodiation activity (minor conversion), not from volume change

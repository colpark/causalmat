Panel (f) is a line/scatter plot, not a microscopy image, so there is no scale bar or imaging technique involved. X-axis is Temperature (°C) from about -100 to 400; Y-axis (on both left and right) is TCC (temperature coefficient of capacitance, presumably), ranging from -1.0 to 0.4. Five curves are plotted for different compositions x = 0, 0.02, 0.04, 0.06, 0.08 (open squares/circles/triangles/inverted triangles/diamonds in red/orange/yellow-green/green/blue, per the legend). All curves show a similar shape: they rise steeply from strongly negative TCC values (about -0.7 to -1.0) at -100 °C, cross into positive territory somewhere between about 0 and 50 °C, reach one or two local maxima (a shoulder near ~50-100 °C and a broader peak near ~200-300 °C, both within roughly +0.1 to +0.3), then decline again toward negative values by 400 °C. Two horizontal dashed reference lines are drawn at TCC = +0.15 and -0.15, apparently marking a tolerance band (e.g., an X7R/X8R-type ±15% capacitance stability window), and the curves for higher x (green, blue) tend to stay within or closer to this band over a broader temperature range than the lower-x curves (red), indicating improved temperature stability of capacitance with increasing dopant/substitution content x.

Panel (a) is a line/scatter graph, not a microscopy image. It plots Current (A) on the y-axis (0–12 A) versus Time (µs) on the x-axis (-0.1 to 0.5 µs), measured at room temperature (@RT) with a 100 Ω load resistor. Four traces correspond to different applied electric field strengths: 20 kV/cm (black squares), 40 kV/cm (red circles), 60 kV/cm (blue triangles up), and 80 kV/cm (green triangles down) — this looks like a pulsed-discharge/breakdown current-transient measurement. Each curve shows a sharp current spike near t≈0 followed by rapid decay and a slower tail toward a small residual steady-state current out to 0.5 µs. Peak current increases monotonically with applied field, from about 2 A at 20 kV/cm up to roughly 9.3 A at 80 kV/cm, and the higher-field curves (60–80 kV/cm) also show a more pronounced, noisy shoulder/plateau in the 0.1–0.3 µs range before settling. There is no scale bar (not a micrograph); it is a standard Cartesian plot with a legend box in the upper right area of the panel.

Figure 5b shows eight Raman mode wavenumbers (ν1–ν8) vs. temperature (25–280 °C) for x=0.06. The ν3 mode (teal curve, top-right panel, shared axis with ν4) decreases monotonically and continuously from ~255 cm⁻¹ at 25 °C to ~245 cm⁻¹ across 75–225 °C, then drops more steeply to ~220 cm⁻¹ at the highest measured temperature (~280 °C). There is no sharp discontinuity, step, or mode splitting/merging visible — the trend is a continuous downward shift throughout, which is broadly consistent with a gradual, PNR-driven evolution rather than a first-order structural transition with an abrupt anomaly.

However, the support is not fully "clean": only ~6 sparsely spaced data points are plotted, and the slope clearly steepens near the top of the range, meaning the curve is not perfectly smooth/linear — this kink could be read either as normal accelerating softening (consistent with gradual PNR dynamics) or as a subtle inflection that a skeptic could argue hints at an underlying transition-like feature. The plot alone (without statistical/error-bar treatment or denser sampling) cannot rule out a subtle discontinuity at that steep-drop region, so the Raman ν3 trace is suggestive of gradual evolution but does not provide unambiguous, high-resolution proof against an abrupt transition.

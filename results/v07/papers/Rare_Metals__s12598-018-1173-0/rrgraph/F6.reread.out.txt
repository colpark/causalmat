This is a 2D line/tribology plot, not a microscopy image. It shows friction coefficient (y-axis, 0.3–1.0) versus sliding distance in meters (x-axis, 0–90 m) for five lubricant/additive concentrations: 0 g·L⁻¹ (black), 0.05 g·L⁻¹ (red), 0.10 g·L⁻¹ (blue), 0.15 g·L⁻¹ (olive/dark yellow), and 0.20 g·L⁻¹ (magenta/purple), distinguished by a color-coded legend in the lower right. All curves start near ~0.4 at very short sliding distance, rise sharply within the first ~5 m, then fluctuate/plateau with noisy oscillations as sliding distance increases to 90 m. The 0.15 g·L⁻¹ (olive) curve shows the lowest steady-state friction coefficient (~0.55–0.70), while 0 g·L⁻¹ (black) and 0.20 g·L⁻¹ (magenta) curves show the highest friction coefficients (~0.75–0.95), with the magenta curve rising notably after ~50 m to become the highest by 80–90 m. There is no scale bar (not a micrograph) and no additional annotations beyond the legend and axis labels.

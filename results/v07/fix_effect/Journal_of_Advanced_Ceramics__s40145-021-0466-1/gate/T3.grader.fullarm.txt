Candidate answer:
Panel F4b (LSV/ORR polarization curves, J vs E) shows NC-1000 (light purple) has a markedly less positive half-wave potential than NPC-1000 (red): NC-1000's current begins dropping sharply around ~0.78-0.80 V, whereas NPC-1000 stays near its limiting current until ~0.83-0.85 V, much closer to the Pt/C (black) curve. Both NC-1000 and NPC-1000 reach essentially the same diffusion-limited current density (~-5 mA/cm2), so the difference is in activity/onset behavior, not in limiting current magnitude.

This is consistent with, but does not directly prove, the pore-confinement/electron-transfer claim. It shows NPC-1000 (porous, N-doped) outperforms NC-1000 (same carbonization temperature, presumably without the ZnCl2-templated fine/medium pores) in overall ORR activity — i.e., pores correlate with better performance. However, panel F4b is a standard RDE LSV curve reporting current vs. potential; it does not directly report electron-transfer number (n) or peroxide yield, which would require Koutecky-Levich analysis or RRDE ring-current data. So the panel supports the general direction of the claim (porosity improves ORR activity) but is not itself evidence for the specific mechanistic claim that pores confine peroxide intermediates to raise electron transfer number.

Question: The claim above says NPC-1000 holds its ORR current advantage over Pt/C across the 8 h methanol-tolerance test and is itself unaffected by methanol. Does the cited panel (F4c) support this as stated?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): Not as stated. The panel shows NPC-1000's current keeps falling by about 2%/h over the full 8 h rather than staying flat, while Pt/C's current levels off near 70% rather than continuing to drop; as a result the gap between the two curves narrows from about 13 points down to about 6 points (a2). So NPC-1000 is not actually unaffected by methanol, and Pt/C does not simply keep dropping - the panel only partially backs the claim.

Grading note: Answer key: the audit ruling on the panel, i.e. the trend recorded in node a2, checked against the panel F4c.

Candidate answer:
Partially. The F4c caption describes an 8 h chronoamperometric (i-t) test of NPC-1000 vs. Pt/C in O2-saturated 0.1 M KOH at 0.35 V/1600 rpm — this supports the claim that NPC-1000 retains more ORR current than Pt/C over 8 h (durability). However, the caption makes no mention of a methanol injection or addition during this run, so nothing in F4c as described substantiates the "unaffected by methanol" / methanol-tolerance portion of the claim. That part would require a separate panel showing a methanol-spike i-t test (comparing the current drop for Pt/C vs. NPC-1000 upon methanol addition), which is not what F4c's caption describes. So F4c supports the durability half of the claim but not the methanol-tolerance half.

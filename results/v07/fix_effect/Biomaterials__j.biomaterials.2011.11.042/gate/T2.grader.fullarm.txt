The panel is a BJH pore-size-distribution curve (dV/dD vs. pore diameter), not an image of channel arrangement/orientation. It shows: 0Co-MBG (black) has a broad peak centered ~5–6 nm with a shoulder near 2 nm; 2Co-MBG (red) has a lower, broader, flatter peak (~5 nm) with reduced total pore volume; 5Co-MBG (green) has the sharpest, tallest, narrowest peak at ~4–5 nm, plus a small rise below 3 nm, and much lower volume at larger pore sizes (>6 nm) than the other two.

This confirms that Co substitution changes the pore-size distribution shape/sharpness and pore volume across the three compositions, which is broadly consistent with (does not contradict) a Co-induced structural perturbation. However, "perturbed ordered orientation of mesopore channels" is a claim about long-range structural order/arrangement (typically evidenced by TEM images of channel arrays or SAXS/XRD low-angle peaks), which a nitrogen-adsorption pore-size-distribution curve cannot directly demonstrate — it measures pore-size uniformity/volume, not spatial orientation or ordering of channels.

Conclusion: F3b provides indirect, partial support (differing, composition-dependent pore-size distributions consistent with altered mesostructure) but does not directly test or confirm the specific claim about disrupted channel orientation; that would require complementary structural imaging (e.g., TEM/SAXS) not present in this panel.

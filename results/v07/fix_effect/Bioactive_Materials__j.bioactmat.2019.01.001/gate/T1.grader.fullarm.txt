Panel F3c shows HP-Mg (grey bars) at all four time points with GS numerically lower than SBF throughout: 7d (~0.15 vs ~3.4), 14d (~0.2 vs ~2.5), 21d (~0.45 vs ~2.05), 28d (~0.78 vs ~2.0). So the directional claim "GS slower than SBF for HP-Mg" is supported at every time point, including the cited 28d values (0.78 vs 2.0). However, the gap and ratio between GS and SBF for HP-Mg shrink monotonically across the series rather than staying constant or growing: the SBF/GS ratio drops from roughly 23-fold at 7d to roughly 4-fold at 21d to only ~2.6-fold at 28d, and the absolute difference falls from ~3.25 mm/y at 7d to ~1.2 mm/y at 28d, as GS-immersed HP-Mg's rate rises steadily while SBF's rate declines. Significance asterisks likewise appear at 7d (largest gap) and diminish/shift at later points, consistent with the effect weakening over time. So while the 28d snapshot cited in the claim is directionally correct, it is actually the weakest evidence point in the series for a GS-vs-SBF difference in HP-Mg, not the strongest — the panel shows the effect is most pronounced early (7d) and progressively narrows by 28d. If "most strongly for HP-Mg" is meant as a cross-metal comparison (HP-Mg's GS/SBF gap being larger than HP-Zn's or P-Fe's at 28d), that comparison is supported by the panel, but as a time-trend statement about HP-Mg it is not — the effect over 7-28d is one of convergence, not reinforcement.

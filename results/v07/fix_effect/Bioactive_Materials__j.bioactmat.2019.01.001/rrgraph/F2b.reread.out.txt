This is panel (b), a 2x3 grid of SEM (scanning electron microscopy) micrographs comparing two conditions across two columns labeled "GS" (left) and "SBF" (right), with three rows labeled A/B, C/D, E/F. Each main image carries a 500 μm scale bar and a smaller inset image (higher magnification, with its own unlabeled scale bar) in the upper-right corner. The top row (A, B) shows the roughest, most textured surface morphology with distinct ridge/wrinkle-like features and a few arrows marking specific surface points (a small circular defect is arrowed in B). The middle row (C, D) shows flatter surfaces with fine linear striations, and D exhibits scattered dark irregular flake/particle deposits not present in C, with an arrow indicating one. The bottom row (E, F) shows the smoothest, most uniform gray surfaces overall, though F still shows faint dark web-like deposits (arrowed) that are absent in E, suggesting a progressive trend where the SBF column shows increasing surface deposition/precipitation relative to the GS column across the rows, while row-to-row (top to bottom) surface roughness decreases.

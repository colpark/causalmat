This is potentiodynamic polarization curve panel, labeled "a)" with "GS" marked in the upper right corner. It plots Potential Applied (V vs. SCE) on the y-axis (from 0.0 down to about -2.1 V) against Log current density (A/cm²) on the x-axis (from -9 to -2). Three sample curves are shown: HP-Mg (black squares), HP-Zn (dark red diamonds), and P-Fe (gray triangles). Each trace shows the characteristic cathodic branch descending from a flat corrosion-potential plateau on the left into a steep active dissolution region as current density increases toward the right (anodic branch), forming the typical V-shaped Tafel polarization curves. The P-Fe curve sits at the least negative potential (near -0.6 to -0.8 V), HP-Zn is intermediate (corrosion potential near -1.2 V) with a passivation-like inflection/hump on its anodic branch around -6 to -5 log current density, and HP-Mg is the most negative (corrosion potential near -1.5 V) with the widest anodic current density range, reaching the highest current densities (up to about 10⁻³ A/cm²) before the curve ends near -2.1 V. No scale bar is present (this is a graph, not a micrograph); axes are linear/log as labeled with tick marks and a legend box in the upper left.

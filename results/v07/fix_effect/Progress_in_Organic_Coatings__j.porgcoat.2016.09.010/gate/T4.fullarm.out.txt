Panel F2c (O 1s high-resolution XPS spectrum, panel c) shows three deconvoluted Ce-related component peaks superimposed under the larger Al-O peak (~531 eV, height ~580, the dominant contributor). Among the Ce components: Ce2O3 (red dashed, centered ~529.5-530 eV) has the tallest peak height, reaching roughly 270 intensity units. Ce-OH (black dotted, centered ~532 eV) is comparable but slightly lower, peaking at roughly 250-260. CeO2 (red dash-dot, centered ~528.5-529 eV) is clearly the smallest of the three, with a peak height of only about 100.

So the relative ranking is: Ce2O3 ≳ Ce-OH > CeO2, with CeO2 markedly weaker than the other two Ce species, consistent with the paper's claim that the Ce conversion film is thin and non-uniform (dominated by oxide/hydroxide forms rather than a well-formed CeO2 phase).

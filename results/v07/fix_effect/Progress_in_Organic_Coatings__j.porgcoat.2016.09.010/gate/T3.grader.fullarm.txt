Question: Given the claim above that the Ce oxide/hydroxide layer on the aluminum nanoparticles is very thin and non-uniform, leaving them only partly covered, what does the Al 2p high-resolution XPS spectrum in Figure 2b (panel F2b) show?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): The Al 2p peak fit resolves an Al2O3 component at about 75.4 eV and a metallic Al peak roughly 4x larger at about 78.2 eV, with the metal peak sitting above (at higher binding energy than) the oxide peak (o20).

Candidate answer:
Panel F2b shows the Al 2p high-resolution XPS spectrum deconvoluted into two components: a dominant peak assigned to metallic Al centered near 78 eV (intensity ~70-73, the tallest feature) and a smaller, broader peak assigned to Al2O3 centered near 75-76 eV (intensity ~15-17).

This dual-peak profile directly supports the claim of thin, non-uniform, partial coverage: if the Ce oxide/hydroxide (and underlying native oxide) layer fully and uniformly encapsulated the nanoparticles, the XPS signal — which probes only the outermost few nanometers — would be dominated or saturated by the oxide/Al2O3 component, with the metallic Al 2p signal strongly attenuated or absent. Instead, the strong, dominant metallic Al peak indicates that the electron beam still reaches and detects the underlying metallic aluminum core through the surface layer, while the smaller Al2O3 peak confirms some oxide is present. Together this is consistent with a thin, incomplete/patchy surface film rather than a thick, continuous coating.

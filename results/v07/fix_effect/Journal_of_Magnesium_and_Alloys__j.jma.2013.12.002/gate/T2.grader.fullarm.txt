Question: Given the context above (Ca added to Mg-9Li-1Al expected to form Al2Ca, and the LA91 and LA91-0.2Ca ingots that were cast), compare the XRD patterns in panels F1c and F1d. What, if anything, differs between the two diffraction patterns?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): Al2Ca diffraction markers appear in the Ca-containing alloy's pattern, near 29.5, 37 and 80.5 degrees, and these markers are absent from the LA91 (no-Ca) pattern (n5).

Grading note: Answer key: the numbers and labels on evidence node n5 (peak positions near 29.5, 37, 80.5 deg and their presence/absence across the two panels), judge-confirmed.

Candidate answer:
Comparison of F1c (as-cast LA91) vs F1d (as-cast LA91-0.2Ca):

Both patterns show the same α-Mg and β-Li peaks at essentially the same 2θ positions (e.g., strong α-Mg peaks near 33°, 37°, 58°, 70°; β-Li peaks near 36°, 51°/65°, 78°), so the base alloy peaks look comparable between the two conditions.

The key difference is that F1d's legend includes an additional phase marker (Al2Ca, filled diamond) that is absent from F1c's legend. Correspondingly, F1d shows extra small diamond-marked peaks not present in F1c — notably a low-angle peak near 30°, a diamond peak overlapping/adjacent to the ~37° α-Mg peak, and a cluster of small diamond peaks in the ~75–85° region. These extra peaks are absent in F1c.

So the patterns are not identical: F1d (Ca-containing alloy) exhibits additional diffraction peaks attributed to Al2Ca that are not present in F1c (Ca-free LA91), consistent with the expected formation of Al2Ca upon Ca addition, while the α-Mg/β-Li peaks otherwise appear similar between the two panels.

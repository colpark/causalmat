Panel (e) shows a grid of infrared thermal camera images (thermographic imaging), used to visualize photothermal heating of a sample (likely nanoparticle solution, e.g., under laser/NIR irradiation) at different concentrations over time. Rows correspond to concentrations of 200, 100, 50, and 25 µg mL⁻¹ plus a H2O control, and columns correspond to irradiation time points from 0 to 6 min in 1-min increments. Each thermal image is a false-color heat map (blue = cool to red/white = hot) with a calibration color bar on the right spanning 25 °C (blue, bottom) to 60 °C (red/white, top); no spatial scale bar is present. The trend shows a clear concentration- and time-dependent temperature rise: the 200 µg mL⁻¹ row develops an intense red/white hot spot by 2–3 min and stays hottest through 6 min, the 100 and 50 µg mL⁻¹ rows show progressively weaker (orange/yellow/green) heating, the 25 µg mL⁻¹ row shows only mild warming, and the H2O control remains blue/cool (near baseline) across all time points, demonstrating dose-dependent photothermal conversion efficiency of the material.

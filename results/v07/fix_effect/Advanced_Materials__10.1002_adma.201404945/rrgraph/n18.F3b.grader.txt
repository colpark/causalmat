Panel (b) is a line/scatter plot (not micrograph, no scale bar) showing photoswitching performance as a function of quantum dot (QD) size. Y-axis: "ON/OFF Ratio" on a logarithmic scale (roughly 1 to a few hundred); X-axis: "QD size [nm]" linear scale from about 4 to 9 nm. Nine data series, each a distinct excitation wavelength from 365 nm to 940 nm (legend: 365, 400, 455, 530, 625, 680, 740, 850, 940 nm), each plotted as connected circular markers with vertical error bars, color-coded from purple/blue/cyan (shorter wavelengths) through red (625 nm, highest curve) to green/olive/orange (longer wavelengths, lowest curves).

Trend: for every wavelength the ON/OFF ratio decreases monotonically as QD size increases from ~4.3 nm to ~9 nm. The red 625 nm curve has by far the highest ON/OFF ratio at all sizes, dropping from ~200 at the smallest QD size to ~30 at the largest. The blue-violet family (365–530 nm) clusters together at intermediate values (~50–100 down to ~15–20). The green (680/740 nm) and orange/olive (850/940 nm) curves are much lower, decreasing from roughly 15 and 5, respectively, down to ~5–7 and ~2 at the largest QD size. Overall the plot demonstrates that switching contrast is highest for red-wavelength readout and for the smallest QDs, declining as QD size grows across all tested wavelengths.

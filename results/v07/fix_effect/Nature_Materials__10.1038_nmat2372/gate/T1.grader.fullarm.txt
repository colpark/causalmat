Question: The claim under test says Li removal restores the pristine molecular structure and that the redox is reversible for both salts. Looking at the EPR panel (F5d) for the recharged Li terephthalate electrode, does the panel support full reversibility, and what does the signal actually show?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): No, the panel does not support full reversibility for Li terephthalate. The recharged electrode still keeps a residual EPR line, at roughly 1/7 of the amplitude seen in the discharged state, indicating the molecular structure is not fully restored on Li removal (a3).

Candidate answer:
Panel F5d (image b4d918ed...D.jpg) shows four overlaid EPR traces (red, green, black, blue) plotted as normalized intensity vs. magnetic field (3400–3480 G), each with a first-derivative lineshape centered near 3441 G. The red trace is essentially flat at zero (no detectable radical/paramagnetic signal), the green trace shows a small derivative signal, the black trace shows a moderate-amplitude signal, and the blue trace shows the largest-amplitude signal (peak ~+10,000 to trough ~-9,000).

Per the caption fragment provided, the black trace is the additional spectrum taken at x=3 (i.e., a recharged/near-fully-delithiated state along the potential–composition curve), included specifically to test reversibility. That black trace still shows a clearly nonzero, moderate EPR signal — it does not collapse back down to the flat red baseline. So rather than fully supporting "restoration of the pristine molecular structure," the EPR data for the recharged sample show a persistent, non-negligible radical/paramagnetic signal, i.e., only partial return toward the pristine (red, EPR-silent) state, not complete reversibility as measured by EPR.

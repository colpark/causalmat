Panel F4a (Ni-MOF, 15,000x) shows densely packed, elongated rod/bar-shaped crystals with smooth, flat, sharply faceted surfaces and well-defined rectangular edges. Panel F4b (MOF-derived NixPyOz, 16,000x) shows rods of similar elongated shape and comparable size/aspect ratio, so the overall rod-like template morphology is preserved between the two conditions. However, the rod surfaces are not the same: in F4b the rod faces are covered with small nanoparticle-like clusters/granules, giving them a rough, textured appearance compared to the smooth, faceted faces in F4a, and additional loose particulate aggregates are visible around the rods in F4b that are not present in F4a. So the answer is: the rod shape/template is retained in both, but the rod surface texture differs — smooth and faceted in Ni-MOF (F4a) versus rough/particle-covered in MOF-derived NixPyOz (F4b) — rather than "no visible difference."

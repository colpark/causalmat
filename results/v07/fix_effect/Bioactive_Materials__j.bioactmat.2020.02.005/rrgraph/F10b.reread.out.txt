Panel (b) shows a photographic antibacterial assay against E. coli using the agar plate (spread/colony-counting) method. Five conditions are compared across columns: Control, TiO2, Er0.5Ce0.1Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O, and Er0.5Ce0.3Ti-O, each imaged under two conditions (rows: "Dark" and "Light"). There is no scale bar (these are whole-plate photographs, not micrographs). In the Dark row, colony density looks fairly similar/high (heavy, near-confluent bacterial growth/streaking) across all samples, including the Er-Ce-Ti-O composites, indicating limited antibacterial effect without light. In the Light row, colony numbers are markedly reduced relative to Control (which retains dense/confluent growth), with TiO2 and the Er-doped samples showing progressively sparser, more countable discrete colonies — Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O and Er0.5Ce0.3Ti-O appear to have the fewest colonies — indicating a light-activated (photocatalytic) antibacterial effect that is enhanced by Er/Ce doping of TiO2.

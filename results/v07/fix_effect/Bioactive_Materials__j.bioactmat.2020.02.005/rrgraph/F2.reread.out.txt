This is a UV-Vis diffuse reflectance/absorbance spectroscopy plot (not a microscopy image — no scale bar present). The x-axis is Wavelength (nm) from 200–800 nm, and the y-axis is Absorbance (a.u.) from 0.0–1.0. Five overlaid spectra are shown for TiO2 and Er-doped TiO2 samples at increasing erbium content: TiO2 (dark blue), Er0.25Ti-O (pink), Er0.5Ti-O (teal/cyan), Er0.75Ti-O (yellow), and Er1Ti-O (purple/violet).

All curves show a broad absorption feature in the UV region (200–380 nm) with a peak/shoulder near 350–370 nm, then a sharp absorption edge dropping steeply between ~380–420 nm (characteristic of the TiO2 bandgap transition), followed by low, flat absorbance in the visible range (420–800 nm) for the plain TiO2 curve. The Er-doped samples, especially Er0.5Ti-O, show additional discrete narrow absorption peaks in the visible/near-IR region at roughly 490 nm, 520–525 nm, and 650–660 nm, which are characteristic Er3+ 4f-4f intra-configurational electronic transitions superimposed on the TiO2 background absorption. The Er0.5Ti-O sample (teal) exhibits the strongest UV absorbance overall (~0.9 peak near 220 nm) and the most pronounced Er3+ peaks (up to ~0.38 at 525 nm), while the undoped TiO2 (dark blue) shows the weakest visible-region absorption, indicating that Er doping introduces additional visible-light absorption features without much change to the fundamental TiO2 band edge position.

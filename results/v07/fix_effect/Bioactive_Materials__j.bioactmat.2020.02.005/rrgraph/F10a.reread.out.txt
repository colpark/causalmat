Panel (a) shows an antibacterial disk-diffusion / plate-count assay against S. aureus, presented as a 2x5 grid of agar plate photographs. Columns compare five materials — Control, TiO2, Er0.5Ce0.1Ti-O, Er0.5Ce0.2Ti-O, and Er0.5Ce0.3Ti-O — while rows compare Dark versus Light conditions. Each plate shows bacterial colonies (small white/cream dots plus streaked inoculation lines) grown on gray agar medium; there is no scale bar, as this is a qualitative colony-count/coverage comparison rather than a micrograph.

Trend: Control plates (both Dark and Light) show dense, near-confluent colony growth. Under Dark conditions, colony density decreases modestly across TiO2 and the Er/Ce-doped Ti-O samples relative to Control. Under Light, the reduction is much more pronounced — TiO2 and especially the Er0.5Ce0.1/0.2/0.3Ti-O samples show markedly fewer colonies than in the Dark row, with colony counts progressively sparser across the doped samples, indicating enhanced photocatalytic/light-activated antibacterial activity for the Er/Ce co-doped TiO2 materials compared to undoped TiO2 and the untreated control.

Candidate answer:
Panels partially support the claim, with caveats about data quality.

Panel F6c (Er 4d): shows a single broad peak fitted at 168.8 eV against very noisy raw data. This binding energy is consistent with Er being present (as Er3+, since Er 4d in this range is typically assigned to the 3+ oxidation state), but the panel itself contains no distinct multiplet or chemical-shift evidence that would independently confirm the 3+ state versus another oxidation state — it only establishes that Er is present with a single peak position.

Panel F6d (Ce 3d): shows the labeled u/u'/u''/u''' and v/v'/v''/v''' positions, which is the standard peak-fitting scheme used to distinguish Ce3+ (u', v') from Ce4+ (u, u'', u''', v, v'', v''') satellite/spin-orbit features — so the labeling scheme is consistent with claiming a Ce3+/Ce4+ mixture. However, the actual red/black trace is dominated by noise with no visibly resolved peaks rising clearly above baseline at most of the marked positions; only the u and v''' region show some signal above noise, so the panel does not visually demonstrate well-resolved Ce3+ and Ce4+ components — the dashed lines appear to be reference assignments rather than fitted peaks clearly extracted from the data.

Overall: the panels are directionally consistent with the text's claim (presence of Er and Ce, with conventional binding-energy assignments matching Er3+ and a Ce3+/Ce4+ mixture), but the signal-to-noise in both spectra is low enough that the specific oxidation-state assignments are not strongly demonstrated by the raw data shown — the support is weak/qualitative rather than quantitatively conclusive.

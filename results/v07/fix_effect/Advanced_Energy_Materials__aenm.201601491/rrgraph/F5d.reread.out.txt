Panel D shows electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS)-derived resistance values plotted against cycle number, from 0 to 250 cycles, with a Nyquist/equivalent-circuit fitting approach implied (not a micrograph — this is a line/scatter plot, no scale bar since it's not imaging data).

Three resistance components are tracked with distinct markers and colors: Re (electrolyte resistance, red circles, dashed line), Rfilm (film resistance, orange diamonds, dashed line), and Rct (charge-transfer resistance, blue open circles, dashed line), each plotted with error bars (presumably standard deviation across replicate cells/measurements).

Trends: Re stays essentially flat and low (~0.5-1 Ω) across all 250 cycles, indicating stable electrolyte/ohmic resistance. Rfilm is also relatively flat and low, fluctuating mildly around 2-4 Ω with modest error bars. Rct shows the most dramatic behavior — it rises sharply from about 3 Ω at cycle 0 to roughly 12 Ω by cycle 30, continues increasing to ~18-20 Ω by cycle 60-100, and plateaus/slightly fluctuates in the 24-28 Ω range from cycle 100 through 250, with the largest and increasing error bars, suggesting growing charge-transfer resistance (e.g., due to interfacial degradation, SEI growth, or electrode passivation) that stabilizes at longer cycling but with more measurement variability.

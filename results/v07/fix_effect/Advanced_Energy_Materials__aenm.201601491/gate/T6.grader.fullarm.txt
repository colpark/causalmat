Question: The claim under test holds that charge is compensated by both V (moving V3+ to V4+ to V5+) and Fe (moving Fe2+ to Fe3+) valence changes. Given the XAS panel (F3d), does the data support this claim as stated?

Answer key (answer_scope: full - the given panels support the whole answer): Not as a full valence swing for Fe. At zero charge the Fe edge already sits about two-thirds of the way toward the Fe3+ line, and across the measured range the edge shifts by only about 1.6 eV of the roughly 5.9 eV span that a complete Fe2+-to-Fe3+ transition would require. The V edge, meanwhile, sits between the V3+ and V5+ positions. So the panel qualifies the claim rather than confirming it outright: some redox activity on both elements is present, but the Fe signal shows only a partial shift, not the full valence change the claim states (o19).

Panel F3d (crop _D.jpg) plots Fe K-edge energy (top, referenced to Fe2+/Fe3+ dashed lines ~7120.5/7126.5 eV) and V K-edge/pre-edge energy (bottom, referenced to V3+/V4+/V5+ dashed lines ~5469.0/5470.0/5470.7 eV) versus normalized charge capacity from 0 to 1.0.

Both series show a monotonic increase in edge energy with charge capacity, which is directionally consistent with progressive oxidation of both Fe and V during charging (supporting the qualitative "both elements contribute to charge compensation" part of the claim). However, the specific oxidation-state endpoints stated in the claim are not well matched by the plotted values: the Fe data points at C=0 already sit close to the Fe3+ reference line (~7124.5-7126 eV), far above the Fe2+ line (~7120.5 eV), so the panel does not show Fe starting near Fe2+ and transitioning to Fe3+ — it looks like Fe is already predominantly Fe3+-like from the start with only a small further shift. Similarly, the V data at C=0 (~5469.8 eV) sits just below the V4+ line (5470.0 eV), not near the V3+ line (5469.0 eV), and rises only to ~5470.6 eV by C=1.0, i.e., toward V5+ but starting near V4+ rather than V3+.

So the panel supports a general trend of both Fe and V oxidizing with charge (consistent with dual-metal charge compensation), but it does not clearly support the specific claimed starting valence states (V3+ and Fe2+) — the edge positions at low charge capacity already lie much closer to V4+ and Fe3+ than to V3+/Fe2+. Overall: partial support — direction/mechanism (both V and Fe redox-active) is consistent with the data, but the precise oxidation-state range (V3+→V4+→V5+, Fe2+→Fe3+) as stated is not fully substantiated by the plotted edge energies, which suggest higher starting oxidation states than claimed.

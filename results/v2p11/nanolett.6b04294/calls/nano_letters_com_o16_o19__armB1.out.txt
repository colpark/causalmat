F4b (SXRD stack, 0.1C) shows the same reflections (major peak ~19.2°, weaker ones ~16.3-16.5°) present in every pattern across the full discharge/charge, with only modest intensity decay. On its own this establishes structural retention: the parent PCO crystalline lattice is not replaced by a new bulk phase's diffraction pattern, and only a small fraction of material appears to lose long-range order (become XRD-silent/amorphous). It says nothing about Co's oxidation state or whether any fraction converts to metal.

F5d (Co K-edge XANES) shows discharged spectra (1st/2nd discharge) retain a strong white line (~1.25 at ~7728 eV), far above Co foil's suppressed white line, and charged spectra fall between CoO and Co3O4. Alone this establishes that Co does not reduce to metallic Co0 on discharge and stays oxidized (roughly Co2+-like on discharge, more oxidized on charge). It is a local/electronic probe and by itself can't confirm the bulk crystal framework is preserved or quantify what fraction of material is affected.

Together: the structural signature persisting (SXRD) plus the absence of a metallic-Co electronic signature (XANES) jointly support that the reaction is predominantly intercalation/solid-solution-like rather than a conversion reaction to Co0 + Na2O-type products — neither the lattice nor the Co oxidation state show hallmarks of full conversion. This cross-validation is stronger than either alone: SXRD rules out a new bulk crystalline conversion phase, XANES rules out bulk metallic Co, closing off the two main signatures conversion would produce. What remains uncertain: the exact phase fraction converted (the modest SXRD intensity loss and the white-line height are not quantitatively tied to a specific fraction), whether any conversion is confined to surface/near-surface regions invisible to bulk-averaging SXRD/XANES, and the precise Co oxidation state distribution.

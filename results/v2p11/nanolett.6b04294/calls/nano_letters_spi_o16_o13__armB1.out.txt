Yes, the upstream SXRD and SAXS evidence is consistent with — and partially supports — the downstream phase-fraction/porosity claim, but only weakly and qualitatively.

F4b shows the SXRD stack for the Na/PCO cell: the main reflection near 2θ≈19.2° (and the weaker one near 16.3°) is present in every trace from OCV through full discharge/charge, only fading modestly in intensity toward the end. This supports "STR/phase/fraction" in the sense that no wholesale phase disappearance or new-phase emergence is visible — the host lattice/phase identity is retained, i.e., phase fraction stays largely stable rather than collapsing to a new crystalline phase.

F3c (2D SAXS contour, q from ~0.003–0.4 Å⁻¹) shows nearly vertical, straight color bands over the full time/voltage range, meaning the scattering intensity at each q is nearly constant across the whole discharge/charge — consistent with little change in nanoscale density heterogeneity (porosity/microstructure) over cycling. F3d confirms this quantitatively: the OCV, 0.45V, 0.136V, 0.11V, 0.02V and 3V curves overlap almost everywhere, with only a small, visible dip/divergence in the ~0.03–0.1 Å⁻¹ range for the 0.11V and 0.02V (deep-discharge) traces.

So the combination supports: the electrode's phase and bulk pore structure are largely conserved through cycling, with a small, localized microstructural perturbation appearing only near full sodiation. It leaves open: whether that small SAXS deviation reflects real pore/porosity change, SEI growth, or an artifact; whether it is reversible on charge; and it gives no quantitative phase-fraction number, only qualitative peak persistence.

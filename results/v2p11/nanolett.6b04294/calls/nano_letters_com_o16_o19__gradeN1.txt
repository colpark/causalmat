Best-guess reconstruction (I have no access to the actual paper, figures, or data — this is inferred from the title and typical practice in this kind of in-operando battery study):

The two measurements are almost certainly (1) in operando synchrotron X-ray diffraction (XRD) and (2) in operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS, XANES/EXAFS at the Co K-edge), paired with AIMD simulations for interpretation.

XRD alone establishes long-range crystallographic structure (STR): it identifies which crystalline phases are present (Co3O4, intermediate rock-salt-like phases, metallic Co, Li2O/NaxOy) and, via Rietveld-type analysis, quantifies their relative phase fractions as lithiation/sodiation proceeds. But XRD is blind to anything that becomes amorphous or nanocrystalline — a common outcome in conversion-reaction electrodes — so it can understate how far the reaction has actually gone.

XAS alone establishes local coordination environment and average Co oxidation state continuously, including in amorphous/disordered regions invisible to XRD. It tracks electronic/chemical reduction but cannot by itself assign discrete crystalline phases or their fractions.

Combined, the two datasets let the authors cross-check crystalline phase fraction (from XRD) against average oxidation-state change (from XAS): if XAS shows continued Co reduction beyond what XRD's crystalline phase fractions would predict, that gap is attributed to amorphous/nanoscale domains reacting outside XRD's detection window. This combination — reinforced by AIMD — likely supports the paper's central claim that Li and Na insertion proceed via distinct pathways, with sodiation stalling at a partially-reduced, more disordered intermediate while lithiation proceeds to more complete, nanocrystalline conversion.

What remains uncertain: exact phase identities, quantitative fraction numbers, and whether amorphization or an intermediate crystalline phase dominates — none of this can be confirmed without the actual figures/data.

Panel F4b (Na/PCO cell) shows a stacked series of in-situ SXRD patterns (16-21° 2θ) collected continuously through a full 0.1C charge/discharge cycle, plotted alongside the voltage-vs-time curve. What this justifies for STR/phase/fraction: the measurement supports that the electrode retains a single, structurally continuous crystalline phase throughout the (dis)charge process - i.e., no bulk transformation into a distinct new crystalline phase, consistent with solid-solution-like (single-phase) behavior rather than a two-phase reaction with a growing/shrinking secondary phase fraction. What it leaves open: this in-situ SXRD trace says essentially nothing about STR/microstructure/porosity. Peak intensity loss is ambiguous - it could reflect partial amorphization, loss of coherently diffracting volume, preferred-orientation/texture drift, or beam/sample-alignment effects, not necessarily pore formation. The measurement cannot establish particle cracking, void/pore development, strain distribution, or real-space morphology; those would require complementary imaging (SEM/TEM) or PDF-type analysis. So the panel alone supports phase-fraction stability but leaves microstructural/porosity evolution entirely undetermined.

Panel F5d (as printed) is a Co K-edge XANES plot (energy axis 7700–7735 eV, the Co K-edge region, not Na), overlaying OCV, 1st/2nd discharge, 1st/2nd charge spectra of the Na/PCO cell against CoO, Co3O4, and Co-foil references. Note: the supplied text bullet calls this "Na XANES," but the actual crop is unambiguously a Co-edge measurement — that label appears to be a mismatch/error, and I'm answering based on what the image actually shows.

What it establishes: across all discharge and charge states, the white-line position/shape stays close to that of the CoO and Co3O4 references and far from the metallic Co-foil trace, which has a distinctly lower, more gradual absorption edge. This supports the conclusion that cobalt remains in an oxidized state throughout cycling — there is no conversion to metallic Co0 during discharge, even at OCV or after full discharge. It also shows a modest, reversible shift in white-line intensity/position between discharge (closer to OCV) and charge (falling between CoO and Co3O4-like character), consistent with a Co-centered redox process (e.g., Co3+/Co2+-like) accompanying Na insertion/extraction, rather than an anionic-only or conversion-type mechanism.

What it leaves open: this qualitative comparison alone does not quantify oxidation state, phase fraction, or perform linear-combination fitting against the references, so it cannot establish precise Co valence numbers or the fractional mixture of CoO-like vs Co3O4-like local environments at each state. It also says nothing about long-range structure/phase identity (that requires diffraction), nor about Na content or site occupancy — only the local Co coordination/valence environment inferred from edge shape.

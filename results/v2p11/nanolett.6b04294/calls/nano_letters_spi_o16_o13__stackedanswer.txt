Panel F4b (Na/PCO cell) shows a stacked series of in-situ SXRD patterns (16-21° 2θ) collected continuously through a full 0.1C charge/discharge cycle, plotted alongside the voltage-vs-time curve. The dominant reflection near 2θ ≈ 19.3° (and a weaker one near 16.3-16.5°) stays at essentially the same 2θ position across the entire time series - no new peak emerges, no existing peak disappears, and no peak splitting is visible. Peak intensity fluctuates modestly (some attenuation and partial recovery) but never drops to baseline.

What this justifies for STR/phase/fraction: the measurement supports that the electrode retains a single, structurally continuous crystalline phase throughout the (dis)charge process - i.e., no bulk transformation into a distinct new crystalline phase, consistent with solid-solution-like (single-phase) behavior rather than a two-phase reaction with a growing/shrinking secondary phase fraction. The persistence of peak position over the full cycle is the direct evidential basis for "phase retained."

What it leaves open: this in-situ SXRD trace says essentially nothing about STR/microstructure/porosity. Peak intensity loss is ambiguous - it could reflect partial amorphization, loss of coherently diffracting volume, preferred-orientation/texture drift, or beam/sample-alignment effects, not necessarily pore formation. The measurement cannot establish particle cracking, void/pore development, strain distribution, or real-space morphology; those would require complementary imaging (SEM/TEM) or PDF-type analysis. So the panel alone supports phase-fraction stability but leaves microstructural/porosity evolution entirely undetermined.

On its own, this SAXS measurement (F3c contour map, q ~0.005–0.4 Å⁻¹, over one full discharge/charge; F3d overlaid I(q) curves at OCV, 0.45V, 0.136V, 0.11V, 0.02V, 3V) supports only a narrow, negative-leaning claim: across the low-q/mid-q window probed, the scattering profile of the Na/PCO electrode stays essentially unchanged through the (dis)charge cycle. The contour bands in F3c run vertically/straight rather than shifting or bending with time (i.e., with state of charge), and the I(q) curves in F3d are nearly superimposable, with only a modest, localized intensity dip in the ~0.03–0.1 Å⁻¹ range appearing at the most reduced/lithiated-analog states (0.11 V and 0.02 V).

What this justifies: it establishes that there is no large-scale, continuous evolution of nanoscale electron-density heterogeneity (i.e., no gross porosity/void or phase-domain size change) detectable by SAXS over most of the (dis)charge, and that whatever structural response does occur is confined to deep discharge (0.11–0.02 V) and is subtle rather than dramatic.

What it leaves open: SAXS alone cannot say whether that small dip reflects a genuine porosity/microstructure change, a phase-fraction change, both, or an instrumental/background artifact — the technique is not phase-selective, so it cannot attribute the dip to a specific chemical phase or distinguish "fraction of a new phase forming" from "pore/domain size shrinking." It also cannot rule out finer changes below its size sensitivity or outside the probed q-range, nor can it establish reversibility on charge from this panel alone.

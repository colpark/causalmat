This measurement (read as PRF/service_capability evidence) settles that Na-Co3O4's limited sodiation reactivity is a comparative, not just isolated, finding. Taken jointly, the retained crystalline oxide phase, absence of metallic Co, near-invariant SAXS pore-scale scattering, and the MD oxidation-degree ranking (Na-Co3O4 ~36% at 2 ps, plateauing after 0.8 ps, versus ~59% for Na-Co3S4 and ~91% for Li-Co3O4) support the conclusion that Na-Co3O4 undergoes an intrinsically lower and earlier-saturating degree of oxidation than either the Li-based analog or the Na-sulfide analog. This gives the structural finding of "minor conversion with no pore-scale reorganization" a chemistry-comparative counterpart: it is not simply that Na-Co3O4 changed little in isolation, but that it changed less, and stopped changing sooner, than the two comparison systems simulated under the same conditions.

What this leaves open is substantial. It does not equate the 2 ps MD timescale with the actual electrochemical cycle timescale probed experimentally, so the ranking and the experimental "minor conversion" are consistent but not directly the same measurement. It does not establish that the cross-chemistry ranking causally explains the SAXS invariance — these remain separate, merely consistent, observations. It says nothing about capacity fading or cycling performance, and cannot adjudicate whether poor sodiation activity or volume change (or something else) drives any fading. It does not explain mechanistically why Na-Co3O4's oxidation degree plateaus early at 0.8 ps, nor link that plateau to the modest SAXS drop or XRD intensity loss.

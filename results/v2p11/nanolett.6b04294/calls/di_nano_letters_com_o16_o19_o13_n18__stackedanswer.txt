What the two panels show: F3c is a smooth 2D SAXS contour (log I vs q vs time) that stays essentially featureless/monotonic in color across the whole discharge-charge cycle — no new bands, no discontinuities tracking with voltage. F3d overlays I(q) curves for OCV, 0.45V, 0.136V, 0.11V, 0.02V and 3V; they collapse onto one master curve over nearly the whole q-range (0.005–0.4 Å⁻¹), with only a slight downward departure of the low-voltage curves (0.11V, 0.02V) in the 0.03–0.1 Å⁻¹ window.

What this settles: SAXS probes electron-density contrast at the ~10–200 Å (mesoscale/nanostructure) length scale. The near-total overlap of I(q) and the straight contours support the claim that no major mesoscale structural reorganization — no significant new pore/void population, no large-scale phase separation or particle-size change — occurs in the electrode over the sodiation/desodiation cycle. That is a legitimate, mechanism-relevant negative result: it rules out gross morphological restructuring as the dominant process, and by elimination points toward mechanisms operating at smaller (atomic/crystallographic) or larger (bulk electrochemical) scales. The small, localized drop at low voltage (deep discharge) is consistent with a minor nanoscale feature appearing only near end-of-discharge, but its magnitude is modest.

What it leaves open: this is a morphological, not chemical, identification — SAXS contrast alone cannot say what phase, compound, or process causes the small low-q dip, nor confirm reversibility, reproducibility (n=1 cell shown, no replicate curves or error bars), or connect it to a specific reaction mechanism. Atomic-scale phase transitions would require complementary XRD/PDF data, which this panel pair does not provide.

This combined result settles a qualitative structural/chemical identification claim, not a mechanistic or quantitative one. What it supports: the crystalline oxide phase of PCO (as detected by SXRD) is largely retained through discharge/charge, since the same reflections persist with only modest intensity loss; and Co does not undergo reduction to the metallic state, since the XANES white line stays strong (~1.25 at ~7728 eV) and clearly distinct from Co foil, with charged-state spectra sitting between CoO and Co3O4 references. Jointly, these two independent lines of evidence (diffraction retention plus a non-metallic, oxidized Co signature) reinforce each other in a way neither could alone — XRD alone couldn't rule out a small below-detection metallic Co fraction, and XANES alone couldn't rule out bulk amorphization of the lattice. Together they justify the conclusion that sodiation causes only slight/partial reduction of PCO without generating metallic Co, and that the material remains structurally largely intact.

What is left open: the quantitative fraction of Co reduced or oxidized, and the precise magnitude/structural meaning of the "modest" intensity loss (disorder vs. minor amorphization vs. particle-level/microstructural effects) are not determined. The mechanism by which PCO resists full reduction is not addressed — only that both signatures happen to persist. The oxidation state of Co in the charged state is only bracketed qualitatively between CoO and Co3O4, not resolved to a specific value or stoichiometry. So this measurement identifies the phenomenon (retained oxide, no metallic Co) but does not establish its extent or its underlying mechanism.

key — the combining proposition
Together SEM (a: deformed, elongated relief with no clear boundaries; b,c: equiaxed grains ~2-4 um with grooved boundaries) and EBSD (a: equiaxed grains mostly ~0.5-1.5 um; c: equiaxed grains ~1.5-3 um) show that panel (a) is not genuinely undifferentiated/deformed as SEM alone suggests -- EBSD reveals it actually contains fine equiaxed grains (~0.5-1.5 um) that SEM's surface-relief/grooving contrast is too coarse to resolve as boundaries. For panel (c) the two methods converge on the same equiaxed, grooved-boundary microstructure with overlapping size estimates (SEM ~2-4 um vs EBSD ~1.5-3 um), cross-validating the coarser grain population by an independent method. Combined, the pair indicates feature size spans a fine population that only EBSD resolves (a, ~0.5-1.5 um) up to a coarser population both methods agree on (c, roughly 1.5-4 um overlap).
limits
  • Only panels (a) and (c) have both SEM and EBSD; panel (b)'s ~2-4 um estimate rests on SEM alone and is not cross-validated by EBSD
  • SEM detects boundaries via topographic grooving from etching while EBSD detects them via crystallographic misorientation -- different physical bases, so the numeric overlap in (c) is approximate agreement between two different kinds of measurement, not one measurement confirmed twice
  • The pair does not establish that SEM and EBSD imaged the identical field/area in panel (a), so it cannot rule out that the 'deformed' SEM region and the equiaxed EBSD region are not exactly the same location
  • Only three panels are given, so no continuous size-versus-state relationship is established, only an ordering across the sampled panels
not identifiableWhether SEM's 'deformed, no clear boundaries' reading of panel (a) reflects a genuinely different (less recrystallized) area than the one EBSD sampled, or whether SEM simply cannot resolve grooved boundaries at the fine ~0.5-1.5 um scale that EBSD detects, cannot be separated from the evidence given.

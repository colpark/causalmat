key — the combining proposition
Considered together, the two panels show that La addition co-produces (i) a structurally distinct set of new XRD reflections (~21-37, 50, 67 deg) absent from both the RE-free and Sm patterns, and (ii) a spatially distinct second, finer bright boundary phase (arrow B) beside the arrow-A particles in the La-containing microstructure, while Sm shows neither the extra reflections nor is called out as having this second boundary feature. Combining the two panels lets one say that La's addition is associated with both a new crystallographic signature and a new dendrite-boundary morphological feature that do not appear with Sm, i.e., La changes phase content in a way Sm does not, with a plausible boundary location for that change.
limits

  • SEM alone only shows a second bright boundary feature next to the arrow-A particles; imaging contrast alone does not establish that this feature is crystallographically distinct rather than, e.g., a compositional gradient, orientation contrast, or different morphology of an existing phase
  • The two panels do not, by themselves, chemically or crystallographically link the SEM boundary feature (arrow B) to the specific XRD reflections -- confirming that the extra reflections and the second boundary phase are the same entity would require additional linking evidence (composition or diffraction taken at that location) not described in these two panels
  • Both observations are qualitative/categorical presence-absence calls (extra peaks yes/no; second boundary phase called out yes/no) rather than measured quantities, so no dose-response or magnitude relationship can be drawn from them
not identifiableWhether the new XRD-detected phase and the SEM-observed second boundary phase (arrow B) are one and the same entity, versus two independently co-occurring changes in the La-containing alloy, cannot be separated from these two panels alone

key — the combining proposition
Together, the two panels support that the La-containing alloy possesses a distinct compound not found in the Sm alloy: the extra XRD reflections (~21-37, 50, 67 deg) seen only in the Al-Cu-Mn-La pattern are matched by a separate DSC dissolution event (shoulder B, ~638 C) beyond the Al2Cu-related peak A (~605 C), while the Sm pattern shows neither the extra reflections nor (by the same logic) the extra thermal event. The convergence of a structural signal (XRD) and an independent thermal signal (DSC) both appearing only with La is stronger support for a La-specific compound than either alone.
limits
  • Neither observation, nor their combination, independently proves the specific identity of the new phase (i.e., that it is Al6Cu6La with a cubic NaZn13-type structure); that assignment is stated as coming from comparison to known literature values for the DSC peak, not derived from indexing the XRD reflections against this pairing
  • The pairing does not quantify how much of the new phase forms, only that its signatures (extra reflections, a distinct dissolution event) are present
  • Absence of extra reflections and of a distinct second DSC event in the Sm alloy shows no detected new phase, not proof that Sm forms literally none - it could be below the detection limit of both methods
  • The two measurements are of different kinds (positional/categorical diffraction counts vs. thermal-event temperatures) and are linked only by co-occurrence in the same alloy, not by a derived quantitative relationship
not identifiableWhether the new phase is specifically Al6Cu6La (cubic NaZn13-type) versus some other unidentified La-containing intermetallic cannot be separated from the XRD+DSC comparison alone; both signatures are consistent with, but not uniquely diagnostic of, that specific compound

key — the combining proposition
Taken together, the SEM finding of a second, finer bright boundary phase beside the arrow-A particles (F1c) and the DSC finding of two distinct dissolution events -- weak peak A (~605 C) and shoulder B (~638 C), assigned to Al2Cu and Al6Cu6La respectively (F3) -- support that the La alloy contains two boundary phases rather than one: the coarser arrow-A particles (consistent with Al2Cu, peak A) and a morphologically distinct finer phase (consistent with the higher-temperature Al6Cu6La shoulder B). Neither panel alone supports two phases: SEM alone shows only a morphological distinction with no chemical/thermal identity, and DSC alone shows only two closely-spaced thermal events with no spatial picture of where they arise.
limits
  • The DSC assignment of shoulder B to Al6Cu6La is a label already given with the observation, not something derived by combining it with SEM; the pairing does not itself prove that assignment.
  • SEM and DSC are not spatially or chemically cross-referenced here (no EDS/mapping tying the finer boundary phase to the shoulder-B event), so the correspondence between the two finer-phase observations is inferred, not directly linked.
  • Peak A (~605 C) and shoulder B (~638 C) are only ~33 C apart and B is described as a shoulder, not a fully resolved peak, so DSC alone only weakly resolves two separate events.
  • Neither observation establishes crystal structure (e.g., a cubic NaZn13-type lattice); that requires diffraction data not present in these two panels.
not identifiableWhether the finer SEM boundary phase is specifically the source of the DSC shoulder-B event (as opposed to the shoulder arising from some other minor constituent) is not identifiable from these two panels alone, since there is no spatial/chemical link tying the SEM feature to the thermal event.

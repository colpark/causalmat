key — the combining proposition
Neither observation alone pins down phase identity: the XRD peak moving from 20.2 deg to ~20.6 deg with the 18.8 deg shoulder fading only shows that the crystalline structure changes shape/position between cast film and fiber (f-P0.00), without an independent phase label attached to that shift; the FTIR intensity changes (gamma 1234 cm-1 weaker, beta 1275/840 cm-1 more prominent in fiber films versus cast film) show band-specific evidence of a gamma to beta shift but no direct crystallographic confirmation. Put together, the two convergent, independently derived signatures (a structural/diffraction change and a vibrational/band-specific change) corroborate that the same transition is being tracked from both directions: the cast film's mixed-phase XRD/FTIR signature shifts toward a beta-dominated one in the electrospun fiber.
limits
  • Does not quantify the beta-phase fraction or degree of crystallinity, only qualitative peak/band presence and relative strength
  • Does not establish that the fading 18.8 deg XRD shoulder specifically corresponds to the gamma band seen in FTIR (or to alpha) -- no explicit peak-to-band assignment is given in either observation
  • Does not establish alpha-phase identity in the cast film, since no alpha-diagnostic FTIR bands are listed among the given evidence
  • Does not establish the process mechanism responsible for the phase shift (e.g., electric-field poling versus mechanical stretching during electrospinning)
  • Single end-point comparison (cast film vs fiber film), not a graded/dose series, so it supports a before/after contrast only, not a relationship across a variable
not identifiableWhether the beta-phase increase in the fiber films is driven by electric-field poling or by mechanical stretching during electrospinning cannot be separated from this evidence -- both operate simultaneously in the electrospinning process, and both observations are static end-point structural/vibrational measurements of the finished fiber rather than process-resolved measurements.

limits

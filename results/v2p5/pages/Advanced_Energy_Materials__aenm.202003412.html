key — the combining proposition
Together, the DFT energetics (Observation A) and the EELS spectra (Observation B) provide convergent evidence that Co oxidation is thermodynamically favorable and does occur experimentally under anodic potential: DFT computes that CoCrOOH has the lowest Delta G for the Co2+->Co3+ step and CoFeOOH the lowest Delta G for the Co3+->Co4+ step (with CoFeCrOOH intermediate and Co(OH)2 highest for both), while EELS independently shows the Co L3/L2 edges shifting to higher energy loss and the L2 intensity growing as potential is raised from dry/open-circuit to 1.4 V and 1.5 V. The pairing lets the computed thermodynamic preference be read as corroborated by an experimental signature of increasing Co oxidation state with potential, rather than resting on either the calculation or the spectroscopy alone.
limits
  • The EELS edge shift and L2 growth are a single lumped experimental signature of rising Co oxidation state as a function of potential; they do not by themselves resolve whether the change corresponds to the Co2+->Co3+ step, the Co3+->Co4+ step, or both, whereas DFT computes these as two separate, composition-specific steps.
  • DFT gives a composition-resolved ranking across four distinct materials (CoCrOOH, CoFeOOH, CoFeCrOOH, Co(OH)2); nothing in the cited observations indicates the EELS measurement itself spans that same set of compositions, so the pairing cannot confirm that the computed cross-composition ordering is reproduced experimentally.
  • One side is a computed thermodynamic quantity (Delta G) and the other is an experimental spectroscopic proxy (edge-loss position and L2 intensity) for oxidation state; these are different kinds of quantities, so agreement in trend does not equate to a quantitative match between predicted energetics and measured oxidation extent.
not identifiableWhether the experimentally observed Co oxidation-state shift (EELS) reflects the Co2+->Co3+ step, the Co3+->Co4+ step, or a mixture of both cannot be separated from the two observations alone, since EELS reports a single edge/intensity signature while DFT treats the two steps as distinct.

key — the combining proposition
The DFT ordering of Delta G for the Co2+->Co3+ and Co3+->Co4+ redox transitions (CoCrOOH lowest for the first step, CoFeOOH lowest for the second, CoFeCrOOH intermediate between the two binaries, Co(OH)2 highest for both) is consistent with, and offered as the mechanistic reason for, the LSV finding that the Fe-Cr-containing catalyst Co5Fe3Cr2 reaches eta10 = 452 mV before any other catalyst tested in neutral 0.5 M KHCO3, outperforming IrO2. Together the two observations support the qualitative claim that adding Fe and Cr to Co(OH)2 lowers the energy needed to advance through the Co2+->Co3+->Co4+ sequence relative to the unmodified Co(OH)2 baseline, and that this correlates with the ternary catalyst reaching the 10 mA cm-2 benchmark at lower overpotential than IrO2.
limits
  • CoFeCrOOH is computed as intermediate between the two binaries (CoCrOOH lowest for step 1, CoFeOOH lowest for step 2), not the lowest for either individual step, so the DFT ordering alone shows a compromise rather than a uniquely optimal ternary pathway; the paper's mechanistic claim of a clean 'Fe-Cr synergy lowering both steps' is stronger than the raw four-way ordering by itself.
  • The DFT panel (F1a) computes Delta G for CoCrOOH, CoFeOOH, CoFeCrOOH and Co(OH)2, while the LSV panel (F5c) reports eta10 for Co5Fe3Cr2 against IrO2 and other catalysts; the pair does not establish that the specific stoichiometry Co5Fe3Cr2 tested electrochemically is the same material as the CoFeCrOOH structure used in the DFT calculation.
  • Delta G is a computed thermodynamic quantity for a single redox step; eta10 is an experimentally measured kinetic overpotential at a fixed current density that also reflects conductivity, active-site density, and mass transport -- the pair cannot show that the thermodynamic ordering is what determines the measured kinetic ranking, only that the two are directionally compatible.
not identifiableWhether the lower eta10 for the Fe-Cr-containing catalyst arises from the lower computed redox-transition energies themselves, or from other unmeasured kinetic/material factors (e.g., conductivity, active surface area) that were not separated from the thermodynamic ordering by these two observations

key — the combining proposition
XPS (Co3+ fraction of Co 2p) and EELS (Co L3 edge position) are two independent spectroscopic signatures of Co oxidation state, measured on the same four samples. Their agreement -- both show Co5Fe5 and Co5Fe3Cr2 elevated (larger Co3+ component; L3 edge blue-shifted 1.35 eV) relative to Co and Co5Cr5 -- cross-validates that the Fe-containing samples genuinely have a higher Co valence/oxidation state, rather than one technique's reading being a method-specific artifact (e.g., XPS peak-fitting/charging bias, or an EELS edge shift arising from a non-valence cause such as coordination or thickness effects).
limits
  • Neither observation, alone or combined, gives an absolute oxidation-state value (e.g., a Co2+/Co3+ ratio or formal valence number) -- only a relative ordering across the four samples.
  • The two measurements are different kinds of quantity (a normalized peak-area fraction from XPS vs. an edge-position energy shift in eV from EELS), so they cannot be combined into a single quantitative correlation -- only compared as concordant/discordant directional evidence on the same sample set.
  • The pair does not establish the electronic mechanism (e.g., charge transfer from Fe) by which Fe incorporation would raise Co valence; it only shows two independent measurements point the same way.
  • Because Co5Fe3Cr2 contains both Fe and Cr, the combined evidence does not by itself isolate Fe as the sole compositional driver versus a Fe-Cr combined effect; that discrimination, if it exists, comes from comparison with Co5Cr5, not from combining XPS with EELS.

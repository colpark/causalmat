key — the combining proposition
Read together, the two EIS traces support a material-level contrast rather than a single-material trend: PCL-LS retains a high impedance floor at 0.01 Hz (only ~10^6.25 to ~10^5.85 ohm cm2 over 7 days, less than one decade of drop), while pure PCL shows independent signatures of coating degradation over the same immersion period -- a collapsing high-frequency phase angle (~72 to ~24 deg at 10^4.8 Hz) and a growing time constant in the 1-10 Hz range. Combining the two panels is what lets the paper say PCL-LS keeps its barrier impedance while pure PCL loses it: neither panel alone names both materials, so the contrast (not just a single trend) depends on both.
limits
  • The two sides are not the same EIS metric: A is a low-frequency impedance magnitude (|Z|, ohm cm2) and B is a high-frequency phase angle (degrees) plus a qualitative time-constant shift; the comparison is across quantity kinds, not a shared axis, so no quantitative ratio or matched threshold can be drawn between the ~0.4-decade |Z| drop and the ~48 deg phase drop.
  • The two observations come from different samples/panels (F7d for PCL-LS, F7a for pure PCL), so any difference in behavior is confounded with whatever else differs between the two coating preparations, not isolated to the LS additive alone.
  • Only endpoint-style values are given for A (day 0 vs day 7) and a general immersion trend for B; this supports an ordering/contrast between the two materials, not a fitted degradation rate or a common time course.

key — the combining proposition
Together, the two FTIR:ATR spectra show that alkali treatment gave the AZ31-OH surface a distinct hydroxyl signature (sharp 3700 cm-1 band, absent on bare AZ31) while the PCL and PCL-LS samples both retain the characteristic PCL bands (2944, 2865 cm-1 CH2; 1723 cm-1 C=O). Combined, this supports that the substrate was chemically converted to a hydroxyl-bearing surface and that the PCL matrix's own chemical bonds persist unchanged when LS is added, i.e. each material's chemical identity/bonding signature is confirmed separately.
limits
  • Both observations are single, surface-averaged FTIR:ATR spectra from separate sample sets (bare/treated AZ31 vs. PCL/PCL-LS films); neither is spatially resolved, so together they cannot speak to how uniformly the coating covers the substrate or whether the coverage is defect-free.
  • No shift, broadening, or new peak in the PCL region (2944, 2865, 1723 cm-1) or hydroxyl region (3700 cm-1) is reported that would indicate a new interfacial bond forming between the substrate hydroxyls and the coating; the data show each material's own bonds intact, not an interaction between them.
  • The samples compared for A (bare AZ31 vs AZ31-OH) are not the same samples compared for B (PCL vs PCL-LS), so the pairing does not directly test the substrate-coating interface itself.
not identifiableWhether the unchanged PCL bands in PCL-LS next to the hydroxylated substrate reflect genuine interfacial chemical bonding versus simple physical co-presence of unreacted materials cannot be separated from these two spectra alone.

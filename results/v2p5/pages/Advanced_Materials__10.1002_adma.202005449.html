key — the combining proposition
The seamless, gap-free interface seen in cross-section (SEM: laminated base merging continuously into the particulate top) is consistent with the electrode being able to deliver measurable, plateau-defined discharge capacity (~88 uAh cm-2 at 20 uA cm-2, ~25 uAh cm-2 at 200 uA cm-2, plateau near 1.75-1.85 V) -- i.e. an unbroken, crack-free MXene-to-active-material junction is compatible with functioning as a continuous electronic/ionic pathway that supports capacity delivery across the tested rates.
limits
  • The SEM image is a single static cross-section, not a series taken at or correlated with different current densities, so it cannot show that interface continuity specifically tracks the drop from ~88 to ~25 uAh cm-2 between 20 and 200 uA cm-2.
  • The pair does not localize the capacity fade to the interface: the fade could equally arise from particle-level ion diffusion limits, MXene sheet conductivity, or electrolyte access, none of which the SEM distinguishes from interfacial resistance.
  • No dose-response or comparative condition (e.g., a sample with a gapped/cracked interface) is present, so the absence-of-gap observation cannot be tied quantitatively to the magnitude or rate-dependence of the capacity values.
  • The plateau voltage (1.75-1.85 V) and capacity values are absolute electrochemical readings, not normalized to active mass or compared against a control structure, so the structural and electrochemical observations sit at different levels of quantity and cannot be directly cross-validated.
not identifiableWhether the capacity fade at higher current density (88 to 25 uAh cm-2) is caused by interfacial charge-transport limitation at the laminated/particulate junction versus intrinsic rate limitation within the LTO/LFP particles or MXene network cannot be separated from this pair.

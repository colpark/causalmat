key — the combining proposition
Together the two measurements suggest that the phase-coexistence phenomenon depends on domain size. XRD (Observation A) shows that near a transition, two structural branches genuinely coexist for PC (004) over a shaded window of about 150-175 K. PL (Observation B), taken at a single fixed temperature of 80 K, shows single-band spectra for the smallest domains (20-50 nm) and the largest domains (>300 um), but a split two-band spectrum (~750 and ~785 nm) only for intermediate domains (50-200 um). Combining the two indicates that only the intermediate-size domains still display coexistence-type behavior down at 80 K, while the smaller and larger domains do not - i.e., the position of the coexistence regime relative to a fixed low temperature differs with domain size, consistent with the coexistence/transition range being size-dependent.
limits
  • Observation A is a temperature scan (giving one coexistence window, ~150-175 K) and does not itself vary domain size, so it cannot show how that window shifts with size.
  • Observation B is a single-temperature snapshot (80 K only) across domain sizes and does not scan temperature, so it cannot independently confirm a coexistence window or its width for each size class.
  • The pair gives only three domain-size groups and one fixed-temperature read for B, and one window for A - too few points to establish a monotonic ordering of transition temperature with size, or to show the window narrowing with size.
  • The two techniques probe different observables (structural XRD branch-splitting vs. optical PL band count), so agreement between them is suggestive, not a direct measurement of the same window.

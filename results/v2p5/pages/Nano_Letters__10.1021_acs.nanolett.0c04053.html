key — the combining proposition
DLS (solution-phase, ensemble) and SEM (dry-state, direct imaging) agree on both the magnitude and the ordering: microfluidic particles sit in the ~300-400 nm (DLS) / ~350-400 nm (SEM) range with a narrow/tight distribution, while hydrothermal particles sit near ~1000 nm (DLS) / ~900-950 nm (SEM) with a broader spread. Because two independent measurement modalities converge on the same size scale and the same narrow-vs-broad contrast, the size difference between the two synthesis routes is corroborated rather than being an artifact of a single technique.
limits
  • DLS reports an intensity-weighted hydrodynamic diameter from particles in suspension (ensemble average, sensitive to aggregation and larger particles skewing the mode upward), while SEM reports a directly measured dry-state core size from individual imaged particles (subject to which particles happen to be imaged/segmented) -- the two numbers are not identical measurement quantities even though both are length scales, so the pair does not establish an exact absolute size, only that the two methods agree on scale and ordering
  • the values are close but not numerically identical (DLS ~300-400 nm vs SEM ~350-400 nm for microfluidic; DLS ~1000 nm vs SEM ~900-950 nm for hydrothermal), consistent with the known solvation/hydrodynamic offset of DLS versus dry SEM core measurement
  • neither observation nor their combination speaks to why the two synthesis routes differ in size or distribution width -- that mechanism is outside these two panels

key — the combining proposition
The microfluidic route yields both a smaller, more narrowly distributed particle size (DLS mode ~300-400 nm vs. a broader ~1000 nm mode for hydrothermal) and a higher encapsulation efficiency for every cargo tested (siRNA ~53 vs ~37%, DOX ~47 vs ~34%, BSA ~59 vs ~43%, each starred), so the two observations co-vary in the same direction across the two synthesis routes -- consistent with smaller/more uniform particle size being associated with higher encapsulation.
limits
  • Particle size and encapsulation efficiency are both compared only across the same two synthesis routes (microfluidic vs hydrothermal), so route is a common cause of both readouts; the pair does not isolate particle size itself as the driver of encapsulation apart from other route-specific differences (e.g., nucleation/growth kinetics, crystal defect density, mixing conditions) that could independently raise encapsulation.
  • Only two conditions (two routes, two size regimes) are compared -- too few points to support a dose-response relationship between size and encapsulation efficiency, only an ordering under one confounded comparison.
  • The size read (DLS mode, nm, single snapshot per sample) and the encapsulation read (%, per cargo, three different cargo types) are different kinds of quantities measured by different techniques, so no direct quantitative size-to-efficiency mapping is given.

  • Other adsorbed or residual components (e.g. leftover BSA, buffer ions) present on the particle could also contribute to the charge shift; the pairing does not rule them out
  • The two observations are different kinds of quantity (a spatial elemental distribution vs. a bulk electrical measurement), so their combination is correlational, not a demonstrated point-to-point mechanism
  • The numeric difference between the DNA-aptamer value (-16 mV) and the RNA-aptamer value (-14.5 mV) is not explained by anything in this pair

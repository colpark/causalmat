key — the combining proposition

HRTEM (shown for the w/HCl sample) establishes, at the local real-space scale, that crystalline patches and amorphous-like regions physically coexist within the same material rather than the sample being a single phase of uniform intermediate order. Put together, the two support reading the XRD peak broadening/background as reflecting a genuine mixed-phase (crystalline + amorphous) character of the material, i.e., that STR/phase differences across conditions correspond to a real spatial coexistence of two distinct phases rather than merely a shift in some single homogeneous order parameter.

  • Does not give a quantitative crystalline/amorphous phase fraction for any sample -- both observations are qualitative/descriptive (peak shape and background level; patch identification by eye), not measured fractions.

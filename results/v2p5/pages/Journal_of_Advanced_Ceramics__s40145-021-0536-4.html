key — the combining proposition
Across all three series, porosity decline and hardness rise track each other with sintering temperature, and the series ranking is preserved end-to-end: S1, which densifies most (8.7 to 0.4%), reaches the highest hardness (9.3 to 15.6 GPa); S2 (7.9 to 0.7%) reaches the next-highest hardness (9.4 to 14.8 GPa); S3, which stays least dense (11.2 to 7.7%), reaches the lowest hardness (9.0 to 14.1 GPa). Put together, the two panels support a consistent inverse covariation between porosity and hardness that holds both within each series as temperature rises and across the three series' final states.
limits
  • Both porosity and hardness are reported as functions of the same sintering temperature for the same three series, so temperature is a common driver behind both trends; the panels show porosity and hardness moving together, not porosity acting directly on hardness.
  • Only three series (S1, S2, S3) with start/end values each are given, which supports an ordering/ranking comparison but is too few points to establish a quantitative dose-response curve linking porosity level to hardness level.
  • No mechanism (e.g., pores as crack-initiation sites, reduced load-bearing cross-section) is evidenced in these two panels themselves to justify the causal arrow beyond the observed covariation.
not identifiableWhether porosity reduction itself drives the hardness increase (n8 causes n12) versus porosity and hardness both being independently driven by sintering temperature (e.g., via grain growth or bonding changes) cannot be separated from these two panels alone.

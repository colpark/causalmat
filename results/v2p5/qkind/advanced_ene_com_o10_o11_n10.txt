Question: What does each of these two measurements establish on its own about STR/chemistry/composition, and what does putting them together add that neither gives alone?

Observation A (XPS): Deconvolved Co3+ component of Co 2p is larger in Co5Fe5 and Co5Fe3Cr2 than in Co and Co5Cr5
Observation B (EELS): Co L3 edge of Co5Fe3Cr2 and Co5Fe5 blue-shifted by 1.35 eV relative to Co and Co5Cr5

Proposition: XPS (Co3+ fraction of Co 2p) and EELS (Co L3 edge position) are two independent spectroscopic signatures of Co oxidation state, measured on the same four samples. Their agreement -- both show Co5Fe5 and Co5Fe3Cr2 elevated (larger Co3+ component; L3 edge blue-shifted 1.35 eV) relative to Co and Co5Cr5 -- cross-validates that the Fe-containing samples genuinely have a higher Co valence/oxidation state, rather than one technique's reading being a method-specific artifact (e.g., XPS peak-fitting/charging bias, or an EELS edge shift arising from a non-valence cause such as coordination or thickness effects).

Limits:
  [0] Neither observation, alone or combined, gives an absolute oxidation-state value (e.g., a Co2+/Co3+ ratio or formal valence number) -- only a relative ordering across the four samples.
  [1] The two measurements are different kinds of quantity (a normalized peak-area fraction from XPS vs. an edge-position energy shift in eV from EELS), so they cannot be combined into a single quantitative correlation -- only compared as concordant/discordant directional evidence on the same sample set.
  [2] The pair does not establish the electronic mechanism (e.g., charge transfer from Fe) by which Fe incorporation would raise Co valence; it only shows two independent measurements point the same way.
  [3] Because Co5Fe3Cr2 contains both Fe and Cr, the combined evidence does not by itself isolate Fe as the sole compositional driver versus a Fe-Cr combined effect; that discrimination, if it exists, comes from comparison with Co5Cr5, not from combining XPS with EELS.

Does this proposition compare quantities of different KINDS?

Be careful about one distinction, because getting it wrong is the common failure here:

  NOT a mismatch: a proposition that RELATES two different quantities -- a yield stress against a
  particle density, a domain size against a spectral feature count, a release rate against a surface
  area. Relating two different quantities across the same samples is what a covariation or a
  mechanism check IS. That is the item working, not an error.

  A mismatch: two quantities SET AGAINST EACH OTHER as if they were the same kind -- a charge
  capacity against a discharge capacity, a rate against an accumulated amount, a test temperature
  against a service temperature, a yield stress against a fracture or flow stress, an instantaneous
  value against a time-averaged one, a value at one cycle against a value at another.

Rule `same_kind` or `mixed`. If mixed, quote both quantities, name each kind, and say whether the
observations contain a same-kind pair that could be used instead. If they do not, the repair is to
state the mismatch as a limit, not to substitute numbers that are not there.
